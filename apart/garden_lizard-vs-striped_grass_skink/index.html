How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Oriental Garden Lizard, as the Striped Grass Skink profile puts it

Calotes versicolor

Non-glossy agamid lizard with crest of spine-like scales on neck and back; arboreal perching habit

Striped Grass Skink: Medium-sized sandy skink with three prominent pale greenish-white longitudinal stripes running down an olive-brown back along riverbank grasslands.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Oriental Garden LizardStriped Grass Skink
Size30–40 cm total length (half of which is the long tail)—
BodyHighly laterally compressed; angular profile—
CrestRow of enlarged scales along nape and spine — most visible in males—
ColourResting = brown to grey-brown; breeding male = vivid orange-red head and throat—
EyesCan move independently — each eye can look in a different direction—
BehaviourPerches upright on vertical surfaces; head-bobs repeatedly; does press-up displays—
Body size—Medium-sized, slender skink with powerful limbs and an elongated tail
Dorsal pattern—Olive-brown with 3 prominent whitish or pale green longitudinal stripes bordered with black (Smith 1935)
Flank pattern—Broad dark brownish lateral band adorned with white spots or dashed lines
Dorsal scalation—Scales on the back carry 2 or 3 distinct low keels (Sharma 2002)
Ventral surface—Uniformly pale greenish-white or yellowish, smooth and glossy
Midbody scales—Arranged in 34 to 38 rows around the body circumference

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
