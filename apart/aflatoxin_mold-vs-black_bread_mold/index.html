Aflatoxin Mold or Black Bread Mold? अफ्लाटॉक्सिन फफूंद या रोटी की फफूंद?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

अफ्लाटॉक्सिन फफूंदAflatoxin MoldAspergillus flavus
रोटी की फफूंदBlack Bread MoldRhizopus stolonifer

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Bread Mold, as the Aflatoxin Mold profile puts it

Grey-black cottony threads with black heads, fast-spreading on bread; not an aflatoxin producer

रोटी की फफूंद — धागे जैसी, तेज़ी से फैलती

Aflatoxin Mold: A yellow-green powdery mould on stored grain, groundnut or maize is treated as A. flavus and the grain as unsafe; the colour of the mould does not tell you how much toxin is present, so the rule is the grain, not the mould.

Black Bread Mold: Bread with white fuzz that turns black within days = Rhizopus stolonifer. It is the most common bread mold globally. Discard it.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Aflatoxin MoldBlack Bread Mold
Appearance on grainGreen, yellow or olive-coloured powdery coating (conidiospores) on grain surface and in grain crevices—
SmellMusty, mouldy odour; distinctive fungal smell in contaminated grain lotsCharacteristic earthy or slightly sweet mouldy smell; stronger after spore release
TextureFine powdery coating; rubs off easily onto fingers and clothFuzzy or cottony; easily rubbed off in early stages; firmer as sporulation occurs
Grain damageAffected grains may be discoloured (yellow-brown spots or streaks); interior may be infected without visible exterior signs—
Affected cropsGroundnuts, maize, rice, wheat; can affect many stored commodities—
Conditions for growthHigh moisture (>80% relative humidity) and warmth (25–35°C) required; grows slowly in cool, dry conditions—
Initial appearance—White, fine, cotton-like mycelium on bread or vegetable surface
Mature appearance—Black or dark grey sporangiophores (fruiting stalks) arising from white mycelium; looks like tiny black dots or tufts
Mycelial growth—Spreads rapidly; covers entire bread loaf within 3–5 days in warm, humid conditions
Sporangia (spore sacs)—Small, round, black or dark grey structures at the tip of fruiting stalks; 0.5–2 mm diameter
Growth rate—Among the fastest fungi; visible growth within 24–48 hours on moist bread
Substrate—Prefers moist, high-carbohydrate substrates (bread, rice, potatoes); less common on protein-rich foods
