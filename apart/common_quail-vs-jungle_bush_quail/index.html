How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Quail, as the Jungle Bush Quail profile puts it

Common Quail]] (Coturnix coturnix)

Larger size, sandy-buff plumage with anchor-mark on male throat, lacking dense black-and-white ventral barring

Common Quail: Common Quail is a small, cryptic ground-dweller of grassland and stubble, identifiable by its small size, mottled plumage, and distinctive male call. The reluctant flight (short bursts) and ground behavior are characteristic.

Jungle Bush Quail: The rufous-chestnut throat and face combined with close black-and-white zebra barring across the lower breast distinguish the male Jungle Bush Quail from all other Indian quails (Baker 1928).

Male Underparts—Lower parts below the throat closely barred black and white; vent and under tail-coverts rufous (Baker 1928, p. 377)
Female Plumage—Upper plumage like male; underparts dull vinaceous or lilac-rufous, lacking black-and-white barring (Baker 1928, p. 378)
Soft Parts—Iris light to dark brown; bill slaty-black, reddish at base; legs dull yellowish to orange (Baker 1928, p. 377)
