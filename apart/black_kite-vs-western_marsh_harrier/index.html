Black Kite or Western Marsh Harrier? चील या दलदल बाज?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

दलदल बाजWestern Marsh HarrierCircus aeruginosus

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Kite, as the Western Marsh Harrier profile puts it

Forked tail; darker plumage without cream crown; scavenger; does not glide low over reeds with wings in a V-shape

चील — दो-शाखी पूँछ, झुका हुआ सिर, मैला भूरा रंग

Western Marsh Harrier: Large dark brown raptor with cream-coloured crown and throat (female) or tri-coloured grey wings (male) quartering slowly and low over reeds with wings in a shallow V is a Western Marsh Harrier.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Black KiteWestern Marsh Harrier
SizeMedium-sized raptor (55–65 cm total length); wingspan 130–150 cm; weight 600–1000 gLarge, heavy-bodied harrier (43–54 cm total length); wingspan around 115–130 cm
PlumageUniformly dark brown; slightly paler, streaked head and underparts—
TailDistinctly forked (notched) tail; twisted constantly to steer in flightLong and narrow; grey in males and dark brown in females, without distinct bars
WingsLong, broad, deeply "fingered" wingtips (outer primaries)—
Bare partsBlack hooked bill with a yellow cere (fleshy base); yellow legsLarge, staring yellow irises in adults; black hooked bill with a yellow cere; long yellow legs
BehaviourEffortless gliding in thermals; bold scavenger, will snatch food near markets—
Flight Style—Quarters low over reedbeds/crops with slow wingbeats and long glides; wings held in a shallow 'V'
Male plumage—Tri-coloured upper wings (grey, black-tipped, brown); rufous abdomen; yellowish-cream cap
Female plumage—Almost entirely dark chocolate-brown with a contrasting cream-yellow crown, throat, and shoulder patch

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
