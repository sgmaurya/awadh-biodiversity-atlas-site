How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Common Earthworm, as the Bristled Pond Worm profile puts it

Vastly larger terrestrial oligochaete with thick opaque body wall, no eyes, and no hair setae

केंचुआ

Bristled Pond Worm: A minute, transparent aquatic worm with dark eye-spots and stiff dorsal bristles that lacks any respiratory gills on its body or tail is Nais barbata.

Side by side आमने-सामने

AppearanceMinute, transparent, active aquatic micro-annelid crawling on vegetation—
EyesA pair of dark lateral eye-spots distinctly visible on the prostomium—

LocomotionSwift crawling on submerged leaves; swims freely with active lateral undulationsExtends then contracts; leaves a slimy trail in moist conditions
Size—10–25 cm long; 4–8 mm diameter; cylindrical, segmented
Colour—Reddish-brown to purplish-brown (dorsal); paler pinkish (ventral)
Segments—100–130 ring-like annuli — each distinctly visible
Clitellum—Thickened, paler band around segments 14–16 — reproductive collar; absent in juveniles
Eyes/Legs—None — purely subterranean; light-sensitive through skin
When visible—After heavy rain — emerge onto surface; common on roads and paths in monsoon

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
