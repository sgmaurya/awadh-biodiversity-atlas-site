Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Red Scorpion, as the Bengal Forest Scorpion profile puts it

Hottentotta tamulus

Reddish-orange or yellowish-brown body; slender narrow pincers; extraordinarily thick tail; venom is medically lethal

Bengal Forest Scorpion: Massive pitch-black forest scorpion with large, coarsely sculptured pincers (chelae) and relatively slender tail; mild venom.

Indian Red Scorpion: A reddish-brown scorpion of medium size with thin pincers and a thick tail is the Indian red scorpion, and the one to treat as a medical emergency in a child.

Size & ColorLarge, robust, heavily built scorpion, glossy dark blackish-brown to deep pitch-black—
Pincers (Chelae)Massive, broad, heavy pedipalp claws with coarsely granulated and lobed manus—
Tail (Metasoma)Relatively slender, segmented tail ending in a curved venomous aculeus with reddish telson—
PectinesSensory comb-like organs beneath the mesosoma bearing 12 to 16 teeth—
Burrowing habitFossorial; excavates deep horizontal burrows under boulders, tree roots, and timber—
Size—50–90 mm including the tail
Colour—Reddish-brown to orange-brown, often with a darker tail tip; some are nearly black on the body
Pincers—Slender and weak, not swollen: a warning sign, since thin-clawed scorpions rely on venom
Tail—Thick, five-segmented, carried curled over the back; the sting bulb large
Body—Granular, with keels (ridges) along the back; the tail segments ridged
Behaviour—Hides by day under objects and in cracks; walks slowly at night; glows blue-green under ultraviolet light
