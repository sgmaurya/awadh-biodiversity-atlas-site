Marbled Toad or Common Indian Kaloula? चितकबरा भेंडुक या रंगीन मेंढक?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

रंगीन मेंढकCommon Indian KaloulaKaloula taprobanica

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Marbled Toad, as the Common Indian Kaloula profile puts it

Larger (50–85 mm), warty skin, long rattling trill call

बहुत बड़ा, घरघराती आवाज़

Marbled Toad: A pale, marbled, warty toad whose head has no black ridges is the marbled toad; ridges mean the common toad.

Common Indian Kaloula: A tiny (25–35 mm), globular, vividly striped red-brown frog in rain pools during monsoon is a painted kaloula. Listen for the rapid "dhup-dhup" call. It spends the day buried in soil.

CallA long, rattling, pulsed trill, lower and more drawn-out than the common toad'sA rapid, pulsed "dhup-dhup-dhup" or "tuk-tuk-tuk" sound
Body shape—Globular, rotund, squat, with a small pointed head
Coloration—Reddish-brown or orange-brown dorsum with prominent dark red and black bands or blotches
Markings—Bold transverse bands or stripes across the back; distinctive pattern gives rise to the name "painted"
Eyes—Very small, round, dark, difficult to see
