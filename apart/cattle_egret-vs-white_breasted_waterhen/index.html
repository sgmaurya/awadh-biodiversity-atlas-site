Cattle Egret or White-breasted Waterhen? गाय बगुला या डहुआ?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Cattle Egret, as the White-breasted Waterhen profile puts it

Entirely white; long yellow bill; long neck; lives on dry land near cattle; no slate-grey upperparts

गाय बगुला — पूरा सफेद शरीर, पीली चोंच

Cattle Egret: Small size, stocky neck, yellow bill (orange in breeding), yellowish-green legs, and terrestrial foraging behavior alongside grazing livestock.

White-breasted Waterhen: Dark slate-grey upperparts, starkly contrasting clean white face and breast, long greenish legs, and a bright rufous-orange undertail patch identify the White-breasted Waterhen.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Cattle EgretWhite-breasted Waterhen
SizeSmall, stocky egret (48–53 cm total length); neck relatively short and thickMedium-sized wader (28–33 cm total length); approximately size of a small domestic hen
ColourationEntirely white in non-breeding plumage; yellow bill and yellowish-green legsSlate-grey upperparts and wings; starkly contrasting clean white face, throat, and breast
Breeding PlumageDistinct orange-buff (golden) feathers on crown, breast, and back in monsoon—
BillRelatively short, stout, and yellow (turns orange-pink during peak breeding)Short, greenish bill with a bright red spot at the base of the upper mandible during breeding
LegsYellowish-grey or olive-green; turn bright pink-red for a few days during breedingLong, pale-greenish toes and legs; adapted for walking on muddy pond margins
BehaviourCommensal association with grazing cattle; runs alongside cows to catch flushed prey—
Undertail—Diagnostic rusty-red or rufous-orange undertail coverts; tail short and frequently flicked
Foraging—Walks at water edges, stepping high; runs rapidly into reedbeds if startled
