How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Kerengga Ant-mimic Spider, as the Black-headed Ant-mimicking Spider profile puts it

Entire body uniformly yellowish-orange or amber, mimicking Oecophylla smaragdina worker ants (Sebastian & Peter 2009)

Kerengga Ant-mimic Spider: A tiny reddish-brown spider with a narrow waist, holding its front legs forward like antennae, moving with jerky ant-like motions on flowers and vegetation, is Myrmarachne plataleoides; the large forward-facing eyes (visible under magnification) and the jumping spider body plan confirm it.

Black-headed Ant-mimicking Spider: A slender ant-like creature that raises its front legs like antennae while walking on six legs, but possesses eight legs, forward-pointing eyes, and male sword-like jaws, is Myrmarachne melanocephala.

SizeSmall, 3–5 mm in total length—
ColourReddish-brown or orange-brown body; darker markings on cephalothorax—
Body shapeDistinctly ant-like, with narrowed petiole (waist)-like connection between cephalothorax and abdomen—
PedipalpsLong, ant-like, extending forward as if they were antennae; palps held together to mimic ant antennae—
LegsRelatively long, held close to the body in an ant-like posture—
MovementJerky, erratic movement like a foraging ant—
EyesEight eyes arranged like other jumping spiders; large forward-facing pair—
Female Size—Body length up to 8 mm (Tikader 1973)
Male Size—Body length up to 10 mm including elongated chelicerae (Tikader 1973)
Ant Mimicry—Constricted body contours closely mimicking head, thorax, and gaster of ants (MacLeay 1839)
Cephalothorax—Anterior cephalic region elevated, shiny black; thoracic region reddish-brown or dark (Tikader 1973)
Chelicerae—Males bear massively enlarged, horizontal chelicerae with long curved fangs (MacLeay 1839)
Locomotion—Sinuous, rapid ant-like running; raises first pair of legs to mimic waving ant antennae
Abdomen—Elongate-oval with a distinct transverse constriction and pale lateral spots (Sebastian & Peter 2009)
