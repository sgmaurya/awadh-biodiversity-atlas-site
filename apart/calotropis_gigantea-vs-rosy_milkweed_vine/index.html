Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Giant Milkweed, as the Rosy Milkweed Vine profile puts it

Larger, woody shrub not a climber; leaves grey-green and woolly; flowers larger and more showy

बड़ा झाड़, झूल पर नहीं चढ़ता

Rosy Milkweed Vine: A delicate, thin-stemmed twining vine with opposite leaves, small rosy-pink fragrant flowers, and milky latex is Oxystelma wallichii.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Giant MilkweedRosy Milkweed Vine
LeavesLarge (10–20 cm), thick, rubbery, grey-green; fine white powder on surface; sessile — no leaf stalk, clasp stem directlyOpposite, ovate to elliptic-oblong, 4–10 cm, glabrous, margins entire
Flowers3–4 cm; five petals purple-tipped with white base; crown-like corona in centre; waxy, mildly fragrant; in axillary clustersSmall (0.8–1.2 cm), rosy-pink or pale yellow, sweetly scented, in small cymose clusters
LatexMilky white sap oozes immediately from any broken part — most diagnostic signMilky white, present in all parts when broken
FruitLarge paired oval pods (8–12 cm), inflated, smooth; split to release seeds with long silky white fibres—
Height1.5–4 m shrub to small tree; pale grey-white corky bark—
HabitatRoadsides, wastelands, sandy and saline soils — most abundant on land where nothing else grows—
Plant habit—Twining climber, herbaceous to semi-woody, 2–4 m, light and delicate
Stems—Slender, glabrous, green or purplish, producing white latex when broken
Fruits—Long, slender follicles, 8–15 cm, containing small, flat seeds with silky hairs

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
