How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Oriental Magpie-Robin, as the White-rumped Shama profile puts it

Oriental Magpie-Robin]] (Copsychus saularis)

Pure white belly and vent (not chestnut-rufous); white wing stripe; shorter tail; frequents open gardens and villages

Oriental Magpie-Robin: Neat black-and-white pattern (grey-and-white in females), long white wing bar, white outer tail feathers, and a tail that is frequently cocked upward identify the Oriental Magpie-Robin.

White-rumped Shama: The combination of an intensely deep chestnut-rufous belly, prominent white rump patch, and extremely long graduated tail distinguishes the White-rumped Shama from all other thrushes and robins.

Post-breedingTail is frequently cocked at a 45 to 90-degree angle, especially when foraging—
SongClear, varied, and melodious whistling song sung from high tree branches—
Habit—Shy, terrestrial and understory flycatcher-thrush in dense shade
Male Plumage—Glossy blue-black upperparts, breast, and mantle; rich chestnut-rufous belly
Female Plumage—Slaty-brown upperparts and pale rufous-buff underparts with shorter tail
Rump—Pure white rectangular patch on lower back, conspicuous during flight
Tail—Exceptionally long, graduated black tail with white outer feather tips
Bill—Slender, sharp, black, adapted for grasping forest invertebrates
Demeanour—Cocking and fanning the long tail vertically while uttering low churring calls
