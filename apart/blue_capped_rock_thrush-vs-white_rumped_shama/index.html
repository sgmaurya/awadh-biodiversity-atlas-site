Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

White-rumped Shama, as the Blue-capped Rock Thrush profile puts it

White-rumped Shama]] (Copsychus malabaricus)

Longer tail with white outer feathers, white rump, black upperparts, lacking blue crown

Blue-capped Rock Thrush: The dazzling combination of bright cobalt-blue cap, black mask, broad white wing patch, and blazing chestnut-orange underparts identifies the male Blue-capped Rock Thrush instantly.

White-rumped Shama: The combination of an intensely deep chestnut-rufous belly, prominent white rump patch, and extremely long graduated tail distinguishes the White-rumped Shama from all other thrushes and robins.

Female Plumage—Slaty-brown upperparts and pale rufous-buff underparts with shorter tail
Rump—Pure white rectangular patch on lower back, conspicuous during flight
Tail—Exceptionally long, graduated black tail with white outer feather tips
Bill—Slender, sharp, black, adapted for grasping forest invertebrates
Demeanour—Cocking and fanning the long tail vertically while uttering low churring calls
