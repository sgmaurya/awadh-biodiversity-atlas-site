Southern House Mosquito or Yellow Fever Mosquito? मच्छर या डेंगू का मच्छर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

मच्छरSouthern House MosquitoCulex quinquefasciatus
डेंगू का मच्छरYellow Fever MosquitoAedes aegypti

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Yellow Fever Mosquito, as the Southern House Mosquito profile puts it

Lyre-shaped white markings on thorax; white-ringed legs; active during day; breeds in clean water

डेंगू मच्छर — चित्तीदार पैर, दिन में सक्रिय

Southern House Mosquito, as the Yellow Fever Mosquito profile puts it

Brown, unmarked, bites at night, breeds in dirty drain water; a filariasis vector

भूरा, रात में, नाली का पानी

Southern House Mosquito: Small size (~3.5–5.5 mm), uniform dull brownish-grey color without white leg rings, resting parallel to walls, and active at night with a high whine, identify the Southern House Mosquito.

Yellow Fever Mosquito: Black and white, a white lyre on the back, white-ringed legs, biting by day near the house: the dengue mosquito.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Southern House MosquitoYellow Fever Mosquito
SizeSmall, delicate fly; body length 3.5–5.5 mm; wingspan 3–4.5 mmSmall, 4–7 mm
ColourationDull brownish-grey or straw-colored overall; lacks bold black-and-white markings—
AbdomenDark brown segments with narrow, creamy-white transverse bands at the base of each segmentBlack with white bands and side patches
LegsLong, slender, uniformly dark brown without white rings or spotsBlack with clean white bands at the joints
Resting PostureRests parallel to the wall surface with the abdomen pointing slightly downward—
Flight SoundProduces a high-pitched, persistent whining buzz near the ears at night—
Colour—Black with bright white markings
Thorax—A white "lyre" or violin shape: two curved outer lines and two straight inner lines
Behaviour—Bites by day, especially early morning and late afternoon; approaches low, bites ankles; rests indoors in dark corners and under furniture
Larvae—"Wrigglers" hanging at an angle from the surface of clean stored water, diving when disturbed
