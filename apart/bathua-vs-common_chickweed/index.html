How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Bathua, as the Common Chickweed profile puts it

Chenopodium album]]`

Erect mealy herb with alternate rhombic leaves and tiny greenish apetalous flower clusters

Bathua: Erect winter annual herb with goosefoot-shaped leaves covered in white waxy powder, and tiny greenish-white flower spikes at the branch tips, is Bathua.

Common Chickweed: Look for the single line of hairs along the succulent green stem and the five white petals each split so deeply that they appear as ten.

BathuaCommon Chickweed
Plant habitErect, branched annual herb, growing up to 30–120 cm high, with a ribbed, often purple-streaked green stemWeak, flaccid, decumbent or procumbent bright green annual herb
LeavesGoosefoot-shaped (ovate-rhombic or lanceolate) with wavy or coarsely toothed marginsOpposite, ovate or cordate-acuminate; lower petioled, upper sessile
Leaf undersideCovered with a whitish, powdery, waxy coating (mealy texture) that repels water droplets—
FlowersTiny (1–2 mm), greenish-white, cup-shaped, arranged in dense, compact spikes at stem tipsSmall, white, solitary or in loose terminal leafy cymes
SeedsTiny (1–1.5 mm), circular, smooth, glossy jet-black, released when the dry plant turns brown—
Stems—Much-branched, cylindrical, marked by a single distinct longitudinal line of hairs
Petals—Five white petals, bipartite nearly to the base, appearing as ten petals
Fruit—Ovoid-oblong capsule opening by six valves, releasing tiny tubercled seeds
