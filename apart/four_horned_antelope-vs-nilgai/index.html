Four-horned Antelope or Nilgai? चौसिंगा या नीलगाय?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Nilgai, as the Four-horned Antelope profile puts it

Nilgai]] (Boselaphus tragocamelus)

Massive horse-sized build; blue-grey coat in bulls; two straight horns

Four-horned Antelope: Solitary habits, small size, dull pale brown pelage, with a more or less rufous tinge above, and four horn points in adult males distinguish the species; Blanford (1891, p. 520) noted: "In jungle this species and hog-deer (Cervus porcinus) may easily be mistaken the one for the other".

Nilgai: Large size (horse-like), sloping back, white throat bib, and white leg rings; blue-grey coat with short horns in males, and sandy-brown coat without horns in females, identify the Nilgai.

Build & SizeSmall slender antelope; height of male at shoulder 25 1/2 inches, at croup 27 inches (Blanford 1891)—
Posterior HornsSmooth, black, conical horns usually 3 to 4 inches long in adult males (Blanford 1891)—
Anterior HornsShorter frontal hornlets usually 1 to 1 1/2 inches long; occasionally absent (Blanford 1891)—

UnderpartsPassing gradually on the sides and limbs into the white of the lower parts (Blanford 1891)—
Size—Large, horse-like antelope; head-body length 180–210 cm; weight 120–240 kg
Male Colour—Dark blue-grey coat; sloping back with high shoulders; short black horns
Female Colour—Sandy-brown to tawny-brown coat; smaller than the male; completely hornless
Throat Mark—A prominent, bright white patch (white bib) on the middle of the throat
Throat Beard—Adult males feature a distinctive, hanging tuft of stiff black hair on the throat
Leg Markings—White rings on the fetlocks and two white spots on each cheek
