Common Cattail or Common Reed? पटेर या सरकंडा?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Reed, as the Common Cattail profile puts it

Common Reed]] (Phragmites karka)

Leaves are thin, flat; stems are hollow; flowers are large, feathery purplish-brown plumes

Common Cattail, as the Common Reed profile puts it

Stems are solid; leaves are thick and sword-like; flower is a sausage-shaped brown poker

पटेर — मखमली चोकलेट जैसे फल, बिना टहनी के पत्ते

Common Cattail: Tall wetland herb (1.5–3 m) with thick, spongy sword-like leaves, and a single chocolate-brown sausage-like velvet spike at the stem tip, is the Common Cattail (Pater).

Common Reed: Giant perennial aquatic grass (2–6 m) with hollow bamboo-like stems, broad leaves wrapping the stem, and terminal feathery purplish-brown flower plumes waving in autumn marsh margins, is the Common Reed (Sarkanda).

SpikeUnmistakable: a long, cylindrical, velvet-textured dark brown spike (15–30 cm) at the stem tip—
Seed dispersalThe dry brown spike bursts open in late autumn, releasing a massive cloud of white, fluffy seed down—
FlowersMonoecious; male flowers are in a pale yellow spike at the very top, and female flowers are in the thick brown spike below, touching each otherLarge, plume-like, branched panicles (30–50 cm), turning purplish-brown and covered in soft silky hairs
Stems (Culms)—Thick (10–25 mm), erect, hollow, bamboo-like, green, and leafy up to the top
Root system—Long, creeping, woody underground rhizomes that spread horizontally in wet soil to shoot new culms
