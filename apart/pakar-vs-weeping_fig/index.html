White Fig or Weeping Fig? पाकड़ या वीपिंग फिग?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

पाकड़White FigFicus virens
वीपिंग फिगWeeping FigFicus benjamina

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

White Fig, as the Weeping Fig profile puts it

Leaves oblong-ovate, deciduous in winter; figs shortly pedunculate

पत्तियाँ चौड़ी, सर्दियां में गिरती हैं

White Fig: A fig with glossy, smooth, small leaves; figs in pairs at branch tips; and descending aerial roots anchoring the stem is pakar.

Weeping Fig: Drooping slender branchlets with glossy, elliptic leaves having extremely close parallel veins and small paired sessile figs separate Ficus benjamina from all other local figs.

Side by side आमने-सामने

White FigWeeping Fig
SizeSmall to medium tree, 5–12 m, or a climbing fig on walls and rocks; multi-stemmed—
LeavesAlternate, simple, elliptic-lanceolate, 4–10 cm, glossy, margins entireOvate-elliptic, 5–12 cm long, glossy dark green with an abrupt slender drip tip (Duthie 1915)
FruitsFig fruits (syconia) 1–2 cm across, borne in pairs at leaf axils on branch tips, white-green ripening to yellow, reddish or purple—
Aerial rootsThin, hair-like, descending from upper branches to soil or rock surface, anchoring the plantSlender aerial roots descending from horizontal branches, sometimes forming pillar boles
BarkSmooth, greyish, exuding white latex when injuredSmooth, light grey, exuding copious sticky white milky latex when cut
Habit—Spreading evergreen tree up to 30 m tall with slender drooping branchlets (Duthie 1915)
Venation—Fine, very dense, parallel secondary veins joining an intramarginal loop
Syconia (Figs)—Axillary, paired, sessile, globose, 8–12 mm across, ripening yellow to dark red (Duthie 1915)
