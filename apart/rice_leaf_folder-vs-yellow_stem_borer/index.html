How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rice Leaf Folder, as the Yellow Stem Borer profile puts it

Rice Leaf Folder]] (Cnaphalocrocis medinalis)

Moth is yellowish-brown with dark bands; larva rolls leaves and feeds within

Rice Leaf Folder: Pale green caterpillar rolling rice leaves lengthwise into a tubular structure and feeding on the inner surface, with dark frass visible inside the roll, is the rice leaf folder.

Yellow Stem Borer: Bright yellowish-white moth with a single black dot on each forewing (female), whose caterpillars bore inside rice stems causing "dead hearts" or empty "white ears", is the Yellow Stem Borer.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Rice Leaf FolderYellow Stem Borer
Adult mothSmall, pale yellowish or tan moth; wingspan 20–24 mm; wings with fine patterns—
LarvaPale green to whitish caterpillar, 13–16 mm; smooth and slender—
Leaf damage signLeaf blade rolled lengthwise into a tubular structure; edges are fastened with silk—
Inside the rollLarva visible inside, feeding on the inner surface; dark frass (droppings) at the opening—
Plant stage affectedMost visible at flag leaf stage (heading time); also on earlier leaves during vegetative stage—
Damage extentOne to several rolled leaves per shoot; severe infestations cause visible leaf browning and wilting—
Adult Female—Bright yellowish-white forewings, each marked with a single distinct black spot in the center; hindwings are pure white
Adult Male—Slightly smaller and darker than the female; forewings are light brown-yellow with a series of tiny grey dots on the margin
Larval Stage—Smooth, dirty white to pale yellow caterpillar with a dark brown head capsule; length up to 25 mm
Damage (Early)—"Dead heart" — central leaf whorl of the vegetative tiller turns brown, dries up, and can be easily pulled out
Damage (Late)—"White ear" or "white head" — empty, bleached white panicles that stand upright without seeds
