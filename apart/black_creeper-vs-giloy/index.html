Black Creeper or Giloy? काली बेल या गिलोय?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Giloy, as the Black Creeper profile puts it

Tinospora cordifolia]]

Succulent climber with heart-shaped leaves, watery sap without white latex, and red berries

Black Creeper: Slender twining woody habit, milky white latex, tiny fragrant twisted-petalled flowers, and paired slender follicles separate Ichnocarpus frutescens from erect aak shrubs and succulent climbers.

Giloy: Heart-shaped deciduous leaves on a climbing woody vine with hanging aerial rootlets, on neem or mango trees in a village setting = giloy. No other common eastern UP climber has all four features together.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Black CreeperGiloy
HabitExtensive woody evergreen twiner with rusty-pubescent branchlets and dark purplish-brown stems—
SapCopious milky white latex exuding from any broken twig or petiole—
LeavesOpposite, elliptic-oblong, dark green and shining above, paler beneathAlternately arranged, broad, heart-shaped (cordate), 10–15 cm long, smooth, looking similar to betel leaves
FlowersSmall, fragrant, purplish to greenish-white, arranged in axillary and terminal rusty paniclesSmall, inconspicuous pale yellow-green flowers (appearing May–July) borne in slender axillary racemes
CorollaSalver-shaped with five twisted lobes, bearded at the throat and margins—
FruitSlender cylindrical paired follicles, curved, tapering to an acute pointSmall, round to ovoid fleshy drupes (6–8 mm), green when raw and ripening to a glossy bright red
Habit & Stem—Perennial woody climbing vine (liana); stems are green and fleshy when young, turning grey-green with a papery skin when older
Aerial Roots—Thread-like, fleshy green aerial roots (2–15 cm long) that hang down from nodes along the climbing stems
