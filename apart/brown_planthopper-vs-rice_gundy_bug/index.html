Brown Planthopper or Rice Gundy Bug? भूरा फुदका या गंधी कीड़ा?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rice Gundy Bug, as the Brown Planthopper profile puts it

Much larger, slender, on the ears; smells

बड़ा, बालियों पर, बदबूदार

Brown Planthopper: Brown wedge-shaped hoppers in clusters at the base of rice just above the water, jumping when tapped, with round scorched patches in the field: brown planthopper.

Rice Gundy Bug: Slender, straw-colored body (~14–17 mm), long antennae with dark joint bands, feeding on developing rice grains, and emitting a strong foul odor, identify the Rice Gundy Bug.

NymphsPale cream to brown, wingless, in the same place—
Damage"Hopperburn": circular patches of yellowed, then dried, then collapsed plants spreading outward from a centre—
HoneydewSticky, with sooty mould on the lower stems—
Colouration—Olive-green or straw-yellow above; pale greenish-yellow underneath
Odour—Releases a strong, pungent, defensive foul smell when disturbed or handled
Proboscis—Long, needle-like piercing-sucking mouthparts folded beneath the chest
Antennae—Long, 4-segmented, slender; yellow with dark brown rings near joints
Behaviour—Clings vertically to rice panicles, blending perfectly with grass stems
