How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Sandpiper, as the Black-winged Stilt profile puts it

Common Sandpiper]] (Actitis hypoleucos)

Smaller; olive-brown upperparts; short legs; constantly bobs tail; lacks white and black pattern

Black-winged Stilt: Slender black-and-white bird with extraordinarily long, bright pink legs and a thin, needle-like black bill wading in shallow water is a Black-winged Stilt.

Common Sandpiper: Small shorebird with olive-brown upperparts, white belly with a greyish smudge on the breast-side, constantly bobbing its tail, and flying low with stiff, bowed wings is a Common Sandpiper.

SizeSlender wading bird (35–40 cm total length); stands high on extremely long legsSmall, active shorebird (19–21 cm total length); relatively short greyish-green legs

PlumagePure white head, neck, and underparts; glossy black wings and back—

FlightTrailing long pink legs extend far beyond the tail in flight; wings are long and pointedLow, fast flight on stiff, bowed wings alternating with short glides; shows a white wing bar
NestBuilds nests in loose colonies on exposed mudbanks or shallow islands—
Tail-bobbing—Diagnostic behaviour of constantly bobbing the tail and rear body up and down
Colouration—Olive-brown upperparts; pure white underparts with a distinct pale greyish-brown smudge on breast-side
Bare Parts—Bill is straight, medium-length, grey-brown; irises are dark brown
Habitat—Found singly on muddy river edges, canal banks, and village pond margins
