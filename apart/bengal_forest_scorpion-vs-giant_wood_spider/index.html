How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Giant Wood Spider, as the Bengal Forest Scorpion profile puts it

Nephila pilipes

Huge yellow-and-black arboreal spider hanging in massive golden aerial orb webs; completely non-fossorial

Bengal Forest Scorpion: Massive pitch-black forest scorpion with large, coarsely sculptured pincers (chelae) and relatively slender tail; mild venom.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Bengal Forest ScorpionGiant Wood Spider
Size & ColorLarge, robust, heavily built scorpion, glossy dark blackish-brown to deep pitch-black—
Pincers (Chelae)Massive, broad, heavy pedipalp claws with coarsely granulated and lobed manus—
Tail (Metasoma)Relatively slender, segmented tail ending in a curved venomous aculeus with reddish telson—
PectinesSensory comb-like organs beneath the mesosoma bearing 12 to 16 teeth—
Burrowing habitFossorial; excavates deep horizontal burrows under boulders, tree roots, and timber—
Female size—Leg span 15–20 cm; body 3–5 cm — one of Asia's largest spiders
Male size—Tiny — 0.5–1 cm body; leg span 2–3 cm — lives at periphery of female's web
Web—Large (up to 1.5 m), orb web; silk noticeably yellow-golden in direct sunlight
Abdomen—Yellow-and-black or silver with yellow spots
Legs—Long, feathery tufts at joints (tibial brushes) — distinctive
Habitat clue—Large web spanning between trees/shrubs at 1–3 m height

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
