Blue-throated Blue Flycatcher or Crimson Sunbird? ब्लू-थ्रोटेड फ्लाईकैचर या क्रिमसन सनबर्ड?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Crimson Sunbird, as the Blue-throated Blue Flycatcher profile puts it

Crimson Sunbird]] (Aethopyga siparaja)

Diminutive canopy nectarivore with crimson plumage, curved bill, and active flower feeding

Blue-throated Blue Flycatcher: The combination of deep blue upperparts, blue throat projecting like a wedge into the rich orange breast, and pure white belly identifies the male Blue-throated Blue Flycatcher.

Crimson Sunbird: The glowing crimson upperparts and breast, bright golden-yellow rump, metallic crown, and elongated central tail feathers distinguish the male Crimson Sunbird from all other sunbirds in northern India.

HabitSmall, upright-perching, shade-dwelling flycatcher of forest undergrowthTiny, energetic nectarivorous bird flitting rapidly among flowering trees

ThroatDistinct blue chin and central throat forming a triangular wedge down the neck—
BreastVivid ferruginous-orange or bright rufous breast contrasting with blue throat—
BellyPure immaculate white abdomen and vent contrasting sharply with orange breast—
FemaleCryptic olive-brown upperparts with rufous tail wash and buffy-orange breast—
PostureSits upright on shaded low twigs, occasionally flicking and spreading the tail—
Male Upperparts—Brilliant crimson mantle and back; forehead metallic dark green; yellow rump
Male Underparts—Fiery crimson chin, throat, and breast; lower belly and vent dark greyish-olive
Female Plumage—Drab olive-green upperparts; pale yellowish-green underparts; short tail
Bill—Slender, sharp, distinctly decurved; dark brown to blackish
Tail—Male has elongated central tail feathers glossed metallic green; female tail short
Flight Pattern—Very swift, direct, whirring flight between flowering shrubs and canopy lianas
