Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rusty Millipede, as the Flat-backed Millipede profile puts it

Rusty Millipede]] [MYR-0002])

Cylindrical body shape; bright red-orange colouration; coils into a three-dimensional spiral; has 40–55 body segments.

Flat-backed Millipede, as the Rusty Millipede profile puts it

Flat-backed Millipede]] (Anoplodesmus saussurii)

Dark brown with yellow lateral flanges; flattened body; does not coil

Side by side आमने-सामने

Flat-backed MillipedeRusty Millipede
Body ShapeDorsoventrally flattened body with prominent lateral yellow-cream flanges (paranota) on each segment—
ColourDark brown to blackish-brown body colour with contrasting bright yellow or cream-coloured edgesBright brick-red to rust-orange — unique; no other common UP millipede has this colouration
LegsPale cream-yellow legs; adults have two pairs of legs per body segment (except the first few)~80–100 pairs; pale cream-yellow; two pairs per body segment
Size15.0–20.0 mm in length; approximately 1.5–2.0 mm wide2–3 cm length; 2–3 mm diameter; cylindrical
Legs per segmentTwo pairs per segment (millipede trait)—
MovementSlow, steady, rhythmic forward gliding; does not run erraticallySlow smooth gliding motion driven by metachronal leg waves
DefenceCoils into a flat spiral when disturbed; releases an almond-like odour (hydrogen cyanide)Coils into tight spiral when disturbed — does NOT form a perfect closed ball
Smell—Faint almond-like smell (hydrogen cyanide) when handled — harmless
Habitat—Under leaf litter, damp logs, compost — post-monsoon garden soil
