How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rohu, as the Kuria Labeo profile puts it

Labeo rohita

Much larger scales with only 40 to 42 in the lateral line; reddish-bronze tinge; thicker body

Kuria Labeo: Deep-bodied river carp with fringed lips and remarkably fine scales (70 to 85 in lateral line); dark longitudinal flank stripes.

Rohu: Large Indian carp + reddish-orange fins + wrinkled lower lip + mid-water behavior in rivers/ponds = rohu. The red fins fade quickly after death — identify in the water or immediately after capture.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Kuria LabeoRohu
Scale countExceptionally small scales with 70 to 85 scales along the complete lateral line—
Mouth & LipsSubinferior mouth with thick, fringed lips possessing a fleshy inner fold—
BarbelsTwo pairs of minute, inconspicuous barbels (one rostral pair, one maxillary pair)—
ColorationDark greenish-black above, silvery on sides with fine longitudinal stripes formed by scale spots—
Body formDeep, elongated, compressed body with a moderately arched dorsal profile—
Size—Large carp, up to 1 m and 25 kg (Talwar & Jhingran 1991); typically 40-60 cm in ponds, larger in rivers
Silver-grey body—with reddish-orange tinge on scales, especially on the sides; each scale edged with a dark outline giving a reticulated (net-like) appearance
Reddish fins—pectoral, pelvic, and caudal fins tinged red or orange — the most reliable identification feature
Lips—Thick, fleshy, transversely wrinkled lower lip — the rostral cap (upper lip) prominent and overhanging; characteristic of Labeo
Terminal mouth—mouth at the front of the snout (vs. inferior in Catla which is downward-facing)
Scales—Large, cycloid (smooth-edged)
