How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Citrine Wagtail, as the Forest Wagtail profile puts it

Citrine Wagtail]] (Motacilla citreola)

Brilliant yellow head and underparts in breeding male, or pale greyish with yellow wash; pumps tail vertically in open marshes

Citrine Wagtail: A wagtail with a yellow or citrine-yellow head in open grasslands — is a Citrine Wagtail. The yellow head, grey back, and tail-wagging behaviour are diagnostic. Distinguished from Yellow Wagtail by larger size, longer tail, and citrine-yellow (not bright yellow) head colour in males.

Forest Wagtail: The olive-brown upperparts, double black breast band, bold yellowish-white wing bars, and unique lateral (side-to-side) tail swaying distinguish the Forest Wagtail from all Motacilla wagtails.

UnderpartsWhite or pale cream—
TailBlack with white outer tail feathers (like other wagtails)—
BehaviourGround walker; tail-wagging; often in open areas—
Habit—Solitary or paired woodland wagtail of shaded forest floors and quiet trails
Breast Pattern—Striking double black breast band: a solid upper crescent and broken lower bar
Tail Movement—Uniquely wags its tail side-to-side (laterally) rather than up and down
Wings—Broad yellowish-white double wing bars and conspicuous pale wing patches
Perching—Freely perches across horizontal branches, unlike open-country wagtails
