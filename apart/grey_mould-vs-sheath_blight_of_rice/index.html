Grey Mould or Sheath Blight of Rice? ग्रे मोल्ड या शीथ ब्लाइट?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

ग्रे मोल्डGrey MouldBotrytis cinerea
शीथ ब्लाइटSheath Blight of RiceRhizoctonia solani

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Sheath Blight of Rice, as the Grey Mould profile puts it

Diverse crops

Aseptate moniloid cells, no conidia

Grey Mould: Check conidiophores and spore clusters: tall dark conidiophores bearing grape-like clusters of grey spores indicate Botrytis cinerea, distinguishing it from Sclerotinia which produces only mycelial sheets and large sclerotia.

Sheath Blight of Rice: Brown, water-soaked, coalescing lesions on leaf sheaths that girdle the stem in warm, wet monsoon conditions = sheath blight.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Grey MouldSheath Blight of Rice
Colony appearanceDense, powdery, smoky grey to ash-brown velvety mould layer—
Affected organsFlowers, tender pods, succulent stems, and ripening soft fruits—
Initial symptomWater-soaked soft circular lesions on succulent host tissues—
Conidial massMassive production of grey-brown airborne spore dust on lesion surface—
Spore groupingConidia arranged in dense grape-like clusters on branched upright stalks—
Conidial shapeObovoid to broadly ellipsoidal, pale brownish, single-celled—
Survival stageSmall, irregular, flattened black sclerotia adhering to dead host tissue—
Rot characteristicSoft, watery disintegration of tissue followed by dry, grey crumbling—
Initial lesion—Oval to circular water-soaked spots on leaf sheaths, starting at the base of the leaf
Lesion color—Tan to brown, with irregular dark brown or reddish margins; appears greasy
Lesion spread—Lesions coalesce and may wrap around the sheath, girding the stem
Lesion surface—May show concentric rings or a target-like pattern
Blade symptoms—Lesions on blades are more angular and irregular; confined between veins
Advanced stage—Severe infection kills the entire leaf sheath and can extend to adjacent sheaths
Sclerotia—In wet conditions, small hard bodies (sclerotia, size of mustard seeds) may be visible on lesions

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
