Six-spotted Ground Beetle or Two-coloured Tiger Beetle? छह-चित्तीदार शिकारी भृंग या दो-रंगा बाघ भृंग?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Two-coloured Tiger Beetle, as the Six-spotted Ground Beetle profile puts it

Much smaller; coppery head and unspotted blue elytra; winged

बहुत छोटा, तांबई सिर और बिना धब्बे वाले नीले पंख

Six-spotted Ground Beetle, as the Two-coloured Tiger Beetle profile puts it

Much larger, flightless, velvety black with six large white spots

बहुत बड़ा, उड़ने में असमर्थ, काला शरीर और छह सफेद धब्बे

Six-spotted Ground Beetle: A very large flightless velvety-black ground beetle with six bold white spots and massive mandibles that sprays acid identifies Anthia sexguttata.

Two-coloured Tiger Beetle: A tiger beetle on firm dirt paths with a bright coppery-red head and thorax contrasting against solid unspotted deep blue or green elytra identifies Cicindela bicolor.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Six-spotted Ground BeetleTwo-coloured Tiger Beetle
HabitVery large, flightless ground beetle with massive mandiblesMedium-sized, bicoloured terrestrial predator running on bare soil
ColorationDeep velvety black, adorned with six bold chalky-white circular spots—
Thoracic spotsTwo large white spots situated laterally on the heart-shaped pronotum—
Elytral spotsFour large white spots on the fused wing covers (two basal, two subapical)—
Chemical defenseSprays concentrated formic acid from pygidial glands with remarkable accuracy—
Head & Thorax—Bright metallic coppery-red, bronze, or golden with fine striations
Elytra—Deep metallic blue, violet, or dark green, completely unspotted
Mandibles—Long, sharply curved, armed with acute internal teeth for seizing prey
Locomotion—Rapid intermittent sprints interspersed with low, buzzing short flights
