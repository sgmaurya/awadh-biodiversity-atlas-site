Black Nightshade, as the Wild Cape Gooseberry profile puts it

Black Nightshade]] (Solanum nigrum)

Flowers are star-like; berries are exposed (lacks the papery lantern husk); black when ripe

Black Nightshade: Soft hairless plant, white star flowers with a yellow anther cone, and drooping bunches of pea-sized berries turning black: makoi. Green berries are not to be eaten.

Wild Cape Gooseberry: Small branched annual herb with soft stems, pale yellow bell flowers with dark inner spots, and round yellow edible berries completely enclosed in papery, 5-angled green-brown lantern husks, is the Wild Cape Gooseberry.

Black NightshadeWild Cape Gooseberry
HabitSoft, branching annual 30–90 cm, hairless or nearly so—
StemGreen or purplish, angled, sometimes with faint ridges—

FruitRound berries 6–10 mm in drooping bunches, green then dull purple-black, with many flat seedsA round, smooth, fleshy yellow berry (10–12 mm), completely enclosed within an inflated, 5-angled, papery green-to-brown lantern-like husk
WhereRich, moist, part-shaded ground near houses and cattle—
Plant habit—Small, erect or sprawling, heavily branched annual herb, growing up to 20–50 cm high, with a hollow, soft green stem
Taste—The ripe yellow fruit has a juicy, sweet and slightly sour tomato-like flavor
