Bahera or Kaim? बहेड़ा या कैम?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

बहेड़ाBaheraTerminalia bellirica
कैमKaimMitragyna parvifolia

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Bahera, as the Kaim profile puts it

Leaves simple, opposite, but larger; fruit a dry drupe with wings

बड़ी पत्तियाँ, डैने वाली फली

Bahera: A large tree with leaves bunched at twig ends, hard winged grey-brown fruits, and leaflessness in April is bahera.

Kaim: A medium riparian tree, leafless in April, with dense round clusters of tiny white-yellow flowers and opposite simple leaves is kaim.

Side by side आमने-सामने

BaheraKaim
SizeLarge deciduous tree, 15–25 m, with a spreading crownMedium deciduous tree, 10–18 m, with a spreading crown

FlowersSmall, greenish-white, clustered in narrow spikes at branch tips, March to AprilSmall, white to yellowish, in dense rounded clusters (balls) at branch tips, April to May
FruitsEllipsoid to obovoid, hard, grey-brown drupes, 3–4 cm, with two prominent wings; ripens September to OctoberSmall, round, woody, 2-seeded capsules
BarkDark grey, deeply furrowedDark grey, rough, fissured
The testLook for a large tree with winged fruit clusters and leaves bunched at the twig endsLook for a tree with opposite simple leaves and small, round flower clusters along riverbanks

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
