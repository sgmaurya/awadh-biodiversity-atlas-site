How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Fusarium Wilt, as the Potato Late Blight profile puts it

Wilting, browning of vascular tissue; no surface lesions

अंदर भूरा, बाहर से नहीं दिखता

Fusarium Wilt: Progressive wilting of one or more branches, with brown or reddish-brown vascular discolouration inside the stem, with no recovery, is Fusarium Wilt. The disease is permanent and fatal to that plant.

Potato Late Blight: Large (5–10 cm), water-soaked, dark brown patches on potato leaf, spreading rapidly, with yellow halo, in cool, wet January–February = late blight. Every other potato disease is either smaller, differently patterned, or less destructive in eastern UP.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Fusarium WiltPotato Late Blight
Early symptomsWilting of one or more branches while rest of plant remains green; yellowing of leaves on infected branch—
Progressive wiltGradual spread to rest of plant; plant finally wilts completely and dies—
Vascular discolourationCut the stem near soil level: interior shows brown or reddish-brown discolouration of vascular tissue (xylem); may see pink fungal growth—
Root symptomsRoots appear dark, rotted; may be invaded by secondary organisms—
Fungal growthPink or white mould may be visible on infected tissue under moist conditions—
TimingCan occur at any growth stage; most obvious when plant is stressed (heat, drought)—
Leaf lesion—Large, water-soaked, dark brown to black patches on leaf blades and stems; irregular shape
Lesion margin—Often surrounded by a narrow, pale halo of yellowing tissue
Lesion progression—Rapidly expands; entire leaf can blacken within 48–72 hours
Stem infection—Brown-black lesions on petioles (leaf stalks) and stems; causes stem collapse
Underside of leaf—White, sparse fungal growth (sporangia and sporangiophores) visible only in very humid conditions; distinctive sign when present
Tuber symptoms (in field)—Purple-brown or red-brown, sunken lesions on tuber surface; often with concentric rings of discolouration
Tuber rot (in storage)—Dry rot, watery rot, or secondary bacterial rot develops; tuber becomes inedible
Whole plant symptom—Plant dies rapidly; field appears as if burned or scalded; canopy collapses
