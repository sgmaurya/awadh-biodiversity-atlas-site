Dwarf Snakehead or Snakehead Murrel? ड्वार्फ स्नेकहेड या सौर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Snakehead Murrel, as the Dwarf Snakehead profile puts it

Channa striata

Grows much larger; dark chevron-like bands along flanks; constructs floating surface bubble nest

Dwarf Snakehead: Small snakehead with concentric dark-and-pale bands on pectoral fins, reddish fin rims, and paternal mouthbrooding.

Snakehead Murrel: Elongated cylindrical body with a flat snake-like head, chevron-shaped dark bands on the sides, white belly, and absence of tail ocellus identify Channa striata.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Dwarf SnakeheadSnakehead Murrel
Body shapeElongate, cylindrical body with a flattened head covered by large snake-like scales—
Pectoral finsBroad pectoral fins marked with striking alternating dark and pale concentric bands—
Fin marginsDorsal and caudal fins narrowly bordered with a vibrant reddish-orange or white margin—
RespirationPaired suprabranchial accessory organs allowing obligate atmospheric air-breathing—
Brooding habitsPaternal mouthbrooder; the male holds and incubates fertilized eggs in his buccal cavity—
Form—Elongated, cylindrical body, compressed towards the tail; head is broad and flattened like a snake
Coloration—Dark greyish-brown to black on the back; sides with 10–12 distinct oblique chevron-like (V-shaped) dark bands; belly white
Fins—Long, continuous single dorsal fin (38–43 rays) and anal fin (23–27 rays) without spines; pectoral fins rounded
Mouth—Large, wide, terminal mouth, with the lower jaw slightly protruding; armed with sharp teeth
Breathing—Access to atmospheric air; rises periodically to the water surface to gulp air

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
