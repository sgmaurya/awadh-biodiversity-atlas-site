How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Climbing Perch, as the Honey Gourami profile puts it

Scaled spiny perch; dorsal fin armed with 16–19 sharp spines; completely lacks thread-like pelvic feelers

Climbing Perch: A hard, spiny, grey fish with a saw-edged gill cover that survives out of water and walks on wet ground is the climbing perch.

Honey Gourami: A diminutive, oval, compressed air-breathing labyrinth fish with thread-like pelvic feelers, whose males display a warm honey-amber body with a velvet-black throat and electric-yellow dorsal edge, is Trichogaster chuna.

Size10–20 cm, occasionally 25 cmDiminutive freshwater labyrinth fish; reaches about 13.7 cm TL (Froese & Pauly 2025)
ShapeOblong, somewhat compressed, with a large head and a mouth with small teeth—
ColourOlive to grey-brown above, paler below; a dark spot at the base of the tail and another behind the gill cover—
FinsLong dorsal and anal fins with strong spines; a rounded tail—
Gill coverThe rear edge serrated with sharp spines, which it spreads when caught: the "climbing" tool and the reason it is hard to hold—
BehaviourGulps air at the surface; survives in a bucket; crawls on land after rain—
Body Shape—Oval, compressed laterally, slightly more slender than the dwarf gourami (Hamilton 1822)
Pelvic Fins—Modified into a single, elongate thread-like sensory filament (Hamilton 1822)
Air-Breathing Organ—Labyrinth organ present in gill chamber allowing atmospheric air-breathing
Male Colour—Golden-amber or honey-orange with a deep velvet-black throat and electric-yellow dorsal edge (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Female Colour—Pale yellowish-brown with a distinct dark brown mid-lateral horizontal stripe (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Dorsal Fin—Long-based, carrying 17–18 stiff spines and 6–9 soft rays (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
