How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Two-Spotted Cricket, as the Tropical House Cricket profile puts it

Field Cricket]])

Jet black or dark brown body; large yellow spots at base of tegmina; field and soil dwelling

Two-Spotted Cricket: Shiny black body, body length 20–35 mm, with a pair of distinct yellow-orange spots at the base of the forewings, identifies Gryllus bimaculatus.

Tropical House Cricket: Pale tan body with dark transverse bars across head and pronotum, male with shortened wing pads and female completely wingless = Gryllodes sigillatus.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Two-Spotted CricketTropical House Cricket
FormRobust, cylindrical body with a large, rounded head and long, thread-like antennae—
ColorationShiny black overall; distinct pair of pale yellow-orange spots at the base of the forewingsPale yellowish-tan or straw-colored with dark brown transverse bands
WingsFully developed forewings (tegmina) covering the abdomen; long membranous hindwings—
LegsStrong hind legs modified for jumping (saltatorial), equipped with spines—
OvipositorFemales possess a long, needle-like ovipositor extending from the rear of the abdomenFemale bears very long, slender, straight needle-like ovipositor
BehaviorNocturnal; males produce a loud, repetitive "chirping" sound by stridulation at night—
Head—Broad, pale with distinct dark transverse bar between compound eyes
Pronotum—Marked with prominent dark brown transverse fascia along hind margin
Male wings—Tegmina shortened, covering only half the abdomen, used for stridulation
Female wings—Flightless; tegmina reduced to tiny minute lateral lobes; wings absent
