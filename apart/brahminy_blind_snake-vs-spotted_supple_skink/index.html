How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Brahminy Blind Snake, as the Spotted Supple Skink profile puts it

Indotyphlops braminus

Completely legless true snake with vestigial eyes beneath head scales and uniform polished brown cylindrical body

Brahminy Blind Snake: Any tiny, glossy, thread-like snake under 3 cm diameter with no visible eyes is a Brahminy Blind Snake and is completely harmless.

Spotted Supple Skink: Extremely slender bronze skink with minute limbs, dark longitudinal dot rows along the back, and smooth snake-like movement through leaf mold.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Brahminy Blind SnakeSpotted Supple Skink
Size15–27 cm, extremely slender—
ColourUniform pale yellow, brown, or dark grey—
HeadBlunt and rounded, not wider than body; mouth tiny, underneath the head—
EyesPresent but covered by a transparent scale; non-functional—
BodyCylindrical, uniform diameter; smooth glossy scales—
TailBlunt, ending in a small spine—
BehaviourBurrowing; found in soil or decomposing matter; never bites—
Body form—Elongated, cylindrical snake-like body with minute, widely separated limbs
Dorsal pattern—Golden-brown or bronze with 4 to 6 distinct longitudinal series of black dots (Smith 1935)
Ventral surface—Uniformly yellowish-white or cream, smooth and glossy
Tail coloration—Thick, cylindrical; tail tip or regenerating tail often bright coral-red or pinkish in juveniles
Lower eyelid—Scaly, movable, lacking an undivided transparent central disc
Digits—Pentadactyle, very short and slender, poorly developed for terrestrial climbing

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
