How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Sandpiper, as the Curlew Sandpiper profile puts it

Actitis hypoleucos

Straight bill, white spur in front of wing, dark rump, and constant tail-bobbing habits

Common Sandpiper: Small shorebird with olive-brown upperparts, white belly with a greyish smudge on the breast-side, constantly bobbing its tail, and flying low with stiff, bowed wings is a Common Sandpiper.

Curlew Sandpiper: Small shorebird with a noticeably downcurved slender black bill and bright white square rump patch revealed in flight.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common SandpiperCurlew Sandpiper
SizeSmall, active shorebird (19–21 cm total length); relatively short greyish-green legs—
Tail-bobbingDiagnostic behaviour of constantly bobbing the tail and rear body up and down—
ColourationOlive-brown upperparts; pure white underparts with a distinct pale greyish-brown smudge on breast-side—
FlightLow, fast flight on stiff, bowed wings alternating with short glides; shows a white wing bar—
Bare PartsBill is straight, medium-length, grey-brown; irises are dark brown—
HabitatFound singly on muddy river edges, canal banks, and village pond margins—
Bill curvature—Slender, elongated black bill evenly and noticeably downcurved towards the tip
Rump pattern—Striking pure white square rump patch boldly contrasting with dark mantle and tail in flight
Winter plumage—Pale grey-brown upperparts with dark feather shaft-lines; prominent whitish supercilium
Underparts—Immaculate clean white breast, belly, and flanks in non-breeding winter dress
Leg structure—Relatively long, slender black to dark plumbeous-grey legs adapted for wading in soft mud

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
