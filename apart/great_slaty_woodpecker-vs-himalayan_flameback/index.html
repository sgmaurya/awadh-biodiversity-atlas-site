How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Himalayan Flameback, as the Great Slaty Woodpecker profile puts it

Himalayan Flameback]] (Dinopium shorii)

Much smaller; bright golden-olive back; bold red crest and black-and-white plumage; solitary habits

Great Slaty Woodpecker: The gigantic body size, uniform powdery slaty-grey colour without golden or red mantle plumage, swan-like neck, and social group foraging separate the Great Slaty Woodpecker from all flamebacks.

Himalayan Flameback: Golden-backed woodpecker with three toes, double black line enclosing a pale brownish throat streak, and white-streaked crown in female.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Great Slaty WoodpeckerHimalayan Flameback
HabitGigantic, social arboreal woodpecker foraging on massive forest tree trunks—
PlumageUniform slaty-grey, frosted or powdered with fine whitish flecks—
Neck and HeadUnusually long, thin swan-like neck with large triangular hammerhead—
BillVery long, heavy, straight, chisel-tipped; ivory-white with dark culmen—
Male MarksNarrow crimson-red moustachial patch at base of bill; buffy-yellow throat—
Female MarksEntirely grey-headed without red moustachial stripes—
CallExtraordinary loud laughing or whinnying cackle echoing through deep forest—
Throat pattern—Double black line running down the centre of the throat enclosing a pale brownish patch
Crown and crest—Bright scarlet-crimson in male; black crown with long white drop-shaped streaks in female
Mantle and back—Brilliant golden-olive to orange-gold upperparts with scarlet wash across lower back and rump
Underparts—Buff-white underbody with black-bordered scales, creating bold chain-like markings on breast
Feet morphology—Tridactyl foot arrangement possessing only three functional toes (two forward, one rear)

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
