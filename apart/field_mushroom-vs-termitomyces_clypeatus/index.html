Field Mushroom or Small Termite Mushroom? जंगली खुम्बी या छोटा दीमक छत्ता?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

जंगली खुम्बीField MushroomAgaricus campestris
छोटा दीमक छत्ताSmall Termite MushroomTermitomyces clypeatus

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Field Mushroom, as the Small Termite Mushroom profile puts it

No perforatorium; not on termite mounds

काँटा नहीं; टीले पर नहीं

Small Termite Mushroom: Smaller than a hand span, grey-brown cap, near a termite mound, with a central spike — Termitomyces clypeatus.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Field MushroomSmall Termite Mushroom
Cap (Pileus)Dry, smooth, convex white cap (3–10 cm diameter), flat at maturity, turns yellowish-brown when bruised—
Gills (Lamellae)Free from the stem; bright pink when young, turning dark chocolate-brown to black as spores mature—
Stem (Stipe)Cylindrical white stem (3–8 cm tall, 1–2 cm diameter) with a thin, single, fragile white ring (annulus)—
VolvaAbsent at the base of the stem (a key feature distinguishing it from deadly Amanita species)—
Flesh & OdorWhite flesh, does not change color significantly when cut, carries a pleasant, mild mushroom-like smell—
Cap Size & Shape—Small to medium cap (5–10 cm wide), strongly humped when young, shield-shaped when old
Perforatorium—Central spike (0.5–1.5 cm long), shorter and less prominent than in T. heimii
Colour—Cap grey-brown to dark brown at the centre, paler buff toward margins
Gills—White, crowded, and free (unattached to the stem)
Stipe (Stem)—White, fibrous (5–10 cm long above ground) with an underground pseudorrhiza
Association—Found emerging near active soil mounds of Odontotermes termites, often with T. heimii

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
