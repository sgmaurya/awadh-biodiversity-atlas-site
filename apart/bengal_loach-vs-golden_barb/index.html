Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Golden Barb, as the Bengal Loach profile puts it

Pethia gelius

Tiny golden barb with black blotches on dorsal base and flanks; lacks bottom-dwelling loach profile

Bengal Loach: Golden-yellow deep loach with 7–8 dark oblique bands, 4 barbel pairs, and erectile subocular spine.

Golden Barb: A miniature, translucent golden-yellow cyprinid lacking barbels, with an incomplete lateral line and distinct black spots on the caudal base and dorsal fin origin, is Pethia gelius.

Bengal LoachGolden Barb
Body shapeDeep, laterally compressed body with an arched dorsal profile and flat ventral lineShort, deep, moderately compressed with an arched dorsal profile (Hamilton 1822)
Color patternBrilliant golden-yellow body crossed by seven to eight broad, oblique dark brown bands—
Subocular spineSharp, retractable bifid subocular spine seated in a deep groove beneath each eye—
BarbelsFour distinct pairs of barbels: two rostral pairs, one maxillary, and one mandibular—
SoundsEmits loud, audible clicking sounds using its pharyngeal teeth during social activity—
Size—Diminutive freshwater cyprinid; reaches about 5.1 cm TL (Froese & Pauly 2025)
Mouth & Barbels—Small terminal mouth; barbels completely absent (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Colour—Translucent honey-gold to shimmering brassy-yellow with a silvery belly (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Diagnostic Spots—Black blotch on caudal base, black patch on dorsal fin base, and spot over anal origin (Hamilton 1822)
Dorsal Spine—Slender, osseous dorsal spine finely serrated along its posterior margin (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Lateral Line—Incomplete, with only 3 to 6 pored scales along the anterior flank (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
