How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Indian Crow, as the Common Mime profile puts it

Model for form clytia, which resembles species of Euploea (Talbot 1939, p. 114); C. clytia is distinguished by its prominent row of yellow marginal spots on the hindwing underside (Talbot 1939, p. 117)

रूप clytia इसकी नकल करता है; कॉमन माइम की पहचान निचले पिछले पंख के किनारे बड़े पीले धब्बों से होती है (कौआ तितली)

Common Indian Crow: A large, slow, dark brown butterfly with a margin of white dots and no tails is the common crow.

Common Mime: A tailless butterfly resembling a Euploea crow or Tirumala tiger in flight, but showing a prominent row of yellow marginal spots on the hindwing underside, is Chilasa clytia (Talbot 1939, pp. 115, 117; Evans 1932, p. 47).

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common Indian CrowCommon Mime
SizeWingspan 85–95 mm; a large butterfly—
ColourDark chocolate brown above, paler brown below; the upper forewing with a bluish gloss in fresh males—
SpotsA row of white spots along the outer margins of both wings, and a few larger white spots near the forewing tip—
BodyBlack with white spots; the male has a brand (a dark scent patch) on the forewing—
FlightSlow, gliding, unhurried, at head height; often along the same path back and forth—
CaterpillarBanded white, black and yellow-brown, with four pairs of long black fleshy filaments, on fig and milkweed—
Wingspan—90–120 mm (Evans 1932, p. 47)
Form clytia (dark form)—Upperside velvety black or soft dark brown; forewing submarginal white spots with one spot shifted inwards; resembles Euploea (Talbot 1939)
Form dissimilis (pale form)—Ground-colour black with creamy-white stripes and spots; hindwing cell white with long discal white streaks; resembles Danaus (Talbot 1939)
Form panope & casyapa—panope: pale brown with merged apical spots forming large apical patches; casyapa: brown-black with two rows of discal patches on forewing (Talbot 1939)
Hindwing underside—Marginal row of comparatively large, very conspicuous yellow spots in both forms (Talbot 1939; Evans 1932, p. 47)
Resting posture—At rest, wings are held over the back like Euploea and Danaus, or sometimes in the horizontal Papilionid position (Talbot 1939, p. 115)
