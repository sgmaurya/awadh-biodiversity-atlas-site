Kusha Grass or Munja Grass? कुश या मूँज?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

कुशKusha GrassDesmostachya bipinnata
मूँजMunja GrassSaccharum munja

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Munja Grass, as the Kusha Grass profile puts it

Much taller, 2–4 m, with a large silky plume; stems pithy and used for baskets

बहुत ऊँची, मोटा डंठल

Kusha Grass: A stiff, sharp-leaved tussock with a dense upright straw-coloured brush of flattened spikelets, on usar or a sandy bank, is kusha.

Munja Grass: Solid stem + thick rhizome clump on earthen banks + sharp-edged leaves + silvery plume in October–November + rope-making history = Saccharum munja.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Kusha GrassMunja Grass
HabitStiff, erect tussock grass 0.5–1.5 m, spreading by stout creeping rhizomes into dense patches—
LeavesLong, narrow, 30–60 cm, hard, rolled or flat, tapering to a sharp point, margins rough enough to cut skinLong, narrow (60–120 cm) with sharp, skin-cutting serrated edges and a prominent white midrib
StemsRigid, solid, pale, with old sheaths at the base—
Flower headA dense, erect, narrow panicle 20–60 cm with short crowded branches, pale straw to purple-tinged, July–October—
SpikeletsFlattened, many-flowered, 5–12 mm, packed along the branches—
WhereUsar patches, bunds, river banks: where few other grasses grow—
Height—Tall perennial grass growing in dense clumps, 2–4 m in height
Stem (culm)—Erect, solid (not hollow), slightly glaucous bluish-grey when young
Inflorescence—Large, feathery, silvery-white terminal plume (30–60 cm long), appearing in October–November
Roots—Deep, extensive, fibrous root mat that anchors soil firmly
