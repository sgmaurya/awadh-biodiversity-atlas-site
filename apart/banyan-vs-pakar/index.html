Banyan or White Fig? बरगद या पाकड़?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

बरगदBanyanFicus benghalensis
पाकड़White FigFicus virens

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

White Fig, as the Banyan profile puts it

Leaves smaller, narrower; no prop roots; figs white-green

पाकड़

Banyan: Large figs with hanging prop-root pillars + oval blunt-tipped leaves + red paired figs + found in village or temple setting = banyan. The prop roots are unique among common UP trees.

White Fig: A fig with glossy, smooth, small leaves; figs in pairs at branch tips; and descending aerial roots anchoring the stem is pakar.

Side by side आमने-सामने

BanyanWhite Fig
Size & CanopyLarge to massive evergreen tree (20–30 m tall) with a wide-spreading, umbrella-like canopy spanning 30–50+ m—
Aerial RootsNumerous cord-like roots hanging from horizontal branches, dropping to the ground to form supportive wooden pillarsThin, hair-like, descending from upper branches to soil or rock surface, anchoring the plant

Drip-TipEntirely absent; the leaf tip is rounded rather than elongated into a tail (distinguishes it from peepal)—
Fruit (Figs)Small (1–2 cm diameter) spherical syconia in axillary pairs, green ripening to a bright, glossy orange-red—
LatexAbundant white milky sap that flows immediately from any broken leaf or cut bark—
Size—Small to medium tree, 5–12 m, or a climbing fig on walls and rocks; multi-stemmed
Fruits—Fig fruits (syconia) 1–2 cm across, borne in pairs at leaf axils on branch tips, white-green ripening to yellow, reddish or purple
Bark—Smooth, greyish, exuding white latex when injured
