Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black-naped Hare, as the Hog Deer profile puts it

Lepus nigricollis

Much smaller lagomorph with long upright ears, black patch on the nape, and hopping bounding movement

Black-naped Hare: Long ears, grizzled rufous-brown coat, long hind legs, and a distinctive black patch on the back of the neck distinguish the Black-naped Hare from other lagomorphs.

Hog Deer: Small squat deer with short legs, dark reddish-brown coat, three-tined antlers in males, and a characteristic low-head ducking run through tall river grass.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Size & ShapeMedium-sized mammal; body length 40–60 cm, weight 1.5–3.5 kg; long hind legs adapted for fast leaping—
ColourRufous-brown fur mixed with black on the back, white underparts; a distinct dark black patch on the back of the neck (nape)—
Head & EarsLarge head with long, erect ears (up to 10 cm) tipped with black; large, dark brown eyes situated laterally—
TailShort, bushy tail, rufous-brown above and white below, held down when runningModerate, bushy tail, brown above and pure white underneath, flashed as an alarm signal
LimbsHind limbs are significantly longer and stronger than forelimbs, allowing high-speed bounds—
Body size—Small, squat, robust deer
Coloration—Dark olive-brown or reddish-brown coat with pale underparts, often developing faint pale spots in summer
Antlers (males)—Slender three-tined antlers mounted on prominent hair-covered bony pedicels (Prater 1971)
Running gait—Runs with the head held low, ducking under grass stems rather than leaping high like other deer
Habitat—Obligate dweller of wet alluvial grasslands, riverine reed beds, and floodplains
