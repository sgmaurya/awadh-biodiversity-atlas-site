Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Kukri Snake, as the Cantor's Black-headed Snake profile puts it

Oligodon arnensis

Features distinct chevron markings on the crown of the head and prominent dark crossbands along the body (Smith 1943)

Cantor's Black-headed Snake: Diminutive slender brown snake with a solid jet-black head, broad yellow nape collar, and specialized many-toothed jaws adapted for eating skinks in leaf litter.

Common Kukri Snake: A small dark snake with white/cream bands on the back only (belly pale and unbanded) and clearly visible eyes that never bites is a Kukri Snake. Always harmless.

Ventral surfaceUniformly pale yellow or cream, with a tiny black dot at the lateral edge of each ventral shield—
DentitionSpecialized dentary bone bearing numerous small, closely set, spatulate teeth for gripping prey—
Size—35–50 cm; slender body
Head—Small, pointed snout, not wider than neck
Belly—Pale cream or yellow, largely unbanded (key difference from krait)
Eyes—Small, round pupils; visible eyes (krait's eyes are hard to see)
Body—Smooth scales; not keeled
Behaviour—Docile; never bites; may flatten body if threatened
