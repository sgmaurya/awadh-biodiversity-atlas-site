Microcystis or Star Jelly? हरी काई (ज़हरीली) या काई (ज़मीन की जेली वाली काई)?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

हरी काई (ज़हरीली)MicrocystisMicrocystis aeruginosa
काई (ज़मीन की जेली वाली काई)Star JellyNostoc commune

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Star Jelly, as the Microcystis profile puts it

Gelatinous jelly blobs on wet ground or mud; not a water bloom; appears within hours of rain

ज़मीन की जेली, पानी की नहीं

Microcystis: Smooth, cloudy, pea-green to blue-green water with earthy or foul smell, starting in May and persisting through October, in eutrophic ponds = likely Microcystis bloom. Warn people and livestock.

Star Jelly: Olive-green rubbery jelly lying loose on bare soil the morning after rain, gone to black crust in the sun, is Nostoc.

Side by side आमने-सामने

MicrocystisStar Jelly
AppearanceCloudy, opaque water; pea-green to bright green to blue-green or olive-brown colour—
TextureSmooth, slimy film or suspension; not individual filaments visible to the eye—
SmellEarthy, musty smell (resembles clean algae) OR foul, rotten smell (indicates toxin production)—
DistributionEntire water body clouded; concentrates at surface where wind pushes it; can form a scum at edges—
ScumA slimy, sticky layer or mat may form on the water surface; bright green or blue-green colour; does not float like water hyacinth—
Particle sizeMicroscopic; individual colonies 10–150 µm (not visible to naked eye but visible as a suspension)—
No filamentsUnlike Nostoc (which forms distinct jelly blobs) or Spirogyra (which forms threads), Microcystis is a cloudy suspension—
Wet—Rubbery, gelatinous sheets or lobed blobs 1–10 cm across, olive-green to blue-green or brownish, lying loose on soil
Dry—Shrinks to a thin, brittle, black or dark brown crust that looks like scraps of burnt paper
Feel—Firm jelly, slippery, slightly elastic; no roots, no leaves, no smell
Under a lens—Made of countless beaded threads in jelly; a hand lens shows the granular look
Where—Bare damp soil in the open: courtyards, path edges, lawns, bunds, field surfaces
When—Appears within hours of rain and vanishes into crust within a day or two of sun
