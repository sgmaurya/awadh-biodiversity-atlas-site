How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Teal, as the Falcated Duck profile puts it

Common Teal]] (Anas crecca)

Noticeably smaller, chestnut head with broad iridescent green eye-patch, lacks falcated tertial plumes (Baker 1929, p. 431)

Common Teal: Common Teal is the smallest winter duck, with males showing a chestnut head and green eye-stripe. If the duck is larger and the bill spatulate (spoon-like), it is a Shoveler. If the head is glossy green (not chestnut), it is a Mallard.

Falcated Duck: The shimmering green and bronze-crested head, crescent-barred breast, and long drooping sickle-shaped tertials distinguish the male Falcated Duck (Baker 1929, pp. 424–425).

Habit—Medium-sized dabbling duck with distinctive drooping sickle-shaped tertials on swimming males
Head Pattern—Crown, lores and cheeks chestnut; bronze below eye, becoming green on nape and bushy crest (Baker 1929, p. 424)
Falcated Plumes—Inner secondaries very long, narrow and sickle-shaped, shafts white, webs glossy black edged grey (Baker 1929, pp. 424–425)
Body Pattern—Mantle grey with narrow crescentic bands of black; upper breast buff or whitish with black crescentic bands (Baker 1929, pp. 424–425)
Stern & Patches—Under tail-coverts velvety-black; patch of buff on each side of under tail-coverts (Baker 1929, p. 425)
Soft Parts—Bill black; iris dark brown; legs and feet drab, olive-grey, or olive-brown, webs and toes black (Baker 1929, p. 425)
