How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Roller, as the Oriental Dollarbird profile puts it

Indian Roller]] (Coracias benghalensis)

Slender build, black bill, lilac-streaked breast, greenish-blue crown and rump, lack of circular flight patch (Baker 1927, p. 224)

Indian Roller: A stocky, medium bird perching on a wire over open farmland, rufous-brown at rest, with a sudden flash of brilliant turquoise-blue when it flies — is the Indian Roller. Nothing else in eastern UP creates this effect.

Oriental Dollarbird: The stout dark build, wide vermilion-red bill and legs, and broad pale blue band near the base of the outer primaries distinguish the Oriental Dollarbird (Baker 1927, p. 229).

BehaviourSits motionless on perch, drops suddenly to ground to capture prey, then returns to a perch—
Habit—Stocky forest roller perching high on bare canopy branches and dead trees
Bill & Feet—Heavy, broad bill deep vermilion with blackish tip; legs and feet vermilion (Baker 1927, p. 229)
Wing Patch—Outer primaries black, "with a broad pale blue band near the base" (Baker 1927, p. 229)
Head & Mantle—Top and sides of head dark brown; mantle dull dark greenish-brown turning brighter on rump (Baker 1927, p. 228)
Throat & Breast—Center of chin and throat deep purple-blue with smalt-blue shaft-stripes; breast brownish-green (Baker 1927, p. 229)
Soft Parts—Iris dark brown to crimson-brown; gape orange or bright vermilion (Baker 1927, p. 229)
