Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Red-wattled Lapwing, as the Northern Lapwing profile puts it

Red-wattled Lapwing]] (Vanellus indicus)

Conspicuous red fleshy facial wattles, black crown and breast patch without crest, loud 'did-he-do-it' calls (Baker 1929)

Northern Lapwing: The long slender recurved black crest, glossy dark green upperparts, cinnamon upper and under tail-coverts, and broad black breast patch distinguish the Northern Lapwing from all other Indian plovers (Baker 1929, p. 180).

Red-wattled Lapwing: Medium size, long yellow legs, black head and chest, red facial wattle, and loud "did-he-do-it" alarm calls identify the Red-wattled Lapwing.

Tail PatternWhite tail with a very broad black subapical band glossed with green (Baker 1929, p. 180)—
Tail-CovertsUpper and under tail-coverts bright cinnamon (Baker 1929, p. 180)—
Soft PartsIris brown; bill black; legs and feet orange-brown (Baker 1929, p. 180)—
Size—Medium wader-like bird (around 32–35 cm total length) with long, slender yellow legs
Plumage—Light bronze-brown upperparts; black crown, throat, and upper breast; white lower breast, belly, and sides of neck
Face—Striking red fleshy wattle in front of the eyes; red bill with a prominent black tip
Nests—A simple shallow scrape on bare ground, often lined with small stones or clay fragments
Behaviour—Highly territorial and aggressive when nesting; flies up screaming with deep, frantic wingbeats
