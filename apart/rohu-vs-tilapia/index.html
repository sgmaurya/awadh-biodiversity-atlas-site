Rohu or Mozambique Tilapia? रोहू या तिलापिया?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

तिलापियाMozambique TilapiaOreochromis mossambicus

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rohu, as the Mozambique Tilapia profile puts it

Elongate, soft-finned carp with large silver or golden scales, no spiny dorsal

चाँदी-सुनहरी, काँटे नहीं

Rohu: Large Indian carp + reddish-orange fins + wrinkled lower lip + mid-water behavior in rivers/ponds = rohu. The red fins fade quickly after death — identify in the water or immediately after capture.

Mozambique Tilapia: A deep-bodied, grey-barred, spiny-dorsalled fish that makes up most of a pond haul and never grows large is the Mozambique tilapia; a black male with a white throat on a nest pit confirms it.

Side by side आमने-सामने

RohuMozambique Tilapia
Size—Usually 10–20 cm in ponds; up to 35 cm where not crowded
Shape—Deep-bodied, compressed, perch-like, with a single long dorsal fin
Colour—Grey to olive with faint dark bars; breeding males nearly black with a white throat and red-edged fins; females and young silvery-grey with a few dark blotches
Head—A steep forehead, large mouth; the male's snout becomes enlarged and duck-like in breeding dress
Fins—Dorsal with 15–17 spines then soft rays, pointed at the back; tail fan-shaped with a dark edge
Behaviour—Males dig round nests in the shallows; females carry eggs and fry in the mouth

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
