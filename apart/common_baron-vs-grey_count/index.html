Common Baron or Grey Count? कॉमन बैरन तितली या ग्रे काउंट तितली?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Baron, as the Grey Count profile puts it

Upperside olivaceous brown with dark brown markings and a series of five white discal spots and two preapical white spots on the forewing, lacking the continuous ash-grey terminal band (Bingham 1905, p. 282, entry 302 as E. garuda)

जैतूनी-भूरा ऊपरी भाग और अगले पंख पर सफेद धब्बे, धूसर बाहरी पट्टी नहीं होती (कॉमन बैरन तितली)

Common Baron: A medium-large dark butterfly with distinctive white and coloured patches on dark wings, found in forest margins and near suitable host trees, is the Common Baron.

Grey Count: A dark brown butterfly with a continuous ash-grey terminal band widening from the forewing apex to cover about one-third of the hindwing at the tornus is Tanaecia lepidea (Bingham 1905, p. 272).

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common BaronGrey Count
SizeMedium to large butterfly, wingspan 45-55 mm—
ColourDark brown to purplish-black with distinctive markings—
PatternWhite or pale patches on forewings; coloured patches on hindwings; pattern varies with subspecies and sex—
UndersidePaler, mottled pattern; eyespots and bands presentMale ochraceous brown, female bright ochraceous; paler on hindwing; margins suffused with lilacine-grey; cells with dark brown sinuous lines and loop-like markings (Bingham 1905, pp. 272–273)
FlightMedium-speed flight; stays in canopy and middle vegetation—
HabitatForest margins and secondary growth; not in open grasslands—
Wingspan—Male and female 70–80 mm (Bingham 1905, p. 273); Evans (1932, p. 150) gives 70–80 mm for lepidea lepidea and 65–75 mm for lepidea miyana
Upperside—Dark brown, paler in the female, with very obscure black markings across cells and oblique discal fascia on forewing (Bingham 1905, p. 272)
Terminal band—Ash-grey continuous band along termen of both wings, broadening from apex of forewing to tornus of hindwing where it covers about one-third of the wing; narrowly bordered outwardly with brown in female; cilia white (Bingham 1905, p. 272)
Male scent patch—Patch of specialized dark scales above vein 4 on upperside of hindwing (Bingham 1905, p. 273)

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
