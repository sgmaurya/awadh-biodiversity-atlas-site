Common Vetch or Lentil? अंकरी या मसूर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Lentil, as the Common Vetch profile puts it

Much smaller plant (20–30 cm); tiny leaflets; tiny pods

मसूर — बहुत छोटा पौधा

Common Vetch: A trailing or climbing winter legume with feathery pinnate leaves ending in a tendril, small purple-pink papilionaceous flowers, and linear pods containing round seeds is ankri.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common VetchLentil
Plant formWinter annual, climbing or trailing, up to 60–90 cm with tendrils—
StemsHerbaceous, angular, with 4 ridges; bearing leaf-like tendrils—
LeavesAlternate, compound, pinnate, 5–8 leaflets, 2–4 cm, linear, tendril-bearingPinnately compound (2–5 cm long) with 4–7 pairs of narrow, oblong-linear leaflets (8–15 mm long); leaf axis ends in a short, unbranched tendril or bristle
FlowersPurple, pink, or occasionally white; solitary or paired, 1.5–2 cmTiny (4–6 mm long), pale blue, lilac, or white, pea-like (papilionaceous) in structure, borne singly or in small clusters of 2–4 on long, slender stalks
PodsLinear, 3–6 cm, containing 4–10 round seeds—
SeedsRound, 3–5 mm, glossy, variously colored (brown, black, mottled)Flat, biconvex (lens-shaped), 3–6 mm in diameter; seeds are grey-brown or greenish-black on the outside, and split open to reveal a bright orange-red or yellow interior
Form—Small, erect or spreading, delicate annual herb (20–45 cm tall), densely covered in fine soft hairs
Fruit—Short, flat, smooth, oblong pod (10–15 mm long, 6–8 mm wide), containing 1 or 2 lens-shaped seeds
