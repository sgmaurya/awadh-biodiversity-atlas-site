How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Brooke's House Gecko, as the Leschenault's Leaf-toed Gecko profile puts it

Hemidactylus brookii

Much smaller with heavily tuberculated, rough dorsal skin bearing 14 to 16 rows of sharp trihedral keels (Smith 1935)

Brooke's House Gecko: A gecko with prominently rough, keeled, bark-like skin found climbing tree trunks and stone walls at night is Brooke's House Gecko.

Leschenault's Leaf-toed Gecko: Large tree-dwelling gecko on rough bark with wavy dark W-shaped dorsal chevrons and a back covered in fine, smooth granular scales.

ColorationBrownish, greyish-brown, or tan; may have darker spots or bandsAshy grey or brownish-grey with a series of dark brown undulating W-shaped dorsal bands
Skin textureVery rough, bark-like with prominent keeled scales and tubercles in regular rows—
FeetExpanded digital pads; well-developed lamellae—
TailTapers gradually; regenerable; generally kept at body levelModerately depressed, slightly swollen at the base, with small flat scales
EyesModerately large; vertical pupils—
Body size—Large, robust arboreal gecko with a broad head and stout limbs
Dorsal scalation—Uniformly fine granular scales, lacking enlarged keeled tubercles on the back (Smith 1935)
Head pattern—A dark streak passing through the eye and continuing onto the shoulder
Digits—Strongly dilated pads with divided lamellae underneath, adapted for gripping bark
