Indian Lotus or Water Lettuce? कमल या जलपत्ता?

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Water Lettuce, as the Indian Lotus profile puts it

Small floating rosette, no large flowers

Indian Lotus: A large, peltate leaf held above water on a long stalk = lotus. Leaf floating on water surface = water lily. They are not related plants — different families, different evolutionary histories.

Water Lettuce: Spongy, pale green, velvet-textured sessile leaves arranged in a tight cabbage-like floating rosette identify Pistia stratiotes.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Indian LotusWater Lettuce
LeavesLarge, round (30–80 cm across), held well above the water on tall stalks; peltate (attached at center)Wedge-shaped, pale yellowish-green, arranged in a tight cabbage-like rosette; soft velvety texture
Leaf SurfaceHydrophobic ("lotus effect") where water beads up and rolls off, keeping the leaf dry and clean—
FlowersLarge (15–25 cm across), fragrant, with 20–25 pink or white tepals and a central yellow receptacleTiny, greenish-white, inconspicuous; hidden in the center of the rosette inside a small leaf-like bract
Seed PodFlat-topped, cone-shaped green receptacle (kamal gatta) containing multiple seed cavities—
RhizomeLong, segmented, cylindrical tubers (kamal kakdi) growing in mud, with ringed air channels inside—
HabitatPerennial standing water bodies like ponds, lakes, oxbow lakes, and temple tanks—
Habitat Class—Free-floating aquatic plant; floats freely on the surface of standing or slow-moving water
Leaf Hairs—Covered in dense, soft, water-repellent hairs (trichomes) that trap air and keep the leaf dry
Petiole—Session leaves; lacks any distinct leaf stalks or petioles
Roots—Massive, feathery, dangling roots (up to 50 cm long); suspended in the water column
