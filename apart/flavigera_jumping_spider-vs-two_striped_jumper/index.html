How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Two-striped Jumper, as the Flavigera Jumping Spider profile puts it

Telamonia dimidiata

Slender, elongate yellowish-white body with two prominent longitudinal red bands; normal head shape

Flavigera Jumping Spider: Extremely flattened, compact beetle-mimicking jumping spider with broad rectangular carapace, golden-bronze sheen, and swollen forelegs.

Two-striped Jumper: Red or brown jumping spider with white longitudinal stripes on vegetation, = Telamonia dimidiata.

Body shapeExtremely dorso-ventrally flattened, compact, and heavily sclerotized beetle-like body—
CarapaceExceptionally broad, rectangular, wider than long, with steep flat cephalic plate—
ColorationDark chestnut-brown to shiny bronze, densely clothed in iridescent yellowish-golden squamose scales—
Front legsFirst pair of legs extraordinarily thickened, robust, and muscular for grasping prey—
GaitCreeps slowly and deliberately flat against leaf surfaces, leaping suddenly onto prey—
Size—7–10 mm body length; legspan 15–20 mm
Pattern—Red or brown with distinct white or pale longitudinal stripes
Legs—Banded; striped pattern continues onto legs
Eyes—Two large forward-facing principal eyes; turns to face observer
Habitat—Vegetation foliage and tall grass rather than walls
Behaviour—Active daytime hunter; quick movements
