How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Brinjal Shoot and Fruit Borer, as the Spotted Pod Borer profile puts it

Leucinodes orbonalis

Smaller white moth with fulvous and black markings; feeds on solanaceous crops rather than legumes

Brinjal Shoot and Fruit Borer: Small white snout moth with variegated black, fulvous, and ferruginous wing patches that rests with its abdomen tilted upwards.

Spotted Pod Borer: Brown crambid moth with large translucent white patches on forewings, pearly white hindwings, and a spotted larva that webs legume flowers and pods.

Larval appearanceStout, cylindrical larva, creamy-pink to bright pinkish-white with a brownish-black head capsule—
Plant symptomDrooping, wilted shoot tips ("deadhearts") in young plants and bored holes plugged with frass in fruits—
Resting postureAdults rest with abdomen upturned and wings slightly angled over the leaf surface during daylight—
Forewing pattern—Fuscous brown with a conspicuous white, semihyaline, black-edged discal patch and pale costal streak
Larval markings—Pale greenish-white caterpillar adorned with distinct double rows of dark brown to black dorsal spots
Field symptom—Floral buds, flowers, and tender pods webbed together into messy silken nests containing granular frass
Adult legs—Exceptionally long, slender legs and antennae noticeably exceeding the length of the forewings

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
