Patha or Shikakai? पाठा या शिकाकाई?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Shikakai, as the Patha profile puts it

Leaves pinnate; stems armed with hooked prickles; pods are thick and fleshy

शिकाकाई — काँटेदार; फली मोटी

Patha: A soft-stemmed climber with distinctive large, round, velvety leaves that feel like cloth, twining over hedges and producing small black fruits is patha.

Shikakai: A prickly feathery-leaved climber with cream flower heads and thick wrinkled brown pods that froth in water is shikakai; the thin papery pods of its wild cousin do not.

Side by side आमने-सामने

PathaShikakai
HabitSlender, twining, herbaceous or weakly woody climber, 1–2 mScrambling, thorny shrub or climber, 3–5 m, sprawling over hedges and trees
LeavesAlternate, orbicular to reniform (kidney-shaped), 5–15 cm broad, velvety-hairy, palmately 5–7 veinedTwice-pinnate, 10–20 cm, with 5–7 pairs of pinnae and many tiny leaflets 5–10 mm; a gland on the leaf stalk
StemsSlender, greenish, twining, mostly herbaceous—
FlowersSmall, greenish-white, unisexual; male and female on separate plants (dioecious)Cream to pale yellow, in round fluffy heads 1 cm across, several on a stalk, March–May
FruitsDrupes, globose, 6–8 mm diameter, green ripening to black; one per flower—
Prickles—Short, hooked, scattered along the stems and leaf stalks; they catch cloth and skin
Pods—Fleshy, thick, 7–12 × 2–3 cm, constricted between seeds, wrinkled and brown when dry
Seeds—6–10 per pod, dark, flattened, 8 mm
The test—A dry pod broken in water froths slightly
