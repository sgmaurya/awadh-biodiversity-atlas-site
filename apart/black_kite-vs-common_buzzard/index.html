How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Kite, as the Common Buzzard profile puts it

Black Kite]] (Milvus migrans)

Distinctive shallow-forked tail when soaring, darker brownish plumage, longer narrower wings, lacks rounded buzzard wing outline (Baker 1928)

Common Buzzard: The rounded wings, relatively short broad barred tail, variable rufous-brown underparts with streaking or barring, and unfeathered lower tarsus identify the Common Buzzard (Baker 1928, p. 142).

Side by side आमने-सामने

Black KiteCommon Buzzard
SizeMedium-sized raptor (55–65 cm total length); wingspan 130–150 cm; weight 600–1000 g—
PlumageUniformly dark brown; slightly paler, streaked head and underparts—
TailDistinctly forked (notched) tail; twisted constantly to steer in flight—
WingsLong, broad, deeply "fingered" wingtips (outer primaries)—
Bare partsBlack hooked bill with a yellow cere (fleshy base); yellow legs—
BehaviourEffortless gliding in thermals; bold scavenger, will snatch food near markets—
Habit—Medium-sized, broad-winged buzzard soaring over open terrain or perching upright
Upperparts—Brown above, feathers edged with rufous; head more rufous (Baker 1928, p. 142)
Tail Pattern—Rufous tail with pale tip, white shafts, and a dark subterminal band with faint barring (Baker 1928, p. 142)
Underparts—Variable rufous, chin paler and streaked with dark brown, remainder showing pale bars and spots (Baker 1928, p. 142)
Tarsus Feathering—Upper frontal third to half of tarsus feathered; lower portion naked and yellow (Baker 1928, p. 142)
Soft Parts—Iris yellow to hazel; cere and feet yellow; bill dark lead with lighter base (Baker 1928, p. 142)
