How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Hydrilla, as the Curled Pondweed profile puts it

Hydrilla verticillata

Leaves arranged in distinct whorls of three to eight along stem; straight with sharp teeth, not undulating

Curled Pondweed: Submerged freshwater plant with translucent, sessile, linear-oblong leaves featuring strikingly crinkled, wavy, serrulated margins.

Hydrilla: Submerged green aquatic weed with slender branched stems, leaf whorls of 3 to 8 with sharply toothed margins, and small yellow-potato-like tubers in the bottom mud, is Hydrilla.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Curled PondweedHydrilla
Growth habitEntirely submerged perennial aquatic herb rooted in benthic mud by creeping rhizomes—
Leaf structureTranslucent, sessile, linear-oblong submerged leaves with strongly undulate, crinkled margins—
Leaf serrationDistinctly and finely serrulate margins with microscopic sharp teeth along the edges—
Stem formSlender, branched, slightly compressed stems reaching toward the surface—
TurionsProduces hard, prickly vegetative winter buds (turions) in leaf axils before summer dieback—
Plant habit—Submerged, heavily branched, rooting perennial aquatic herb, forming dense underwater columns up to 1–3 meters
Leaves—Arranged in whorls of 3 to 8 leaves at each stem node; leaflets are linear-lanceolate (6–15 mm) with sharp, microscopic teeth
Stem—Slender, cylindrical, round, bright green, easily fragmented into tiny growing pieces
Tubers—Produces small, yellowish, potato-like underground tubers (5–10 mm) at the root tips that survive dry periods in mud
Feel—The leaves feel rough and rasping when rubbed downward due to small spines on the central vein underside

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
