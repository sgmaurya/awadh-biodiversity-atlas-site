Banyan or Cluster Fig? बरगद या गूलर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

बरगदBanyanFicus benghalensis
गूलरCluster FigFicus racemosa

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Cluster Fig, as the Banyan profile puts it

Figs borne on trunk and branches, not on branchlets; no prop roots

गूलर — तने पर अंजीर

Banyan: Large figs with hanging prop-root pillars + oval blunt-tipped leaves + red paired figs + found in village or temple setting = banyan. The prop roots are unique among common UP trees.

Cluster Fig: A tree with sandpaper-rough leaves and figs growing directly on the trunk in clusters is the cluster fig; no other tree in the district combines these characters.

Drip-TipEntirely absent; the leaf tip is rounded rather than elongated into a tail (distinguishes it from peepal)—
Fruit (Figs)Small (1–2 cm diameter) spherical syconia in axillary pairs, green ripening to a bright, glossy orange-red—
LatexAbundant white milky sap that flows immediately from any broken leaf or cut bark—
Size—Small to medium deciduous tree, 5–15 m, with a spreading crown
Fruits—Fleshy syconia (fig fruits) 2–4 cm across, growing directly from the trunk and older branches in racemes, yellow-brown ripening to reddish-brown
Bark—Greyish-brown, smooth, with white latex exuding from cuts
The test—Look for figs growing from the trunk and thick branches, not the twig tips — this is the signature of the cluster fig
