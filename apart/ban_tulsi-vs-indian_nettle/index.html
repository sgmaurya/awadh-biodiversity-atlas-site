Ban Tulsi or Indian Nettle? बन तुलसी या कुप्पी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Nettle, as the Ban Tulsi profile puts it

Stems lack milky latex; leaves are rhomboid-ovate; female flowers are held in cup-like leafy bracts.

कुप्पी / अहरित — बिना दूध वाले तने, कप जैसे कोष्ठ

Indian Nettle: A bushy herb with broadly toothed, densely hairy leaves that may cause mild itching, and very small flowers in spikes is Acalypha indica.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Ban TulsiIndian Nettle
FormErect, branched, bushy annual or perennial herb (30–80 cm tall), covered in stellate (star-shaped) hairs—
LeavesLanceolate or ovate-lanceolate leaves (3–7 cm long) with serrated margins, carrying two small glands at the leaf baseOvate to oblong, 4–10 cm, coarsely toothed, hairy on both surfaces
FlowersSmall, unisexual, whitish-yellow flowers arranged in terminal spikes; male flowers at the top, female at the baseMales in short racemes; females in longer catkin-like spikes, small and inconspicuous
LatexBroken stems or petioles exude a watery, whitish-yellow milky sap (latex)—
FruitsSmall, oblong, three-lobed capsule (3–4 mm long) containing three shiny, pitted seedsSmall capsules with distinctive lobed appearance
Plant habit—Annual herb, 20–60 cm, bushy, branching from base
Stems—Herbaceous, green, hairy, angular
