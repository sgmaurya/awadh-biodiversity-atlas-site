Indian Beech or Indian Elm? करंज या छिल्ली?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

करंजIndian BeechMillettia pinnata
छिल्लीIndian ElmHoloptelea integrifolia

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Beech, as the Indian Elm profile puts it

Has pinnate compound leaves; pinkish flowers; seed pod is hard, woody, oval, lacks wings

करंज — पिन्नेट संयुक्त पत्ते, चपटी लकड़ी जैसी फलियाँ

Indian Beech: Medium semi-evergreen tree with glossy pinnate leaves (5–9 leaflets), pale pink pea-like flowers in summer, and short, flat, thick woody beaked oval pods containing a single seed, is the Indian Beech.

Indian Elm: Large deciduous tree with rough grey warty bark, simple leaves smelling of cut grass, and millions of round, papery disc-like winged fruits that spin through the air in spring, is the Indian Elm.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Indian BeechIndian Elm
Tree habitMedium-sized, fast-growing, semi-evergreen tree, up to 12–15 meters high with a dense, spreading dome-like canopyLarge deciduous tree up to 15–25 meters high, with a clean, straight trunk and a broad, spreading canopy
LeavesPinnate compound leaves, with 5–9 leaflets; leaflets are glossy, ovate, smooth, and bright greenAlternately arranged, simple, ovate-elliptic, smooth, and paper-thin, smelling strongly of cut grass when crushed
FlowersSmall, pea-like, pale pinkish-purple or white, arranged in short, crowded clusters (racemes)—
FruitShort, flat, thick, woody, yellowish-grey, oval pod, ending in a short curved beakA flat, papery, circular winged fruit (samara), containing a single seed in the center, ends in a deep notch
SeedsUsually a single, red-brown, bean-like seed inside each woody pod—
Bark—Greyish-brown, rough, covered with small, warty pustules (corky lenticels), scaling off in thin flakes
Seed dispersal—The papery wings allow the wind to carry the seeds over long distances by spinning through the air
