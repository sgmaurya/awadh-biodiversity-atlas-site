Giant Catfish or Walking Catfish? मल्ला या मांगुर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

मल्लाGiant CatfishWallago attu
मांगुरWalking CatfishClarias batrachus

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Walking Catfish, as the Giant Catfish profile puts it

Much smaller; has a very long dorsal fin extending along the back; 8 whiskers; can breathe air

मांगुर — लंबी पीठ की पंख, मूंछदार

Giant Catfish, as the Walking Catfish profile puts it

Grows much larger; huge mouth with long whiskers; dorsal fin is short and lacks air-breathing organ

मल्ला — बड़ा मुँह, शल्क-रहित

Giant Catfish: Enormous mouth extending past the eyes, scaleless silvery-grey body, very short dorsal fin, and very long anal fin identify Wallago attu.

Walking Catfish: Scaleless dark body with a long dorsal fin, 8 whiskers around the mouth, and stout sharp pectoral spines used for crawling, identify Clarias batrachus.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Giant CatfishWalking Catfish
FormScaleless, elongated, laterally compressed body; tapers sharply towards the tailElongated, cylindrical body; completely scaleless and covered in a thick protective mucus layer
MouthEnormous, wide mouth; the cleft of the mouth extends backwards beyond the eyes—
Barbels2 pairs of feelers; maxillary barbels are very long, extending past the pectoral fins; mandibular barbels short—
FinsVery short, tiny dorsal fin (only 5 rays) on the upper back; exceptionally long anal fin (85–95 rays) extending along the bellyVery long dorsal fin (60–75 rays) extending along the back; long anal fin (45–55 rays) along the belly
ColorationUniform silvery-grey or greenish-brown on the back; shading to creamy white on the bellyDark greyish-brown to olive-black on the back; belly is pale, muddy yellowish-white
SizeLarge freshwater catfish, commonly growing 80–150 cm in length in river channels—
Head—Broad, flattened, covered with hard bony plates; mouth surrounded by 4 pairs of long, tactile barbels (whiskers)
Pectoral Spines—Stout, sharp, finely serrated pectoral spines (stings) used for defense and crawling on land
Locomotion—Unique snake-like wriggling movement on land, using pectoral spines as anchors
