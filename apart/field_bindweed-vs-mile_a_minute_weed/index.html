Field Bindweed or Mile-a-minute Weed? हिरनखुरी या मीलभर खरपतवार?

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Field Bindweed, as the Mile-a-minute Weed profile puts it

Field Bindweed]] (Convolvulus arvensis)

Has arrowhead-shaped leaves (not heart-shaped); flowers are large white-pink trumpets

Field Bindweed: Trailing or twining winter vine with arrowhead-shaped leaves, small white or pink funnel-shaped flowers that close by noon, and a deep white root system, is the Field Bindweed.

Mile-a-minute Weed: Highly rapid creeping green vine with opposite, heart-shaped leaves, exuding a bitter smell when crushed, and bearing dense, flat-topped clusters of tiny white flowers, is the Mile-a-minute Weed.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Field BindweedMile-a-minute Weed
Plant habitSlender, trailing or twining perennial vine, growing 20–100 cm long, wrapping counter-clockwise around cropsRapidly climbing, twining, herbaceous perennial vine, forming dense tangled mats over surrounding vegetation
LeavesAlternately arranged, simple, arrowhead-shaped (sagittate or hastate), with two sharp pointing lobes at the baseOppositely arranged, simple, heart-shaped (cordate), with long-pointed tips and wavy, shallowly toothed margins
FlowersTrumpet or funnel-shaped (1.5–2.5 cm wide), single or in pairs, pure white or marked with light pink bandsTiny (3–5 mm), white or cream-white, grouped in dense, flat-topped branched clusters (corymbs)
Root systemExtensive, deep, fleshy white taproot system that spreads horizontally and shoots up new plants—
Flowering phaseFlowers open in the early morning sunlight and close tight by noon or early afternoon—
Growth rate—Extremely rapid vegetative growth, creeping and covering large bushes within weeks (hence the name "mile-a-minute")
Seeds—Tiny, black, four-angled achenes, with a crown of white bristly hairs (pappus) for wind dispersal
