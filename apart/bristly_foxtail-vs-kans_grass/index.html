Bristly Foxtail or Kans Grass? चिपकू घास या काँस?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

काँसKans GrassSaccharum spontaneum

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Kans Grass, as the Bristly Foxtail profile puts it

Much taller (1–1.5 m); plume-like inflorescence

कंस — बहुत लंबा, सफेद पंख

Bristly Foxtail: A medium grass with a compact, spike-like inflorescence bearing stiff barbed bristles that stick to clothing is Setaria verticillata.

Kans Grass: Tall clump-forming grass (1.5–4 m) growing on river sands, with narrow leaves having cutting glass-like margins and a white central vein, and massive silver-white feathery flower plumes waving in autumn, is the Kans Grass.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Bristly FoxtailKans Grass
Size30–90 cm tall annual grass—
GrowthTufted, upright stems; branching base—
LeavesLinear, 10–25 cm long, 4–8 mm wide; slightly hairyVery long (50–120 cm) and narrow (5–15 mm), with a white central vein, and sharp, cutting glass-like margins
InflorescenceCompact cylindrical panicle (spike-like), 5–12 cm long; golden or purple-tinged—
BristlesShort, stiff bristles below each spikelet; barbed, causing them to adhere to clothing and animal fur—
The testPull the panicle on your sleeve — bristles catch and adhere; "sticky" feeling—
Plant habit—Tall, erect, clump-forming perennial grass growing 1.5–4 meters high, with extensive underground creeping rhizomes
Stems (Culms)—Slender, solid, bamboo-like, green or yellowish, filled with a dry pith (not juicy like sugarcane)
Flowers—Large (20–50 cm), plume-like terminal clusters (panicles), covered in dense, long, silvery-white silk hairs
Root system—Extremely deep, branching network of roots and rhizomes that secure loose sandy river soil

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
