Black Nightshade or Prostrate Sandmat? मकोय या छोटी दूधी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

छोटी दूधीProstrate SandmatEuphorbia prostrata

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Nightshade, as the Prostrate Sandmat profile puts it

Erect bushy herb; white star-shaped flowers and juicy black berries

सीधा पौधा, सफेद तारे जैसे फूल, काली बेरी

Black Nightshade: Soft hairless plant, white star flowers with a yellow anther cone, and drooping bunches of pea-sized berries turning black: makoi. Green berries are not to be eaten.

Prostrate Sandmat: Prostrate ground-hugging mat exuding milky sap with tiny pinkish cyathia and nodding capsules bearing stiff white hairs strictly along the three keels identifies Euphorbia prostrata.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Black NightshadeProstrate Sandmat
HabitSoft, branching annual 30–90 cm, hairless or nearly soFlat, prostrate annual herb forming circular ground-hugging mats 10–30 cm across (Duthie 1915)
StemGreen or purplish, angled, sometimes with faint ridges—
LeavesAlternate, 3–10 cm, oval with a pointed tip, edges wavy or shallowly toothed, dark greenOpposite, oblong-elliptic to obovate, 3–8 mm long, finely toothed towards apex (Hooker 1887)
FlowersWhite, star-shaped, 1 cm, five petals bent back, a cone of yellow anthers in the centre; 3–8 in a drooping cluster from the stem between leaves—
FruitRound berries 6–10 mm in drooping bunches, green then dull purple-black, with many flat seeds—
WhereRich, moist, part-shaded ground near houses and cattle—
Stems—Slender, reddish-purple or pale green, puberulous on upper surface with milky latex (Hooker 1887)
Cyathia—Axillary, solitary or few in short cymes, minute, purplish, with tiny pink glands (Duthie 1915)
Capsule—Subglobose, 3-angled, with prominent stiff spreading hairs strictly on keels (Hooker 1887)
Seeds—Ovoid-quadrangular, pale reddish-brown, transverse-furrowed on faces (Hooker 1887)
