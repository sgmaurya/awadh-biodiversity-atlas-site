How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rusty Millipede, as the Banded Indian Centipede profile puts it

Two pairs of legs per segment (vs. one pair in centipedes); slow-moving; no venom; curls into spiral; detritivore

Banded Indian Centipede, as the Rusty Millipede profile puts it

Banded Indian Centipede]] (Scolopendra morsitans, MYR-0001)

One pair of legs per segment (not two); flat body; fast-moving; venomous; not red

Banded Indian CentipedeRusty Millipede
Size8–15 cm long; among the largest invertebrates in UP homes and gardens2–3 cm length; 2–3 mm diameter; cylindrical
ColourBanded dark brown/olive body with alternating yellowish crossbandsBright brick-red to rust-orange — unique; no other common UP millipede has this colouration
HeadFlat, reddish-orange to red; 4 ocelli (eye clusters) each side—
ForcipulesLarge, curved, reddish-orange "pincers" at front — venom-injecting modified legs—
Legs21–23 pairs; yellowish; last pair elongated and backward-pointing (mimics antennae)~80–100 pairs; pale cream-yellow; two pairs per body segment
ActivityNocturnal — rarely seen in daytime; found under stones, bark, or inside buildings at night—
Movement—Slow smooth gliding motion driven by metachronal leg waves
Defence—Coils into tight spiral when disturbed — does NOT form a perfect closed ball
Smell—Faint almond-like smell (hydrogen cyanide) when handled — harmless
Habitat—Under leaf litter, damp logs, compost — post-monsoon garden soil

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
