How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Long-legged Buzzard, as the Common Buzzard profile puts it

Long-legged Buzzard]] (Buteo rufinus)

Noticeably larger, longer wings, sandy-cinnamon tail unbarred or faintly barred towards tip, tarsus feathered on upper half to two-thirds (Baker 1928, p. 138)

Common Buzzard: The rounded wings, relatively short broad barred tail, variable rufous-brown underparts with streaking or barring, and unfeathered lower tarsus identify the Common Buzzard (Baker 1928, p. 142).

Long-legged Buzzard: Long-legged buzzard is uniquely identified by its pale brown plumage and extraordinarily long yellow legs. No other common raptor in the region has such distinctive long, bare yellow legs.

HabitMedium-sized, broad-winged buzzard soaring over open terrain or perching upright—
UpperpartsBrown above, feathers edged with rufous; head more rufous (Baker 1928, p. 142)—

UnderpartsVariable rufous, chin paler and streaked with dark brown, remainder showing pale bars and spots (Baker 1928, p. 142)—
Tarsus FeatheringUpper frontal third to half of tarsus feathered; lower portion naked and yellow (Baker 1928, p. 142)—
Soft PartsIris yellow to hazel; cere and feet yellow; bill dark lead with lighter base (Baker 1928, p. 142)—
Size—Large buzzard; 58–65 cm total length
Legs—Distinctively long yellow legs (diagnostic); feathered to tarsus
Plumage—Variable; tawny-brown to russet-brown overall
Head—Relatively small; pale lores
Wings—Long; pale patches on wing coverts visible in flight
Tail—Pale; barred; relatively short
Flight—Soars high with wings in shallow V; hunts from perches
Behaviour—Perches on low posts, telephone poles and ground
