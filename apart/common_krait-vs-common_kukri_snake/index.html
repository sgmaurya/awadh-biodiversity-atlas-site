Common Krait or Common Kukri Snake? करैत या कुकरी साँप?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

करैतCommon KraitBungarus caeruleus
कुकरी साँपCommon Kukri SnakeOligodon arnensis

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Krait, as the Common Kukri Snake profile puts it

Much larger (100+ cm); bands encircle entire body including pale belly; eyes tiny, almost invisible; venomous, nocturnal, deadly

करैत बहुत बड़ा, पूरे शरीर पर पट्टियाँ, आँखें छोटी, जहरीला

Common Krait: Highly polished steel-black body with narrow white crossbands starting midbody, combined with enlarged hexagonal vertebral scales along the backbone, identifies Bungarus caeruleus.

Common Kukri Snake: A small dark snake with white/cream bands on the back only (belly pale and unbanded) and clearly visible eyes that never bites is a Kukri Snake. Always harmless.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common KraitCommon Kukri Snake
FormSlender, cylindrical body; skin is highly polished, glossy, and iridescent steel-black or blue-black—
Markings40–50 narrow white crossbands, often occurring in distinct pairs; bands are absent on the head/neck, starting midbody—
ScalesA row of distinctly enlarged, hexagonal vertebral scales running down the center of the back—
HeadSmall, flat, scarcely distinct from the neck; eyes are small, dark, with indistinct pupilsSmall, pointed snout, not wider than neck
BehaviourStrictly nocturnal; inactive by day, coiling its body and hiding its head beneath the coils when threatenedDocile; never bites; may flatten body if threatened
Size—35–50 cm; slender body
Dorsal pattern—Black or dark brown with 12–20 cream or white crossbands (not encircling belly)
Belly—Pale cream or yellow, largely unbanded (key difference from krait)
Eyes—Small, round pupils; visible eyes (krait's eyes are hard to see)
Body—Smooth scales; not keeled

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
