Bahera or Indian Jujube? बहेड़ा या बेर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

बहेड़ाBaheraTerminalia bellirica
बेरIndian JujubeZiziphus mauritiana

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Jujube, as the Bahera profile puts it

Smaller; thorns on branches; fruit small, reddish, sweet, edible

कांटे, छोटे मीठे लाल फल

Bahera: A large tree with leaves bunched at twig ends, hard winged grey-brown fruits, and leaflessness in April is bahera.

Side by side आमने-सामने

BaheraIndian Jujube
SizeLarge deciduous tree, 15–25 m, with a spreading crown—
LeavesSimple, alternate, elliptic to obovate, 10–20 cm, entire margins, clustering at twig endsAlternately arranged, simple, oval; distinct three main longitudinal veins starting from the base; dark green above, pale woolly-white below
FlowersSmall, greenish-white, clustered in narrow spikes at branch tips, March to AprilTiny (3–5 mm), yellow-green, 5-petalled, with a strong, slightly unpleasant scent, clustered in leaf axils
FruitsEllipsoid to obovoid, hard, grey-brown drupes, 3–4 cm, with two prominent wings; ripens September to October—
BarkDark grey, deeply furrowedDark grey or blackish, rough, cracking into irregular vertical scales
The testLook for a large tree with winged fruit clusters and leaves bunched at the twig ends—
Form—Small tree or spreading thorny shrub (5–12 m); branches are drooping and grow in a zig-zag pattern
Spines—Sharp, paired thorns at the leaf bases; one thorn is straight (1–2 cm), while the other is short and hooked backward
Fruit—Round to oblong fleshy drupe; turns green-yellow to orange-red when ripe; contains a single hard, rugose stone

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
