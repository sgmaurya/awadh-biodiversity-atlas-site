Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Drongo, as the Indian Grey Hornbill profile puts it

Black Drongo]] (Dicrurus adsimilis / related)

Much smaller (28 cm); entirely black body; deeply forked tail; lacks curved bill and casque

Black Drongo: A glossy black open-country bird on a wire, with a deep V-shaped forked tail and repeated sallies for insects, is Black Drongo in eastern UP farmland.

Indian Grey Hornbill: Large grey-brown bird with a long tail, distinctive flap-and-glide flight, and a massive curved bill topped by a pointed casque, moving in the canopy of mature trees, is an Indian Grey Hornbill.

Black DrongoIndian Grey Hornbill
SizeMedium-sized bird (about 28 cm total length); slim, upright postureLarge bird (50–60 cm total length) with a long tail and a massive curved bill
PlumageEntirely glossy blue-black (sheen visible in good light); juveniles duller brown-black—
TailLong and deeply forked (V-shaped or fish-tailed); outer feathers curve outward slightly—
Bill & LegsShort, black legs; fairly strong black bill with bristles at the base—
Favourite perchProminent lookouts: electricity wires, fence posts, bare branches, or cattle backs—
BehaviourSallies out to catch flying insects and returns to perch; mobs crows and raptors aggressivelyStrictly arboreal; moves actively in the canopy of mature fruiting trees
Casque—A prominent, pointed, horn-like growth (casque) on top of the upper bill, blackish-grey
Colouration—Uniform brownish-grey overall; pale grey neck and underparts; dark primary feathers
Tail Pattern—Long, graduated tail; grey with a black band near the end and broad white tips
Flight—Distinctive undulating flap-and-glide sequence; wings make a rustling sound in flight
