How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Northern Bronzeback, as the Indo-Chinese Sand Snake profile puts it

Northern Bronzeback]] (Dendrelaphis biloreatus)

Arboreal slender build; distinct head pattern; lacks four sharp dark dorsal stripes

Indo-Chinese Sand Snake: Smooth scales and 4 or 5 dark brown longitudinal stripes on a pale olive or buff body separate Psammophis condanarus from keeled-scaled keelbacks and arboreal bronzebacks (Smith 1943, p. 304).

Northern Bronzeback: Slender bronze tree snake with large golden eyes, dark temporal streak, and brilliant turquoise interstitial skin visible when inflated in orchard trees.

Body size—Slender, elongated arboreal snake
Coloration—Rich bronze-brown above, with a prominent black temporal stripe extending from the eye onto the neck
Interstitial skin—Striking turquoise or bright powder-blue skin visible between the dorsal scales when inflated
Ventral scales—Pale greenish-white or yellowish, strongly notched and keeled laterally for vertical tree climbing (Smith 1943)
Eyes—Exceptionally large with golden irises and circular pupils, conferring acute diurnal vision
Tail—Very long, slender, tapering to a fine point, comprising roughly one-third of the total length
