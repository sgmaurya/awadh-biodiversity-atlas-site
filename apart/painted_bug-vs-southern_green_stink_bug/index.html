Painted Bug or Southern Green Stink Bug? रंगीन बग या हरा खटमल?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Southern Green Stink Bug, as the Painted Bug profile puts it

Southern Green Stink Bug]] (Nezara viridula)

Much larger (12–15 mm); entirely grass-green; feeds on solanaceous crops

Painted Bug, as the Southern Green Stink Bug profile puts it

Painted Bug]] (Bagrada hilaris)

Much smaller (5–7 mm); black body with orange and white markings; feeds on mustard

Painted Bug: Very small, shield-shaped flat bug about 5–7 mm long, glossy black with orange and white spots, feeding on crucifers (mustard/cabbage), is the Painted Bug.

Southern Green Stink Bug: Bright grass-green, shield-shaped flat bug about 12–15 mm long that releases a strong foul-smelling liquid when touched is the Southern Green Stink Bug.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Painted BugSouthern Green Stink Bug
SizeVery small, shield-shaped bug; body length around 5–7 mm (about half the size of the Green Stink Bug)—
ColourationGlossy black overall, marked with a central orange stripe on the head and scutellum, and orange and white spots on the body marginsEntirely bright grass-green; has a row of three tiny white or yellow spots on the front edge of the scutellum
Feeding damageLeaves show tiny, white spots at feed sites; heavily damaged leaves shrivel and look burnt ("bagrada burn")—
NymphsSmall, rounded, orange-red at first, gradually developing black patches as they growHighly variable; early stages are black with red and white spots, changing to green-and-yellow before adult phase
SeasonalityAppears suddenly in massive numbers during winter, especially when mustard crops are young—
Adult shape—Distinctive shield-shaped, flat-bodied bug; body length around 12–15 mm
Defensive scent—Emits a strong, sharp, unpleasant-smelling oily fluid from glands on the sides of the thorax when touched
Sucking organ—A long, needle-like beak (rostrum) tucked under the head and body when not feeding
