How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Long-tailed Minivet, as the Scarlet Minivet profile puts it

Long-tailed Minivet]] (Pericrocotus ethologus)

Male has a narrower scarlet wing patch; female has a greyish throat and paler yellow underparts

Long-tailed Minivet: Slender canopy passerine with long graduated tail; male jet-black and scarlet with isolated inverted-U wing loop; female olive-grey with bright yellow forehead and throat.

Scarlet Minivet: The large size, glossy black head and mantle contrasting with fiery scarlet underparts in the male, and grey back, orange-yellow forehead and bright yellow underparts in the female identify the Scarlet Minivet.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Long-tailed MinivetScarlet Minivet
Male colorJet-black head, throat, mantle contrasting with brilliant scarlet-red belly—
Female colorOlive-grey crown and mantle, bright yellow forehead band and rich yellow underparts—
Wing patternSecondaries carry isolated red (male) or yellow (female) patch with inverted-U loop—
Tail shapeStrongly stepped long graduated tail with black centre and red/yellow outer feathers—
Social habitGregarious canopy gleaner traveling in active whistling flocks during winter—
Habit—Highly arboreal, gregarious canopy insectivore of moist deciduous and evergreen forests
Size—Largest of the minivets, slender and long-tailed, noticeably larger than a Little Minivet
Male Plumage—Glossy black head, throat, and mantle contrasting brilliantly with fiery scarlet breast, belly, rump, and outer tail feathers (Baker 1924, p. 319)
Female Plumage—Ash-grey above with orange-yellow forehead and narrow supercilium; bright yellow below (Baker 1924, p. 319)
Wings—Black with a broad, conspicuous scarlet (male) or yellow (female) wing patch
Bill & Legs—Black bill and slender black legs
Movement—Restless; hunts among the branches or seizes insects in the air (Baker 1924, p. 321)
