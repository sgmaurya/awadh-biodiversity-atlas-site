Cluster Fig or White Fig? गूलर या पाकड़?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

गूलरCluster FigFicus racemosa
पाकड़White FigFicus virens

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

White Fig, as the Cluster Fig profile puts it

Leaves smaller and smoother; figs on branch tips, not the trunk; aerial roots common

छोटी चिकनी पत्ती, शाखाओं पर अंजीर

Cluster Fig, as the White Fig profile puts it

Larger leaves, rough texture; figs on the trunk, not branch tips; no aerial roots

बड़ी खुरदरी पत्ती, तने पर अंजीर

Cluster Fig: A tree with sandpaper-rough leaves and figs growing directly on the trunk in clusters is the cluster fig; no other tree in the district combines these characters.

White Fig: A fig with glossy, smooth, small leaves; figs in pairs at branch tips; and descending aerial roots anchoring the stem is pakar.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Cluster FigWhite Fig
SizeSmall to medium deciduous tree, 5–15 m, with a spreading crownSmall to medium tree, 5–12 m, or a climbing fig on walls and rocks; multi-stemmed
LeavesSimple, alternate, ovate to elliptic, 5–15 cm, with entire or slightly lobed margins, rough texture aboveAlternate, simple, elliptic-lanceolate, 4–10 cm, glossy, margins entire
FruitsFleshy syconia (fig fruits) 2–4 cm across, growing directly from the trunk and older branches in racemes, yellow-brown ripening to reddish-brownFig fruits (syconia) 1–2 cm across, borne in pairs at leaf axils on branch tips, white-green ripening to yellow, reddish or purple
BarkGreyish-brown, smooth, with white latex exuding from cutsSmooth, greyish, exuding white latex when injured
The testLook for figs growing from the trunk and thick branches, not the twig tips — this is the signature of the cluster fig—
Aerial roots—Thin, hair-like, descending from upper branches to soil or rock surface, anchoring the plant
