How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Banded Indian Centipede, as the Flat-backed Millipede profile puts it

Flat body, but carries only one pair of legs per segment; fast-moving predator; possesses venomous claws.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Flat-backed MillipedeBanded Indian Centipede
Body ShapeDorsoventrally flattened body with prominent lateral yellow-cream flanges (paranota) on each segment—
ColourDark brown to blackish-brown body colour with contrasting bright yellow or cream-coloured edgesBanded dark brown/olive body with alternating yellowish crossbands
LegsPale cream-yellow legs; adults have two pairs of legs per body segment (except the first few)21–23 pairs; yellowish; last pair elongated and backward-pointing (mimics antennae)
Size15.0–20.0 mm in length; approximately 1.5–2.0 mm wide8–15 cm long; among the largest invertebrates in UP homes and gardens
Legs per segmentTwo pairs per segment (millipede trait)—
MovementSlow, steady, rhythmic forward gliding; does not run erratically—
DefenceCoils into a flat spiral when disturbed; releases an almond-like odour (hydrogen cyanide)—
Head—Flat, reddish-orange to red; 4 ocelli (eye clusters) each side
Forcipules—Large, curved, reddish-orange "pincers" at front — venom-injecting modified legs
Activity—Nocturnal — rarely seen in daytime; found under stones, bark, or inside buildings at night

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
