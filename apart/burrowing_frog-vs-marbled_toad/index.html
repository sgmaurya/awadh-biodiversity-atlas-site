Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Burrowing Frog, as the Marbled Toad profile puts it

A frog, not a toad: smooth skin, a hard spade on the heel, rounder body; hops

चिकनी त्वचा, एड़ी पर फावड़ा

Indian Burrowing Frog: Globose body shape, short head, short limbs, and a large black, spade-shaped metatarsal tubercle on the inner hind foot identify the Indian Burrowing Frog.

Marbled Toad: A pale, marbled, warty toad whose head has no black ridges is the marbled toad; ridges mean the common toad.

Side by side आमने-सामने

HeadBroad and short head with a rounded snout; eyes large and protrudingWithout the bony black ridges that run along the eyes of the common toad; the skin of the head smooth
ColourationSandy-brown or olive-grey dorsum with dark mottling; white or marbled underside—
SkinSmooth to finely warty skin; folds above the ears (tympanum)Warty but less so than the common toad; the parotoid glands behind the eyes oval, moderate
MovementCrawls or makes clumsy, short hops; burrows backward into sandy soil in seconds—
Colour—Pale olive, grey or sandy, marbled with darker blotches; often a pale line down the back; belly whitish
Toes—Webbed about halfway; a pale tubercle on the heel
Call—A long, rattling, pulsed trill, lower and more drawn-out than the common toad's
