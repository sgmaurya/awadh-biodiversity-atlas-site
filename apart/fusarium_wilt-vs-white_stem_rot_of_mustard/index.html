How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Fusarium Wilt, as the White Stem Rot of Mustard profile puts it

Fusarium Wilt]]**

Plant wilts; vascular tissue brown or yellow, not white and mushy

Fusarium Wilt: Progressive wilting of one or more branches, with brown or reddish-brown vascular discolouration inside the stem, with no recovery, is Fusarium Wilt. The disease is permanent and fatal to that plant.

White Stem Rot of Mustard: White, moldy lesion on mustard stem with black sclerotia visible inside; fallen plant in February–March = white stem rot.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Fusarium WiltWhite Stem Rot of Mustard
Early symptomsWilting of one or more branches while rest of plant remains green; yellowing of leaves on infected branch—
Progressive wiltGradual spread to rest of plant; plant finally wilts completely and dies—
Vascular discolourationCut the stem near soil level: interior shows brown or reddish-brown discolouration of vascular tissue (xylem); may see pink fungal growth—
Root symptomsRoots appear dark, rotted; may be invaded by secondary organisms—
Fungal growthPink or white mould may be visible on infected tissue under moist conditions—
TimingCan occur at any growth stage; most obvious when plant is stressed (heat, drought)—
Initial symptom—Soft, water-soaked lesion on stem; may start at stem base or any point where leaves contact stem
Lesion color—White to pale brown; mycelium visible as white cotton-like growth
Sclerotia—Hard, black, irregular bodies (2–10 mm) visible inside or on infected stem; look like mustard seeds or charcoal pieces
Stem collapse—Stem becomes soft, mushy, and collapses; plant falls over
Smell—Characteristic musty, fungal odor from infected tissue
Leaf symptoms—Leaves above infection point wilt and die; stem becomes hollow and brittle
