Spot Blotch of Wheat or Wheat Leaf Rust? गेहूँ का झुलसा या गेहूँ का रतुआ?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

गेहूँ का झुलसाSpot Blotch of WheatBipolaris sorokiniana
गेहूँ का रतुआWheat Leaf RustPuccinia triticina

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Wheat Leaf Rust, as the Spot Blotch of Wheat profile puts it

Orange or reddish pustules (not brown spots); rounded; occur on leaf undersurface

पत्ती रस्ट — नारंगी छोटे उभार

Spot Blotch of Wheat: Oval to elongate brown lesions with darker borders and light centres, appearing first on lower leaves in cool weather (November–January), are Spot Blotch. Every wheat field in eastern UP has it.

Wheat Leaf Rust: Orange-rust dust on lower wheat leaf surface in February–March = wheat leaf rust. Every other disease is either rarer in eastern UP or visually distinct.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Spot Blotch of WheatWheat Leaf Rust
Leaf spotBrown, oval to elongate lesions 2–4 mm, with a darker border and a light (tan) centre; often with concentric rings—
Spread patternSpreads from lower to upper leaves as the crop grows; often starts at seedling stage—
Sheath blightLesions on leaf sheaths and stems; may girdle the stem, causing death of upper parts—
Grain discolourationInfected grains appear dark brown or black (smutty appearance)—
Lesion odourInfected tissue may have a characteristic fungal smell—
TimingMost visible November–February in eastern UP; during the cool, wet phase of crop growth—
Lesion colour—Reddish-orange to rust-brown pustules (uredinia) on wheat leaves
Pustule appearance—Small (0.5–2 mm diameter), circular to oval, slightly raised
Leaf surface affected—Predominantly on the lower (abaxial) surface, sometimes on upper surface
Pustule arrangement—Scattered or clustered; may coalesce into streaks or patches
Leaf damage pattern—Chlorosis (yellowing) around pustules; necrosis (browning) in severe infections
Stem and spike symptoms—Can appear on leaf sheaths and occasionally on the flowering head (spike); orange-red discolouration
Spore dust—Reddish-orange powdery spores (uredospores) rub off easily onto fingers or clothing
Late infection sign—Black, elongated streaks (telia) on leaves near harvest if cool, wet weather persists
