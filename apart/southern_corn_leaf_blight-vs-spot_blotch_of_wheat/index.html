How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Spot Blotch of Wheat, as the Southern Corn Leaf Blight profile puts it

[[wheat\

Wheat]], [[barley\

Southern Corn Leaf Blight: Check lesion boundaries and spore shape: elongate tan lesions strictly bounded by parallel leaf veins with curved conidia indicate Cochliobolus heterostrophus, whereas huge cigar-shaped lesions crossing veins indicate Exserohilum turcicum.

Spot Blotch of Wheat: Oval to elongate brown lesions with darker borders and light centres, appearing first on lower leaves in cool weather (November–January), are Spot Blotch. Every wheat field in eastern UP has it.

Affected organPredominantly foliar blades, expanding to leaf sheaths and ear husks under severity—
Canopy impactLower foliage withers first, spreading upward into an extensive scorch—
Leaf spot—Brown, oval to elongate lesions 2–4 mm, with a darker border and a light (tan) centre; often with concentric rings
Spread pattern—Spreads from lower to upper leaves as the crop grows; often starts at seedling stage
Sheath blight—Lesions on leaf sheaths and stems; may girdle the stem, causing death of upper parts
Grain discolouration—Infected grains appear dark brown or black (smutty appearance)
Lesion odour—Infected tissue may have a characteristic fungal smell
Timing—Most visible November–February in eastern UP; during the cool, wet phase of crop growth
