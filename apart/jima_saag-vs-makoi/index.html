Jima Saag or Black Nightshade? जिमा साग या मकोय?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

मकोयBlack NightshadeSolanum nigrum

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Nightshade, as the Jima Saag profile puts it

Erect bushy herb; white star flowers; succulent round black berries

सीधा पौधा, सफेद तारे जैसे फूल, काली बेरी

Jima Saag: Prostrate green mat with clear sap, whorled spathulate leaves with a distinct bitter taste, and axillary clusters of tiny greenish-white flowers identifies Glinus oppositifolius.

Black Nightshade: Soft hairless plant, white star flowers with a yellow anther cone, and drooping bunches of pea-sized berries turning black: makoi. Green berries are not to be eaten.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Jima SaagBlack Nightshade
HabitDiffuse prostrate or ascending annual herb forming spreading green mats 15–45 cm across (Duthie 1903)Soft, branching annual 30–90 cm, hairless or nearly so
FoliageFalse-whorled or opposite, spathulate to oblanceolate, 1.0–3.0 cm long, entire (Hooker 1879)—
StemsMuch-branched from the base, slender, terete, glabrous or sparsely puberulous—
FlowersAxillary fascicles of 2–6 on slender pedicels, 3.0–4.5 mm across, greenish-white (Hooker 1879)White, star-shaped, 1 cm, five petals bent back, a cone of yellow anthers in the centre; 3–8 in a drooping cluster from the stem between leaves
FlavourConspicuously bitter, refreshing herbal taste when chewed fresh—
SeedsTiny, subreniform, reddish-brown, bearing a slender curved white appendaged strophiole (Hooker 1879)—
Stem—Green or purplish, angled, sometimes with faint ridges
Leaves—Alternate, 3–10 cm, oval with a pointed tip, edges wavy or shallowly toothed, dark green
Fruit—Round berries 6–10 mm in drooping bunches, green then dull purple-black, with many flat seeds
Where—Rich, moist, part-shaded ground near houses and cattle
