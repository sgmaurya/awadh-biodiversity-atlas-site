Chickpea or Pigeon Pea? चना या अरहर?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

चनाChickpeaCicer arietinum
अरहरPigeon PeaCajanus cajan

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Pigeon Pea, as the Chickpea profile puts it

Grows as a tall woody shrub (1.5–3 m); leaves are trifoliate (only 3 leaflets); flowers are large and yellow.

अरहर — लंबा झाड़ीदार पौधा, तीन बड़े पत्रक, बड़े पीले फूल

Chickpea, as the Pigeon Pea profile puts it

Much shorter herb (under 50 cm); leaves are pinnately divided with many small leaflets; flowers are pink/white (not large yellow).

चना — छोटा पौधा, संकीर्ण विभाजित पत्तियां, गुलाबी/सफेद फूल

Side by side आमने-सामने

ChickpeaPigeon Pea
FormSmall, erect, bushy annual herb (20–50 cm tall), covered in sticky glandular hairsTall, woody, branched leguminous shrub (1.5–3 m tall) with a deep taproot and ribbed, hairy green stems
LeavesAlternately arranged, pinnately compound (5–8 cm long) with 9–15 pairs of small, ovate leaflets carrying sharply serrated (toothed) marginsAlternately arranged, trifoliate (3 leaflets); leaflets are oblong-lanceolate (5–10 cm long, 2–4 cm wide), velvety green above, pale silvery-green with fine hairs below
Glandular HairsEntire plant is covered in sticky glandular hairs that secrete a sour-tasting acid dew (malic and oxalic acid)—
FlowersSmall (8–12 mm long), pea-like, borne singly on long axillary stalks; color is typically pinkish-purple (Desi types) or pure white (Kabuli types)Large (1.5–2 cm long), pea-like (papilionaceous), bright yellow or yellow with reddish-purple stripes on the back of the largest petal (standard)
FruitSmall, swollen, oblong, inflated pod (1.5–2.5 cm long, 1–1.5 cm wide), covered in sticky hairs, containing 1 or 2 wrinkled seedsLinear-oblong, flat, hairy pod (5–9 cm long) marked with dark oblique lines or mottling between the seeds; ending in a sharp beak
SeedsAngular, wrinkled, beak-shaped seed (5–10 mm size) resembling a ram's head, ranging from dark brown (Desi) to cream-white (Kabuli)Round to oval (4–6 mm diameter), slightly compressed, light brown, tan, or cream-colored, with a small white scar (hilum)
