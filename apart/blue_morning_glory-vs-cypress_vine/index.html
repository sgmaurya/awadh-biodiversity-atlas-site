Blue Morning Glory or Cypress Vine? नीली घुइयाँ या कामलता?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

नीली घुइयाँBlue Morning GloryIpomoea nil
कामलताCypress VineIpomoea quamoclit

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Blue Morning Glory, as the Cypress Vine profile puts it

Broader, less dissected leaves; blue or purple flowers; less feathery foliage

पत्तियाँ चौड़ी, नीले फूल

Blue Morning Glory: Slender twining annual vine with hairy stems, deeply 3-lobed leaves, and large sky-blue or purple funnel flowers that open in the morning and close by afternoon, is the Blue Morning Glory.

Cypress Vine: A twining climber with extremely fine, feathery cypress-like foliage and bright scarlet star-shaped flowers is Cypress Vine.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Blue Morning GloryCypress Vine
Plant habitSlender, fast-growing annual twining vine, climbing up to 2–5 meters over surrounding hedges—
StemCovered in dense, rough, spreading brown hairs (retrorse hairs) that point downwardTwining, herbaceous, slender, green, smooth
LeavesAlternately arranged, simple, broad (5–15 cm), deeply split into three pointed lobes (ivy-like)Alternate, pinnate or bipinnate, deeply dissected into very fine, linear segments resembling cypress foliage
FlowersLarge (5–8 cm wide), trumpet-like, opening bright sky-blue or purple in the morning, fading to pink-purple before closingSolitary or in pairs, funnel-shaped, scarlet to orange-red (rarely white), star-shaped when opened flat, 2–3 cm across
ToxicityTOXIC SEEDS: The black seeds (Kala Dana) contain LSA alkaloids. Do NOT ingest. Highly toxic to humans and cattle.—
Fruit—Globular capsule, 0.8–1 cm diameter
Tendrils—Twining stem acts as the climbing mechanism (no true tendrils)
The test—Feathery, cypress-like leaves; bright red star-shaped flowers; twining herbaceous vine
