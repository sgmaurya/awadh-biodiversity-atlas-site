Forest Wagtail or Grey Wagtail? फॉरेस्ट वैगटेल या ग्रे खंजन?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Grey Wagtail, as the Forest Wagtail profile puts it

Grey Wagtail]] (Motacilla cinerea)

Blue-grey upperparts, bright yellow undertail-coverts, and extraordinarily long tail pumped vertically; haunts running streams

Forest Wagtail: The olive-brown upperparts, double black breast band, bold yellowish-white wing bars, and unique lateral (side-to-side) tail swaying distinguish the Forest Wagtail from all Motacilla wagtails.

Grey Wagtail: Grey Wagtail is distinguished by its very long tail, grey back, yellow underparts (at least on undertail-coverts), and strong association with flowing water. The tail's constant bobbing motion is characteristic of all wagtails.

HabitSolitary or paired woodland wagtail of shaded forest floors and quiet trails—
SizeSlender wagtail roughly the size of a [[white_browed_wagtail\Small passerine; 18–20 cm long (including long tail)

Tail MovementUniquely wags its tail side-to-side (laterally) rather than up and down—
WingsBroad yellowish-white double wing bars and conspicuous pale wing patches—
UpperpartsUniform olive-brown above, with prominent whitish supercilium and dark eye-stripe—
PerchingFreely perches across horizontal branches, unlike open-country wagtails—
Build—Slender, long-tailed; upright posture
Plumage—Grey back; black and white head pattern; yellow underparts (breeding males)
Non-breeding—Duller; less yellow underparts; white throat
Tail—Characteristically long, constantly bobbing up and down
Legs—Dark; long for a small bird
Bill—Fine, pointed; adapted for catching insects
Behaviour—Walks and runs on ground near water; constantly wags tail
Flight—Undulating; often along water course
