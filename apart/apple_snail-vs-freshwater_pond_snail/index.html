Freshwater Apple Snail or Freshwater Pond Snail? घोंघा या मीठे पानी का घोंघा?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

घोंघाFreshwater Apple SnailPila globosa
मीठे पानी का घोंघाFreshwater Pond SnailLymnaea acuminata

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Freshwater Apple Snail, as the Freshwater Pond Snail profile puts it

Much larger (5–8 cm); round, globular shell; has a hard operculum; different egg-laying habit

घोंघा / सेब घोंघा — बहुत बड़ा आकार, ढक्कन युक्त खोल

Freshwater Pond Snail: Small size (15–30 mm), fragile, translucent yellowish shell, extremely large body whorl with a short, sharply pointed spire, and absence of an operculum identify Lymnaea acuminata.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Freshwater Apple SnailFreshwater Pond Snail
ShellLarge, round (globose); diameter 5–8 cm; heavier than it looks—
Shell colourGreenish-olive to dark brown; banded in some individuals—
OperculumHard, horny, oval lid that seals shell opening — very diagnosticAbsent (unlike Apple Snails); retracts directly into the shell without a closing lid
SiphonLong fleshy tube extended to water surface in low-oxygen water—
EggsPink-orange clusters of round eggs, each 3–4 mm, laid above waterline—
BodyGrey-black with yellowish mottling; large muscular foot; eyes on short stalksSoft, pale greyish-green or yellowish body with broad, flat, triangular tentacles
Shell Form—Thin, fragile, ovate-oblong shell with a very large body whorl and a short, highly pointed spire
Shell Color—Pale yellowish-horn to translucent brownish-grey; surface is relatively smooth and shiny
Aperture—Exceptionally large, oval opening (aperture), occupying more than three-quarters of the total shell height
Movement—Slow gliding over aquatic plants, mud surfaces, or floating upside down on the water film
