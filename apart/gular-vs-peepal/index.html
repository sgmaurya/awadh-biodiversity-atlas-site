Cluster Fig or Peepal? गूलर या पीपल?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

गूलरCluster FigFicus racemosa
पीपलPeepalFicus religiosa

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Cluster Fig, as the Peepal profile puts it

Figs in dense clusters on trunk, not in leaf axils

गूलर — तने पर अंजीर

Cluster Fig: A tree with sandpaper-rough leaves and figs growing directly on the trunk in clusters is the cluster fig; no other tree in the district combines these characters.

Peepal: A heart-shaped leaf with a long thin drip-tip + a large open-crowned tree + figs in pairs at leaf bases + village or temple location = peepal. The drip-tip is the most reliable single feature.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Cluster FigPeepal
SizeSmall to medium deciduous tree, 5–15 m, with a spreading crown—
LeavesSimple, alternate, ovate to elliptic, 5–15 cm, with entire or slightly lobed margins, rough texture aboveShiny, heart-shaped (cordate) leaves with a characteristically long, narrow, pointed drip-tip (2–5 cm)
FruitsFleshy syconia (fig fruits) 2–4 cm across, growing directly from the trunk and older branches in racemes, yellow-brown ripening to reddish-brown—
BarkGreyish-brown, smooth, with white latex exuding from cutsSmooth, pale grey to whitish on young trees, becoming scaly with thin papery flakes on older trunks
The testLook for figs growing from the trunk and thick branches, not the twig tips — this is the signature of the cluster fig—
Size & Shape—Large deciduous or semi-evergreen tree (20–30 m tall) with a massive, wide-spreading crown
Leaf Petiole—Long, slender, flexible stalks (5–10 cm) that cause the leaves to tremble and shimmer in the slightest breeze
Fruit (Figs)—Small, round (1–1.5 cm diameter) syconia borne in pairs in leaf axils; green ripening to dark purple-black
Growth Habit—Frequently begins life as a hemi-epiphyte in wall crevices, building cracks, or forks of other trees
