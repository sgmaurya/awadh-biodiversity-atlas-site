Palash, as the Siris profile puts it

Palash]] [FLR-0037])

Leaves are trifoliate (only 3 large leaflets); flowers are bright orange-red (not cream-yellow); pods contain a single seed at the tip.

Palash: Crooked trunk, large leathery trifoliate leaves (three leaflets), and masses of bright orange-red parrot-beak shaped flowers on leafless branches in spring identify the Palash tree.

Size & ShapeMedium-sized tree (10–15 m tall) with a crooked, irregular trunk and twisted branches—
BarkRough, fibrous, ash-colored or dark brown bark, exuding a bright red gum when cutGreyish-brown, rough, cracking into irregular rectangular flakes on older trunks
LeavesDistinctive large trifoliate leaves; leaflets are broad, leathery, and rounded, often described as 'three leaves of Dhak'Alternately arranged, bipinnately compound (15–40 cm long); 2–4 pairs of pinnae, each carrying 6–10 pairs of large, oblong, asymmetrical leaflets
FlowersLarge, densely clustered, bright orange-red or salmon-colored flowers shaped like a parrot's beakGlobose, brush-like, fragrant clusters (5–8 cm diameter) of greenish-yellow to cream flowers with numerous long stamens
Fruit & SeedsFlat, yellowish-brown, single-seeded woody pods (10–15 cm long) covered with fine silvery hairs—
Form—Large deciduous tree (15–25 m) with a broad, spreading canopy; older branches are grey-brown and smooth
Fruit—Large, flat, papery straw-yellow to brown pods (15–30 cm long, 3–5 cm wide), containing 6–12 flat rounded seeds; bulges over seeds are visible
Acoustics—Dry pods rattle continuously in the wind, a key auditory identification feature during the dry season
