How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Banded Pond Snail, as the Pot-mouthed Bithynia profile puts it

Bellamya bengalensis

Much larger viviparid snail with rounded globose whorls and dark spiral color bands

Banded Pond Snail: A thumb-top-sized, pointed, banded, lidded pond snail that gives birth to live shelled young is the banded pond snail.

Pot-mouthed Bithynia: Small ovate-conical freshwater snail with an expanded, thickened, pot-like aperture and a hard calcareous concentric operculum.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Banded Pond SnailPot-mouthed Bithynia
SizeShell 25–40 mm tall, 18–28 mm wide—
ShapeConical, with 5–6 rounded whorls separated by a deep groove (suture); the spire tall and pointed—
ColourOlive-green to yellowish-brown, usually with three or four dark spiral bands; often coated with algae—
OpeningOval, sealed by a thin, horny, oval lid (operculum) with concentric rings—
AnimalGrey-brown with a pair of tentacles; the male's right tentacle thickened—
YoungTiny shelled snails released alive, sometimes dozens from one female—
Shell shape—Ovate-conical, moderately thin but solid shell with several convex whorls
Aperture—Subcircular to oval, with a distinct flared, thickened, and pot-like expanded peristome margin
Operculum—Calcareous, rigid, concentric operculum fitting tightly into the flared aperture
Spire—Moderately elevated with sharp apex and clearly impressed sutures separating whorls
Shell color—Pale olive-green to yellowish-brown, often coated with dark fine organic silt

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
