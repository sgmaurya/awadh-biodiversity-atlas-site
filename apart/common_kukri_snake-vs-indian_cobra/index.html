Common Kukri Snake or Indian Cobra? कुकरी साँप या नाग?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

कुकरी साँपCommon Kukri SnakeOligodon arnensis
नागIndian CobraNaja naja

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Cobra, as the Common Kukri Snake profile puts it

Much larger; can spread hood; large eyes; venomous

बहुत बड़ा, फन फैलाता है, जहरीला

Common Kukri Snake: A small dark snake with white/cream bands on the back only (belly pale and unbanded) and clearly visible eyes that never bites is a Kukri Snake. Always harmless.

Indian Cobra: Spreading a wide flat hood when threatened, combined with a black-and-white spectacle mark on the back of the hood and two dark patches underneath, identifies Naja naja.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common Kukri SnakeIndian Cobra
Size35–50 cm; slender body—
HeadSmall, pointed snout, not wider than neck—
Dorsal patternBlack or dark brown with 12–20 cream or white crossbands (not encircling belly)—
BellyPale cream or yellow, largely unbanded (key difference from krait)—
EyesSmall, round pupils; visible eyes (krait's eyes are hard to see)—
BodySmooth scales; not keeled—
BehaviourDocile; never bites; may flatten body if threatened—
Form—Slender to moderately heavy-bodied snake; smooth, glossy scales without pits
Hood—Distinctive ability to expand the neck ribs to form a wide, rounded hood when threatened
Hood Mark—A black and white "spectacle" design (resembling a pair of spectacles) on the dorsal side of the hood
Underside—Two dark spots near the edges on the ventral side of the hood, separated by light bands
Coloration—Highly variable; usually yellowish-brown, olive-brown, or greyish-black; sometimes with faint crossbands

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
