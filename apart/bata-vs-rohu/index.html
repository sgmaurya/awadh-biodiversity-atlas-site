Bata or Rohu? बाटा या रोहू?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

बाटाBataLabeo bata
रोहूRohuLabeo rohita

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Rohu, as the Bata profile puts it

Larger (40+ cm); darker colouration; different gill raker count; larger head

बड़ी रोहु — गहरा रंग, ज़्यादा बड़ा

Bata: A medium-sized silvery carp 15–30 cm long with a robust build, moderate-sized scales, and no distinctive markings, found in rivers and large ponds during monsoon, is Labeo bata; gill raker count (80–120) and scale counts confirm identification.

Rohu: Large Indian carp + reddish-orange fins + wrinkled lower lip + mid-water behavior in rivers/ponds = rohu. The red fins fade quickly after death — identify in the water or immediately after capture.

Side by side आमने-सामने

BataRohu
SizeMedium; 15–30 cm in length; robust buildLarge carp, up to 1 m and 25 kg (Talwar & Jhingran 1991); typically 40-60 cm in ponds, larger in rivers
ColourSilvery-white to golden-silver; flanks lighter than back—
Body shapeRobust, laterally compressed; terete (rounded) in cross-section—
HeadLarge, blunt snout; oblique mouth—
ScalesModerate-sized, cycloid; small scales on cheeksLarge, cycloid (smooth-edged)
FinsDorsal fin with 14–15 rays; anal fin with 11–13 rays; pectoral and pelvic fins greyish—
EyesModerately sized, positioned laterally; golden ring around pupil—
Silver-grey body—with reddish-orange tinge on scales, especially on the sides; each scale edged with a dark outline giving a reticulated (net-like) appearance
Reddish fins—pectoral, pelvic, and caudal fins tinged red or orange — the most reliable identification feature
Lips—Thick, fleshy, transversely wrinkled lower lip — the rostral cap (upper lip) prominent and overhanging; characteristic of Labeo
Terminal mouth—mouth at the front of the snout (vs. inferior in Catla which is downward-facing)
