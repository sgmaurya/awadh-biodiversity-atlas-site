Hornwort or Hydrilla? जलनीम या जल-जड़ी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

जलनीमHornwortCeratophyllum demersum
जल-जड़ीHydrillaHydrilla verticillata

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Hydrilla, as the Hornwort profile puts it

Leaves in whorls of 3–8 with toothed margins; roots present; plant rooting in sediment

3-8 पत्तियों के चक्र, कांटों वाली, जड़ें होती हैं

Hornwort, as the Hydrilla profile puts it

Leaves are deeply divided into needle-like forks, arranged in whorls; lacks true roots; feels stiff

सींग-पत्ती — सुई जैसे बंटे पत्ते, जड़ों का पूर्ण अभाव

Hornwort: A fully submerged, free-floating plant with needle-like, finely divided leaves in stiff whorls and no roots is hornwort.

Hydrilla: Submerged green aquatic weed with slender branched stems, leaf whorls of 3 to 8 with sharply toothed margins, and small yellow-potato-like tubers in the bottom mud, is Hydrilla.

Side by side आमने-सामने

HornwortHydrilla
Plant habitEntirely submerged, branching, free-floating, dark greenSubmerged, heavily branched, rooting perennial aquatic herb, forming dense underwater columns up to 1–3 meters
LeavesDeeply divided, needle-like, arranged in whorls, rigid, stiff texture when handledArranged in whorls of 3 to 8 leaves at each stem node; leaflets are linear-lanceolate (6–15 mm) with sharp, microscopic teeth
RootsNone; plant is fully independent of soil—
FlowersTiny, obscure, emerging briefly at water surface—
FruitSmall, hard, spine-tipped, 1-seeded nut—
FeelStiff, brittle; feels like fine wires when squeezedThe leaves feel rough and rasping when rubbed downward due to small spines on the central vein underside
Stem—Slender, cylindrical, round, bright green, easily fragmented into tiny growing pieces
Tubers—Produces small, yellowish, potato-like underground tubers (5–10 mm) at the root tips that survive dry periods in mud
