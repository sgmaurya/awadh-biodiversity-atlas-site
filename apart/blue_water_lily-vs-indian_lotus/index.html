Blue Water Lily or Indian Lotus? नीलकमल या कमल?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

नीलकमलBlue Water LilyNymphaea nouchali
कमलIndian LotusNelumbo nucifera

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Lotus, as the Blue Water Lily profile puts it

Leaves held high above water on tall stalks; peltate leaf base (no notch); flowers much larger and pink/white

कमल — पानी से ऊपर उठा हुआ

Blue Water Lily: Flat, floating round leaves with a deep V-notch and wavy edges, combined with star-like, pale blue or violet flowers that open in the morning, identify Nymphaea nouchali.

Indian Lotus: A large, peltate leaf held above water on a long stalk = lotus. Leaf floating on water surface = water lily. They are not related plants — different families, different evolutionary histories.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Blue Water LilyIndian Lotus
Habitat ClassFloating-leaved rooted aquatic plant; anchored by a buried perennial rhizome—
LeavesRound to oval (15–30 cm wide), floating flat on the water surface; margins wavy or undulatingLarge, round (30–80 cm across), held well above the water on tall stalks; peltate (attached at center)
Leaf NotchDistinct deep V-shaped notch (sinus) at the base of the leaf extending to the stalk—
FlowersStar-shaped, solitary (5–15 cm across) with pale blue, violet, or lavender-pink petals and a yellow centerLarge (15–25 cm across), fragrant, with 20–25 pink or white tepals and a central yellow receptacle
Flowering HabitOpens in the morning (around 7–8 AM) and closes by mid-afternoon (around 2–3 PM)—
FruitGlobose, spongy berry that ripens underwater after the flower stalk curls downward—
Leaf Surface—Hydrophobic ("lotus effect") where water beads up and rolls off, keeping the leaf dry and clean
Seed Pod—Flat-topped, cone-shaped green receptacle (kamal gatta) containing multiple seed cavities
Rhizome—Long, segmented, cylindrical tubers (kamal kakdi) growing in mud, with ringed air channels inside
Habitat—Perennial standing water bodies like ponds, lakes, oxbow lakes, and temple tanks
