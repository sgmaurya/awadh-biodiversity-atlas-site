Siris or Tamarind? शिरीष या इमली?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

शिरीषSirisAlbizia lebbeck
इमलीTamarindTamarindus indica

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Siris, as the Tamarind profile puts it

Leaves are much larger; pods are large, flat, papery, and straw-coloured (rattle in the wind); lacks the sour pulp.

शिरीष — बड़े पत्ते, चपटी सूखी फलियां

Side by side आमने-सामने

SirisTamarind
FormLarge deciduous tree (15–25 m) with a broad, spreading canopy; older branches are grey-brown and smoothLarge tree (15–25 m) with a massive, spreading, rounded crown; trunk is short and very thick
LeavesAlternately arranged, bipinnately compound (15–40 cm long); 2–4 pairs of pinnae, each carrying 6–10 pairs of large, oblong, asymmetrical leafletsPinnate compound leaves (5–15 cm long) with 10–18 pairs of small, oblong, opposite leaflets; semi-evergreen
FlowersGlobose, brush-like, fragrant clusters (5–8 cm diameter) of greenish-yellow to cream flowers with numerous long stamensSmall (1.5–2.5 cm), pale yellow with reddish-pink veins, arranged in loose terminal clusters
FruitLarge, flat, papery straw-yellow to brown pods (15–30 cm long, 3–5 cm wide), containing 6–12 flat rounded seeds; bulges over seeds are visibleCurved, indehiscent, cinnamon-brown, woody pod (8–15 cm long) with a brittle shell containing acid pulp
BarkGreyish-brown, rough, cracking into irregular rectangular flakes on older trunksDark greyish-brown, rough, cracking vertically into thick rectangular plates
AcousticsDry pods rattle continuously in the wind, a key auditory identification feature during the dry season—
Seeds—Hard, shiny, dark brown or black, squarish seeds (3–10 per pod) embedded in the pulp
