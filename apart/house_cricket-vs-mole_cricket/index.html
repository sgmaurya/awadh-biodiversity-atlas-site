Oriental Mole Cricket]])

Fossorial front legs modified for digging; subterranean burrower; velvety cylindrical body

House Cricket: Pale straw-yellow body, distinct dark transverse bands across the head, fully developed wings in both sexes, and domestic habitat = Acheta domesticus.

Oriental Mole Cricket: Velvety-brown cylindrical body, short hind legs lacking jumping power, broad shovel-like foreleg tibiae with tooth-like cusps, found in moist soil and attracted to lights = Gryllotalpa orientalis.

Size—Body about 25–34 mm in females (measurements in the Orthoptera Species File); males a little smaller
Forelegs—Highly modified, broad, flat, shovel-like with dark tooth-like structures for digging
Colouration—Uniform velvety-brown or yellowish-brown; lighter ventral surface
Pronotum—Large, oval, heavily sclerotized (hardened); covers head partially
Posterior legs—Short, thick; not adapted for jumping like true crickets
Behaviour—Crawls rapidly on ground; digs immediately when placed on soil
Sound—Males produce characteristic rhythmic chirping from burrow entrances at night
