Bristled Pond Worm or Finger-gilled Pond Worm? ब्रिसल्ड पॉन्ड वर्म या फिंगर-गिल्ड पॉन्ड वर्म?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Finger-gilled Pond Worm, as the Bristled Pond Worm profile puts it

Posterior extremity expanded into a ciliated branchial fossa with gill lobes; lacks eye-spots; inhabits mud tubes

फिंगर-गिल्ड पॉन्ड वर्म

Bristled Pond Worm, as the Finger-gilled Pond Worm profile puts it

Lacks respiratory gills altogether; simple conical posterior extremity; eye-spots usually present

ब्रिसल्ड पॉन्ड वर्म

Bristled Pond Worm: A minute, transparent aquatic worm with dark eye-spots and stiff dorsal bristles that lacks any respiratory gills on its body or tail is Nais barbata.

Finger-gilled Pond Worm: A slender, transparent aquatic worm with a cup-shaped ciliated gill fossa bearing finger-like lobes at the tail tip is Dero digitata.

Body shape—Slender, transparent aquatic micro-annelid inhabiting silty mud
Tail fossa—Posterior end expanded into a ciliated branchial fossa bearing leaf-like gills
Branchial gills—Two pairs of true leaf-shaped gills and a pair of secondary marginal lobes
Setae—Ventral bundles of bifid crotchets; dorsal bundles start from segment six
Habit—Burrows head-downwards in soft sediment; extends waving posterior gills into water
Movement—Active undulating swimmer when disturbed; builds fragile mucus-silt tubes
