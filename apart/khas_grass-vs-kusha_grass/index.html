Vetiver or Kusha Grass? खस या कुश?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

खसVetiverChrysopogon zizanioides
कुशKusha GrassDesmostachya bipinnata

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Kusha Grass, as the Vetiver profile puts it

Stiffer and sharper, dense narrow brush-like head, on usar; roots creep sideways

कड़ी, ऊसर पर, जड़ फैलती है

Vetiver: A tall non-spreading clump grass with a loose purplish plume whose roots smell of khus is vetiver; no other grass of the district has scented roots.

Kusha Grass: A stiff, sharp-leaved tussock with a dense upright straw-coloured brush of flattened spikelets, on usar or a sandy bank, is kusha.

Side by side आमने-सामने

VetiverKusha Grass
HabitDense, erect, clump-forming grass 1–2 m, clumps up to a metre across, never spreading by runnersStiff, erect tussock grass 0.5–1.5 m, spreading by stout creeping rhizomes into dense patches
LeavesLong, narrow, stiff, 45–100 cm × 1 cm, folded at the base, rough-edged, green turning strawLong, narrow, 30–60 cm, hard, rolled or flat, tapering to a sharp point, margins rough enough to cut skin
StemsUpright, smooth, with a pithy coreRigid, solid, pale, with old sheaths at the base
Flower headAn open, oblong panicle 15–40 cm of many whorled branches, purple-brown to grey, August–NovemberA dense, erect, narrow panicle 20–60 cm with short crowded branches, pale straw to purple-tinged, July–October
SpikeletsIn pairs, one stalked, with short awns and a bristly baseFlattened, many-flowered, 5–12 mm, packed along the branches
RootsThe clue: massive, spongy, straight-down roots that smell of khus when crushed or dried—
Where—Usar patches, bunds, river banks: where few other grasses grow
