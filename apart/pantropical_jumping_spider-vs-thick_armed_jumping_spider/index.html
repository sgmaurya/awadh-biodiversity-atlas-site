How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Pantropical Jumping Spider, as the Thick-armed Jumping Spider profile puts it

Plexippus paykulli

Distinct bold white longitudinal median stripe on black body; normal slender front legs

Thick-armed Jumping Spider: Tiny jumping spider with massively swollen, paddle-like, black-fringed first pair of legs and dark bronze-sheened body.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Pantropical Jumping SpiderThick-armed Jumping Spider
SizeMale 8–10 mm; female 10–12 mm — compact, box-shaped body—
MaleJet-black with bold white lateral stripes on abdomen; white spots at rear; legs banded black-white—
FemaleBrownish-grey with pale chevron pattern; less contrasted—
EyesTwo enormous forward-facing principal eyes — most diagnostic feature; turns to face observerEnormous anterior median eyes providing binocular vision; smaller lateral eyes
MovementShort quick dashes; freezes; turns to face you; never builds a prey-catching webDeliberate, jerky stalking movement punctuated by sudden rapid forward leaps
CheliceraeIridescent blue-green in male (visible in direct sunlight)—
HabitatWalls, window sills, door frames, vegetation — ubiquitous on plastered surfaces—
Forelegs—Greatly enlarged, thickened, and flattened first pair of legs with a dense ventral fringe of black hairs
Body color—Glossy dark brown to jet-black with metallic bronze reflections and white scale patches
Cephalothorax—High, box-like, flat-topped cephalic region with vertical sides
