Hydrilla or Many-branched Stonewort? जल-जड़ी या मैनी-ब्रांक्ड स्टोनवॉर्ट?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Hydrilla, as the Many-branched Stonewort profile puts it

True flowering plant with distinct leafy whorls of serrated true leaves; veins visible; white female flowers

झांजी

Hydrilla: Submerged green aquatic weed with slender branched stems, leaf whorls of 3 to 8 with sharply toothed margins, and small yellow-potato-like tubers in the bottom mud, is Hydrilla.

Many-branched Stonewort: A soft, unencrusted submerged alga with twice-branched whorled branchlets surrounded by a clear gelatinous mucilage cloud is Nitella hyalina.

Side by side आमने-सामने

HydrillaMany-branched Stonewort
Plant habitSubmerged, heavily branched, rooting perennial aquatic herb, forming dense underwater columns up to 1–3 meters—
LeavesArranged in whorls of 3 to 8 leaves at each stem node; leaflets are linear-lanceolate (6–15 mm) with sharp, microscopic teeth—
StemSlender, cylindrical, round, bright green, easily fragmented into tiny growing pieces—
TubersProduces small, yellowish, potato-like underground tubers (5–10 mm) at the root tips that survive dry periods in mud—
FeelThe leaves feel rough and rasping when rubbed downward due to small spines on the central vein underside—
Habit—Submerged, delicate, bushy charophyte alga growing rooted in fine mud
Branching—Heteroclemous; nodes bear two distinct tiers of branchlets forming dense whorls
Mucilage—Fresh growing whorls and gametangia typically enveloped in a clear mucous cloud
Color—Bright translucent grass-green to dark emerald-green; soft and flexible
Calcification—Weakly calcified or uncalcified; lacks the brittle crust typical of most stoneworts
Axis—Slender central axis without a cortex; internodes clear and transparent
