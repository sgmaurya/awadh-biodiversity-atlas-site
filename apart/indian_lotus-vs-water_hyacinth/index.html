Indian Lotus or Water Hyacinth? कमल या जलकुंभी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

जलकुंभीWater HyacinthEichhornia crassipes

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Water Hyacinth, as the Indian Lotus profile puts it

Water Hyacinth]] (Pontederia crassipes)

Floating, with violet flower spikes; invasive

Indian Lotus, as the Water Hyacinth profile puts it

Emergent circular leaves held above water on central petiole; flowers very large, pink

कमल — जड़ मिट्टी में

Indian Lotus: A large, peltate leaf held above water on a long stalk = lotus. Leaf floating on water surface = water lily. They are not related plants — different families, different evolutionary histories.

Water Hyacinth: Floating mat + round shiny leaves + swollen spongy petioles + feathery purple-black roots = water hyacinth. The inflated petiole is unmistakable once seen.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Seed PodFlat-topped, cone-shaped green receptacle (kamal gatta) containing multiple seed cavities—
RhizomeLong, segmented, cylindrical tubers (kamal kakdi) growing in mud, with ringed air channels inside—
HabitatPerennial standing water bodies like ponds, lakes, oxbow lakes, and temple tanks—
Form—Free-floating perennial aquatic herb forming dense interlocking mats
Petioles—Swollen, bulbous, spongy, and air-filled; 5–12 cm long; provide buoyancy
Roots—Dense, feathery, dark purple-black, hanging suspended in water; 30–100 cm long
Stolons—Horizontal runners producing clone daughter plants, enabling rapid vegetative spread
