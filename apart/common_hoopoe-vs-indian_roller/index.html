Common Hoopoe or Indian Roller? हुदहुद या नीलकंठ?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

हुदहुदCommon HoopoeUpupa epops
नीलकंठIndian RollerCoracias benghalensis

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Roller, as the Common Hoopoe profile puts it

Shorter heavy bill; brilliant blue wings; lack of crest and black-white zebra stripes on body

नीलकंठ — नीले पंख, भारी चोंच, सिर पर कलगी नहीं

Common Hoopoe: The combination of a long decurved bill, sandy-buff body, bold black-and-white wings, and a spectacular erectile crest separates the Common Hoopoe from all other Indian birds.

Indian Roller: A stocky, medium bird perching on a wire over open farmland, rufous-brown at rest, with a sudden flash of brilliant turquoise-blue when it flies — is the Indian Roller. Nothing else in eastern UP creates this effect.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common HoopoeIndian Roller
SizeMedium-sized slender bird (25–32 cm total length); approximately size of a Myna30–34 cm total length; stocky, compact build
CrestLarge, crown-like fan of black-tipped, sandy-buff feathers; erectile at will—
ColourationSandy-orange head, neck, and breast; black-and-white striped wings and tail—
BillLong, thin, slightly down-curved, and dark; adapted for soil probingStout, slightly hooked, black
FlightDistinctive undulating, erratic flight resembling a giant butterfly—
ForagingWalks actively on the ground, probing lawns and loose soil—
Plumage (perched)—Rufous-brown back and head; pale lilac-purple throat and breast
Plumage (in flight)—Wing coverts brilliant turquoise-blue; flight feathers deep cobalt-blue with a broad turquoise band
Favourite perch—Power lines, bare treetops, fence posts — exposed lookout posts over farmland
Behaviour—Sits motionless on perch, drops suddenly to ground to capture prey, then returns to a perch

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
