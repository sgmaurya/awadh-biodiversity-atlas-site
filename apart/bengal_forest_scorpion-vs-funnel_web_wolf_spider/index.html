How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Funnel-web Wolf Spider, as the Bengal Forest Scorpion profile puts it

Hippasa agelenoides

Brown terrestrial spider spinning horizontal sheet-web leading into a funnel retreat; lacks pincers and tail

Bengal Forest Scorpion: Massive pitch-black forest scorpion with large, coarsely sculptured pincers (chelae) and relatively slender tail; mild venom.

Funnel-web Wolf Spider: Medium brown spider with funnel-web in grass, large eyes, rapid movement: Funnel-web Wolf Spider.

Bengal Forest ScorpionFunnel-web Wolf Spider
Size & ColorLarge, robust, heavily built scorpion, glossy dark blackish-brown to deep pitch-black—
Pincers (Chelae)Massive, broad, heavy pedipalp claws with coarsely granulated and lobed manus—
Tail (Metasoma)Relatively slender, segmented tail ending in a curved venomous aculeus with reddish telson—
PectinesSensory comb-like organs beneath the mesosoma bearing 12 to 16 teeth—
Burrowing habitFossorial; excavates deep horizontal burrows under boulders, tree roots, and timber—
Size—Medium spider; body length 10–15 mm (females larger than males)
Colour—Predominantly brown or grey-brown; carapace with darker markings
Eyes—Eight eyes arranged in characteristic lycosid pattern; two large posterior median eyes
Body shape—Robust, muscular body; long legs relative to body size
Legs—Relatively long and powerful; dark brown with banding
Abdomen—Tapered, with darker markings and lighter patches; females may be darker
Web—Sheet web with a funnel entrance; often located in grass tussocks or low vegetation
