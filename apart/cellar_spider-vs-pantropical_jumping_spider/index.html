Pantropical Jumping Spider, as the Long-bodied Cellar Spider profile puts it

Pantropical Jumping Spider]] [SPD-0003])

Compact, box-shaped body; legs are short and robust; hunts visually on walls without webs; lacks the thread-like legs.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Long-bodied Cellar SpiderPantropical Jumping Spider
FormSmall, elongated, cylinder-shaped body; legs are extremely long, thin, and hairless—
LegsLeg span reaches 5.0–7.0 cm; pale translucent brown, with dark joints—
ColourPale grey, light brown, or yellow-grey body; cephalothorax has a dark, skull-like patch—
WebLarge, messy, irregular three-dimensional cobweb built in corners of walls and ceilings—
EyesEight small eyes; six arranged in two prominent lateral triads (groups of three) and two tiny eyes at the frontTwo enormous forward-facing principal eyes — most diagnostic feature; turns to face observer
DefenceRapidly vibrates or whirls its body when disturbed, turning into a visual blur to escape predators—
Size—Male 8–10 mm; female 10–12 mm — compact, box-shaped body
Male—Jet-black with bold white lateral stripes on abdomen; white spots at rear; legs banded black-white
Female—Brownish-grey with pale chevron pattern; less contrasted
Movement—Short quick dashes; freezes; turns to face you; never builds a prey-catching web
Chelicerae—Iridescent blue-green in male (visible in direct sunlight)
Habitat—Walls, window sills, door frames, vegetation — ubiquitous on plastered surfaces

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
