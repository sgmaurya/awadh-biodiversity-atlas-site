Tropical Fire Ant or Weaver Ant? लाल चींटी या बयनी चींटी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

लाल चींटीTropical Fire AntSolenopsis geminata
बयनी चींटीWeaver AntOecophylla smaragdina

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Weaver Ant, as the Tropical Fire Ant profile puts it

Much larger (8–10 mm); long legs; nests in tree leaves, not in soil; stings by biting and spraying formic acid

बयनी चींटी / माटा — पेड़ों पर रहना, बड़ा आकार

Tropical Fire Ant, as the Weaver Ant profile puts it

Smaller, reddish-brown; ground-nesting; painful sting (not bite)

Tropical Fire Ant: Reddish-orange body, dark abdomen, workers with highly variable head sizes (polymorphic), and an aggressive swarming response from soil mounds with a burning sting, identify the Tropical Fire Ant.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Tropical Fire AntWeaver Ant
SizePolymorphic workers; body length 2.4–6.0 mm; queens up to 8 mm—
ColourationShiny reddish-orange or yellowish-red; abdomen (gaster) is darker brown—
PolymorphismSoldiers (major workers) have massive, square heads; minor workers are small—
PetioleTwo distinct nodes connecting the thorax and the gaster—
NestLoose, irregular earthen mounds in sunny locations or under stonesLiving leaf nest in tree canopy — leaves stitched together with silk
AggressionExtremely high; swarms out and attacks instantly if the nest is disturbed—
Major workers—Large (8–10 mm), orange-red, large head, powerful mandibles
Minor workers—Smaller (4–6 mm), paler orange
Queen—20–25 mm; greenish tint to gaster — "smaragdina" = emerald-like
Behaviour—Extremely aggressive territory defenders; painful bite
Habitat—Almost always in fruit tree canopies, especially mango
