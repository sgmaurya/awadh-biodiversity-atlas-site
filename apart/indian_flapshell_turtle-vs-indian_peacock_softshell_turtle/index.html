Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Flapshell Turtle, as the Indian Peacock Softshell Turtle profile puts it

Lissemys punctata

Much smaller adult size (carapace up to 275 mm; Smith 1931); possesses soft cutaneous flaps on the plastron covering retracted hindlimbs

Indian Peacock Softshell Turtle: Large riverine softshell turtle with bold yellow circular spots across head and snout, tubular proboscis, and (in young) distinct peacock-eye ocelli on an olive carapace.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Indian Flapshell TurtleIndian Peacock Softshell Turtle
CarapaceSoft, smooth, and leathery oval shell; lacks hard, bony plates (scutes); greenish-brown with yellow spots—
PlastronYellowish-white under-shell featuring hinged flaps of skin that cover and protect the hind limbs when retracted—
HeadSmall, greenish-grey, with three distinct black stripes; snout is pointed, ending in a short proboscis (tube-like nose)—
LimbsFlattened and webbed with three claws on each foot, adapted for swimming and burrowing in mud—
SizeCarapace length ranges from 18 to 28 cm; weighs between 1.0 to 3.5 kg—
DefenceRetracts its neck and limbs completely, closing the plastic flaps; stays buried under mud when disturbed—
Shell type—Large, low, oval disc covered with smooth leathery skin devoid of horny scutes
Juvenile pattern—Carapace adorned with 4 to 6 conspicuous eye-like ocelli (black center ringed in yellow and black)
Adult coloration—Olive-green or dark greyish-olive, with ocelli becoming faint or disappearing with maturity
Head pattern—Dark olive-green with large, prominent bright yellow spots on the snout, cheeks, and temples (Smith 1931)
Snout—Cylindrical, elongated, projecting forward into a distinctive tubular proboscis
Limbs & Digits—Paddle-like limbs with three large claws on each foot and extensive swimming webs

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
