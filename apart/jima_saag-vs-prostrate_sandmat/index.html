Jima Saag or Prostrate Sandmat? जिमा साग या छोटी दूधी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Prostrate Sandmat, as the Jima Saag profile puts it

Exudes milky white latex; leaves opposite; capsule with keel hairs

तोड़ने पर सफेद दूध निकलता है

Jima Saag: Prostrate green mat with clear sap, whorled spathulate leaves with a distinct bitter taste, and axillary clusters of tiny greenish-white flowers identifies Glinus oppositifolius.

Prostrate Sandmat: Prostrate ground-hugging mat exuding milky sap with tiny pinkish cyathia and nodding capsules bearing stiff white hairs strictly along the three keels identifies Euphorbia prostrata.

FlowersAxillary fascicles of 2–6 on slender pedicels, 3.0–4.5 mm across, greenish-white (Hooker 1879)—
FlavourConspicuously bitter, refreshing herbal taste when chewed fresh—
SeedsTiny, subreniform, reddish-brown, bearing a slender curved white appendaged strophiole (Hooker 1879)Ovoid-quadrangular, pale reddish-brown, transverse-furrowed on faces (Hooker 1887)
Leaves—Opposite, oblong-elliptic to obovate, 3–8 mm long, finely toothed towards apex (Hooker 1887)
Cyathia—Axillary, solitary or few in short cymes, minute, purplish, with tiny pink glands (Duthie 1915)
Capsule—Subglobose, 3-angled, with prominent stiff spreading hairs strictly on keels (Hooker 1887)
