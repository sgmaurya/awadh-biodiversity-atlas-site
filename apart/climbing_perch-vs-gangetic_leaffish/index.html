Climbing Perch or Gangetic Leaffish? कवई या भेदई?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

भेदईGangetic LeaffishNandus nandus

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Climbing Perch, as the Gangetic Leaffish profile puts it

Anabas testudineus

Darker, compact body; smaller mouth; prominent serrated opercular spines; specialized air-breathing labyrinth organ

Climbing Perch: A hard, spiny, grey fish with a saw-edged gill cover that survives out of water and walks on wet ground is the climbing perch.

Gangetic Leaffish: Mottled brassy-brown leaffish with cavernous protrusible mouth extending past eye, and 12 to 14 sharp dorsal spines; weed dweller.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Climbing PerchGangetic Leaffish
Size10–20 cm, occasionally 25 cm—
ShapeOblong, somewhat compressed, with a large head and a mouth with small teeth—
ColourOlive to grey-brown above, paler below; a dark spot at the base of the tail and another behind the gill cover—
FinsLong dorsal and anal fins with strong spines; a rounded tailLong spiny dorsal fin with 12 to 14 strong spines; rounded caudal fin
Gill coverThe rear edge serrated with sharp spines, which it spreads when caught: the "climbing" tool and the reason it is hard to hold—
BehaviourGulps air at the surface; survives in a bucket; crawls on land after rain—
Body shape—Deep, laterally compressed, oblong body mimicking a submerged rotting leaf
Mouth structure—Highly protrusible, cavernous mouth with cleft extending far behind the eye
Coloration—Mottled brassy-brown to greenish-brown with irregular dark blotches and vertical marbling
Ambush habit—Drifts or rests motionlessly among submerged weeds, striking rapidly at small fish

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
