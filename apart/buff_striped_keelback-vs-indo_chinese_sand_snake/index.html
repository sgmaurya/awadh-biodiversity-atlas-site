How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Buff-striped Keelback, as the Indo-Chinese Sand Snake profile puts it

Buff-striped Keelback]] (Amphiesma stolatum)

Heavily keeled dorsal scales; two pale yellow dorso-lateral stripes; found in wetlands

Indo-Chinese Sand Snake: Smooth scales and 4 or 5 dark brown longitudinal stripes on a pale olive or buff body separate Psammophis condanarus from keeled-scaled keelbacks and arboreal bronzebacks (Smith 1943, p. 304).

Side by side आमने-सामने

Buff-striped KeelbackIndo-Chinese Sand Snake
FormSlender, cylindrical body; head is distinct from the neck; tail is long and thin—
MarkingsTwo bright, cream-yellow or buff longitudinal stripes running down the back from neck to tail—
ColourOlive-green, greyish-brown, or bronze ground colour, often with black spots between the stripes—
ScalesStrongly keeled (ridged) dorsal scales, giving the skin a rough texture—
HeadDistinctly defined, with large, symmetrical shields; snout is rounded—
EyesLarge, with gold-flecked irises and round pupils—
BehaviourExtremely gentle and non-aggressive; active during daylight hours near wet vegetation—
Build & Size—Slender, elongate snake; total length 1075 mm, tail 250 mm (Smith 1943)
Dorsal Pattern—Pale olive or buff above with dark brown longitudinal stripes edged with black (Smith 1943)
Ventral Surface—Yellow or yellowish white, black line along each side at outer margin of ventrals (Smith 1943)
Head Markings—Head brown, with more or less distinct longitudinal markings (Smith 1943)
Habits—Extremely active in its movements; fond of ascending low bushes (Smith 1943)
