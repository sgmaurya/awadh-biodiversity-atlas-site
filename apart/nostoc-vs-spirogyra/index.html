Star Jelly or Water Silk? काई (ज़मीन की जेली वाली काई) या पानी की काई?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

काई (ज़मीन की जेली वाली काई)Star JellyNostoc commune
पानी की काईWater SilkSpirogyra

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Water Silk, as the Star Jelly profile puts it

Floating silky threads in water, bright grass-green, not a firm jelly on soil

पानी में रेशमी धागे

Star Jelly, as the Water Silk profile puts it

Firm jelly blobs, not threads; on soil as much as in water

जेली, धागे नहीं

Star Jelly: Olive-green rubbery jelly lying loose on bare soil the morning after rain, gone to black crust in the sun, is Nostoc.

Water Silk: Bright green, fine, unbranched, slippery threads in still water that will not stay in the hand are water silk.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Star JellyWater Silk
WetRubbery, gelatinous sheets or lobed blobs 1–10 cm across, olive-green to blue-green or brownish, lying loose on soil—
DryShrinks to a thin, brittle, black or dark brown crust that looks like scraps of burnt paper—
FeelFirm jelly, slippery, slightly elastic; no roots, no leaves, no smellSlippery, silky, slimy; threads slide apart and cannot be held between fingers
Under a lensMade of countless beaded threads in jelly; a hand lens shows the granular lookEach thread a single row of cylindrical cells, each with one to several green spiral ribbons
WhereBare damp soil in the open: courtyards, path edges, lawns, bunds, field surfacesStill, shallow, sunlit, nutrient-rich water
WhenAppears within hours of rain and vanishes into crust within a day or two of sun—
Form—Masses of fine, unbranched, bright grass-green threads, floating or draped over stones and plants
Colour—Clean green when growing; yellow-green and frothy with bubbles in hot sun; brown when dying
Bubbles—Mats often lifted to the surface by trapped oxygen on sunny afternoons
