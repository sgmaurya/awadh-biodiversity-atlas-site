Freshwater Apple Snail or Freshwater Mussel? घोंघा या सीप?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

घोंघाFreshwater Apple SnailPila globosa
सीपFreshwater MusselLamellidens marginalis

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Freshwater Mussel, as the Freshwater Apple Snail profile puts it

Lamellidens marginalis]] (Freshwater Mussel)

Two-valved, flat, dark brown shell with no spiral; a filter feeder buried in mud

Freshwater Apple Snail, as the Freshwater Mussel profile puts it

Univalve (single spiral shell); very different body plan — gastropod not bivalve

Side by side आमने-सामने

Freshwater Apple SnailFreshwater Mussel
ShellLarge, round (globose); diameter 5–8 cm; heavier than it looksElongate-oval; 8–14 cm length; solid and heavy
Shell colourGreenish-olive to dark brown; banded in some individuals—
OperculumHard, horny, oval lid that seals shell opening — very diagnostic—
SiphonLong fleshy tube extended to water surface in low-oxygen water—
EggsPink-orange clusters of round eggs, each 3–4 mm, laid above waterline—
BodyGrey-black with yellowish mottling; large muscular foot; eyes on short stalks—
Exterior—Greenish-brown to dark olive-black; concentric growth rings visible
Interior—Brilliant silvery-white iridescent nacre (mother-of-pearl)
Foot—Large muscular tongue-shaped appendage for burrowing
Siphons—Two short tubes visible at shell gap when feeding
Habitat—Sandy-muddy river beds, partly buried; 30–100 cm depth
Sign—Empty shells on riverbanks — best indicator; look for middens

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
