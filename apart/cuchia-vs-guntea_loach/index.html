How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Cuchia, as the Guntea Loach profile puts it

Monopterus cuchia

True swamp eel completely lacking pectoral, pelvic, and dorsal fins; smooth cylindrical body

Cuchia: A long, dark, eel-like fish with no side fins, a single gill opening below the head, and a single nostril at the mouth, living in mud, is the cuchia; the air-breathing chamber and dormancy distinguish it from true eels.

Guntea Loach: Slender muddy loach with sharp bifid subocular spine, 3 barbel pairs, and intestinal air-breathing.

Side by side आमने-सामने

CuchiaGuntea Loach
Size25–50 cm, occasionally to 70 cm—
ShapeEel-like, cylindrical body; no dorsal, anal or pelvic fins; a small tail fin—
ColourDark brown to olive-black with yellowish or white bars and spots; paler below—
HeadSmall, pointed, with a small mouth and small eyes—
Gill openingA single slit on the underside of the head, not on the side—
BehaviourNocturnal, burrowing; seen at the surface in monsoon—
Body shape—Slender, elongate, cylindrical body with truncated or slightly rounded caudal fin
Subocular spine—Strong, sharp, erectile bifid spine resting in a groove beneath each eye
Barbels—Three pairs of barbels: two rostral pairs and one maxillary pair with sensory flaps
Color pattern—Pale yellowish-olive with a lateral series of dark blotches forming a jagged stripe
Respiration—Intestinal air-breathing capability allowing survival in hypoxic, foul mud

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
