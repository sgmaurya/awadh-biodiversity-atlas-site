Neem or Silver Oak? नीम या सिल्वर ओक?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

नीमNeemAzadirachta indica
सिल्वर ओकSilver OakGrevillea robusta

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Neem, as the Silver Oak profile puts it

Pinnate leaves (not bipinnate); small white flowers; bitter smell

पंखदार पत्तियाँ; सफ़ेद छोटे फूल; कड़वी गंध

Neem: Compound leaves with strongly toothed, asymmetric-base leaflets + strong bitter smell when crushed + found near village settlements = neem. Bakain (Melia azedarach) is the closest lookalike but its leaflets have a symmetric base.

Silver Oak: A tall tree with silvery-green, feathery bipinnate leaves and curved orange-red one-sided flower clusters is Silver Oak.

Side by side आमने-सामने

NeemSilver Oak
Size & CrownMedium to large semi-evergreen tree (15–20 m tall) with a dense, rounded, spreading canopy—
LeavesAlternate, compound pinnate (20–40 cm long) with 9–17 lance-shaped, sharply toothed leaflets; base of each leaflet is distinctly asymmetricBipinnate, feathery, 15–30 cm long, silvery-green (hence "silver" oak)
FlowersSmall, fragrant, star-shaped white-to-cream flowers (5–6 mm across) in large branched axillary clustersOrange-red, curved, one-sided racemes (bottle-brush appearance), December–March
FruitSmooth, olive-shaped drupe (1–2 cm long), green ripening to a bright yellow with sweetish-bitter pulpWoody follicles, 2–3 cm, containing one winged seed
BarkDark grey-brown, rough, deeply furrowed with long vertical fissures on mature trunksDark greyish-brown, becoming rough and fissured with age
AromaPungent, bitter, medicinal aroma when leaves, twigs, or bark are crushed—
Size—Tall, evergreen to semi-deciduous tree, 15–25 m tall, narrow crown
Leaflets—Fine, linear, giving a fern-like appearance
The test—Feathery silvery leaves and curved orange-red flowers identify it distinctly
