How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Two-Spotted Cricket, as the African Mole Cricket profile puts it

Field Cricket]])

Standard cricket body; long hind legs adapted for jumping; lacks shovel-like digging claws

Two-Spotted Cricket: Shiny black body, body length 20–35 mm, with a pair of distinct yellow-orange spots at the base of the forewings, identifies Gryllus bimaculatus.

African Mole Cricket: Medium size (~25–35 mm), velvety-brown cylindrical body, short hind legs, and broad spade-like front legs modified for digging identify the African Mole Cricket.

Two-Spotted CricketAfrican Mole Cricket
FormRobust, cylindrical body with a large, rounded head and long, thread-like antennae—
ColorationShiny black overall; distinct pair of pale yellow-orange spots at the base of the forewings—
WingsFully developed forewings (tegmina) covering the abdomen; long membranous hindwings—
LegsStrong hind legs modified for jumping (saltatorial), equipped with spinesHind legs are short, thick, and not adapted for jumping
OvipositorFemales possess a long, needle-like ovipositor extending from the rear of the abdomen—
BehaviorNocturnal; males produce a loud, repetitive "chirping" sound by stridulation at night—
Size—Medium-sized, stout insect; body length 25–35 mm; wingspan 20–28 mm
Forelegs—Highly modified, broad, flat, and spade-like with dark teeth-like structures for digging
Colouration—Uniformly velvety-brown to yellowish-brown; abdomen is lighter underneath
Pronotum—Large, oval, and heavily hardened (pronotal shield); covers the head partially
Behaviour—Crawls rapidly on the ground; tries to dig immediately when placed on soil
