Khirni or Mahua? खिरनी या महुआ?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

खिरनीKhirniManilkara hexandra
महुआMahuaMadhuca longifolia

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Mahua, as the Khirni profile puts it

Leaves softer, not notched at tip; flowers large, fleshy, cream-coloured

पत्तियाँ नरम, फूल बड़े और गूदेदार

Khirni: Stiff leathery obovate leaves with notched (emarginate) tips, white latex, deeply fissured dark grey bark, and small sweet golden-yellow berries ripening in summer distinguish Manilkara hexandra.

Side by side आमने-सामने

KhirniMahua
HabitLarge evergreen tree 12–20 m tall with a dense, spreading rounded canopy (Duthie 1911)—
BarkDark grey to blackish, deeply and longitudinally cracked, rough and rough-platedDark grey-brown, longitudinally fissured, rough
LeavesObovate or oblong-elliptic, 5–12 cm long, rigidly coriaceous, emarginate at the apex (Duthie 1911)Clustered at branch tips, oblong-elliptic, 15–30 cm, leathery, glabrous
ExudateMilky white latex exudes abundantly from cut bark, petioles, and unripe fruits—
FlowersSmall, pale yellow or whitish, 6–8 mm across, solitary or clustered in leaf axils (Duthie 1911)Cream/yellowish, fleshy, tubular, nocturnal, strongly fragrant; fall intact to ground

Tree form—Medium–large (10–20 m), dense spreading crown
Season clue—March–April nights: strong sweet scent; fallen cream flowers cover ground by dawn
