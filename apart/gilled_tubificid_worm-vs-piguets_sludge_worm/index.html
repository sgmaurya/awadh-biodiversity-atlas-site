Gilled Tubificid Worm or Piguet's Sludge Worm? गिल्ड ट्यूबीफिसिड वर्म या पिगिट्स स्लज वर्म?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Piguet's Sludge Worm, as the Gilled Tubificid Worm profile puts it

Much smaller body size; builds distinct elevated chimney tubes of mud

काफी छोटा आकार, कीचड़ की चिमनी बनाना

Gilled Tubificid Worm, as the Piguet's Sludge Worm profile puts it

Much larger body size; possesses paired finger-like gills on tail; does not build chimneys

बहुत बड़ा शरीर, पूंछ पर गलफड़े

Gilled Tubificid Worm: An aquatic reddish worm in pond mud with conspicuous paired finger-like gills lining the dorsal and ventral margins of its waving tail identifies Branchiura sowerbyi.

Piguet's Sludge Worm: A tiny aquatic worm building small mud chimneys on pond sediment, featuring an unsegmented tail zone and oar-shaped dorsal paddle setae, identifies Aulodrilus pigueti.

Gilled Tubificid WormPiguet's Sludge Worm
HabitElongate, reddish-pink benthic tubificid worm burrowing vertically in aquatic mudSmall, tubicolous, delicate aquatic tubificid building elevated mud chimneys

ColorationBright reddish-pink to orange due to abundant haemoglobin in bloodTranslucent pale pink to orange-red; slender and easily fragmented
PostureAnterior body buried in mud while the posterior gill-bearing tail undulates in water—
RetractionSnaps instantly back into mud tube when disturbed by shadows or water vibrations—
Chimney Tube—Erect, smooth, cylindrical tube of fine mud grains protruding above sediment
Setae—Dorsal bundles contain distinctive oar-shaped or spoon-like paddle setae
Tail Zone—Posterior end features an unsegmented, ciliated growing zone lacking setae
