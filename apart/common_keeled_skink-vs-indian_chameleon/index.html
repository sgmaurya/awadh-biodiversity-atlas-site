Common Keeled Skink or Indian Chameleon? बामनिहा या असली गिरगिट?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Chameleon, as the Common Keeled Skink profile puts it

Nocturnal; skin is soft and covered in tiny granules (no overlapping keeled scales); tail has no lateral bands; expanded toe pads for climbing.

घरेलू छिपकली — निशाचर, खुरदरी त्वचा

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common Keeled SkinkIndian Chameleon
FormCylindrical, heavy-bodied lizard with short, well-developed limbs; tail is long and tapers—
ColourShiny bronze-brown or olive-brown above, with a pale yellow lateral band running from snout to tail—
ScalesDorsal scales are overlapping and carry 3–5 distinct ridges (keels), giving a rough appearance—
HeadShiny, covered with large symmetrical shields; snout is pointed—
EyesClear, with round pupils and well-developed, movable eyelidsEach eye moves independently — turret-like; can look two directions simultaneously
BehaviourDiurnal, fast-running ground dweller; makes a loud rustling noise in dry leaves when running—
Size—20–30 cm including prehensile tail
Body—Laterally compressed (leaf-shaped profile); high casque (helmet-like head ridge)
Feet—Zygodactyl — toes fused into opposing groups (3+2 and 2+3) for gripping
Tail—Prehensile — used as a fifth limb; tightly coiled when resting
Movement—Slow, deliberate, swaying gait; clings to branches

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
