How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Black Nightshade, as the Bathua profile puts it

Black Nightshade]] (Solanum nigrum)

Leaves are thin, simple; flowers are white and star-shaped; fruit is a round black toxic berry

Bathua: Erect winter annual herb with goosefoot-shaped leaves covered in white waxy powder, and tiny greenish-white flower spikes at the branch tips, is Bathua.

Black Nightshade: Soft hairless plant, white star flowers with a yellow anther cone, and drooping bunches of pea-sized berries turning black: makoi. Green berries are not to be eaten.

SeedsTiny (1–1.5 mm), circular, smooth, glossy jet-black, released when the dry plant turns brown—
Habit—Soft, branching annual 30–90 cm, hairless or nearly so
Stem—Green or purplish, angled, sometimes with faint ridges
Fruit—Round berries 6–10 mm in drooping bunches, green then dull purple-black, with many flat seeds
Where—Rich, moist, part-shaded ground near houses and cattle
