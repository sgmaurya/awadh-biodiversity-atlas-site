How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Rat Snake, as the Russell's Viper profile puts it

Non-venomous; slender head; checkered black spots on a yellow-green body; lacks the large nostrils and vertical pupils; semi-aquatic.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Indian Rat SnakeRussell's Viper
SizeLarge snake, 1.5–2 m (record to 3 m); very fast mover—
ColourBrown to olive-brown above with irregular darker spots/stripes on posterior body—
UndersideYellowish-white, cream; uniform without strong patterning—
HeadElongated, slightly distinct from neck; large eyes with round pupilsDistinctly triangular, flat-topped, and twice as wide as the neck; snout is blunt and raised
No hoodCritical distinction from cobra — dhaman never spreads a hood—
BehaviourRuns immediately when disturbed; hisses loudly and vibrates tail when cornered (bluff)—
Form—Stout, heavy-bodied snake; tail is short and tapers rapidly
Markings—Three longitudinal rows of dark brown oval spots with black borders and white edges, forming a chain-like pattern
Scales—Heavily keeled (ridged) dorsal scales, giving the snake a rough, dry texture
Nostrils—Extremely large and conspicuous, positioned on the sides of the snout
Eyes—Large, with gold-flecked irises and distinct vertical, slit-like pupils
Defence—Coils tightly, raises the front of its body, and produces a remarkably loud, continuous pressure-cooker-like hiss

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
