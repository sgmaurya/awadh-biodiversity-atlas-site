How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Adanson's Jumping Spider, as the White-tipped Jumping Spider profile puts it

Body larger; male has bold white crescent band on rear head and white spots on black abdomen (Metzner 2025)

White-tipped Jumping Spider: A tiny jumping spider with a dark body, pale lateral stripes, and prominent white tips on its pedipalps and front leg tarsi, actively prowling low foliage without a web, is Icius alboterminus.

Male ColourDark brown to black; features a striking white crescent-shaped band on the back of the head and white spots on the abdomen—
Female ColourUniformly lighter sandy-brown or light brown, with less distinct darker chevrons on the abdomen—
EyesTwo massive, shiny, forward-facing principal eyes; turns its body to face observersLarge forward-pointing anterior median eyes ringed with fine golden-brown hairs (Metzner 2025)
MovementQuick, rapid runs followed by sudden pauses; hops or jumps over cracks and onto prey—
BehaviourStrictly active hunter; does not construct webs for capturing prey; spins a small silk cell for resting and egg-laying—
Female Size—Body length up to 5 mm (Caleb 2014)
Male Size—Body length up to 4.5 mm (Caleb 2014)
White Tips—Distal segments of male pedipalps and first legs capped with bright white setae (Caleb 2014)
Carapace—Dark chestnut-brown with metallic bronze sheen and pale marginal hair bands (Caleb 2014)
Abdomen—Elongate-oval with a dark median longitudinal band bordered by creamy-white lateral stripes (Caleb 2014)
Hunting Style—Agile visual stalk-and-pounce foliage predator without a capture web
