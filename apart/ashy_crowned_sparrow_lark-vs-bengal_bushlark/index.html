Ashy-crowned Sparrow-Lark, as the Bengal Bushlark profile puts it

Ashy-crowned Sparrow-Lark]] (Eremopterix griseus)

Male has solid black belly; female has finch-like bill and lacks bright rufous wing patches (Ali & Ripley 1987)

Ashy-crowned Sparrow-Lark: A small, dumpy lark, always in flocks on open ground. Males show distinctive ashy crown and black-and-white facial pattern. Runs on ground; flies with whirring wings. Highly gregarious; easily identified by behaviour and flock association.

Bengal Bushlark: The stout conical bill, heavily dark-spotted breast, and prominent chestnut wing panels identify the Bengal Bushlark (Baker 1926, pp. 337–338).
