How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Greater Racket-tailed Drongo, as the Blue-capped Rock Thrush profile puts it

Greater Racket-tailed Drongo]] (Dicrurus paradiseus)

Completely glossy black with crested forehead and racket tail streamers; canopy dweller

Blue-capped Rock Thrush: The dazzling combination of bright cobalt-blue cap, black mask, broad white wing patch, and blazing chestnut-orange underparts identifies the male Blue-capped Rock Thrush instantly.

Greater Racket-tailed Drongo: The prominent forward-curving forehead crest and twin long bare tail wires tipped with twisted flat rackets distinguish the Greater Racket-tailed Drongo from all other drongo species.

FemaleCryptic olive-brown upperparts and pale buff underparts heavily scaled with dark crescents—
PostureErect, motionless perching on a low horizontal bough before making a pouncing drop—
Plumage—Entirely glossy blue-black with metallic sheen on crest and breast
Crest—Prominent tuft of backward-curved frontal feathers above base of bill
Tail—Outer tail feathers elongated into bare shafts tipped with twisted spatulate rackets
Bill—Heavy, arched, black with bristly rictal setae at the gape
Eyes—Deep red to reddish-brown in mature adults
Flight Pattern—Undulating, graceful flight with the trailing rackets streaming behind
