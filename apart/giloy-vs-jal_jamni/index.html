Giloy or Broom Creeper? गिलोय या जल जमनी?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

जल जमनीBroom CreeperCocculus hirsutus

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Giloy, as the Broom Creeper profile puts it

Similar cordate leaves; much more vigorous, woody climber; berries yellow, not red; medicinally more famous

पत्तियाँ बड़े, बेल मजबूत, फल पीले

Giloy: Heart-shaped deciduous leaves on a climbing woody vine with hanging aerial rootlets, on neem or mango trees in a village setting = giloy. No other common eastern UP climber has all four features together.

Broom Creeper: Soft, non-woody climber + soft-hairy, cordate leaves + inconspicuous tiny flowers + small red berries = Cocculus hirsutus. The softness of the entire plant (stem and leaf hairs) distinguishes it from woody climbers.

Side by side आमने-सामने

GiloyBroom Creeper
Habit & StemPerennial woody climbing vine (liana); stems are green and fleshy when young, turning grey-green with a papery skin when older—
Aerial RootsThread-like, fleshy green aerial roots (2–15 cm long) that hang down from nodes along the climbing stems—
LeavesAlternately arranged, broad, heart-shaped (cordate), 10–15 cm long, smooth, looking similar to betel leavesSimple, alternate, broadly ovate to cordate, 4–8 cm long, softly hairy on both sides
FlowersSmall, inconspicuous pale yellow-green flowers (appearing May–July) borne in slender axillary racemesTiny, greenish-yellow, in clusters, inconspicuous, dioecious (male and female on different plants)
FruitSmall, round to ovoid fleshy drupes (6–8 mm), green when raw and ripening to a glossy bright red—
Stem—Slender, wiry, twining, soft-hairy, 1–3 m long
Berries—Small, red, 0.6–1 cm diameter, fleshy, containing 1 seed
Habit—Non-woody climber; does not smother other plants
Surface—Entire plant soft-hairy (hirsute)
