How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Hog Deer, as the Four-horned Antelope profile puts it

Hog Deer]] (Axis porcinus)

Easily mistaken in jungle; stouter deer build; lacks facial hornlets; branched antlers in males

Four-horned Antelope: Solitary habits, small size, dull pale brown pelage, with a more or less rufous tinge above, and four horn points in adult males distinguish the species; Blanford (1891, p. 520) noted: "In jungle this species and hog-deer (Cervus porcinus) may easily be mistaken the one for the other".

Hog Deer: Small squat deer with short legs, dark reddish-brown coat, three-tined antlers in males, and a characteristic low-head ducking run through tall river grass.

Build & SizeSmall slender antelope; height of male at shoulder 25 1/2 inches, at croup 27 inches (Blanford 1891)—
Posterior HornsSmooth, black, conical horns usually 3 to 4 inches long in adult males (Blanford 1891)—
Anterior HornsShorter frontal hornlets usually 1 to 1 1/2 inches long; occasionally absent (Blanford 1891)—
Dorsal PelageDull pale brown with a more or less rufous tinge above (Blanford 1891)—
UnderpartsPassing gradually on the sides and limbs into the white of the lower parts (Blanford 1891)—
Body size—Small, squat, robust deer
Coloration—Dark olive-brown or reddish-brown coat with pale underparts, often developing faint pale spots in summer
Antlers (males)—Slender three-tined antlers mounted on prominent hair-covered bony pedicels (Prater 1971)
Running gait—Runs with the head held low, ducking under grass stems rather than leaping high like other deer
Tail—Moderate, bushy tail, brown above and pure white underneath, flashed as an alarm signal
Habitat—Obligate dweller of wet alluvial grasslands, riverine reed beds, and floodplains
