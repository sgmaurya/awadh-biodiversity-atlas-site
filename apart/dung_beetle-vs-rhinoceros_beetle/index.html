Elephant Dung Beetle or Asian Rhinoceros Beetle? गोबर भृंग या गैंडा भृंग?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

गोबर भृंगElephant Dung BeetleHeliocopris dominus
गैंडा भृंगAsian Rhinoceros BeetleOryctes rhinoceros

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Asian Rhinoceros Beetle, as the Elephant Dung Beetle profile puts it

Does not feed on dung (attacks coconut/palm trees); horn is on head but body is elongated and cylindrical; legs lack dung-rolling adaptations

गेंडा भृंग — पेड़ों पर रहता है, बेलनाकार शरीर

Elephant Dung Beetle: Massive size (50–70 mm), dull black body, shovel-like head, and massive dung-burying habits under cattle pads identify Heliocopris dominus.

Asian Rhinoceros Beetle: Very large, shiny black beetle (35–50 mm), males with large forward-curving horn on head, found near rotting palm wood and compost heaps, is the Asian Rhinoceros Beetle.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Elephant Dung BeetleAsian Rhinoceros Beetle
FormMassive, heavily armored, oval-cylindrical body; one of the largest dung beetles in India—
ColorationPitch black to dark reddish-black, with a dull or slightly glossy surface—
Head and PronotumBroad head with a shovel-like front margin (clypeus); males have a prominent horn on the head—
LegsThick, powerful legs; forelegs are strongly toothed (fossorial) adapted for digging and rolling dungThick, powerful, adapted for burrowing
WingsHard, leathery forewings (elytra) with longitudinal grooves (striae), covering the abdomen—
BehaviorNocturnal or crepuscular; active flyer, attracted to light; creates large, underground dung balls—
Adult size—35–50 mm long; very robust, stout beetle
Colour—Black or dark brown, shiny wing covers
Male's distinctive feature—Large, prominent horn-like projection on the head, curving upward and backward
Female appearance—No horn; head is smoother, smaller
Larvae—Large, white, C-shaped grubs, 60–70 mm when mature
Larval habitat—Deep in the soil, inside rotting palm wood, coconut stumps, compost heaps

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
