Giant Dung Beetle or Asian Rhinoceros Beetle? गोबर भृंग या गैंडा भृंग?

Two look-alikes of the Basti–Ayodhya countryside side by side: what each profile says tells them apart, then their identification tables in one view.

How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Asian Rhinoceros Beetle, as the Giant Dung Beetle profile puts it

Also large and black, but a chafer of palms and compost with one long horn; never at dung

गैंडा भृंग

Giant Dung Beetle: Large glossy-black beetle at cattle dung, shovel-like head, toothed digging legs, the male with a short curved horn on the head = Catharsius molossus.

Asian Rhinoceros Beetle: Very large, shiny black beetle (35–50 mm), males with large forward-curving horn on head, found near rotting palm wood and compost heaps, is the Asian Rhinoceros Beetle.

Side by side आमने-सामने

Giant Dung BeetleAsian Rhinoceros Beetle
SizeLarge: about 25–35 mm, males up to about 45 mm (summary accounts after Arrow 1931); smaller than Heliocopris—
ColorationShiny black to glossy black, sometimes with metallic sheen—
Head and PronotumBroad head with shovel-like clypeus; males have a curved horn on head—
LegsThick, powerful, toothed; front legs fossorial (adapted for digging)Thick, powerful, adapted for burrowing
Body shapeOval to broadly convex; more compact than giant species—
ElytraHard forewings with longitudinal grooves (striae) covering abdomen—
Adult size—35–50 mm long; very robust, stout beetle
Colour—Black or dark brown, shiny wing covers
Male's distinctive feature—Large, prominent horn-like projection on the head, curving upward and backward
Female appearance—No horn; head is smoother, smaller
Larvae—Large, white, C-shaped grubs, 60–70 mm when mature
Larval habitat—Deep in the soil, inside rotting palm wood, coconut stumps, compost heaps

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
