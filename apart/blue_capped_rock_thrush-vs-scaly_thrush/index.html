How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Blue-capped Rock Thrush, as the Scaly Thrush profile puts it

Blue-capped Rock Thrush]] (Monticola cinclorhynchus)

Smaller with bright cobalt-blue crown and orange belly (male); scaled female lacks golden tone

Blue-capped Rock Thrush: The dazzling combination of bright cobalt-blue cap, black mask, broad white wing patch, and blazing chestnut-orange underparts identifies the male Blue-capped Rock Thrush instantly.

Scaly Thrush: The large plump thrush build, golden-olive ground color, and overall covering of dense black crescent scales distinguish the Scaly Thrush from all other woodland birds.

PostureErect, motionless perching on a low horizontal bough before making a pouncing drop—
Plumage—Golden-buff to olive-brown overall, densely scaled with black crescentic marks
Head—Pale golden-buff face with dark crescent behind ear-coverts and pale eye ring
Underwing—Conspicuous black and white patterned underwing band visible in flight
Bill—Stout, dark brown bill adapted for probing and flicking damp leaf litter
Feeding Motion—Rhythmic, characteristic 'bob and shudder' movement while pausing on the ground
Flight—Explosive, silent flight when flushed from litter into low dense cover
