How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Common Wolf Snake, as the Kheri Kukri Snake profile puts it

Common Wolf Snake]] (Lycodon aulicus)

Slender brown body with yellow or white crossbands; prominent snout

Kheri Kukri Snake: The Kheri Kukri Snake is distinguished from the Common Kukri Snake by its unpatterned reddish or buff-coloured dorsal surface, lacking bold transverse dark bars (Smith 1943, p. 204).

Side by side आमने-सामने

Common Wolf SnakeKheri Kukri Snake
FormSlender, slightly flattened body; tail is moderate and tapers to a fine point—
HeadFlat and spatulate (pear-shaped), scarcely wider than the neck; snout is broad and rounded—
Markings10–20 broad white or cream-coloured crossbands that start immediately behind the head/neck and expand on the sides—
ScalesSmooth, shiny, and uniform on the back; lacks enlarged vertebral scales—
EyesSmall, entirely black, with vertical slit-like pupils (often hard to see)—
BehaviourExcellent climber; found inside houses, on rafters, or climbing walls at night; mimics krait behaviour when cornered but does not hiss—
Build & Form—Smooth-scaled colubrid snake; described from North Kheri Division (Smith 1943)
Dorsal Colour—Fawn or buff-coloured above (reddish or pinkish in life) (Smith 1943)
Ventral Colour—Whitish below, lacking dark markings (Smith 1943)
Pattern—Unpatterned dorsal surface; an immaculate form without bold bars (Smith 1943)

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
