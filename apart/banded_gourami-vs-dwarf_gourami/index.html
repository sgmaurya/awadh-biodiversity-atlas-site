Banded Gourami: A striped lateral-compressed fish with long thread-like ventral fins and a labyrinth organ is a gourami; the banded gourami is distinguished by 4–5 dark stripes and no elaborate colour in females.

Dwarf Gourami: A diminutive, oval, compressed air-breathing labyrinth fish with thread-like pelvic feelers and vivid alternating diagonal stripes of scarlet-red and turquoise-blue, is Trichogaster lalius.

Body Shape—Oval, deep, strongly compressed laterally with rounded contours (Hamilton 1822)
Pelvic Fins—Modified into a single, elongate thread-like sensory filament (Hamilton 1822)
Air-Breathing Organ—Labyrinth organ present in gill chamber allowing atmospheric air-breathing (Daniels 2002)
Male Colour—Brilliant alternating diagonal bands of vivid scarlet-red and iridescent turquoise-blue (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Female Colour—Paler silvery-grey with subtle, faint greyish-orange vertical bars (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
Dorsal Fin—Long-based, carrying 15–17 stiff spines and 7–8 soft rays (Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
