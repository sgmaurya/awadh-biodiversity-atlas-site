How to tell them apart कैसे पहचानें

Indian Rat Snake, as the Monocled Cobra profile puts it

Indian Rat Snake]] (Ptyas mucosa)

Lacks dilatable hood; prominent dark cross-bars on tail; large eyes; harmless colubrid

Monocled Cobra: The yellow or orange-coloured, O-shaped monocellate mark upon the hood immediately distinguishes Naja kaouthia (Smith 1943, p. 431).

Side by side आमने-सामने

Indian Rat SnakeMonocled Cobra
SizeLarge snake, 1.5–2 m (record to 3 m); very fast mover—
ColourBrown to olive-brown above with irregular darker spots/stripes on posterior body—
UndersideYellowish-white, cream; uniform without strong patterning—
HeadElongated, slightly distinct from neck; large eyes with round pupils—
No hoodCritical distinction from cobra — dhaman never spreads a hood—
BehaviourRuns immediately when disturbed; hisses loudly and vibrates tail when cornered (bluff)—
Build & Hood—Large elapid snake with dilatable neck hood; scales on neck 25 to 31 (Smith 1943)
Mid-body Scales—Scales at mid-body 19 to 21, scales in front of vent 17 or 15 (Smith 1943)
Ventrals & Subcaudals—Ventrals 164 to 196, subcaudals 43 to 58 (Smith 1943)
Hood Mark—Yellow or orange-coloured, O-shaped, or monocellate mark upon the hood (Smith 1943)
Coloration—Olivaceous or brownish to black above; belly paler than back (Smith 1943)

Profiled, not yet recorded. This account is written from published sources for eastern Uttar Pradesh. Nobody has yet confirmed this species in the project area, so nothing here is an eyewitness report.
