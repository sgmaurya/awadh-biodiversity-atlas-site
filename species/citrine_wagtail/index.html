सिट्रीन खंजन / Citrine Wagtail

Motacilla citreola Pallas, 1776 — Motacillidae

सिट्रीन खंजन — यह एक सुंदर पीले रंग की खंजन है जो मध्य एशिया से सर्दियों में भारत आती है। नर का सिर चमकीले पीले रंग का होता है — जिससे इसका नाम "सिट्रीन" (साइट्रस जैसा पीला) मिलता है। मादा और गैर-प्रजनन पक्षी कम रंगीन होते हैं। ये White Wagtail की तरह ही चलती हैं, पर रंग अलग है। खुली घास और तालाबों के किनारे पर दिखाई देते हैं।

Field tip: A small yellow-headed wagtail in open areas or grasslands during winter — is a Citrine Wagtail. The yellow head and grey back distinguish it from White Wagtail. Males are diagnostic; females duller but still show yellow tones.

Size: 16–17 cm long; wingspan 23–28 cm. Weight 16–24 g. Similar in size to White Wagtail.

Plumage (male breeding): Most striking feature: head bright yellow or citrine-yellow, with a thin black line through the eye and along the nape. Back and rump grey-green or olive-grey. Wings darker grey. Tail black with white outer tail feathers (diagnostic). Underparts white or cream.

Plumage (female and non-breeding male): Much duller. Head greenish-yellow or pale yellow (not bright). Eye-stripe present but less bold. Back and rump greenish-grey. Underparts pale cream-white.

Legs and feet: Long, dark, typical of wagtails. Adapted for ground walking.

Bill: Thin, pointed, dark.

Eyes: Dark iris.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Flight call: Sharp, metallic chip-chip or tit-tit, given in flight.
  2. Song: Soft warbling song with whistles, rarely heard on wintering grounds.
  3. Contact calls: Soft chirps between birds.
हिंदी: तेज़ "चिप-चिप" की आवाज़। उड़ते समय कूकना।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ground insectivore: Citrine Wagtails are ground feeders, walking rapidly over grass and exposed ground, picking small invertebrates.

Feeding: Primarily insectivorous:

  • Small insects gleaned from ground
  • Aquatic insects from wetland edges
  • Small seeds and berries occasionally in late winter
  • Never probe like Tringa species

Feeding behaviour: Active ground walker with constant movement. Characteristic tail-pumping gait like other wagtails.

Social behaviour: Often solitary or in pairs during winter. Less gregarious than some other wagtail species. May form loose flocks during migration.

Habitat preference: Prefer open grasslands, wetland edges, and cultivated fields. Avoid dense vegetation.

Alert behaviour: Moderately wary; give sharp calls when flushed and fly to nearby open ground.

Flight: Direct flight with bouncing motion typical of wagtails.

हिंदी: घास पर चलते हुए कीड़े खोजता है। अकेले या जोड़ों में रहता है। पूँछ हिलाते रहते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP from October, with main arrival November. Departs February–March, with most birds gone by March.

Breeding range: Central Asia from Kazakhstan eastward to Mongolia and western China. Breeds April–August in grasslands and wetlands.

Migration: Long-distance migrant. Diurnal and nocturnal migration.

Wintering: Solitary or in pairs on open grasslands and wetland edges. Return to same wintering sites year after year.

Moult: Complete moult occurs before arrival; breeding plumage acquired on wintering grounds in early spring.

Timing in UP: Early arrival October (vanguard); main arrival November; peak December–February; departure February–March.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Listed under Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Ecological value: Important insectivore in grasslands and wetland edges; helps control small insect populations.

Wetland health indicator: Presence indicates productive grasslands and open wetland edges.

Cultural significance: Known for its striking yellow head coloration; noted in bird survey literature.

The Citrine Wagtail is a winter visitor to Gonda district. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025) recorded this species at Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary in Gonda district, documenting it during their comprehensive March 2024 to February 2025 survey. The species is expected in open grasslands and wetland edges throughout eastern UP, particularly in areas with low vegetation and exposed ground. Citrine Wagtails are less common than White Wagtails in eastern UP but occur at suitable habitats. Less gregarious than White Wagtails, typically solitary or in pairs. Peak occurrence should be November–March. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Central Asia; winters across southern Asia from Middle East to Southeast Asia and India.

In India: Scattered winter visitor, particularly in northern and central regions; found on grasslands and wetland edges.

In Uttar Pradesh: Regular but uncommon winter visitor to grasslands and wetland edges, particularly in northern and central districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected winter visitor to open grasslands and wetland margins. October–March, though not regularly recorded. More localized than White Wagtail.

Field rule: A wagtail with a yellow or citrine-yellow head in open grasslands — is a Citrine Wagtail. The yellow head, grey back, and tail-wagging behaviour are diagnostic. Distinguished from Yellow Wagtail by larger size, longer tail, and citrine-yellow (not bright yellow) head colour in males.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Pallas in 1776 in Reise durch verschiedene Provinzen des Russischen Reichs (volume 3) as Motacilla citreola. Type locality: Russia.

Subspecies. Three subspecies are recognised:

  • M. c. citreola — Western populations
  • M. c. werae — Central Asian populations
  • M. c. calcarata — Eastern populations

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Motacillidae (wagtails and pipits). Genus Motacilla contains the wagtails (7 species).

Phylogenetic notes. Motacilla citreola is sister to M. alba (White Wagtail), with which it shares many behavioural traits.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. The global population is large and stable. The species is adaptable to various grassland types and is not globally threatened.
