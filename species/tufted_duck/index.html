टफ्टेड बत्तख / Tufted Duck

Aythya fuligula (Linnaeus, 1758) — Anatidae

टफ्टेड बत्तख — सर्दियों में उत्तरी यूरोप और एशिया से आने वाली एक छोटी डाइविंग बत्तख, जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के बड़े तालाबों में अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक देखी जाती है। नर के काले सिर पर पीछे की ओर एक अनोखी कलगी (tuft) होती है, जिससे यह नाम मिला। यह गोता लगाकर पानी के कीड़े खाती है।

Field tip: A small diving duck with a drooping crest on the crown of the head — is a Tufted Duck. Males are black and white; females are brown. The tuft is distinctive, though not always obvious when feathers are slicked down. Often in flocks on larger water bodies.

Size: 40–47 cm long; wingspan 67–82 cm. Weight 550–850 g (males heavier).

Plumage (male): Head and neck black with purple iridescence (visible in bright light). Distinctive drooping crest (tuft) of elongated feathers on crown and nape. Back black. Breast black. Flanks white or pale grey. Belly white. Stern (tail coverts) black. Wing coverts dark; speculum white.

Plumage (female): Head, neck, and back dark brown. White cheek patch and pale lore visible. Smaller crest than male (sometimes barely visible). Flanks brown; belly whitish. White under tail coverts (diagnostic). Speculum white.

Bill: Blue-grey with black tip; short and slender.

Legs and feet: Dark grey or olive-grey; placed far back on body (diving adaptation).

Eyes: Yellow (males); dark brown with pale ring (females).

Sexes: Dimorphic; males dramatically more striking than females.

Vocalisations. Relatively quiet species:

  1. Drake call: Soft whistles and growls during courtship.
  2. Duck (female) call: Soft croaking and grunting sounds.
हिंदी: बहुत शांत बत्तख। कम आवाज़ करती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Aquatic invertebrate specialist: Tufted Ducks are diving ducks that feed primarily on:

  • Aquatic invertebrates (aquatic insect larvae, particularly chironomids)
  • Small fish and fish fry
  • Aquatic vegetation (seeds and shoots, secondary food)
  • Occasional mollusks and crustaceans

Feeding behaviour: Skilled divers; dive 1–5 m to reach bottom sediments and aquatic vegetation. Dive frequently throughout the day. Highly adaptable to various lake types.

Social behaviour: Highly gregarious in winter; form flocks, often in mixed-species groups with other diving ducks. Females may outnumber males in some populations.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosts on open water; diurnal resting and feeding periods throughout the day. Very active and restless compared to other diving ducks.

Territorial: Non-territorial during winter; however, males defend mates during courtship season (late winter).

Predators: Eagles, harriers, pike, and other large predators may take adults. Chicks vulnerable to corvids and aquatic predators.

हिंदी: बहुत सक्रिय, गोता लगाने वाली बत्तख। झुंड में रहती है। पानी के कीड़े खाती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives October–November from breeding grounds; departs March–April. Does not breed in India.

Breeding range: Northern Europe (Britain, Scandinavia) to Siberia and Asia. Breeds March–July at elevation 0–1000 m.

Migration: Breeds on temperate and boreal freshwater lakes; southward migration begins August–September. Arrives in Indian subcontinent October–November; remains through February–March; departure peaks April.

Wintering: Distributed on larger lakes and reservoirs; prefers water bodies with adequate depth and food supply.

Moult: Complete moult before autumn migration; eclipse plumage acquired and maintained during wintering.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Golden Eagle, White-tailed Eagle, Marsh Harrier, Peregrine Falcon, Osprey, Pike
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, wild dog, fox, wild cat, hyena
  • At nest (breeding grounds): Corvids, weasels, martens, rats
  • Parasites: Waterfowl helminths, protozoa, lice, parasitic mites

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

No crop damage: Tufted Ducks are specialist invertebrate feeders confined to larger water bodies; virtually no reported crop damage.

Ecosystem indicator: Used as an indicator species for water body health due to their dependence on aquatic invertebrates and sensitivity to pollution.

Hunting: Hunted historically in Europe; now protected in India under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Aquaculture: Sometimes perceived as a pest in fish farms due to predation on fish fry, though impact is generally minimal.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Tufted Ducks are expected at the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and larger water bodies in eastern UP from October through April. Mishra et al. (2019) recorded this species at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary during October 2017–March 2018. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022) recorded it in Ayodhya district wetlands. This species requires larger water bodies with adequate depth and good food supply. Published habitat and range information place it in lakes and reservoirs throughout eastern UP during winter months.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds across northern Europe and Asia, from Britain to Kamchatka; winters across Europe, Middle East, South Asia, East Africa, and East Asia.

In India: Regular winter visitor to larger wetlands and reservoirs across northern and central regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to major lakes and reservoirs, with records from the Gangetic plain and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to larger water bodies including Bakhira Bird Sanctuary and Ayodhya wetlands. Present October–April, with peak numbers January–February. Prefers larger, deeper water bodies.

Field rule: A small diving duck with a black head and distinctive drooping crest on the crown (males), or brown head with smaller crest (females) — is a Tufted Duck. The white flanks of males are distinctive. Females show white under tail.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Aythya fuligula was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th Edition, volume 1, page 128, as Anas fuligula. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; populations across the range are homogeneous with no significant geographic variation.

Family and order placement. Order Anseriformes; family Anatidae; genus Aythya contains the diving ducks (pochards). Aythya fuligula is one of 12 recognized species in the genus.

Phylogenetic notes. Aythya fuligula is closely related to other Aythya species, particularly to the Ring-necked Duck (A. collaris) of North America and African Pochard (A. erythrophthalma).

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Global population is large (>1 million) and stable. The species has successfully adapted to human-modified wetlands including urban lakes and reservoirs. Range has expanded northward in response to climate change.
