ब्लैक-टेल्ड गॉडविट / Black-tailed Godwit

Limosa limosa (Linnaeus, 1758) — Scolopacidae

ब्लैक-टेल्ड गॉडविट — यह एक लंबी-पैर वाली, सफेद और भूरी चिड़िया है जो सर्दियों में नम जगहों पर आती है। इसकी लंबी सीधी चोंच पानी में कीड़े निकालने के लिए डिजाइन की गई है। इसका नाम इसकी काली पूँछ से आता है जो उड़ान में दिखाई देती है। ये चिड़ियाँ झुंडों में दिखाई देती हैं और लंबी दूरी तय करती हैं।

Field tip: A long-legged wader with long straight bill on mudflats or in shallow water. White rump and black tail are very distinctive in flight. Often in flocks. Elegant, upright posture when standing.

Size: 37–39 cm long; wingspan 62–70 cm. Weight 190–340 g. A medium-sized wader with notable sexual size dimorphism (females larger).

Plumage (winter, as seen in UP): Crown and back brown or grey-brown, sometimes with darker markings. Rump bright white, very conspicuous. Tail black or dark with white base. Upperparts brown or brownish-grey. Underparts white or pale. Breast and flanks grey-brown or pale. Undertail coverts white.

Plumage (breeding, not seen in UP): Completely transformed. Breast, neck, and upper back rufous-orange or burnt-orange. Very striking. Females duller than males.

Bill: Long, straight, slightly upturned at tip. Dark with pale base. Pointed, adapted for probing in mud.

Legs: Long, dark grey or dark. Placed far back, giving upright posture.

Feet: Short toes.

Eyes: Small, dark.

Tail: Black or dark, with white base. White base visible in flight.

Rump: Bright white, very conspicuous in flight. Diagnostic feature.

Sexes: Females notably larger than males. Males more brightly coloured in breeding plumage.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Main call: Loud, harsh "wee-cah" or "weedah", often given in flight.
  2. Alarm calls: Sharp calls when alarmed.
  3. Contact calls: Quieter calls within flocks.
हिंदी: जोर से "वी-काह" या "वीदा" की आवाज़। झुंडों में चिल्लाती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Omnivore: Feeds on invertebrates, small fish, and aquatic invertebrates. Occasionally takes vegetation.

Feeding behaviour: Probes bill deep into mud and shallow water, searching for invertebrates. Walks through shallow water, probing as it goes. Also gleans prey from surface.

Habitat preference: Prefers mudflats, wet fields, wetland margins, and shallow water. Open areas near water.

Gregarious: Highly gregarious, particularly in winter. Flocks can be very large, sometimes with other godwits and waders.

Wading behaviour: Wades in shallow water and walks on mudflats. Elegant, upright posture when standing.

Flight: Swift, direct flight. Often seen in flight in flocks. Calls frequently in flight.

Active feeder: Continuously probing for food. Active throughout the day.

हिंदी: दलदली जगहों पर चोंच से कीड़े निकालता है। झुंडों में रहता है। बहुत सक्रिय शिकारी है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Long-distance migrant: Breeds in central Asia. One of longest-distance migrants among waders.

Winter visitor: Arrives in September–October from breeding grounds. Departs March–April.

Breeding range: Central Asia, from Volga eastward. Breeds on wet grasslands and marshes, April–July.

Migration: Extraordinary long-distance migrant. Undertakes flights of 10,000 km or more. Nocturnal migrant.

Wintering: Social in flocks on shared wintering grounds. Roost together at night.

Moult: Complete moult on wintering grounds. Breeding plumage acquired before departure.

Timing in UP: Early arrival September (vanguard); main arrival October; peak October–February; departure March–April.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आना शुरू करते हैं, अप्रैल तक चले जाते हैं। बहुत लंबी दूरी से आते हैं। झुंडों में सर्दी काटते हैं।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Common Kestrel, Egyptian Vulture, larger raptors
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, fox, mongoose
  • Chick predators (breeding only): Corvids, large raptors, jackals

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is illegal.

Insect control: Feeds on aquatic invertebrates. May help control pest insects and mosquitoes in wetlands.

Agricultural habitat use: Uses wet fields and seasonally flooded areas. Generally not considered a pest.

Wetland indicator: Presence indicates wetland health and productivity. Indicator of suitable wintering habitat.

Conservation concern: Listed as Near Threatened due to loss of breeding habitat in central Asia and staging areas. Declining populations globally.

Cultural significance: Known locally as "dhotee" or similar names. Part of traditional winter wader fauna.

Black-tailed Godwit is a long-distance winter migrant to Asia and is expected throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh, particularly in mudflats, wet fields, and wetland margins. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species is often found in flocks with other godwits and waders. The distinctive white rump and black tail are excellent identification features in flight. The loud "wee-cah" call and elegant posture make it identifiable. Peak occurrence should be October–February. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in central Asia. Winters throughout Asia from India to Southeast Asia and Australia. Migratory globally.

In India: Widespread winter visitor to wetlands and suitable habitat across the country. Common throughout.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread winter visitor to suitable wetland habitat throughout the state. Common and regular.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor to mudflats, wet fields, and wetland margins. Expected September–April. Often overlooked due to similarity to other godwits.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. L. l. limosa (Linnaeus, 1758) — European and central Asian populations (including those wintering in India). Multiple subspecies recognized.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Scolopacidae (sandpipers, phalaropes, snipes, and godwits). Genus Limosa contains four godwit species.

Phylogenetic notes. Limosa limosa is closely related to Bar-tailed Godwit (L. lapponica), with which it overlaps in India.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Near Threatened (NT) globally due to population declines driven by loss of breeding habitat in central Asia and staging areas along migration routes. Drainage of wetlands in central Asia is the primary threat. Population declining.
