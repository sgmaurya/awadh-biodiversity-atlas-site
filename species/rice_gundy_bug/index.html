गंधी कीड़ा / Rice Gundy Bug

Leptocorisa varicornis (Fabricius, 1803) — Alydidae

गंधी कीड़ा (राइस गंधी बग) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के धान के खेतों में पाया जाने वाला एक अत्यधिक हानिकारक और सुप्रसिद्ध निवासी कीट (true bug) है। इसे छूने या परेशान करने पर इसके शरीर से निकलने वाली एक तीव्र, अप्रिय गंध (stench) के कारण इसे "गंधी" या "गंधी कीड़ा" कहा जाता है। इसका शरीर पतला, लंबा और मटमैले हरे-भूरे रंग का होता है। यह कीट धान की फसल को उसके "दुग्ध अवस्था" (milky stage) के दौरान सबसे अधिक नुकसान पहुँचाता है। यह अपने सूई जैसे मुँह (proboscis) से विकसित हो रहे धान के दानों में छेद करके उनका सारा दूध चूस लेता है, जिससे दाने अंदर से खाली और सफेद (empty grains or chaffy grains) रह जाते हैं। यह उत्तर प्रदेश के धान किसानों के लिए आर्थिक नुकसान का एक बड़ा कारण है।

Field tip: Walk through a paddy field during the milky stage (September–October). Look for narrow, straw-colored bugs with long legs and antennae clinging to the green ears of rice. If you brush against the plants, you will immediately notice a strong, pungent, bug-like stench in the air.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for adult specimens in local rice fields):

MeasurementRange
Body length14.0–17.0 mm

Body details: The body is highly elongated, narrow, and cylindrical. The dorsal surface is olive-green or light brownish-yellow, providing excellent camouflage within grasses. The head is small and triangular with prominent, dark brown compound eyes. The antennae are nearly as long as the body, 4-segmented, and yellowish with dark bands at the terminal joints. The piercing-sucking mouthparts (proboscis) are extremely long, extending back to the hind legs when at rest. The legs are long, slender, and pale green.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Scent-defended seed specialist.

  • Feeding style: Pierces the protective glumes of developing rice grains during the milk stage and injects saliva to dissolve the starch before sucking it out.
  • Defensive stench: When disturbed, scent glands located on the metathorax release a volatile, pungent liquid containing hexanal and aldol compounds, producing a powerful foul odour that deters avian and arachnid predators.
  • Diurnal pattern: Avoids the intense midday heat. Most active during the early morning (6:00 to 9:00 AM) and late afternoon (4:00 to 7:00 PM), when they feed on rice panicles.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Granivorous herbivore.

  • Impact on grasses: Feeds primarily on the developing seeds of wild and cultivated grasses, affecting seed viability and plant reproduction.
  • Trophic link: Serves as a host for parasitic wasps and is preyed upon by specialized spiders and birds, despite its chemical defense.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Breeding: Peak reproduction occurs in September and October, synchronizing with the rice heading stage.
  • Egg laying: The female lays dark reddish-brown, disc-shaped eggs in neat, linear rows of 10–20 on the upper leaf surface of rice or wild grasses. Eggs hatch in 5–8 days.
  • Nymphal stage: Nymphs are bright green, wingless, and extremely slender. They pass through 5 instars over 18–22 days, feeding on leaves and soft stems before targeting grain ears.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Host plants:

  • Cultivated hosts: Paddy (Oryza sativa [FLR-0014 Paddy]), Maize (Zea mays [FLR-0054 Maize]), Sorghum, and Pearl Millet.
  • Wild hosts: Jungle rice (Echinochloa colona), Elephant grass (Pennisetum purpureum), and various wild sedges.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Spiders: Jumping spiders (such as [SPD-0003 Pantropical Jumping Spider]) and long-jawed orb weavers feed on nymphs and adults.
  • Insects: Dragonflies (like [INS-0025 Slender Skimmer]) capture flying adults. Assassin bugs ambush them on rice stems.
  • Parasitoids: Tiny wasps (Gryon and Hadronotus species) parasitize and destroy Gundy Bug egg clusters.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Major agricultural pest.

  • Chaffy grain damage: Sucking of the milky grain sap results in white, empty, chaffy grains (empty grains), directly reducing crop yield and quality.
  • Traditional control: Farmers walk through fields early in the morning holding hand-nets to catch sluggish bugs. Some hang rotten crabs or fish on bamboo poles in the field; the strong odour attracts Gundy Bugs, which are then collected and destroyed.
  • Weed management: Clearing wild grasses (मेड़ की सफाई) around the field edges before the rice flowers removes the bugs' alternative breeding sites.

A common pest in all blocks of Basti district, especially in low-lying rice-growing zones like Vikramjot, Vikrampur, and Harraiya. During September, the smell of gundy bugs is a familiar sign of agricultural stress in the region.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia (India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka) and Southeast Asia.

In India: Found in all paddy-growing states, especially in the eastern and northeastern plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident across all agricultural plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Highly abundant in all lowland rice fields.

Field rule: Slender, straw-colored body (~14–17 mm), long antennae with dark joint bands, feeding on developing rice grains, and emitting a strong foul odor, identify the Rice Gundy Bug.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Johan Christian Fabricius described the Rice Gundy Bug as Gerris varicornis in 1803 in Systema Rhyngotorum (p. 260). The type locality was India. The genus name Leptocorisa combines the Greek leptos (slender) and koris (bug). The specific name varicornis is Latin for "varied antennae", describing the light and dark banded pattern on the antennae.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognized.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Hemiptera, suborder Heteroptera, family Alydidae (broad-headed bugs), subfamily Micrelytrinae.

Phylogenetic notes. Leptocorisa varicornis is closely related to Leptocorisa acuta, sharing evolutionary pathways that shifted feeding habits from wild forest grasses to cultivated cereals, making them major agricultural pests.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) by the IUCN Red List. It is highly abundant across agricultural landscapes with no conservation concerns.
