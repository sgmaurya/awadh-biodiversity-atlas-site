खेत का केकड़ा / Jacquemont's Field Crab

Paratelphusa jacquemontii (Rathbun, 1905) — Gecarcinucidae

खेत का केकड़ा (भारतीय मीठे पानी का केकड़ा) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के धान के खेतों, तालाबों और नहरों के किनारे पाया जाने वाला एक आम जलीय जीव है। इसका शरीर एक कड़े, चपटे और चौड़े कवच (carapace) से ढका होता है, जिसका रंग गहरा भूरा या मटमैला पीला होता है। इसके पास पांच जोड़ी पैर होते हैं, जिनमें से पहली जोड़ी बड़े, मजबूत चिमटों (chelipeds) में बदली होती है, जिसका उपयोग यह रक्षा और भोजन पकड़ने के लिए करता है। यह केकड़ा मिट्टी के मेड़ों (bunds) में 30 से 60 सेंटीमीटर गहरे बिल बनाता है। यह मुख्य रूप से रात में सक्रिय होता है और सड़ने वाले पौधों, मरे हुए जीवों और छोटे कीड़ों को खाकर तालाबों की सफाई में मदद करता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription

ColourationDark chocolate-brown, olive-brown, or dull yellowish-brown; matches muddy soil
ChelipedsStrong claws (chelipeds), slightly unequal in size; used for crushing food and defense

Field tip: Look on clay bunds of rice fields or the banks of village ponds, especially during rainy nights. If disturbed, they run sideways quickly toward their water-filled burrows or raise their claws in a defensive posture.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for adult crabs in the Gangetic plains):

MeasurementRange
Carapace width40–55 mm

Carapace and Body: The carapace is hard, calcified, and broader than long, with a smooth, slightly convex dorsal surface. The front margin is slightly curved down. The lateral margins are finely serrated behind the eyes. The abdomen is tucked under the thorax; it is narrow and T-shaped in males, and broad and rounded in females (forming a pouch for carrying eggs).

Appendages: The first pair of legs are modified into strong claws (chelipeds). The remaining four pairs are simple walking legs with pointed tips (dactyli) for walking on slippery mud. They do not have swimming paddles.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Nocturnal burrower.

  • Burrow construction: These crabs excavate deep (30–60 cm), angled burrows in clayey mud. The burrows go down to the local water table, allowing them to remain moist even when the surface soil dries out.
  • Activity cycle: Mostly nocturnal, emerging from their burrows after dusk to forage on land or in shallow water, especially when relative humidity is high.
  • Aestivation: During the dry summer months, they seal the entrances of their burrows with mud plugs and remain dormant inside the moist chamber until the monsoon rains arrive.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Bioturbator and scavenger.

  • Soil aeration: Their digging activity mixes soil layers (bioturbation), aerates the root zone of wetland plants, and increases water penetration in agricultural soils.
  • Nutrient recycler: As omnivores and detritivores, they consume decaying leaf litter and animal carcasses, accelerating organic decomposition.
  • Prey base: They are a vital food source for many species of fish, water snakes, monitor lizards, herons, egrets, and small mammals like mongooses.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Mating: Occurs in water during the early monsoon season (June–July).
  • Direct development (No free-swimming larva stage): Unlike marine crabs, which release thousands of microscopic, free-swimming larvae (zoea) into the open sea, this species shows direct development.
  • Egg care: The female carries 100–300 large, orange-coloured eggs under her broad abdomen (brood pouch). She regularly ventilates and cleans the eggs.
  • Hatching: The eggs hatch directly into fully formed, miniature crabs (crablits) that remain attached to the mother's abdomen for several days before dispersing into the mud. This adaptation prevents the larvae from being swept away by fast-flowing monsoon currents.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary food items:

  • Detritus: Decaying plant leaves (such as rice stalks and weeds) and organic matter in mud.
  • Prey: Small pond snails, aquatic worms, insect larvae (like mosquito wrigglers), and slow-moving tadpoles.
  • Scavenging: Dead fish, frogs, and other organic waste.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Egrets (such as the [BRD-0029 Cattle Egret] and Little Egret), Pond Herons, and storks.
  • Reptiles: Checkered Keelback snakes and monitor lizards.
  • Mammals: Small Indian Mongooses (Herpestes auropunctatus) and feral dogs.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Irrigation pest and protein source.

  • Bund damage: By digging extensive burrows in the mud bunds (aar) of rice fields, they weaken the soil structure. This causes water to leak out of the fields, making it difficult for farmers to maintain correct water levels.
  • Traditional food: They are collected from fields during the monsoon and eaten by several rural communities in Basti, serving as a free, high-protein food source.
  • Traditional medicine: In some eastern UP villages, crab soup is traditionally consumed as a remedy for respiratory infections and joint pains.

Extremely common in the agricultural landscapes of Basti district. During July and August, hundreds of small mud chimneys (excavated soil piled around burrow entrances) can be seen along the borders of rice fields. Children often catch them by flooding their burrows or using simple string traps with small fish as bait.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Endemic to the Indian Subcontinent (found across India, Nepal, and Bangladesh).

In India: Widespread in the freshwater basins of northern, eastern, and central India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant in all agricultural districts, particularly in the waterlogged Gangetic plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Found in every clay-rich agricultural field, village pond, and seasonal marsh.

  2. How long do the young crablits remain attached to the mother's abdomen before dispersing? — Capture brooding females, keep them in clay pots, and record dispersal times.
  3. What is the preference of foraging crabs for fresh rice shoots compared to decomposing weeds? — Conduct choice experiments in small aquariums.
  4. Does the use of chemical fertilizers in rice fields cause crabs to migrate out of the fields? / क्या धान के खेतों में रासायनिक खादों के छिड़काव से केकड़े खेतों से बाहर पलायन करते हैं? — Compare crab activity before and after fertilizer application.
  5. How do crabs prevent their burrows from collapsing during heavy monsoon floods? / मॉनसून की भारी बारिश के दौरान केकड़े अपने बिलों को ढहने से कैसे बचाते हैं? — Observe burrow structure and mud reinforcement behaviors.

Field rule: Mud-brown colouration, carapace wider than long, five pairs of legs with strong claws, and deep burrows in agricultural clay bunds identify Jacquemont's Field Crab.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Mary J. Rathbun described this species as Potamon (Potamonautes) jacquemontii in 1905 in her work Les crabes d'eau douce (Potamonidae) published in Nouvelles Archives du Muséum d'Histoire Naturelle (Paris, Series 4, Vol. 7, p. 185). The type locality was India. The genus name Paratelphusa combines the Greek para (near/beside) and Telphusa, a nymph of Greek mythology associated with a freshwater spring. The specific name jacquemontii honors the French botanist and explorer Victor Jacquemont.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Decapoda, family Gecarcinucidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Paratelphusa jacquemontii is considered by some modern taxonomists to be a synonym or closely related variant of Barytelphusa cunicularis, but is retained under Paratelphusa in regional references due to distinct fronto-orbital features.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) because it is widely distributed, highly abundant in agricultural wetlands, and faces no major threat of extinction, although local populations are affected by pesticide pollution.
