जलकाग / Indian Cormorant

Phalacrocorax fuscicollis (Wattson, 1883) — Phalacrocoracidae

जलकाग — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की नदियों और तालाबों का एक काली चिड़िया, जो पूरे साल रहती है और मछली का शिकार करती है। यह पानी में पूरी तरह डूबकर मछली पकड़ने में माहिर है। इसकी गर्दन काली होती है (Little Cormorant से अलग), और यह Great Cormorant से बहुत छोटी होती है। इसके लंबे पैर नदी के तट पर चलने-फिरने के लिए बिल्कुल सही हैं। ये कौवे की तरह कर्कश आवाज़ निकालती हैं।

Field tip: The Indian Cormorant is a medium-sized black water bird with a long neck and green legs. The brown patch on the upper neck is distinctive. Look for it swimming low in water, diving repeatedly, and perching with wings open to dry.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 63–66 cm tall; wingspan 105–125 cm. Weight 700–1000 g. Intermediate in size between Little Cormorant (smaller) and Great Cormorant (larger).

Plumage: Predominantly glossy black-green overall. Breeding birds develop white head and neck patches (April–May). Distinctive brown or buff colouring on the upper throat and upper neck (diagnostic feature separating from Little Cormorant, which is all black).

Head: Black crown. Upper throat and neck buff-brown or grey-brown.

Bill: Long, slender, sharply pointed, dark, with a slight hook at tip — perfectly adapted for gripping slippery fish.

Legs and feet: Long, greenish; fully webbed. Placed far back on body, making walking awkward but diving extremely efficient.

Eyes: Small, dark; adapted for underwater vision.

Sexes: Identical in plumage. Females may be slightly smaller.

Juveniles: Duller, more brown overall.

Vocalisations. Vocal at times:

  1. Contact and territorial call: Harsh, guttural gark-gark or craak-craak at colonies and roosts; frequency 0.5–2.0 kHz.
  2. Courtship call: Softer grunting and hissing at nests.
  3. Alert call: Sharp croaking when disturbed on water.
हिंदी: तीव्र, कर्कश आवाज़। कॉलोनी में शोरगुल करता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Aquatic predator: The Indian Cormorant feeds almost exclusively on fish:

  • Small fish species (Puntius, Aplocheilus, snakeheads, gobies)
  • Aquatic larvae and juvenile fish
  • Rarely small crustaceans

Diving and feeding: Dives from the surface and pursues prey underwater using fully webbed feet. A single dive lasts 20–40 seconds and covers 10–20 meters. Eyes have special adaptations for underwater vision.

Social behaviour: Gregarious; roosts communally at night. Forms colonies during breeding season.

Habitat use: Strongly associated with rivers and river systems; less common on still-water lakes. Prefers clear or moderately clear water where prey is visible.

Perching behaviour: Perches upright on rocks, dead trees, or riverbank stumps, with wings held semi-open to dry (important thermoregulation and feather maintenance).

Predators: Fish eagles, marsh harriers; snakes and monitor lizards take eggs and chicks.

Population indicator: Its presence indicates productive river systems with abundant fish populations.

हिंदी: नदियों में पूरी तरह डूबकर मछली पकड़ता है। पेड़ों पर बैठकर पंख सुखाता है। हमेशा झुंड में रहता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round in eastern UP; no migration.

Breeding: April–June (pre-monsoon to monsoon). Colonial nester in heronries (often shared with egrets, Little Cormorants, painted storks).

Nesting: Large platform nest of sticks in tall riverside trees. Trees: tamarind, peepal, banyan, acacia. Colony sites are traditional, reused for many years.

Eggs: 3–4 pale blue eggs. Both parents incubate ~25 days.

Chicks: Altricial; fed regurgitated fish by both parents. Fledge at ~8 weeks.

Roosting: Communal night roosts on rocks and dead trees.

Moulting: Complex moult pattern; begins post-breeding (August–September), completes by December.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Fish Eagles, Marsh Harrier
  • Eggs and chicks: Snakes, monitor lizards, crows, occasionally otters
  • Adults: Large eagles (rare); snakes if caught on nesting site
  • Parasites: Waterfowl helminths

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: Listed in Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, amended 2022. Fully protected; hunting prohibited.

Fisheries: Historically viewed as a fish predator and sometimes persecuted in fishing communities, but generally tolerated. Fish-eating birds are important for ecosystem balance.

Cultural references: Named जलकाग (jal kaag, "water crow") because of its dark colour and harsh calls resembling crows.

Scientific importance: Indicator species for river health and water quality. Its presence indicates productive, well-oxygenated river systems.

Fishing heritage: Historically used for "cormorant fishing" (a traditional practice in some parts of India, though rare in eastern UP).

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

This species is NOT a field record. The description below indicates where Indian Cormorants should be looked for based on published information.

Year-round presence: Expected throughout eastern UP on the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and associated lakes and ponds.

Habitat preference: Strongly associated with rivers; more common on flowing water than still lakes.

Peak visibility: More vocal and visible during breeding season (April–June). Year-round, but roosting sites are most obvious at dawn and dusk.

Nearest published record: The Ghaghra river near Balrampur and other river stretches support established colonies. ZSI records confirm their presence throughout the region.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph clearly showing the bird, preferably with the diagnostic brown neck patch visible, with date and GPS location, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Endemic to Indian subcontinent. No subspecies recognised (monotypic).

In India: Distributed throughout, associated with river systems and large water bodies.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread on major rivers, particularly the Ganges and its tributaries.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident on the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and associated wetlands throughout the year.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Wattson in 1883. Type locality is India.

Subspecies. Monotypic — Phalacrocorax fuscicollis fuscicollis. No subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Suliformes; family Phalacrocoracidae (cormorants). The genus Phalacrocorax is the largest cormorant genus globally.

Phylogenetic notes. Recent molecular studies confirm Indian Cormorant as distinct from other Phalacrocorax species. The brown-necked morph is unique among Indian cormorants.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern globally. Population stable and widespread in suitable habitats. Protected under Schedule IV of Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (amended 2022) in India.
