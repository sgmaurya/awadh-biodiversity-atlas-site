रोज़ी पिपिट / Rosy Pipit

Anthus roseatus Blyth, 1847 — Motacillidae

रोज़ी पिपिट — सर्दियों में हिमालय के ऊंचे ग्लेशियरों और बुग्यालों से उत्तर भारत के गीले मैदानों में आने वाला एक आकर्षक पिपिट (चरचरी) है। यह दलदली घास के मैदानों, धान के कटे हुए खेतों और पानी से भरे गड्ढों के किनारे जमीन पर तेजी से दौड़ता हुआ दिखता है। जाड़े के अंत में जब यह वापस पहाड़ों की ओर लौटने को होता है, तो इसके सीने और गले पर हल्का गुलाबीपन उभर आता है जो इसे अन्य पिपिटों से अलग बनाता है।

Field tip: Look in boggy ground, flooded pastures, and shallow edges of jheels during mid-winter. In non-breeding plumage it resembles other pipits but has exceptionally heavy black streaking on the upperparts and flanks, plus diagnostic yellow underwing patches visible in flight.

इसकी पीठ और पसलियों पर काली धारियां बहुत घनी होती हैं और उड़ते समय पंखों के नीचे पीलापन दिखाई देता है।
वसंत आते-आते इसके सीने पर हल्का गुलाबी रंग छाने लगता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1926 as Anthus roseatus):

MeasurementRange
Wing82–94 mm (Baker 1926)

The Rosy Pipit is a medium-sized, strikingly dark pipit. In non-breeding winter plumage, the whole upper plumage is blackish, each feather broadly margined with olive-brown or greyish-olive. The wings are dark brown with olive-buff edges to coverts and quills. The tail is dark brown edged with olive, with the outermost pair of feathers largely white on the outer web and diagonally white on the inner web.

The supercilium is broad, buffish or pale fulvous in winter, becoming vinous-pink in breeding dress. The underparts are pale fulvous or buff-white, heavily marked with bold black streaks across the breast and flanks. The underwing coverts and axillaries are bright yellow, a key feature in hand and in flight.

The bill is horny-black above and fleshy-yellow at the base of the lower mandible. Iris dark brown. Legs and feet are fleshy-brown with darker claws. Sexes are similar, though females often have slightly less intense pink flush in spring.

Vocalisations. The flight call is a distinctive, somewhat metallic, sharp sip-sip or tseep, higher in pitch and thinner than the call of the Meadow Pipit. When flushed from wet grass, it utters a double ti-ti call.

इसके शरीर का ऊपरी हिस्सा काली और भूरी धारियों से भरा होता है।
उड़ते समय इसके पंखों के भीतरी हिस्से पर चटक पीला रंग चमकता है।
यह जमीन से उड़ते वक्त तीखी 'सीप-सीप' जैसी सीटी बजाती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Anthus roseatus is intensely hydrophilic compared to its congeners, showing an almost exclusive preference for wet mud, flooded grassland, and damp jheel borders in winter. It avoids dry cultivated fields or stony barrens, replacing open-scrub pipits where soils remain saturated.

It feeds entirely on the ground, walking with rapid, measured steps, constantly scanning the mud and grass roots for invertebrates. When foraging, it periodically stops and wags its tail downward in typical pipit fashion, though less rhythmically than a true wagtail. It occurs singly, in pairs, or in loose scattered feeding parties across damp floodplains.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Baker (1926, p. 286) records breeding in Garhwal, Kashmir, Ladakh, and Tibet, placing the nest among tufts of grass or under a stone. In winter, birds descend from high montane breeding grounds into the plains of northern India and Assam.

They remain in saturated wetlands and marshy ground through the winter before departing northward to alpine breeding grounds as warm weather arrives.

हिंदी:

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Baker (1926, p. 287) notes that this pipit feeds on all kinds of small insects, frequently wading into shallow water in pursuit of them. Prey items include:

  • Aquatic insect larvae (chironomids and mosquito grubs)
  • Small beetles, weevils, and rove beetles inhabiting damp mud
  • Small aquatic snails and tiny crustacea in shallow pools
  • Flies, midges, and small grasshoppers

Occasionally, it ingests tiny seeds of marsh grasses and sedges during periods of cold weather when insect activity is subdued.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds from the mountains of Afghanistan, across the Himalayas, through the Tibetan Plateau to central China. Winters at lower elevations in the Himalayan foothills, northern and north-eastern India, northern Myanmar, and northern Indochina.

In India: Winter visitor across the northern plains, the Terai, and the valleys of Assam and Bengal.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to wet river valleys, oxbow lakes, and marshy lowlands across northern and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to the flood basins of the Rapti, Ghaghra, and Kuwana rivers; recorded on wintering grounds in the core districts.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Edward Blyth in 1847 in the Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal (vol. 16, part 1, p. 437), based on a manuscript name by Brian H. Hodgson. The type locality is Nepal.

Subspecies. Usually treated as monotypic, though eastern Tibetan and Chinese populations with slightly darker mantles have sometimes been separated as A. r. pelzelni.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Motacillidae (pipits and wagtails).

Phylogenetic notes. Anthus roseatus belongs to the montane pipit assemblage closely related to the Water Pipit (Anthus spinoletta) and the Meadow Pipit (Anthus pratensis).

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC) on the IUCN Red List due to its expansive range across alpine Asia and stable wintering populations across South Asian lowlands.
