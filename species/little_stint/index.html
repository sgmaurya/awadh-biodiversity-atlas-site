लिटिल स्टिंट / Little Stint

Calidris minuta (Leisler, 1812) — Scolopacidae

लिटिल स्टिंट — यह सबसे छोटी चिड़िया है जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में आती है। आर्कटिक टुंड्रा से सर्दियों में भारत आती है। इसकी पीठ भूरी-लाल धब्बेदार होती है (सर्दियों में), और यह बहुत सक्रिय, तेज़ गति से चलने वाली होती है। इसके छोटे पैर और छोटी चोंच दिखते हैं। बड़ी तादाद में झुंड में आती है।

Field tip: The tiniest wader seen in flocks on exposed mudflats or wet fields during winter — is a Little Stint. The tiny size, short bill, rapid pecking behaviour, and gregarious flocking are diagnostic. Often mixed with other small sandpipers.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 12–14 cm long; wingspan 25–30 cm. Weight 17–30 g. One of the smallest waders in the region.

Plumage (winter): Upperparts grey-brown or drab, lightly streaked. Head and neck grey-brown. Supercilium (eyebrow) white and prominent. Underparts white; breast faintly streaked with grey. Rump dark with white sides.

Plumage (breeding/spring): Warmer coloration with rufous-buff on upperparts and upper breast.

Legs and feet: Thin, black, very short relative to body.

Bill: Extremely short and thin, pointed, black. One of the most diagnostic features.

Eyes: Dark, small.

Sexes: Females slightly larger; little sexual dimorphism.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact/flight call: A short, sharp kit or tit, given constantly in flight.
  2. Alarm call: Repeated sharp kit kit kit when alarmed.
हिंदी: लगातार "कित-कित" की आवाज़। उड़ते समय कूकना।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Rapid forager: Little Stints are hyperactive, constantly moving and rapidly pecking at the substrate for small invertebrates. "Tick-tick-tick" feeding style unique among larger waders.

Feeding: Entirely insectivorous:

  • Small aquatic insect larvae (midges, small chironomids)
  • Tiny terrestrial insects
  • Minute crustaceans and amphipods
  • Never probe like Tringa species; pick only from surface

Gregarious: Highly social, forming large flocks during migration and wintering. Often associate with other small sandpipers.

Habitat preference: Exposed mudflats with minimal vegetation; wet fields; exposed banks. Often on the most barren areas avoided by larger waders.

Flight: Fast, direct flight with flickering wingbeats. Show striking white rump with dark centre in flight.

Alert: Moderately wary; flocks take off and circle repeatedly before landing.

हिंदी: बहुत तेज़ गति से चलते-फिरते हैं। "टिक-टिक-टिक" करते हैं। बड़े झुंड में होते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP from July (early arrivals), with main arrival August–September. Departs March–May, with most birds gone by May.

Breeding range: Arctic tundra of northern Siberia and Russia, from Kola Peninsula to Siberia. Breeds May–August above treeline.

Migration: Extreme long-distance migrant; migrates 8,000–10,000+ km. Among the longest migrations of any bird. Diurnal and nocturnal migration.

Wintering: Highly gregarious, forming mixed-species flocks with other small sandpipers. Concentrate on productive mudflat systems.

Moult: Complete moult occurs en route or on wintering grounds; breeding plumage acquired on wintering grounds in spring.

Timing in UP: Early arrival July–August (vanguard); main arrival August–September; peak December–February; departure March–May.

हिंदी: जुलाई से आना शुरू करते हैं, मई तक चले जाते हैं। आर्कटिक से सबसे लंबी यात्रा करते हैं।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Peregrine Falcon, Merlin, Marsh Harrier, Indian Eagle-Owl
  • Mammalian predators: Fox, mongoose, wild cat
  • Predation pressure: High due to small size and gregariousness; often targeted by raptors hunting flocks

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Listed under Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Ecological value: Important insectivore on mudflats; helps control small insect populations.

Wetland health indicator: Abundance of Little Stint flocks indicates productive wetlands with rich invertebrate fauna.

Cultural significance: Mentioned in bird survey literature as indicator of good wetland conditions.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी विधि अधिनियम 1972 के अंतर्गत संरक्षित है। इसका झुंड बड़े जलाशयों की अच्छी स्थिति दर्शाता है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Little Stint is a winter visitor to wetlands of eastern Uttar Pradesh. Mishra et al. (2019) recorded this species at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary in Sant Kabir Nagar district (adjoining Basti), documenting it in their October 2017 to March 2018 survey. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025) also documented the species at Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary in Gonda district, and Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022) recorded it in Ayodhya district wetlands. The species is expected on exposed mudflats and wet fields throughout eastern UP, particularly at major river systems and reservoirs. Often arrives in flocks. Peak occurrence should be August–March. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Arctic tundra from Kola Peninsula to Siberia; winters across southern Eurasia, Africa, and Asia.

In India: Widespread winter visitor and passage migrant, particularly abundant on productive wetlands and mudflats.

In Uttar Pradesh: Regular winter visitor and passage migrant throughout the state, particularly common on the Ganges, Ghaghra, and Rapti river systems.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common winter visitor to exposed mudflats and wet areas. Expected July–May, with peak occurrence August–March. Often the most numerous small sandpiper.

Field rule: The tiniest wader seen in large flocks on mudflats, with hyperactive pecking behaviour and a very short bill — is a Little Stint. The size (12–14 cm) is diagnostic; no other sandpiper is as small or as gregarious.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Leisler in 1812 in Nachträge zu Leislers Nachträgen zu Bechsteins Naturgeschichte as Tringa minuta. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. Two subspecies are recognised:

  • C. m. minuta — Palearctic (occurs in India)
  • C. m. nigriceps — Far East Russia (breeds farther east)

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Scolopacidae. Genus Calidris contains the "stint" sandpipers, with about 15 species.

Phylogenetic notes. Calidris minuta is closely related to C. temminckii (Temminck's Stint), forming a clade within Calidris.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. The global population is estimated at 1–2 million individuals and appears stable. The species is highly adaptable to various wetland types and benefits from the availability of stopover sites during migration.
