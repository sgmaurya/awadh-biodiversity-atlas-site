ग्रे बुशचैट / Grey Bushchat

Saxicola ferreus Hodgson, 1845 — Muscicapidae

ग्रे बुशचैट — यह छोटी सुंदर चिड़िया हिमालय से सर्दियों में भारत आती है। इसका शरीर भूरा-धूसर होता है और पूंछ काली होती है। यह खुली घास के मैदानों और झाड़ियों वाली जगहों पर रहती है। इसे कीड़े खाते हुए देखा जा सकता है — यह घास में छोटी छलाँगें लगाती हुई आगे बढ़ता है और कीड़े पकड़ता है। सर्दियों में इसकी तेज़, मजेदार कूकना सुनाई देती है।

Field tip: A small grey-brown chat with a distinctive dark tail, found on open grasslands and scrubland during winter in eastern UP. Watch for short, jerky movements on the ground between grass tufts and low bushes. The bird flicks its tail nervously. Often seen in loose groups in suitable habitat. The combination of pale plumage, dark tail, and ground-dwelling habit is distinctive.

Size: 12–13 cm long; wingspan 18–20 cm. A small, compact chat.

Plumage (male): Upperparts pale grey-brown. Crown greyish. Lores (area between eye and bill) whitish. Ear-coverts greyish. Back buff-grey. Rump and upper tail-coverts pale buff. Tail entirely black or very dark brown (diagnostic). Wings pale brown, coverts slightly paler. Underparts mostly white or pale buff.

Plumage (female): Overall duller than male; browner tones. Back browner. Tail dark brown. Similar pattern to male but less contrasting.

Head: Small, rounded. Eyes dark; positioned for ground foraging vision.

Bill: Short, thin, black.

Legs: Short, dark grey to black.

Sexes: Sexually dimorphic; females duller than males.

Vocalisations. Active, vocal bird:

  1. Call: A loud, harsh chat-chat-chat or chit-chit-chit, given frequently.
  2. Alarm: Scolding notes when disturbed or defending territory.
  3. Song: Simple melodious warble mixed with harsh notes.
हिंदी: तेज़ "चेट-चेट" या "चित्-चित्" आवाज़। बहुत बोलती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ground forager: Feeds primarily on insects and other invertebrates found on or near the ground. Feeds on:

  • Small insects (beetles, ants, flies)
  • Spiders
  • Small caterpillars
  • Grasshoppers and small orthopterans

Foraging technique: Hops and runs on ground in short, jerky movements. Picks food from grass, ground, or low vegetation. Often pauses to scan.

Social behaviour: Winter populations are often in loose groups or loose flocks in suitable habitat. Territorial during breeding season.

Roosting: Roosts in grass or low bushes; often roosting communally in winter.

Tail flicking: Constantly flicks tail, raising it above body in characteristic nervous manner.

हिंदी: घास पर कीड़े खाता है। तेज़ गति से दौड़ता है। पूंछ झटकते हुए।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Absent during breeding season (April–September); returns to eastern UP from October–November and remains through March–April.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in October; peak numbers December–January; departure begins March, completed by April.

Breeding range: Himalayas, Central Asia, and northern regions.

Migration: Altitudinal or latitudinal migrant, moving to lower elevations in winter.

Timing in UP: October arrival; peak December–January; March–April departure.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Grey Bushchat is expected as a winter visitor to grasslands and scrubland of eastern Uttar Pradesh from October to April. Based on its range, it should occur on open grasslands, cultivated fields with low scrub, and river grasslands. Look for small flocks of active birds on the ground, often with their tails raised. The combination of pale plumage, dark tail, and ground-dwelling habit is distinctive. Peak occurrence should be December–February. A first record would require a clear photograph showing the pale plumage, dark tail, and small size, with place and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Himalayan region and Central Asia. Winters in plains of northern South Asia.

In India: Winter visitor to grasslands and open areas of northern and northeastern regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to grasslands, particularly in northern and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to open grasslands and scrubland, October–April.

Field rule: A small, pale grey-brown chat with a distinctive dark tail, found on dry grasslands, flicking its tail nervously, is a Grey Bushchat. The pale plumage and dark tail are diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Brian Hodgson in 1845 in Zoological Miscellany as Saxicola ferrea. Type locality: Nepal.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae (Old World flycatchers and chats). The genus Saxicola contains the stonechats and bushchats.

Phylogenetic notes. Grey Bushchat is closely related to other Saxicola species and shares the typical chat morphology of small size and ground-feeding adaptations.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). The global population is large. The species is adaptable to various grassland and scrubland types.
