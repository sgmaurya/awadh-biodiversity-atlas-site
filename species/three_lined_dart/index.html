थ्री-लाइन्ड डार्ट / Three-lined Dart

Pseudagrion decorum (Hagen, 1865) — Coenagrionidae

थ्री-लाइन्ड डार्ट — एक मध्यम आकार की सूतिका कीट जिसका सिर और शरीर नीले रंग का होता है, लेकिन गहरी काली धारियाँ (तीन प्रमुख धारियाँ: mid-dorsal और दो lateral) इसे अन्य नीली व्याधपतंगों से अलग करती हैं। यह उथले पौधों से भरे जलाशयों में पाई जाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription

ColourPredominantly blue body with three prominent black stripes on thorax

EyesWidely separated
FemaleGreenish-blue or yellowish-green

Field tip: The three prominent black stripes running down the thorax (one central, two lateral) distinguish this damselfly from other blue Pseudagrion species, which typically have different stripe patterns.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Body length32–37 mm
Hindwing length17–20 mm
Wing span36–40 mm

Adult male: Head blue with small black markings. Thorax predominantly blue with three prominent black longitudinal stripes: one mid-dorsal (down the center) and two lateral (on the sides), occupying nearly half the thoracic width. Abdomen predominantly blue with black bands at abdominal joints. Wings transparent.

Adult female: Head and thorax greenish-blue to yellowish-green, with similar stripe pattern. Abdomen greenish with darker markings.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Pseudagrion decorum is a medium-sized damselfly of vegetation-rich freshwater wetlands. It is moderately sensitive to pollution and prefers areas with good aquatic plant growth. Males perch on vegetation and intercept flying insects, while females forage among plants for oviposition sites.

Larval Stage

Naiads are slender, greenish-brown, found among submerged vegetation. Larval duration is approximately 10–12 weeks. Peak emergence occurs in spring and post-monsoon seasons.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Adult prey:

  • Small midges and gnats
  • Mosquitoes
  • Tiny flies

Larval prey:

  • Mosquito larvae
  • Small crustaceans and worms

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Warblers, flycatchers, swallows
  • Fish: Small fish fry
  • Spiders: Tetragnathid and jumping spiders
  • Larger insects: Dragonfly naiads

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Not documented as a pest. Feeds on small insects including mosquitoes, potentially contributing to mosquito control in wetland areas.

GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. Expected in vegetation-rich ponds and wetlands throughout eastern UP, year-round. Look for the blue damselfly with three distinctive black stripes on the thorax. A photograph showing the three-stripe pattern clearly, with date and location, would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia; widespread from Pakistan and India to Myanmar and Thailand.

In India: Common in plains and foothills.

In Uttar Pradesh: Scattered records in wetlands.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all suitable water bodies; unconfirmed locally.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. Do the three black stripes vary in width between individuals? — Capture and measure stripe widths on multiple males.
  2. How does stripe pattern differ from other Pseudagrion species? — Photograph and compare P. decorum, P. rubriceps, and P. microcephalum.
  3. What is the larval development time at different water temperatures? — Rear naiads; compare in warm and cool water.
  4. तीन धारियों का क्या कार्य हो सकता है? (What could be the function of the three stripes?) — Speculate on camouflage, thermoregulation, or species recognition.
  5. How does female choice correlate with male stripe pattern? — Observe mating and rejection behaviors; compare male stripe quality.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Hagen described Pseudagrion decorum in 1865. Type locality: India. The species name refers to the decorative appearance (decorum = proper, becoming).

Subspecies. Monotypic.

Family and order placement. Order Odonata, suborder Zygoptera, family Coenagrionidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Pseudagrion decorum is closely related to other Asian Pseudagrion species, representing successful radiations into similar ecological niches.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC). Broad range and stable populations where habitat persists.
