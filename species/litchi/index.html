लीची / Litchi

Litchi chinensis Sonn. — Sapindaceae

लीची — दक्षिण-पूर्वी एशिया का यह छोटा से मध्यम आकार का सदाबहार फल वाला पेड़ है जो ऊपरी दक्षिण भारत और हिमालय की तलहटी में अब खेती की जाती है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में यह अभी एक दुर्लभ पेड़ है, कुछ गाँवों के बागों में ही पाया जाता है। लीची का फल गहरे लाल या गुलाबी रंग का, ऊबड़-खाबड़ छिलका वाला, और मीठा-रसीला होता है; इसे छीलकर सफेद गूदे को खाते हैं। बसंत के फूल सुगंधित होते हैं और मधुमक्खियों को आकर्षित करते हैं।

Field tip: Look in old homestead fruit gardens for a medium tree with feathery leaves and in May–June for clusters of distinctive bumpy red fruits. The sweet white flesh is the giveaway. Fruit is sold in bazaars in early summer.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: मध्यम पेड़, पंखनुमा पत्तियाँ, और गर्मियों में उबड़-खाबड़, लाल-गुलाबी, मीठे फल। छिलके के नीचे सफेद गूदा।

Trunk and bark: Straight or slightly crooked, bark grey-brown, fissured with age. Wood pale, moderately hard.

Leaves: Alternate, even-pinnate, 15–30 cm long. Leaflets opposite, oblong, 5–10 cm, with a prominent midvein creating a grooved appearance. Leaflets entire, glossy green above, paler below. No terminal leaflet.

Flowers: Unisexual, male and female on the same tree, though male flowers are more numerous. Each flower small, 3–5 mm, greenish-yellow, with five sepals and five petals. Flowers in dense, much-branched terminal panicles 10–20 cm long. Sweet fragrance, pollinated mainly by bees.

Fruit: A syncarp (composite fruit) of 1–6 carpels fused at the base, each carpel forming a lobe 2–4 cm in diameter. The outer skin (pericarp) is thin, warty or bumpy, bright red, pink, or occasionally brown when ripe. The flesh (aril) is translucent white or cream, juicy, sweet to subacid. A single large brown seed per fruit; some cultivars are seedless.

हिंदी: पत्तियाँ दोनों ओर 4-5 जोड़ी, सिरे पर कोई पत्ती नहीं। फल का गूदा सफेद, मीठा, और गर्मियों में फलता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A tropical and subtropical fruit tree native to southern China, now cultivated widely in warm regions. The tree demands higher cold season chilling (120–400 hours below 15°C) than is reliably available in the Basti plains, making reliable fruiting difficult here. It tolerates well-drained soils and is drought-hardy once established. Growth is moderate, and trees begin bearing in 3–5 years from grafted stock, with peak production at 10–20 years. Mature trees yield 30–50 kg of fruit per season in suitable climates, though yields in UP remain low and erratic.

The early-spring flowers are worked by bees and attract Asian Honey Bee and other pollinators. Ripe fruits are taken by Indian Flying Fox and birds such as mynas and parakeets. The fruit falls and rots beneath the tree, enriching the soil.

हिंदी: यह दक्षिण की गर्म जलवायु वाला पेड़ है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में ठंड पूरी नहीं मिलती, इसलिए फल कम आता है। मधुमक्खियाँ और पक्षी इसके फूल और फल खाते हैं।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Asian Honey Bee and other bees on the flowers in spring.
  • Fruit dispersers: Indian Flying Fox and birds including Common Myna and Rose-ringed Parakeet.
  • Companions in orchards: Often grown with Mango, Jamun, and aam padri in mixed fruit gardens.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating caterpillars and mites on new growth in monsoon.
  • Stem borers in young trees; can be managed with pruning.
  • Powdery mildew on flowers in humid conditions, reducing fruit set.
  • Anthracnose (fruit rot) in wet seasons; controlled by pruning for air circulation.
  • Fruit flies (Oriental Fruit Fly) damage developing fruit; bagging reduces damage.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and ethnobotanical use:

The fruit is eaten fresh, prized for its sweet translucent flesh. In southern India and China, it is also dried and sold as a confection.

Today. Litchi is a premium fruit crop in suitable climates, but erratic in the northern plains due to insufficient winter chill. In Basti, it remains a specialty fruit for those orchardists willing to nurture individual trees. Fruit is sold in bazaars during May–June at relatively high prices.

Nutritional value. The fruit contains vitamin C, copper, and antioxidants. The flesh is 80% water and about 16% carbohydrate.

Horticultural challenges in eastern UP. The main constraint is that the region does not receive enough winter chill hours. Flowering is unreliable, and fruit set is sporadic. Grafted trees are essential; seed-grown trees take 7–10 years to fruit and produce inferior fruit.

हिंदी: गर्मी का फल; मई-जून में बाज़ार में मिलता है, महँगा होता है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में सर्दी पूरी न मिलने से फल बहुत कम आता है। शहरी बागों में सजावट के लिए लगाया जाता है।

A rare cultivated tree in Basti, found only in the private homestead gardens of orchardists willing to experiment with southern fruit species. Expect it singly or in small groups in older fruit gardens in towns and well-to-do villages. Any tree found is worth recording with its age and yield, as litchi remains marginal to the region's agriculture.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to subtropical southern China, now cultivated across tropical and subtropical Asia, Africa, the Americas, and the Pacific.

In India: Cultivated mainly in Himalayan foothills (Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Darjeeling) and in peninsular India. Attempts to grow it in the plains are limited.

In Uttar Pradesh: Sporadic cultivation in the Bijnor, Meerut and other western districts foothills; very rare in the eastern plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Extremely rare, only in private homestead gardens; never wild.

Field rule: A medium evergreen tree with grooved pinnate leaves, fragrant greenish flowers in early spring, and bumpy bright red sweet white-fleshed fruits in May–June is litchi; no wild tree looks like it in UP.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Pierre Sonnerat in 1782 in his Voyage à la Nouvelle Guinée. The genus name Litchi is derived from the Chinese name lichee or li zhi. The species name chinensis refers to China, its native region.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Sapindaceae (soapberry family), order Sapindales, alongside reetha and longan (Dimocarpus).

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies place Litchi within the Asian clade of Sapindaceae, closely related to Dimocarpus and Nephelium.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widely cultivated globally; status in its native China is not fully assessed.
