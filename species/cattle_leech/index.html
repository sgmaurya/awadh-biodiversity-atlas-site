जोंक / Indian Cattle Leech

Hirudinaria granulosa (Savigny, 1822) — Haemadipsidae

जोंक — यह एक काली, लंबी, पतली, कीड़े जैसी दिखने वाली प्राचीन जीव है जो घास और पानी की नम जगह पर रहती है। इसके शरीर पर कोई पैर नहीं होते; बजाय इसके इसके आगे-पीछे दो चूसक (सक्शन कप) होते हैं जो गायों, भैंसों और कभी-कभी इंसानों की चमड़ी पर चिपक जाते हैं। जब यह चिपकती है, तो इसके मुँह से एक रसायन निकलता है जो खून को पतला करता है और दर्द नहीं करता, इसलिए जानवर को पता नहीं चलता कि यह खून चूस रहा है। गायों के लिए कोई बड़ी समस्या नहीं, पर अगर बहुत हो तो कमजोरी आ सकती है।

MovementCrawls by muscular contraction, moving from anterior sucker to posterior

Field tip: Search in moist grass and vegetation near cattle grazing areas, particularly during and after monsoon. A dark brown-black, slimy, worm-like creature with two suckers (one at each end) is a cattle leech. The posterior sucker is larger and more prominent than the anterior. Leeches move with a looping motion, pulling the anterior end forward and then contracting the body to bring the posterior sucker forward.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: काली, लंबी, पतली। दोनों सिरों पर गोल चूसक, पिछले वाला बड़ा। घास में रेंगती है। गायों पर चिपकी हो सकती है।

Body structure: The body consists of 34 segments (metameres), each marked by grooves on the surface. The integument (skin) is smooth and covered with mucus. The body wall consists of muscle layers arranged in circular and longitudinal directions.

Anterior sucker (oral disc): Small, located at the mouth; contains the pharynx and jaw-like structures.

Posterior sucker: Large, disc-shaped, used primarily for attachment and locomotion.

Internal anatomy: The leech possesses a crop (enlarged portion of the midgut) and a coelom (body cavity) filled with fluid. The nervous system consists of a ventral nerve cord with segmental ganglia. The circulatory system is composed of a dorsal and ventral blood vessel; the body cavity fluid supplements circulation.

Clitellum: A saddle-like band in the anterior region, more visible during the reproductive season, used for cocoon production.

Sexual organs: Hermaphroditic; each individual has both male and female reproductive organs.

हिंदी: 34 खंड, हर खंड पर रेखाएँ। मुँह पर छोटा चूसक, पीछे बड़ा। शरीर में खून-जैसा द्रव। नर और मादा दोनों अंग।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Haematophagous parasite. Hirudinaria granulosa is an obligate blood-feeder, subsisting entirely on blood. The leech is a terrestrial or semi-aquatic form, found in moist soil, grass, and vegetation. It is an ambush predator, remaining motionless on the ground or vegetation until a suitable host approaches. Upon host contact, the leech rapidly attaches by pressing its anterior sucker onto the skin, secretes anticoagulant substances, and begins feeding.

Feeding mechanism. The leech uses jaw-like structures or muscular pharynx to pierce the skin and initiate bleeding. Anticoagulant compounds (mostly histamine-like substances and prostaglandins) are secreted to prevent blood clotting. A single feeding can yield a blood meal of 5–10 ml for large leeches.

Host preference. Hirudinaria granulosa is a generalist, feeding on cattle, buffalo, deer, and humans. It shows no strong host specificity and will feed on whatever large animal is available. In agricultural settings, cattle and buffalo are the primary hosts.

Habitat and activity. The species is found in moist grasslands, areas of cattle grazing, and near water bodies. It is most active during the monsoon (June–October) and cool season (November–February), when soil moisture is high. During the dry season, leeches retreat to moist microhabitats beneath logs, in muddy banks, and in vegetation debris.

Reproduction and cocoons. After mating (which involves reciprocal sperm transfer, though simultaneous hermaphrodites), each individual produces a cocoon containing 15–30 eggs. The cocoon is buried in moist soil or leaf litter. Incubation takes 2–3 weeks, and hatchlings emerge as miniature leeches. Lifespan is 1–2 years in the field.

हिंदी: गायों पर चिपकता है, गर्म महीनों में। खून पीता है, दो हफ़्ते तक एक बार खाता है। प्रजनन में दोनों पति और पत्नी होते हैं।

Seasonal activity pattern in eastern UP:

  • June–October (monsoon): Peak activity; high populations in grasslands
  • November–February (cool season): Continued activity; reproduction
  • March–April (dry season): Activity declines; retreat to moist refugia
  • May–June (hot season): Minimal activity; populations low in exposed areas

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Blood diet exclusively:

  • Cattle and buffalo (primary hosts)
  • Wild herbivores and ungulates
  • Humans (opportunistically)
  • Any large mammal in the habitat

The leech feeds exclusively on blood and cannot digest plant material. After a large blood meal, the leech can survive for several months without feeding, as the blood meal is stored in the crop.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Ground-foraging birds (storks, cranes, herons) and other predatory birds consume leeches
  • Reptiles: Snakes and lizards feed on leeches
  • Mammals: Shrews, toads, and ground-dwelling carnivores prey on leeches
  • Parasites: Internal parasites (nematodes and trematodes) infect leeches
  • Pathogens: Bacterial and fungal infections in moist environments

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Parasite burden in livestock. Hirudinaria granulosa can become a significant pest in cattle and buffalo herds in regions with high populations. Heavy leech burdens result in: chronic blood loss and anaemia, reduced milk production in dairy cattle, weight loss in beef cattle, and skin infections (secondary bacterial infections at attachment sites).

Impact on animal health. While a single leech feeding causes minimal harm, multiple leeches feeding simultaneously (which is common in endemic areas) can cause significant blood loss. Studies document blood loss of 50–200 ml per animal per day during monsoon season in heavily infested herds. This leads to reduced fitness, compromised immune function, and increased susceptibility to other diseases.

Control measures. Traditional and modern control approaches include: pasture rotation to reduce leech encounters, hand-removal of leeches (checking animals daily), topical acaricides (pesticides effective against arachnids are less effective on leeches), salt sprays (salt irritates leeches), and herbal repellents (neem, turmeric). Prevention through habitat management (reducing standing water, avoiding very damp pastures during peak season) is most effective.

Harmlessness to humans in most contexts. While Hirudinaria granulosa will feed on humans opportunistically, the species is not medically important in the context of disease transmission. The bite site may become secondarily infected if scratched excessively.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia, from India through the Indo-Chinese region to Sri Lanka.

In India: Throughout the subcontinent, particularly in regions with high rainfall and cattle grazing.

In Uttar Pradesh: Found in all districts; more common in the eastern, less-arid regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Likely present in cattle grazing areas during monsoon and winter; expected to be common given the region's climate and cattle husbandry.

Field rule: A dark brown-black slimy worm 2–7 cm long with two suckers (small front, large back), found in moist grass near cattle, is Hirudinaria granulosa; microscopic examination of jaw structure confirms identification.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Savigny in 1822 in his work on Egyptian fauna. The genus Hirudinaria (from Latin hirudin, leech, with Latin suffix -aria) includes blood-feeding leeches of Asia.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies recognised; populations across Asia show minor variation.

Family and order placement. Order Hirudinida (leeches), family Haemadipsidae (land leeches), characterised by obligate blood-feeding and terrestrial habit.

Phylogenetic notes. Annelida (leeches, earthworms, polychaetes) is an ancient phylum with a fossil record extending to the Cambrian period. Leeches are derived from polychaete worms and evolved blood-feeding and anticoagulant secretion as key adaptations.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in suitable habitats; no conservation concern.
