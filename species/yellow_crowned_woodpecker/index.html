येलो-क्राउन्ड कठफोड़वा / Yellow-crowned Woodpecker

Leiopicus mahrattensis (Sykes, 1832) — Picidae

येलो-क्राउन्ड कठफोड़वा — यह छोटा कठफोड़वा पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के पेड़ों पर साल भर पाया जाता है। इसकी सबसे बड़ी पहचान इसकी पीली कलगी (yellow crest) है, जिसे नर पक्षी खड़ा कर सकते हैं। यह काले और सफ़ेद धारियों वाली छोटी चिड़िया है जो पेड़ों की छाल पर कीड़े निकालने के लिए धड़ाम-धड़ाम करती है।

Field tip: A small woodpecker with a distinctive golden or yellow crest (raised by males) and a yellow rump. Spends much time moving up tree trunks, probing for insects. The drumming sound helps locate it. Solitary or in pairs.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange

Size: 16–17 cm long; wingspan 28–32 cm. Weight 26–32 g.

Plumage (male): Crown and nape yellow or golden; erectile crest. Back black with white spots and barring. Rump golden-yellow (diagnostic). Upper tail coverts black. Tail black. Wings black with white wing coverts and spots. Lores and eyebrow stripe yellow or whitish. Cheeks white. Throat white. Breast white with black spotting and streaking. Belly white.

Plumage (female): Very similar to male but lacking the yellow crown crest; crown mostly black with yellow mottling.

Bill: Relatively long and pointed, grey.

Legs and feet: Short, grey.

Tail: Stiff and pointed, adapted for supporting the bird while climbing.

Vocalisations

  1. Drumming: A rapid series of taps on wood (rolling trill); does not occur as frequently as in larger woodpeckers but is a diagnostic sound.
  2. Call: Sharp pik or chick notes; sometimes in series.
  3. Song: Limited; mostly reliant on drumming and calls.
हिंदी: पेड़ों पर "धड़ाम-धड़ाम" बजाता है। "पिक-पिक" की तेज़ आवाज़। कम गाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Bark-gleaner: Forages on tree trunks and branches, moving methodically upward, probing crevices and peeling bark to extract insects.

Feeding: Primarily small insects and insect larvae hidden in bark and crevices. Beetles, ants, and spiders. Unlike larger woodpeckers, rarely excavates deep into wood.

Foraging technique: Moves vertically up the trunk using stiff tail for support. Frequently stops to peer into crevices. Picks insects from bark surface or from beneath loose bark.

Solitary: Usually solitary or in pairs; not gregarious.

Territorial: Resident and territorial; maintains year-round territory.

Tree preference: Favours old, dead or dying trees where bark is loose and insects abundant.

हिंदी: पेड़ों की छाल पर कीड़े निकालता है। धीरे-धीरे ऊपर चढ़ता है। पूरे दिन व्यस्त रहता है। अकेले या जोड़े में रहता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Year-round resident; breeds locally (January–April).

Breeding: Excavates own cavity in dead tree (unlike some woodpeckers that use existing cavities). Nest is a hole; lays 2–3 white eggs.

Clutch: 2–3 white eggs.

Incubation: 12–14 days; both parents incubate.

Fledging: Chicks fledge in approximately 18–20 days.

Moult: Complete annual moult after breeding.

हिंदी: साल भर यहाँ रहता है। जनवरी से अप्रैल तक प्रजनन करता है। पेड़ों में सुरंग बनाता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary food items:

  • Bark-boring insects and their larvae
  • Beetles and weevils
  • Ants
  • Spiders
  • Small caterpillars

Extracted from bark crevices and beneath loose bark; does not excavate as deeply as larger woodpeckers.

हिंदी: छाल में छिपे कीड़े खाता है। बीटल और चींटियाँ इसका भोजन हैं। गहराई में नहीं जाता।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Raptors: Shikras and small falcons may prey on woodpeckers
  • Snakes: May raid nests
  • Parasites: Ectoparasites and endoparasites typical of woodpeckers

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest control: By feeding on bark-boring insects and beetles, assists in natural control of wood-damaging pests in orchards and timber.

Cavity creation: By excavating nesting cavities, indirectly benefits other cavity-nesting species that reuse abandoned holes.

Damage potential: May occasionally peck at buildings or wooden structures when searching for insects, but damage is minimal.

Legal status: Protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972. Not hunted.

GBIF holds published records from these districts. Expected year-round in dry woodlands, gardens with mature trees, and scattered teak forest. More often heard (drumming and calls) than seen. Most active during early morning and late afternoon. Numbers depend on availability of old or dead trees that harbour insects. The first local record would be a photograph showing the yellow crest and golden rump, with place and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent; resident from the Himalayas to the peninsula.

In India: Throughout India at lower elevations; common in dry deciduous forests and cultivated areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident throughout the state; expected in suitable habitat.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Year-round resident in dry woodlands, scattered teak forest and gardens with mature trees.

Field rule: Yellow-crowned woodpecker is a small woodpecker with a distinctive golden crest (in males) and golden rump. If smaller and lacking the golden features, it may be a pygmy woodpecker. If larger with a grey head, it is a grey-headed woodpecker.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Sykes in 1832 in Proceedings of the Zoological Society of London as Picus mahrattensis. Type locality is the Deccan, India.

Subspecies. Monotypic; only subspecies is L. m. mahrattensis.

Family and order placement. Order Piciformes; family Picidae (woodpeckers). Genus Leiopicus contains small, bark-gleaning woodpeckers of Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to other Asian bark-gleaning woodpeckers in the genus Leiopicus.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Widespread and common resident throughout the Indian subcontinent in suitable dry deciduous forest and cultivated habitats.
