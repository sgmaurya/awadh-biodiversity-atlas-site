टेल्ड जे तितली / Tailed Jay

Graphium agamemnon (Linnaeus, 1758) — Papilionidae

टेल्ड जे तितली — एक बड़ी, सुंदर काली तितली जो हरे-सफेद रंग के धब्बों के साथ होती है। नाम "Tailed Jay" इसकी लंबी पूँछ से आया है जो दूसरी जयों से ज़्यादा स्पष्ट है। यह तितली निबू, संतरे और अदरक के पौधों पर पाई जाती है, विशेष रूप से गाँवों के बागों में। इसके कैटरपिलर सबसे ऊपरी तने को खाते हैं, जिससे पौधों की शाखाएँ कुछ ख़राब दिखती हैं, पर समग्र उत्पादन पर असर नहीं होता। यह एक शानदार तितली है जिसे देखना हर किसी को अच्छा लगता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Wing colorBlack or dark brown base color
White/pale patchesProminent white or greenish-white patches on wings, larger than in Graphium doson
ForewingLarge white or pale-green patch
HindwingWhite or pale patches; longer, more prominent tail than G. doson
Tail lengthNoticeably longer than G. doson; extends well beyond hindwing margin
BodyBlack thorax and abdomen with some pale scaling
SizeWingspan 65–95 mm; larger than G. doson

Field tip: Look in citrus gardens and Ruta patches. Males perch on leaves, often in groups. The larger size, longer tail, and larger white/pale patches distinguish it from the Common Jay (G. doson). Fast flight with frequent gliding.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बड़ी तितली, काली, बड़े सफेद-हरे धब्बे। पूँछ Common Jay से ज़्यादा लंबी। निबू, अदरक, नीबू के पौधों पर।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Perching and territorial. Males perch on plant leaves or stems for extended periods, occasionally flying out to intercept passing butterflies. They are territorial, chasing away intruders. Females lay eggs on young leaves of host plants. The butterfly is more visually prominent than G. doson, often attracting attention in gardens.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Ruta graveolens (Rue; called Adesso in Hindi) — primary host
  • Citrus species (lemon, orange, lime, grapefruit)
  • Other Rutaceae plants including Zanthoxylum and Murraya

Adult food:

  • Nectar from various flowers
  • Occasional mud-puddling

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Flycatchers, bee-eaters, and other insectivorous birds
  • Parasitoid wasps: Multiple species parasitize caterpillars and pupae
  • Spiders: Both web and hunting spiders
  • Ants: Attack pupae and early-instar caterpillars

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pollinator and minor defoliator. The Tailed Jay is an excellent pollinator of flowers in gardens and cultivated areas. Its caterpillars feed on Ruta and citrus plants; when populations are high, visible defoliation can occur on upper leaves and stems, but damage is rarely severe enough to impact yield. The species is generally considered beneficial as a pollinator.

हिंदी: फूलों को परागित करती है। निबू और अदरक के पौधों पर कैटरपिलर होते हैं, पर नुकसान आम तौर पर कम होता है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Expected in Basti and Ayodhya districts in citrus gardens, village home gardens with Ruta and spice plants, and open woodland edges. Most visible during monsoon and post-monsoon seasons (July–November). GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet confirmed from Basti–Ayodhya.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Tropical Asia from India through Southeast Asia to Indonesia and northern Australia.

In India: Found throughout the country from plains to 1,500 meters in the Himalayas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common resident in citrus- and Ruta-growing areas across all districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Found in village gardens and cultivated areas throughout the year; more abundant in monsoon and post-monsoon.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. What is the ratio of Tailed Jay to Common Jay butterflies in citrus gardens in your village? — Survey gardens; count and identify both species.
  2. On which host plants are caterpillars of the Tailed Jay most abundant? — Examine Ruta, citrus, and other Rutaceae; compare caterpillar densities.
  3. How do Tailed Jay males differ in territorial behavior from Common Jay males? — Observe and compare behavior in the field.
  4. What percentage of pupae are parasitized by wasps? — Collect pupae; rear indoors; record parasitoid emergence.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th ed., p. 471, from East Indian specimens.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies; G. a. agamemnon (nominotypical) occurs in South and Southeast Asia, including India.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Papilionidae (swallowtail butterflies).

Phylogenetic notes. Graphium agamemnon is closely related to Graphium doson; recent molecular work confirms their sister-species relationship. Both species are in the subgenus Graphium sensu stricto.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. A common, widespread species with no conservation concerns.
