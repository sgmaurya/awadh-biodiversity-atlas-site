गुलाब चश्म / Yellow-eyed Babbler

Chrysomma sinense (Gmelin, 1789) — Paradoxornithidae

गुलाब चश्म — मैदानी इलाकों की लंबी घास और रीड में रहने वाली बाबुल की तरह चिड़िया है। झुंडों में रहती है, जमीन के पास घूमती है, कीड़े और बीज खाती है। सर्दियों में इसे गाते हुए घास की चोटी पर देखा जा सकता है। इसकी ख़ूबसूरत पीली आँखें इसे पहचानने का आसान तरीका हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSparrow-sized body with a long tail; about 18 cm (Grimmett et al. 2011)
PlumagePlain warm brown above, rufous on the wings; white below with buff flanks; white lores and short white eyebrow
EyesYellow to orange-yellow iris inside a bright orange-red ring of bare skin
TailLong, graduated, dark brown; often cocked or waved as the bird clambers in grass
CallLoud whistled warble from a grass stem; short cheep and churring contact notes
BehaviourSmall parties clambering through tall grass, reeds and sugarcane; rarely far from cover

Field tip: A brown-and-white, long-tailed bird clambering through tall grass or sugarcane in a loose party, cocking its tail, is this. Close to, the yellow eye in its orange-red ring settles it; no other small grassland bird here has one. Often heard before it is seen: a loud, sweet whistled song from the top of a grass stem, most of all in the cool months.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: About 18 cm from bill to tail tip (Grimmett et al. 2011), of which the tail is roughly half; the body is sparrow-sized.

Plumage: Upperparts plain warm brown, unstreaked, with rufous-brown wings. Lores and a short eyebrow white, giving a bright-faced look. Chin, throat and breast white, shading to buff on the flanks and belly. Tail long, strongly graduated and dark brown, the outer feathers shorter than the central pair.

Bare parts: Iris yellow to orange-yellow; the skin around the eye bare and orange-red, which with the pale eye makes the "spectacled" face of the Hindi name (गुलाब चश्म, rose spectacles). Bill short, stout and black; legs and feet yellowish to orange-flesh.

Sexes alike. Juveniles are duller, with a browner eye and a paler eye-ring.

Vocalisations. A loud, sweet, whistled warble, usually given from an exposed grass stem or reed top with the tail hanging, carries across a field; the usual contact note in the flock is a short cheep and a low churring. Birds sing most in the cool season and at the start of the rains (Ali & Ripley 1973).

हिंदी: घास की चोटी से तेज़, मीठी सीटी जैसा गीत। झुंड में हल्की "चीप" की आवाज़।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A bird of tall grass: Lives in small parties that work through tall grass, reed beds, scrub and the edges of sugarcane, clambering up stems and dropping back into cover. It takes insects, spiders and small caterpillars from the stems and leaves, and grass seeds and nectar from flowering heads (Ali & Ripley 1973). It keeps low and flies weakly, with whirring wings and the long tail trailing, from one patch of cover to the next.

Song post: A singing bird climbs to the top of a grass stem or reed, sits in the open with its tail hanging, and sings loudly; this is the usual way the species is noticed.

Predators: The small hawks and falcons of open country, and snakes and crows at the nest.

Winter: Easier to see in the cool season, when parties gather in the grass left standing on bunds and along drains, and the males sing.

हिंदी: छोटे झुंडों में लंबी घास, नरकट और गन्ने में रहती है। कीड़े और बीज खाती है। सर्दियों में घास की चोटी से गाती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Year-round resident. Breeds chiefly in the monsoon, June to September in northern India (Ali & Ripley 1973). The nest is a deep cup of grass blades and fibre bound with cobweb, slung between upright grass or reed stems or in a low bush, a little above the ground; the eggs, usually three or four, are pinkish-white marked with red-brown (Ali & Ripley 1973). Both parents feed the young. Outside the breeding season the birds keep in small parties that shift about locally with the state of the grass and the cane harvest.

हिंदी: साल भर मैदान में रहता है। बारिश में संतान पैदा करता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds of prey: Shikra and other small hawks of open country
  • At the nest: Snakes such as the rat snake, and House Crow
  • Cane harvest: Cutting of sugarcane and burning of grass on bunds in the breeding season destroys nests
हिंदी: बाज़ और चील इसका शिकार करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest control: Beneficial species; feeds on grasshoppers, crickets and other agricultural pests.

Agricultural habitat: Uses sugarcane fields, particularly margins and reed borders, as feeding and breeding habitat. Presence indicates healthy grassland ecosystems.

Legal status: Like all wild birds in India it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

IUCN status: Least Concern (BirdLife International 2016), with a very large range; the loss of tall grassland to cultivation is the one pressure usually named.

हिंदी: कीटों को नष्ट करता है। आम तौर पर सुरक्षित है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent, from Himalayan foothills to the Deccan.

In India: Suitable grassland habitats throughout; increasingly localized due to grassland loss.

In Uttar Pradesh: Grassland and reed-bed habitats across the state, particularly in the plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident in grassland and reed-bed margins along river floodplains; expected in tall grassland and sugarcane field edges.

Field rule: A long-tailed brown-and-white bird clambering in tall grass, with a yellow eye in an orange-red ring and a stout black bill, is the yellow-eyed babbler. Prinias are smaller and slighter with thin bills; grassbirds are streaked; jungle babblers are big, dull and on the ground.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by J. F. Gmelin in 1789 as Parus sinensis, with the type locality given as China although the bird is not Chinese; it was later made the type of the genus Chrysomma Blyth, 1843.

Subspecies. Four are usually recognised (Rasmussen & Anderton 2005): C. s. hypoleucum across the Gangetic plain and the Nepal terai to Bangladesh, which is the form here; C. s. sinense in peninsular India; C. s. saturatius in Myanmar; C. s. nasale in Sri Lanka.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes. Long treated as a babbler (Timaliidae) and then as a sylviid warbler, Chrysomma now sits with the parrotbills in Paradoxornithidae on molecular evidence (Cai et al. 2019).

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern (BirdLife International 2016): a very large range and no evidence of a steep decline, though the loss of tall grassland to cultivation is named as a pressure.
