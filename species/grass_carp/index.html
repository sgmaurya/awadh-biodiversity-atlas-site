ग्रास कार्प / Grass Carp

Ctenopharyngodon idella (Valenciennes, 1844) — Cyprinidae

ग्रास कार्प — एशिया का बड़ी कार्प मछली, पानी के पौधे खाती है। भारत में मछली पालन के लिए लाई गई, पर अब तालाब और नहर में अपने आप भी दिखती है। तेज़ी से बढ़ती है और बहुत खा जाती है — इसी वजह से कुछ देशों में यह परेशानी बन गई। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में अभी तक आम नहीं पर जल संरक्षण योजनाओं में बढ़ रही है।

Field tip: A large, plain green cyprinid with no barbels and a terminal mouth is likely a grass carp. Distinguished from common carp (Cyprinus carpio) by the lack of barbels. The body is more slender and uniformly darker than silver carp. In eastern UP ponds, any very large grazing fish eating water plants is probably this species.

Morphology आकारिकी

Body: Elongate, slightly laterally compressed. Colour olive to dark green above, silvery-white on belly; coloration intensifies with age. Body depth at dorsal origin about one-third of standard length.

Head: Large, flattened above; mouth terminal, large, reaching below eye; teeth pharyngeal (throat teeth), adapted for grinding plant material. No barbels on mouth.

Fins: Dorsal fin with 7 rays, originating above pelvic fin insertion; anal fin with 8 rays; pectoral fins large; tail moderately forked.

Scales: Moderate-sized, cycloid, with 41–43 scales in lateral line.

हिंदी: बड़ी, हरी मछली। दाँत गले में; दाढ़ी नहीं। पंखे बड़े, पूँछ कांटी हुई।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Macrophyte grazer: Grass carp is an obligate herbivore, feeding almost exclusively on aquatic and terrestrial plants. Adults consume up to 40% of their body weight daily in vegetation — algae, water lilies, hydrilla, potamogeton, grasses, and even emergent vegetation. This feeding habit has made it valuable for aquaculture (weed control) but a major invasive threat in natural systems where it can denude waterbodies of all vegetation.

Growth rate: Exceptionally fast under good conditions; reaches market size (1.5–2 kg) in 2–3 years in ponds with supplementary feeding. Growth is limited only by water temperature, food availability, and stocking density.

Reproductive behaviour: Requires flowing water or large reservoirs for successful breeding; rarely spawns in static ponds. Sexual maturity reached at 4–5 years. Eggs are pelagic (floating); larvae drift with current.

Stress tolerance: Tolerates poor water quality, low oxygen, and wide temperature range. Survives in stagnant, heavily silted ponds where other fish fail.

हिंदी: पानी की पूरी घास खा जाती है। बहुत तेजी से बढ़ती है। ठंडे पानी को भी सहन करती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Aquatic plants: water lilies (Nymphaea), hydrilla, potamogeton, naiad
  • Algae: filamentous algae, blanket weed
  • Terrestrial plants: grasses at water margins, fallen leaves
  • Zooplankton (juveniles): daphnia, copepods, ostracods
  • Detritus and silt

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Large fish: Large catfish, snakeheads when young
  • Birds: Herons, cormorants (on juveniles)
  • Humans: Fished for food; harvested from culture systems
  • Parasites: Ichthyophthirius, dactylogyrid gill flukes

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Aquaculture staple: Grass carp is widely cultivated in composite fish culture across India, introduced from China in the 1970s. In eastern UP, it is reared in ponds with common carp, silver carp and catfish. Valued for:

  • Rapid growth and high survival
  • Biological weed control (reduces chemical herbicide use)
  • Good feed conversion efficiency
  • Meat quality

Invasive impact (documented globally but limited locally): In temperate countries (North America, parts of Europe), escaped grass carp have become established in natural waterbodies, causing catastrophic devegetation of lakes and rivers. The risk exists in India as well, particularly with climate change expanding its range. In Uttar Pradesh, documented escapes from aquaculture into river systems are increasing.

Management concern: Release into natural waterbodies is prohibited in several countries. Farmers in eastern UP should use sterile (triploid) grass carp to prevent accidental establishment in connected natural waters.

Grass carp is to be expected in larger fish-culture ponds and tanks in Basti and Ayodhya districts where composite fish farming is practised, particularly in government fisheries projects and progressive farmer ponds. The species is not yet naturalized in wild river systems of the region, but escapees from aquaculture increase the risk. A confirmed local record would be documentation of fish in natural waterbodies connected to agricultural runoff, with photographs and place/date information.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to China and eastern Siberia; now widely introduced to temperate and tropical aquaculture regions worldwide.

In India: Introduced in the 1970s; now cultured in most states with significant aquaculture activity, particularly in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, West Bengal and Uttar Pradesh.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultured in ponds throughout the state; most common in Bundelkhand and eastern districts; naturalized populations rare but expanding.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in government and private fish ponds where composite culture is practised; not yet naturalized in open river systems.

Field rule: A large green cyprinid with a terminal mouth, no barbels, and eating water plants is grass carp. The absence of barbels (present in common carp) and the large size distinguish it from other carps in eastern UP ponds.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Ctenopharyngodon idella was first described by Achille Valenciennes in 1844 in Histoire Naturelle des Poissons. The type locality is the Amur River, Russian Far East. The genus name Ctenopharyngodon means "comb-tooth eater", referring to the pharyngeal teeth; the species epithet idella is of uncertain etymology but possibly refers to the Idel River (Volga region).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Cyprinidae (carp family), order Cypriniformes. The Cyprinidae is the largest family of freshwater fish, distributed across the Northern Hemisphere and Africa.

Phylogenetic notes. Grass carp is monotypic within the genus Ctenopharyngodon, distinct from other cyprinids by its obligate herbivory and derived pharyngeal dentition.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. The species is extensively cultured and its wild populations in China are stable.
