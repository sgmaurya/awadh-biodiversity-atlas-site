नीला केंचुआ / Indian Blue Worm

Perionyx excavatus (Perrier, 1872) — Megascolecidae

नीला केंचुआ — पूर्वी UP के गीले जंगल के फर्श पर सड़ी पत्तियों में रहने वाला केंचुआ, जो नीले-बैंगनी रंग की चमक के लिए जाना जाता है। यह लाल केंचुए की तरह खेतों में नहीं, बल्कि पानी-भरी जगहों पर रहता है। बरसात में खेतों की गीली मेड़ों पर और तालाब के किनारे सड़ी पत्तियों के ढेर में मिलता है। बहुत तेज़ दौड़ने वाला केंचुआ है — छुओ तो तुरंत भाग जाता है। जब मिट्टी में जैविक कचरा बहुत अधिक है, तब यह अपने आप आ जाता है।

Field tip: In a wet compost pile or monsoon-damp leaf litter heap, a small blue-violet worm that runs extremely fast when exposed is the Indian blue worm. The blue iridescence is unmistakable. It is far more active and nervous than the red worm, and cannot tolerate drying.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: गीली जगह पर, सड़ी पत्तियों में, एक नीले-बैंगनी रंग का छोटा केंचुआ जो तेज़ दौड़ता है। छुओ तो भाग जाए। लाल केंचुए से छोटा, और नीला।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (specimens from wet compost and forest litter in eastern UP):

MeasurementRange
Body length80–150 mm
Weight0.15–0.35 g

Colour and iridescence: The dorsal (back) surface is blue to violet, with a distinctive iridescent (metallic, shifting) sheen. The intensity of the blue varies depending on light angle and soil moisture — appears brightest in natural sunlight. The ventral (belly) surface is paler, greyish or pinkish-brown. The iridescence is a structural property of the cuticle (outer skin), not a pigment.

Body segments: 80–150 segments; the body is cylindrical, slightly tapered anteriorly (towards the head) and posteriorly (towards the tail). No visible rings like some larger earthworms.

Clitellum: The reproductive saddle is located around segments 9–11 (very anterior compared to other earthworms). It is saddle-shaped, light brown, and less prominent than in Eisenia fetida.

Setae: Eight small setae per segment, arranged in pairs on the ventral surface; used for locomotion and grip on substrate.

हिंदी: नीले रंग की चमक वास्तव में त्वचा की संरचना से आती है, रंग नहीं। सूख जाए तो चमक चली जाती है। छोटा, पतला, और बहुत तेज़ दौड़ने वाला।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Epigeic litter-dweller: Perionyx excavatus is an epigeic (surface-dwelling) earthworm of high-moisture, high-organic-matter habitats. Unlike Eisenia fetida (which tolerates manure beds) or native endogeic species (which burrow deep), Perionyx is strictly confined to wet leaf litter and flooded organic matter.

Ecological niche: Found naturally in:

  • Forest floor leaf litter and humus in the Basti region (especially sal and mixed deciduous forests)
  • Swamp margins and monsoon-flooded grasslands
  • Banks of monsoon-swollen streams and drainage channels
  • Permanently wet, high-organic-content habitats

Reproduction: Sexual; cross-fertilizing. Cocoons are laid in moist leaf litter. Less prolific than Eisenia fetida, with lower reproductive rates (typical in tropical native species).

Moisture dependence: Extremely sensitive to drying. A worm left on dry soil for more than a few hours will desiccate and die. This limits it to permanently wet microhabitats.

Movement: Exceptionally fast and jerky; when exposed or threatened, it thrashes violently and runs at high speed — visible even to the naked eye. This behaviour may be an escape strategy in flood-prone habitats (move quickly to higher ground when water levels change).

Predators: Frogs, toads, aquatic birds, and aquatic predatory insects consume them readily in swamp and monsoon habitats.

हिंदी: बहुत गीला इलाका चाहिए। सूख जाए तो मर जाता है। बहुत तेज़ दौड़ता है — सोचा जा सकता है कि वह बाढ़ से भागने की कोशिश कर रहा है। जंगल की गीली पत्तियों में अपने आप रहता है।

Peak activity: May–October in eastern UP (monsoon onset to post-monsoon). Population abundances are highest in August–September when forests and wetlands are flooded. Declines in winter dry season (November–February), though not eliminated.

हिंदी: मानसून में सबसे ज़्यादा संख्या। सर्दी में कम, पर पूरी तरह नहीं।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Decomposer, not predator:

Perionyx excavatus feeds on dead organic matter only — it is a detritivore.

  • Primary diet: Partially decomposed leaves, humus, fine root fragments
  • Secondary diet: Fungal biomass and bacterial biofilms on decomposing matter
  • Microbial consumption: Like most earthworms, it ingests significant quantities of soil microbes, which are digested and contribute to nutrient cycling

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Amphibians: Frogs and toads (genus Bufo, Fejervarya, Polypedates) actively forage in wet leaf litter and swamp margins; eat Perionyx readily.
  • Aquatic and semi-aquatic birds: Herons, egrets, pond herons, and waders feed at swamp margins.
  • Aquatic insects: Large predatory aquatic insect larvae (dragonfly nymphs, water beetles) prey on earthworms in flooded substrates.
  • Snakes: Semi-aquatic snakes may consume them opportunistically.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Not a domesticated species: Unlike Eisenia fetida, Perionyx excavatus cannot be reliably cultivated in controlled vermicompost beds — it escapes or dies if conditions are not perfectly wet and high in organic matter. It is not recommended for commercial vermicomposting.

Ecosystem indicator: Its presence in high numbers indicates:

  • High soil moisture and humidity
  • Rich organic matter accumulation
  • Healthy litter layer (sign of forest health or well-maintained wetland)

Compost potential (limited): If a gardener has a very wet, high-moisture compost pile (e.g., in a monsoon region kept deliberately flooded), Perionyx may colonize naturally and contribute to decomposition. However, it is less efficient at straw and woody matter than Eisenia fetida.

Traditional knowledge: In some villages of the Gangetic plain, the presence of blue worms in wet fields or near ponds is noted as a sign of good soil biology; they are not intentionally collected or farmed.

Seasonal appearance: After the monsoon begins (June–July), farmers working in wet fields notice a sudden abundance of these worms in the top soil and on plants touching wet ground. This is interpreted as a sign of adequate soil moisture and decomposition.

हिंदी: खेत को भर पानी देने के बाद, नीले केंचुए अपने आप दिखने लगते हैं। यह अच्छी निशानी है कि मिट्टी जीवंत है। पर इसे उगाया नहीं जा सकता — गीला चाहिए, सर्दी भी ठीक न हो तो यह मर जाता है।

Perionyx excavatus is expected in:

  • Swamp margins and permanent grasslands of the Gandak and Ghaghra river systems
  • Sal forest litter and mixed deciduous forest floors of Basti district
  • Monsoon-flooded drainage channels and irrigation canal margins
  • Wet leaf-litter piles and compost heaps in gardens with permanent moisture

Peak visibility: July–September (monsoon and post-monsoon). Farmers and wetland managers in Basti district may notice sudden increases in blue worms in wet fields or forest edges after heavy rainfall.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to South and Southeast Asia; found from India through Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. Widespread in tropical and subtropical regions with significant monsoon rainfall.

In India: Distributed throughout tropical and subtropical regions with high rainfall — Western Ghats, Northeast India, Gangetic flood plains, and coastal areas. Most abundant in states with forest cover (Karnataka, Odisha, West Bengal, Assam, Meghalaya).

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in eastern districts with high rainfall and forest cover; less common in the drier western districts. In Basti district, present in the sal forests and wetland areas of the eastern part.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in forests and monsoon-wet habitats; abundance increases after the monsoon begins.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Perionyx excavatus was described by Édouard Perrier in 1872 as Perichaeta excavata in Nouvelles observations sur les Lombriciens (Nouvelles Archives du Muséum d'Histoire Naturelle, Paris). It was later placed in the genus Perionyx by Michaelsen. The species epithet excavatus (Latin: "dug out, hollowed") refers to its habit of living in wet, excavated leaf-litter substrates.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognized.

Family and order placement. Order Crassiclitellata, family Megascolecidae (the large earthworm family of the Old World tropics, including Indian native species Metaphire, Octochaetus, and Drawida). The genus Perionyx contains approximately 20 species across tropical Asia, all epigeic and moisture-dependent.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies place Perionyx as a derived genus within Megascolecidae. Its morphological and ecological specialization for wet habitats distinguishes it from the more cosmopolitan Eisenia (Lumbricidae). The blue iridescence is shared with other tropical Megascolecidae species, suggesting a functional or sexual-signalling role in humid tropical environments.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a widespread species in undisturbed forest and wetland habitats across tropical Asia, it faces no conservation concern. Habitat loss (wetland drainage, forest clearance) could reduce its abundance locally.
