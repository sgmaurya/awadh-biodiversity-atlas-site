गेहूँ का झुलसा / Spot Blotch of Wheat

Bipolaris sorokiniana (Sacc.) Shoemaker — Pleosporaceae

गेहूँ का झुलसा — गेहूँ की सबसे आम और हानिकारक बीमारी, जो पत्तियों, तनों, और दानों पर धब्बे बनाती है। सर्दियों में और नमी में यह बहुत तेजी से फैलता है। संक्रमित गेहूँ का दाना काला हो जाता है, और अनाज की गुणवत्ता खराब हो जाती है। बीज से भी यह रोग पैदा होता है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में हर साल का संकट। अच्छे बीज, प्रतिरोधी किस्में, और खेत की सफाई से इसे नियंत्रित कर सकते हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Leaf spotBrown, oval to elongate lesions 2–4 mm, with a darker border and a light (tan) centre; often with concentric rings
Spread patternSpreads from lower to upper leaves as the crop grows; often starts at seedling stage
Sheath blightLesions on leaf sheaths and stems; may girdle the stem, causing death of upper parts
Grain discolourationInfected grains appear dark brown or black (smutty appearance)
Lesion odourInfected tissue may have a characteristic fungal smell
TimingMost visible November–February in eastern UP; during the cool, wet phase of crop growth

Field tip: Spot Blotch is easily recognized by the brown, oval spots with darker borders and light centers on wheat leaves. Early infection on seedlings can kill the plant; later infection reduces grain quality. The disease is most severe in cool, moist winters. Cut several leaves from different plants in a suspect field; observe the spots.

Morphology and Symptomatology आकृति और लक्षण

Fungal structures (visible with microscope):

StructureAppearance
ConidiophoreDark brown, unbranched or branched; emerges from leaf surface
Conidium (spore)Distinctive: large, obclavate (club-shaped), multi-celled (3–8 cells), brown, 40–100 × 15–20 µm
PseudotheciaSmall, dark, dot-like; may be visible on heavily infected tissue
LesionBrown, with darker border and light centre; may show concentric rings

Disease progression:

  1. Seedling blight: Infection at soil level or on young leaves; seedlings may die.
  2. Leaf spot (flag leaf stage): Lesions develop on lower leaves first; spread upward as the crop grows.
  3. Grain discolouration: Fungus may colonize developing grains; seeds appear dark or smutty.
  4. Timing in eastern UP: Disease is slowest in October (autumn); accelerates in November–December (cool, moist); peaks January–February; declines in March as temperatures rise.

Ecology and Substrate पारिस्थितिकी और आधार

Obligate plant pathogen: Bipolaris sorokiniana is a plant pathogen requiring living wheat or cereal tissue.

Moisture and temperature requirements:

  • Optimal infection: 15–25°C; high humidity; cool conditions
  • Rapid spread: Most rapid at 18–22°C with wet foliage
  • Stress to fungus: >28°C slows growth; dry conditions inhibit spore production
  • Spore release: Requires free moisture (dew, rain); spores are splash-dispersed

Host range: Primarily wheat and barley; can infect other cereals and grasses but is most damaging to wheat.

Transmission:

  • Seed-borne (major source): Infected seed carries spores on the surface and internally
  • Air-borne (secondary): Spores are wind- and splash-dispersed during the growing season
  • Soil-borne: Spores survive in crop residue and soil; contribute to inoculum in subsequent crops

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Economic impact:

  • Yield loss: can be heavy in a bad season, depending on variety, timing and weather.
  • Quality loss: Infected grains are discoloured (black or dark) and reduced in value
  • India context: Spot Blotch causes annual losses in wheat crops across northern India. In eastern UP the losses are particularly high due to cool, moist winters.

Management — in order of priority:

  1. Disease-free seed (MOST EFFECTIVE):
  • Certified seed source: Use seed from official seed agencies or certified producers
  • Seed treatment: Hot-water treatment (52°C for 30 minutes) or dry heat (70°C for 10 minutes) can reduce seed-borne inoculum
  • Seed inspection: Visual inspection; discard dark or discoloured seeds
  • Cost: Certified seed costs ₹1500–2000 per quintal (vs. ₹500–800 for farmer-saved seed); investment justified by reduced disease
  1. Resistant varieties (HIGHLY EFFECTIVE):
  • Recommended in Uttar Pradesh: DBW 187, PBW 621, HD 3086, RAJ 4015 (all have high resistance)
  • Avoid susceptible varieties: PBW 373, HD 2967 (high susceptibility)
  • Varietal rotation every 5–7 years to prevent pathogen adaptation
  1. Cultural practices:
  • Field sanitation: Remove crop residue from previous wheat crop; burn or bury; do not leave on field
  • Crop rotation: Avoid planting wheat on fields with heavy residue from previous wheat crop
  • Spacing: Adequate spacing and pruning of lower leaves reduces humidity within canopy
  • Irrigation: Avoid overhead irrigation; water at soil level to keep foliage dry
  1. Fungicide treatment (supplementary):
  • Seed treatment: Carbendazim or Trichoderma applied to seed before planting
  • Foliar spray: Mancozeb or Propiconazole sprayed at early tillering and flag-leaf emergence (two sprays total)
  • Timing: First spray when disease is first observed; second spray 10–14 days later
  • Note: Fungicides are cost-effective only for high-value wheat (bread wheat, durum)
  1. Integrated approach for farmers:
  • Primary control: Use certified, disease-free seed of resistant variety
  • Secondary control: Treat seed with hot water or fungicide
  • Ongoing: Remove crop residue; maintain field sanitation; use resistant varieties
  • Monitoring: Scout the field from November onward; start fungicide spray if disease is observed on lower leaves

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Spot Blotch is expected in every wheat field in Basti and Ayodhya districts. Disease incidence varies with:

  • Seed source: Infected seed = high early disease; disease-free seed = minimal or delayed disease
  • Variety grown: Susceptible varieties (PBW 373) show heavy damage; resistant varieties (DBW 187) show much less
  • Weather: Cool, wet winters (November–February) favor disease; warm, dry winters suppress it

In most years, disease severity is moderate to high in susceptible varieties. Farmers using disease-free seed of resistant varieties experience minimal losses.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Found in all temperate and subtropical wheat-growing regions worldwide; particularly damaging in South Asia.

In India: Present in all wheat-growing states; endemic in northern and central India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in all wheat-growing districts; eastern UP experiences high disease pressure due to cool winters.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all wheat fields; disease severity depends on variety and seed source.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Originally described as Helminthosporium sorokiniana by Sacc. (1883). Later moved to Bipolaris by Shoemaker (1959). The species epithet sorokiniana honours the Russian mycologist Sorokina.

Subspecies. No subspecies are formally recognized.

Family and order placement. Order Pleosporales, family Pleosporaceae. Bipolaris is a genus of plant pathogens; related species affect maize, sorghum, and other crops.

Phylogenetic notes. Bipolaris sorokiniana is closely related to B. oryzae (rice blast) and other cereal pathogens.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As an obligate plant pathogen, conservation status is not applicable.
