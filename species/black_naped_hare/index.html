खरगोश / Black-naped Hare

Lepus nigricollis F. Cuvier, 1823 — Leporidae

खरगोश — उत्तर प्रदेश के कृषि क्षेत्रों, घास के मैदानों और परती भूमि (fallow land) में पाया जाने वाला एक अत्यंत सामान्य और जाना-माना देशी शाकाहारी स्तनधारी जीव है। इसे इसकी गर्दन के पीछे (नाप) पर बने काले रंग के धब्बे (black nape patch) और लंबे कानों से आसानी से पहचाना जाता है। यह वन प्रणालियों और ग्रामीण खेतों दोनों में एक महत्वपूर्ण प्राथमिक उपभोक्ता (primary consumer) है और पारिस्थितिकी तंत्र में बाज, सियार (jackal), जंगली बिल्ली (jungle cat) और कोबरा जैसे शिकारियों के लिए मुख्य आहार (prey) का काम करता है। यह संकट के समय अपने पैर को जमीन पर जोर से पटक कर (foot thumping) चेतावनी देता है और पकड़े जाने पर एक बहुत ही तेज, उच्च आवृत्ति वाली चीख (distress scream) निकालता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size & ShapeMedium-sized mammal; body length 40–60 cm, weight 1.5–3.5 kg; long hind legs adapted for fast leaping
ColourRufous-brown fur mixed with black on the back, white underparts; a distinct dark black patch on the back of the neck (nape)
Head & EarsLarge head with long, erect ears (up to 10 cm) tipped with black; large, dark brown eyes situated laterally
TailShort, bushy tail, rufous-brown above and white below, held down when running
LimbsHind limbs are significantly longer and stronger than forelimbs, allowing high-speed bounds

Field tip: Look for a rabbit-like animal with grizzled brown fur, long ears, and a prominent black patch on the nape of its neck. Unlike domestic rabbits, it has longer hind legs and a wild, alert behavior.

Morphology आकारिकी

Body: Compact, muscular body with a highly flexible spine. The fur (pelage) is coarse, grizzled rufous-brown on the upper parts, intermixed with black hairs. The belly and chin are white. A very prominent black or dark-brown patch covers the back of the neck (nape), extending from the base of the ears to the shoulders.

Limbs & Senses: The hind limbs are exceptionally long and powerful, enabling the hare to cover great distances in single bounds. Ears are large, mobile, and vascularized, assisting in thermoregulation. Eyes are large and set on the sides of the head, providing nearly 360-degree vision.

Vocalisations

Hares are typically silent to avoid detection, but possess a distinct and dramatic vocal repertoire in specific situations.

  • Distress scream: When caught or severely threatened by a predator, it produces a very loud, high-pitched, piercing scream ranging between 1.5–3.5 kHz. This sound can carry over long distances and may startle the predator or warn other nearby individuals.
  • Alarm signals: While not a vocal sound, the hare uses foot thumping (striking its strong hind feet against the ground) to make a low-frequency thump that warns conspecifics of approaching danger.
  • Chattering: Occasional low grunts or tooth chattering during aggressive interactions or mating chases.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Lepus nigricollis is predominantly crepuscular and nocturnal, feeding from dusk until dawn and resting in dense cover during the day. Rather than digging underground burrows, it rests in shallow depressions in grass or soil called "forms." When flushed, it relies on its camouflage until the last moment, then runs at speeds up to 50–60 km/h in a characteristic zig-zag pattern to evade capture.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Breeding: Breeds throughout the year, with peak reproductive activity during the monsoon season.
  • Reproduction: Courtship involves elaborate chases and "boxing" matches where individuals stand on hind legs and strike each other with forepaws.
  • Gestation & Young: Gestation lasts 41–43 days. The female gives birth to a litter of 1–4 leverets. Unlike rabbits, leverets are precocial — born fully furred, with open eyes, and able to move shortly after birth.
  • Lifespan: Typically 3–5 years in the wild, limited by high predation rates.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary food items:

  • Grasses: Actively grazes on various native grasses, such as Cynodon dactylon (Doob grass).
  • Crops: Feeds on young shoots of agricultural crops, particularly wheat and mustard in winter.
  • Herbs & Seeds: Consumes tender agricultural weeds, wild flowers, and fallen seeds.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Mammals: A crucial prey species for the Golden Jackal ([MAM-0005 Golden Jackal]) and Jungle Cat ([MAM-0003 Jungle Cat]). Feral dogs also hunt them.
  • Reptiles: Large reptiles like the Indian Cobra ([REP-0005 Indian Cobra]) prey on young leverets.
  • Birds: Large raptors (hawks, eagles) and large owls (such as [BRD-0019 Spotted Owlet] for juveniles).

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Crop Damage. During the winter sowing season, small groups of hares may forage on young wheat and mustard shoots, causing minor localized crop damage. They are also occasionally hunted by local communities for meat, though this is restricted under wildlife protection laws.

A very familiar animal to farmers across Basti district, regularly seen darting across village dirt roads or foraging along the margins of sugarcane and mustard fields at dawn and dusk. Known locally as Khargosh or Khara.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Indian subcontinent (India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka) and introduced to islands like Java, Mauritius, and Reunion.

In India: Widespread throughout the country, excluding the high-altitude Himalayan ranges.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common statewide in scrub forests, grasslands, and agricultural fringes.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Extremely common across rural agricultural areas, orchards, and riverbank scrublands.

Field rule: Long ears, grizzled rufous-brown coat, long hind legs, and a distinctive black patch on the back of the neck distinguish the Black-naped Hare from other lagomorphs.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Frédéric Cuvier described the species as Lepus nigricollis in 1823 in the Dictionnaire des Sciences Naturelles (Volume 26, page 307). Type locality: Malabar coast, India. The species name nigricollis is derived from the Latin niger (black) and collum (neck), referring to the black nape.

Subspecies. In Uttar Pradesh, the recognized subspecies is the Rufous-tailed Hare, Lepus nigricollis ruficaudatus Geoffroy, 1834, characterised by its rufous-grey pelage and reddish tail.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Lagomorpha, family Leporidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Lepus nigricollis is closely related to the Cape Hare (Lepus capensis) and belongs to the subgenus Lepus.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC). Very common, highly adaptable to human-dominated agricultural landscapes, and possesses a high reproductive rate, making it resilient to moderate hunting pressure.
