व्हाइट-टेल्ड स्टोनचैट / White-tailed Stonechat

Saxicola leucurus (Blyth, 1844) — Muscicapidae

व्हाइट-टेल्ड स्टोनचैट — खुले मैदानों में रहने वाली एक छोटी पक्षी जो साल भर पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में पाई जाती है। नर का सिर काला, पीठ काली, और पूंछ सफ़ेद होती है। यह पत्थरों और झाड़ियों पर बैठती है और कीड़ों का शिकार करती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall; 12–13 cm long
Male plumageBlack head, back and throat; white rump and tail
Female plumageBrown, more uniform; white or pale tail
PostureUpright; often perches on low bushes and grass stems
BehaviourFlycatches from perch; tail frequently cocked
EyeDark and alert
FlightShort, fluttering; displays white tail

Field tip: A small, upright stonechat of open grasslands. Males are distinctive with black head and back contrasting with white rump and tail. Females are brown overall. Often perches conspicuously on grass stems, low bushes, and rocky outcrops. Typical stonechat behaviour of sallying out to catch flying insects.

Size: 12–13 cm long; wingspan 17–21 cm.

Plumage (male): Head, nape, back, and throat black. Rump white or creamy, contrasting with black back. Tail white with black central feathers (creating a white-framed effect). Wings black with white spots on coverts. Underparts buff or creamy.

Plumage (female): Head, nape and back brownish-black or greyish. Rump white or creamy. Tail pale buff or creamy-white. Wings darker brown. Underparts buff-white. Generally duller than male.

Eyes: Dark brown; small and alert.

Bill: Short, slender, slightly pointed; dark. Adapted for gleaning insects.

Legs: Dark grey or black.

Sexes: Clearly dimorphic. Males much darker (almost black-and-white); females browner and duller.

Juveniles: Similar to females but with spotting on breast.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A short, warbled series of notes, given from exposed perch during breeding season.
  2. Contact call: A soft chak or tac-tac, characteristic clicking call.
  3. Alarm call: A sharp whit-whit-whit when disturbed.
हिंदी: नरम कर्कश आवाज़। "टैक टैक" की कॉल सबसे ज्यादा सुनाई पड़ती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Foraging: White-tailed Stonechats hunt from perches, sallying out to catch flying insects. Primary prey:

  • Small flies and gnats
  • Small moths
  • Grasshoppers and crickets
  • Spiders

Feeding technique: Perch-and-pounce predator; watches from elevated position and darts out to catch prey in air or on ground.

Social behaviour: Territorial; pairs defend territories year-round. Males sing to defend territory and attract mates.

Habitat preference: Open grasslands, scrubland, cultivated fields with short vegetation. Avoid dense forest; need clear perches and open hunting space.

Activity: Primarily diurnal; most active in early morning.

Predators: Shrikes, smaller raptors, snakes.

हिंदी: अपना इलाका बचाती है। झाड़ियों से कीड़ों को हवा में पकड़ती है। पूरे साल यहीं रहती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Year-round resident: Present throughout the year in eastern UP.

Breeding season: March–June. Males sing during breeding season to defend territory.

Nest: Open cup nest built low in grass or shrub.

Clutch: 4–5 eggs per clutch; 2 broods per breeding season.

Incubation and fledging: Incubation period approximately 14–15 days; fledging period 14–16 days.

Moult: Prebasic moult July–September.

हिंदी: साल भर यहीं रहता है। मार्च में प्रजनन शुरू करता है। घास में घोंसला बनाता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shrikes, small raptors.
  • Reptilian predators: Snakes.
  • Mammalian predators: Cats, foxes (occasionally).

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Pest control: Insectivorous bird that helps control insect populations in grasslands and agricultural areas.

Peak visibility: March–June (breeding season) when males are more vocal and conspicuous.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: North Africa, Middle East, and Indian subcontinent; resident in most of range.

In India: Occurs across the Indian subcontinent, particularly in open grasslands and scrubland.

In Uttar Pradesh: Occurs throughout the state as a year-round resident.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident throughout the region in suitable habitat.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Edward Blyth in 1844 from Indian specimens.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognized across the range; S. l. leucurus occurs in the Indian subcontinent.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae (true flycatchers and Old World chats). The genus Saxicola comprises the stonechats and wheatears.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern (LC). Widely distributed and populations stable.
