टुइयाँ तोता / Plum-headed Parakeet

Psittacula cyanocephala (Linnaeus, 1766) — Psittacidae

टुइयाँ तोता — बस्ती-अयोध्या क्षेत्र में साल भर रहने वाला एक छोटा, सुंदर तोता, जिसका नर पक्षी का सिर नीले-बैंगनी रंग का होता है (जिससे 'plum-headed' नाम मिला), और शरीर हरा होता है। इसका मादा पक्षी नीले रंग की जगह पीला सिर रखता है। यह तोता अपनी तेज़ आवाज़ के लिए प्रसिद्ध है और फलों, बीजों और फूलों पर भोजन करता है। ये झुंड में रहते हैं, अक्सर 8-10 या अधिक पक्षियों के समूह में देखे जाते हैं। यह पक्षी मंदिरों, बगीचों और पेड़ों के कोटर में घोंसला बनाता है।

Field tip: A small green parakeet with a distinctly blue-headed male (unmistakable) and yellow-headed female. Easily identified by loud screeching calls and flock behavior. Found in orchards and wooded areas around villages.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length330 mm
Wing160–180 mm
Tail120–150 mm
Bill18–22 mm

Plumage (male): Head and upper neck covered in deep plum or violet-blue, extending from the crown to the nape. The blue is often mixed with purple or pink tones. Back, rump and wings bright grass-green. Tail long and graduated, mostly green with blue-green tips. Underparts paler green, becoming pale blue-green on breast. Wing feathers (flight feathers) dark green with pale edges.

Plumage (female): Entirely green except for the head and upper neck, which are pale yellow, golden-yellow, or greenish-yellow. Some females show faint plum coloration on the head. Otherwise morphologically similar to male.

Bill: Upper mandible red to coral-red; lower mandible paler, yellowish. The bill is strongly curved and powerful for seed-cracking.

Legs and feet: Pale grey or greenish-grey.

Eyes: Pale yellow-white with dark pupil; surrounded by narrow bare eye-ring.

Sexes: Highly dimorphic in head colour, making males and females easy to distinguish.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact call: A loud, distinctive "tee-tee-tee-tee" or "kit-kit-kit-kit", given in flight and at rest.
  2. Alarm call: Harsh screams and chattering; given when disturbed or in response to predators.
  3. Flight calls: Constant chatter while flying, especially when flocking.
हिंदी: तेज़ "टीक" आवाई, फ्लाइट में लगातार चहचहाहट। झुंड में शोरगुल।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Gregarious forager: Plum-headed Parakeets are highly social birds, moving in flocks of 8–15 or more. Flocks are noisy and conspicuous, calling constantly in flight and at rest. They feed while clinging to branches and twigs, using their strong bills to crack open seeds.

Feeding behaviour: Forage in fruit-bearing trees and vegetable crops. They hang upside-down like other parakeets when feeding, using their feet to manipulate food. Feed primarily in early morning and late afternoon.

Habitat preference: Favour deciduous and mixed forests, mango groves, orchard gardens, and cultivated areas. Adapt well to human-modified landscapes.

Nesting: Breed in tree cavities, particularly in old mango trees, neem trees, and dead branches. They often reuse cavities created by woodpeckers.

Activity: Diurnal. Most active during feeding times at dawn and dusk. Retreat to roosting sites in tall trees in evening.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहता है, लगातार शोर करता है। बगीचों, मंदिरों और पेड़ों के कोटर में मिलता है। सुबह और शाम को खूब सक्रिय।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shikra, Eurasian Kestrel, Peregrine Falcon
  • Mammalian predators: Domestic cat, mongoose, occasionally jackal
  • Nest predators: Crows, jungle mynas, snakes in tree cavities
  • Parasites: Avian malaria, helminths
हिंदी: बाज और कबूतरबाज़ का शिकार। घोंसलों में कौवे और सांप।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Crop damage: Plum-headed Parakeets can cause significant damage to ripening grain crops, particularly jowar and maize, and to fruit crops such as mangoes, guavas, and pomegranates. Flocks may descend on orchards and completely strip fruit-bearing trees of their crop.

Legal status: Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (Schedule IV). Hunting is prohibited, though farmers may use non-lethal deterrents such as scarecrows, loud noises, or netting.

Cultural significance: In classical Sanskrit literature, parakeets are mentioned as popular captive birds. Some communities regard them with affection; others resent the crop damage.

Conservation: Populations remain stable across their range. They benefit from afforestation and orchard gardens.

Plum-headed Parakeets are expected as year-round residents throughout Basti and Ayodhya districts, particularly in areas with mango groves, orchards, and mixed deciduous forest patches. They are likely to be encountered in populated areas, villages, and agricultural landscapes. Flocks are noisy and conspicuous, making them relatively easy to locate and document. Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent, including India, Nepal, Bhutan, and Bangladesh. Locally abundant in tropical and subtropical regions.

In India: Distributed throughout the plains and foothills up to 2,000 m; common in most provinces.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread resident in deciduous forests, orchards, and cultivated areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Year-round resident; common in mango groves, orchards, and wooded villages.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The Plum-headed Parakeet was first described by Carl Linnaeus in 1766 in Systema Naturae (12th edition), volume 1, page 141. The type locality is the Indian subcontinent. The genus Psittacula is a group of small Asian parakeets with 14 recognized species.

Subspecies. Psittacula cyanocephala cyanocephala — the nominate and only subspecies, found throughout the range including India.

Family and order placement. Order Psittaciformes; family Psittacidae (parakeets and parrots). The genus Psittacula contains small ring-necked parakeets and related species.

Phylogenetic notes. Psittacula species are closely related and share ecological niches across Asia. Molecular studies show the plum-headed group (including P. cyanocephala and P. roseata) forms a subclade.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) due to its wide range, stable populations, and ability to exploit human-modified habitats. The species is not threatened by current hunting or habitat loss.
