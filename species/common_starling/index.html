तिलियर / Common Starling

Sturnus vulgaris Linnaeus, 1758 — Sturnidae

तिलियर — यह काली चमकदार चिड़िया सितंबर से अप्रैल तक झुंड में पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खेतों, घास के मैदानों और बस्तियों में रहती है। सर्दियों में इसके पंखों पर सफेद धब्बे दिखाई देते हैं। यह जमीन पर रहती है और अनाज, कीड़े और फल खाती है। इसकी तेजी से टहलने वाली चाल और सीटी जैसी आवाज़ इसे पहचान देती है।

Field tip: In open cultivation and grassland in winter, look for a dark starling with white spots, walking or hopping on the ground in small to large flocks. The yellow bill (at least at base) and frequent vocalizations are distinctive. The habit of gleaning on the ground separates it from other dark birds.

Size: 19–23 cm long; wingspan 31–40 cm. Weight 60–100 g. A medium-sized, stocky starling.

Winter plumage (in India): Black with heavy white spotting. Each feather has a white tip, giving a spotted or speckled appearance. Wing feathers dark. Tail dark. The white spotting becomes less obvious as feather tips wear away toward spring.

Breeding plumage (Europe/Asia): Glossy black, iridescent in sunlight. White spotting mostly absent or very reduced. Much brighter appearance than winter birds.

Bill: Pale yellow to yellow, sometimes with darker culmen; long and pointed; bright yellow in breeding season.

Legs and feet: Red to pink; sometimes dusky in worn plumage.

Eyes: Dark.

Sexual dimorphism: Minimal; females may show slightly less iridescence.

Juveniles: Duller brown, with less white spotting than winter adults.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A rambling, varied warble with hisses and clicks.; highly variable.
  2. Call: Sharp cher or check, repeated in series.
  3. Contact calls: Soft whistles and chirps within flocks.
हिंदी: नर की गान बहुत तरह की आवाजों का मिश्रण है। तेज़ "चेर" या "चेक" कॉल। झुंड में सीटी जैसी आवाज़।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ground gleaner: Common Starling is highly gregarious, feeding on the ground. It feeds on:

  • Grain (wheat, rice, barley)
  • Seeds of various plants
  • Insects and invertebrates (grasshoppers, beetles, ants, termites)
  • Fruits and berries
  • Earthworms and larvae

Feeding behaviour: Walks on ground, probing with bill; forages in loose flocks; often follows cattle and plowed fields to catch disturbed insects.

Social behaviour: Highly gregarious, especially in winter. Forms large flocks, sometimes numbering thousands, that roost communally at night and perform spectacular aerial maneuvers.

Flock dynamics: Flocks move together in coordinated waves and clouds; constantly vocalizing.

Habitat preference: Open cultivation, grassland, parks, gardens, residential areas, cultivation margins. Avoids dense forest.

Predators: Peregrine Falcon, Merlin, larger raptors.

Roosting: Forms massive communal roosts in large trees, reed beds, and man-made structures at night.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहती है। जमीन पर चलती-फिरती है। खेतों में अनाज खोजती है। कीड़े भी खाती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Absent during breeding season (April–July); returns to eastern UP from September and remains through May.

Arrival and departure: First flocks arrive in August–September; numbers peak in December–February; departure begins in March–April, with most birds gone by May.

Breeding (elsewhere): Breeds in Europe and Asia, from Britain east to Japan. Nest in tree cavities, building cavities, and cliffs. Clutch of 4–6 eggs.

Moult: Complete moult on breeding grounds before arrival; heavy white spotting on new plumage worn away during winter.

Roosts: Forms massive communal roosts; roosting behavior changes seasonally.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। यूरोप और एशिया में घोंसला बनाता है। बहुत बड़े झुंड में रहती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Peregrine Falcon, Merlin, larger raptors
  • Mammalian predators: Domestic cat, occasionally mongoose or jackal
  • Parasites: Blood parasites, internal helminths, ectoparasites
हिंदी: बड़ी बाजें शिकार करती हैं। बिल्ली भी खतरनाक है। साथ में झुंड होने से सुरक्षा मिलती है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is prohibited.

Grain damage: Large flocks may damage ripening grain crops, particularly wheat and millet. Farmers sometimes use noise-makers or scarecrows to deter flocks. However, damage is seasonal and localized.

Pest control benefit: Consumes large numbers of grasshoppers, beetles, and other agricultural pests, providing significant pest control benefit.

Cultural relevance: Known from ornithological records and literary references dating back centuries. Mentioned in Mughal hunting records.

Scientific value: Population monitoring important for understanding winter visitor impacts on agriculture; flock behavior provides insights into collective movement and decision-making.

हिंदी: वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम 1972 के अनुसूची IV के अंतर्गत पूरी तरह संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार निषिद्ध है।

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from September to May, particularly in open cultivation and grassland areas of Basti and Ayodhya districts. Published handbooks document this species as a common winter visitor across northern India.

Habitat preference: Open cultivation, grassland, parks, gardens, residential areas with open ground.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from Central Asian breeding grounds are settled.

Nearest published record: Handbook of the Birds of India and the Western Palearctic document this species as a common winter visitor to the Indo-Gangetic Plain. Its conspicuous flocks make it highly visible.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph or video showing the bird clearly in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date), such as a flock feeding in a field, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeding range in Europe and western and central Asia, from the Atlantic east to Japan. Winters throughout Africa, Middle East, and Asia, including India, Southeast Asia, and southern China.

In India: Distributed across the Indian subcontinent as a winter visitor (September–May). Common and widespread in open cultivation areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Very common in winter in cultivated areas and open land, particularly on the plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular and common winter visitor in open cultivation; expected on range grounds rather than confirmed in local surveys.

Field rule: Common Starling in winter is a dark bird heavily spotted with white, walking on the ground in flocks. If the bird shows white spotting only on head and neck with buff coloring, or if it is crested, it is a different species.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae as Sturnus vulgaris. Type locality is Sweden.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognized; S. v. vulgaris and related forms winter in the Indian subcontinent.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Sturnidae (starlings and mynas). The genus Sturnus contains the true starlings.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular work has placed Sturnidae as distinct from other passerines, with relationships to mynas and oxpeckers.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widely distributed, one of the most successful bird species. The species is highly adaptable to human-modified habitats. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
