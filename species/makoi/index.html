मकोय / Black Nightshade

Solanum nigrum L. — Solanaceae

मकोय — बाड़ की छाया और सब्ज़ी की क्यारी में उगने वाला आधा-मीटर का नरम पौधा, छोटे सफ़ेद तारे जैसे फूल जिनके बीच पीले पुंकेसर, और गुच्छों में मटर जैसे फल जो हरे से काले हो जाते हैं। पके काले फल बच्चे खाते हैं, पत्ती का साग बनता है, और आयुर्वेद में यह "काकमाची" जिगर की दवा है। कच्चा हरा फल ज़हरीला है — यह याद रखना ज़रूरी है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
HabitSoft, branching annual 30–90 cm, hairless or nearly so
StemGreen or purplish, angled, sometimes with faint ridges
LeavesAlternate, 3–10 cm, oval with a pointed tip, edges wavy or shallowly toothed, dark green
FlowersWhite, star-shaped, 1 cm, five petals bent back, a cone of yellow anthers in the centre; 3–8 in a drooping cluster from the stem between leaves
FruitRound berries 6–10 mm in drooping bunches, green then dull purple-black, with many flat seeds
WhereRich, moist, part-shaded ground near houses and cattle

Field tip: Near a cowshed or in a shaded vegetable bed, a soft green plant with little white stars and bunches of black pea-sized berries. The yellow anther cone and the nodding berry cluster rising from the side of the stem, not from a leaf axil, mark the nightshades.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: छायादार, खाद वाली ज़मीन का नरम पौधा; फूल सफ़ेद तारा, बीच में पीले पुंकेसर; फल गुच्छों में, हरे से काले। फल का गुच्छा पत्ती के जोड़ से नहीं, तने के बीच से निकलता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (Indian plains):

MeasurementRange
Height30–90 cm
Leaf3–10 × 2–6 cm
Flower diameter8–12 mm
Berry diameter6–10 mm
Seeds per berry20–60

Stem and leaves: Herbaceous, glabrous to sparsely hairy; leaves ovate-rhombic, thin, with a winged stalk.

Flowers: In extra-axillary umbel-like cymes, a feature of the genus; corolla white, sometimes tinged purple, stamens five with large yellow anthers opening by terminal pores.

Fruit: A globose berry, shining black or dull purple-black when ripe, green and bitter when unripe; the green fruit and leaves contain solanine-type glycoalkaloids that fall sharply as the fruit ripens.

A complex, not a species. What Indian floras call Solanum nigrum is a group of closely related forms differing in chromosome number and berry colour; Solanum americanum is the common tropical lowland member and may be the usual plant of the district. The field characters and uses apply to the whole group.

हिंदी: पौधा चिकना, पत्ती पतली; फूल तने की बगल से गुच्छे में; फल पकने पर काला और मीठा-सा, कच्चा हरा और कड़वा — कच्चे फल और पत्ती में सोलेनिन ज़हर जो पकने पर घट जाता है। "मकोय" असल में कई मिलती-जुलती जातियों का समूह है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A plant of rich, disturbed, moist ground, which in a village means the cowshed, the manure heap, the irrigated vegetable plot and the foot of the hedge. It grows fast, flowers within a month, and fruits for months; birds, especially Red-vented Bulbul and Common Myna, eat the ripe berries and sow the seed along fences and under trees. Cattle leave it. The flowers are buzz-pollinated by bees that shake pollen from the anther pores.

हिंदी: खाद वाली, नम, आधी छाया की ज़मीन का पौधा — गौशाला, कूड़े का ढेर, सब्ज़ी की क्यारी, बाड़ के नीचे। बुलबुल और मैना पके फल खाकर बीज फैलाते हैं। मधुमक्खियाँ फूल को झकझोरकर पराग लेती हैं।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Berry eaters: Red-vented Bulbul, Common Myna, House Sparrow.
  • Same family in the same places: Brinjal, Tomato, Chilli, Dhatura, Yellow-berried Nightshade, Wild Cape Gooseberry.
  • Companions on manured ground: Bathua, Spiny Amaranth.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Shares the pests of brinjal: the shoot and fruit borer, leaf beetles (Epilachna), aphids and whitefly, and can carry them between crops.
  • Leaf spot and early blight fungi; virus diseases of tomato and brinjal persist in it.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Toxicity first. Unripe green berries and, to a lesser degree, the raw leaves contain solanine and related alkaloids that cause vomiting, diarrhoea and worse; children have been poisoned by green fruit. Ripe black berries from the Indian plant are widely eaten without harm, and cooked leaves are a common pot-herb, but the cooking matters and the identification matters.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Food. Ripe berries eaten fresh; leaves cooked as makoi ka saag, in the south as manathakkali keerai, with the water changed. A famine and everyday green across India.
  • Liver. Kakmachi in Ayurveda, a classical remedy for liver enlargement, jaundice and dropsy, in Kirtikar & Basu and Chopra; a component of several marketed "liver tonics".
  • Skin and fever. Leaf poultices for swellings and burns; decoctions for fever.
  • Weed and reservoir. A nuisance in vegetable fields, and a bridge for brinjal and tomato pests and viruses between seasons.

Today. Sold as a green in some markets; gathered for the herbal trade; a routine weed otherwise.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The glycoalkaloids and polyphenols of Solanum nigrum have been studied for liver protection and for activity against cancer cells in the laboratory; nothing clinical. The nutritional value of the cooked leaves is real and documented, and that is the practical lead: a free green that most people have stopped eating.

हिंदी: पहले ज़हर: कच्चा हरा फल और कच्ची पत्ती ज़हरीली — बच्चों को हरे फल से बचाएँ। पके काले फल और उबली पत्ती का साग सब जगह खाया जाता है। आयुर्वेद में काकमाची जिगर और पीलिया की दवा। सब्ज़ी के खेत में यह बैंगन-टमाटर के कीड़े और वायरस पालता है। मुफ़्त का पौष्टिक साग जिसे लोग खाना भूल गए हैं।

To be expected near every cowshed, in shaded vegetable plots and under hedges across the district, from the rains to the end of winter. Which member of the complex grows here is a question for a botanist with a microscope; a photograph of flowers and ripe fruit is a fine first record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: The Solanum nigrum complex occurs on every continent; S. nigrum in the strict sense is Eurasian, S. americanum pantropical.

In India: Throughout, to about 2,500 m.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common everywhere on rich ground.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in every village.

  4. Does makoi carry brinjal pests? — Check plants near a brinjal field for beetles and borers.
  5. Has any child been made ill by green berries in the village? — Ask the ASHA worker.

Field rule: Soft hairless plant, white star flowers with a yellow anther cone, and drooping bunches of pea-sized berries turning black: makoi. Green berries are not to be eaten.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Linnaeus, Species Plantarum 1: 186 (1753). Solanum is the classical Latin name of a nightshade; nigrum, "black".

Subspecies. Several subspecies and many segregate species have been recognised within the complex (S. nigrum sensu stricto, S. americanum, S. villosum and others); Indian populations are mostly referred to S. nigrum in the broad sense.

Family and order placement. Solanaceae, the potato and tomato family; order Solanales. Solanum is among the largest plant genera, with over a thousand species.

Phylogenetic notes. The black nightshades form the Morelloid clade of Solanum; chromosome counts (diploid, tetraploid, hexaploid) separate the members more reliably than appearance.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. A worldwide weed.
