स्ट्रीक्ड वीवर / Streaked Weaver

Ploceus manyar (Horsfield, 1821) — Ploceidae

स्ट्रीक्ड वीवर — यह एक छोटी, पीली-भूरी चिड़िया है जो साल भर पानी वाली जगहों के पास मिलती है। इसका सबसे खास काम है पत्तियों को जोड़कर अपना घोंसला बनाना, जिसे हाथ से बुना हुआ माना जाता है। ये चिड़ियाँ झुंडों में रहती हैं और एक ही पेड़ पर बहुत सारे घोंसले बनाती हैं। अनाज खाने वाली यह चिड़िया गर्मियों में सबसे अधिक दिखाई देती है।

Field tip: A small, streaky buff-yellow weaver bird in loose flocks around reed beds and wetlands. Look for colonies of hanging, woven nests. More buff-coloured and heavily streaked than other weavers. Colonies can be very noisy and active.

Size: 14–15 cm long; wingspan 20–25 cm. Weight 13–18 g. A small, compact weaver.

Plumage (male breeding): Bright yellow-buff or golden-buff overall. Back, crown, and upper parts with heavy dark or blackish streaking. Breast with prominent dark streaking. Underparts buff-white with streaking on breast and flanks. Wings darker with buff or yellowish edges. Tail buff-brown.

Plumage (female and non-breeding male): Duller overall. Less prominent yellow. Streaking less bold. More subdued colouration than breeding males.

Bill: Pointed, slightly curved; adapted for cracking seeds. Dark with pale base.

Legs and feet: Short, pale.

Eyes: Dark.

Tail: Short, square-ended tail; buff-brown.

Sexes: Males in breeding plumage brighter and more boldly marked than females.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Colony calls: Continuous loud chirping and chattering in colonies. Very noisy environment.
  2. Individual calls: Harsh, chirping calls and squeaks.
  3. Alarm calls: Sharp calls when alarmed.
हिंदी: कॉलोनी में बहुत तेज़ आवाज़। "चिर-चिर-चिर" की आवाज़ें। सतर्क रहती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Granivore: Feeds primarily on grass seeds and grain. Extremely efficient at gleaning seeds from standing grain and grass.

Feeding: Forages on the ground and low vegetation, picking seeds. Often hangs from grass heads to access seeds. Moves through vegetation actively.

Highly gregarious: Extremely social. Forms large colonies that may number hundreds or even thousands of birds. Nests colonially.

Weaving behaviour: Males construct elaborate hanging nests by weaving strips of reeds, grasses, and leaves. Nests are distinctive and characteristic.

Breeding behaviour: Males weave nests to attract females. Multiple nests may be built by same male. Females select nest and mate.

Colonial life: Colonies are very noisy and active. Constant vocalizations throughout the day.

Flock foraging: Move in flocks through suitable habitat. Can cause significant grain damage in cultivated areas.

हिंदी: झुंडों में रहता है। अनाज खाता है। पत्तियों से घोंसला बनाता है। पानी के पास रहता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Year-round resident: Present throughout the year in eastern UP, though numbers may fluctuate seasonally.

Breeding: Mainly June–September, with peak activity July–August. Males build nests and display to attract females. Multiple broods possible.

Breeding colonies: Large colonies form in reed beds and trees near water bodies. Colonies may be occupied year after year.

Non-breeding season: Continue to flock together. Move more widely in search of food sources.

Moult: Post-breeding moult occurs after breeding season; timing variable.

Seasonal pattern: Active in flocks throughout year. Breeding season most obvious time when males in bright plumage and actively weaving nests.

हिंदी: साल भर मिलता है। गर्मियों में घोंसले बनाता है। जुलाई-अगस्त में सबसे सक्रिय रहता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Common Kestrel, Shikra, crows, Indian Eagle-Owl
  • Mammalian predators: Cat, mongoose, rats, weasels
  • Nest predators: Crows, snakes, rats, mongooses
  • Chick parasites: Internal and external parasites
हिंदी: शिकारी चिड़ियाँ इसका शिकार करती हैं। कौवे और सांप अंडों को नुकसान पहुँचाते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is illegal.

Agricultural pest: Can cause significant damage to ripening grain crops, particularly wheat, rice, and millets. Large flocks can cause substantial crop losses. Control involves netting, noise-making, and other non-lethal deterrents.

Grain consumption: Consume large quantities of grain and grass seeds. Cause damage by feeding on ripening grain heads.

Mitigation: Farmers use scaring devices, nets, and other methods to protect crops. Traditional methods include noise-making and bird-scaring.

Cultural significance: Known as "gidh" or "jali chiriya" in Hindi. Mentioned in agricultural literature due to crop damage.

Ecological role: Important seed disperser for grasses and wild plants. Prey for raptors.

Streaked Weaver is a year-round resident throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh, particularly in areas with reed beds, wetlands, and cultivated areas adjacent to water bodies. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species breeds colonially in reed beds and trees near water, and can be encountered throughout the year, though numbers may fluctuate with season. Large colonies can be very conspicuous with their woven nests and constant vocalization. A first record would require a dated photograph showing a weaver nest or the distinctive streaked plumage, with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent from the Himalayan foothills southward. Largely resident throughout its range.

In India: Distributed throughout India in suitable wetland habitat. Common in northern and central India. Resident throughout.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common and widespread throughout the state in wetland areas, reed beds, and cultivated areas adjacent to water bodies.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Year-round resident in reed beds, wetlands, and cultivated areas. Expected throughout the year with local population fluctuations.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Horsfield in 1821. Type locality: Java, Indonesia (but common throughout Asia).

Subspecies. P. manyar manyar (Horsfield, 1821) — Indian populations. Some subspecific variation across range.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Ploceidae (weaver birds). Genus Ploceus contains numerous weaver species, characterized by their elaborate hanging nests.

Phylogenetic notes. Ploceus manyar is closely related to other Ploceus species, particularly Baya Weaver (P. philippinus), with which it overlaps in range.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and common. The species is adaptable to various habitat types and has no major conservation threats.
