घोंघाई / Large Grey Babbler

Turdoides malcolmi (Sykes, 1832) — Leiothrichidae

घोंघाई — यह मध्यम आकार की ग्रे और भूरी चिड़िया पूरे साल झाड़ीदार इलाकों में झुंड में रहती है। यह सामाजिक पक्षी है और झुंड के सदस्य एक-दूसरे की देखभाल करते हैं। इसकी बहुत तेज़ और उत्साहपूर्ण आवाज़ "घोंघा-घोंघा" सुनकर इसे तुरंत पहचाना जा सकता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeLarge babbler; 25–27 cm long
PlumageAshy-grey above; buff-brown on back; whitish below with grey flanks
TailLong, graduated, grey and buff-brown
BehaviourGregarious; boisterous and vocal; moves through bushes in tight groups
VoiceDistinctive, loud, repetitive calls

Field tip: A large, noisy, social babbler, always in flocks. Grey and buff-brown colouration; long, graduated tail. Loud, boisterous calls make it very conspicuous. Moves through dense bushes in loose flocks; difficult to observe individuals but flocks easily heard.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length250–270 mm

Tarsus28–32 mm

Size: 25–27 cm long; weighs 45–70 g. A large babbler.

Plumage: Ashy-grey on head, neck and upper back. Back buff-brown with grey edges. Rump buff. Tail long, graduated, grey with buff-brown edges and tips. Underparts whitish to pale buff; flanks washed with grey. Lores and orbital ring whitish.

Bill: Longish, dark, slightly curved; adapted for foraging in vegetation.

Legs and feet: Dark; strong for climbing through bushes.

Sexes: Similar; no obvious sexual dimorphism.

Juveniles: Duller; less contrasting plumage.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact and flock calls: Loud, boisterous, repetitive "ghongh-ghongh" or "toor-toor" sounds.
  2. Alarm call: Loud chatter and scolding.
  3. Song: Rambling, repetitive vocalizations; social calling to maintain flock cohesion.
हिंदी: ज़ोर-ज़ोर से "घोंघा-घोंघा" करता है। दिन भर गाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Highly gregarious: Lives in groups of 5–10 or more birds year-round. Groups maintain close social bonds. Social roosting and foraging.

Foraging: Searches through dense vegetation for insects and seeds. Feeds on:

  • Insects (beetles, caterpillars, grasshoppers, ants)
  • Spiders
  • Small seeds and fruits
  • Occasionally leaf buds

Cooperative behaviour: Groups forage together; members help in nest building and chick rearing.

Movement: Moves through bushes in tight flocks; rarely exposed; stays in cover. Flight is weak and fluttering; moves from bush to bush.

Social calls: Constantly calling to maintain group cohesion. Calls serve territorial and social functions.

हिंदी: झुंड में सब कुछ करता है। एक-दूसरे की मदद करते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Breeding season: March–July in eastern UP; peak May–June.

Nest: Rough, ball-like or cup-shaped nest of dry grass, leaves and twigs, built low in bushes or trees (1–3 m high). Often builds multiple nests (for roosting).

Clutch: 4–5 eggs; pale greenish with markings.

Incubation and fledging: Shared by group members. Incubation 12–14 days; fledging 12–14 days.

Cooperative breeding: Non-breeding group members assist in feeding chicks and defending territory.

Moult: Complete post-breeding moult; July–September.

हिंदी: झुंड के सभी सदस्य घोंसले के देखभाल में मदद करते हैं।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shrikes, raptors, crows
  • Mammalian predators: Snakes, jackals, domestic cats
  • Parasites: Avian parasites, mites, lice

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Schedule IV.

Agricultural value: Feeds on crop pests (insects); beneficial in cultivation areas.

Cultural relevance: Known to villagers; associated with scrubland and waste areas; their boisterous calls make them conspicuous.

Ecological role: Seed dispersal (some fruits consumed); pest control; food for raptors.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Large Grey Babbler should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Year-round presence: GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain. Expected resident in eastern UP in open woodlands, thorn scrub, and cultivation with scattered trees. Very conspicuous due to loud vocalizations; easily detected by voice.

Where to look: Scrubland with dense bushes; thorn forests; margins of cultivation with scattered trees. Listen for loud, boisterous calls.

Seasonality: Year-round; numbers may shift locally with vegetation conditions.

Detection: Easy to locate by loud calls. Look for flocks moving through bushes in loose formation.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing multiple birds in flock (note the long graduated tail), with location GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page of this atlas.

हिंदी: झाड़ीदार इलाकों में साल भर दिखता है। आवाज़ से आसानी से पहचाना जा सकता है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent, from the foothills to peninsular India; resident.

In India: Widespread in suitable dry and semi-arid habitats.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common resident in northern and central regions, less common in wetter eastern areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in scrubland and waste areas; may be more localized due to habitat requirements.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Sykes in 1832. Turdoides malcolmi Sykes, 1832. Type locality: Deccan, India.

Subspecies. Monotypic: T. malcolmi (Sykes, 1832).

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Leiothrichidae (laughingthrushes and allies). Genus Turdoides comprises large Old World babblers of Africa and Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Turdoides underwent taxonomic revision in recent years; previously Argya. T. malcolmi is a well-defined Asian species.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Population is stable. Adaptable to human-modified habitats. Protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 in India.
