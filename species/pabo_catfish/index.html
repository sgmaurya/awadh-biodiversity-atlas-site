पाबो कैटफिश मछली / Pabo Catfish

Ompok pabo (Hamilton, 1822) — Siluridae

पाबो कैटफिश मछली — नदियों, नालों और गहरे तालों में पाई जाने वाली एक कोमल, स्वादिष्ट और दुर्लभ देसी कैटफिश (बटरफिश) है। इसका शरीर बिना छिलके वाला, चिकना और चपटा होता है। अन्य पाबदा मछलियों के विपरीत, इसका निचला जबड़ा आगे नहीं निकला होता और इसकी ऊपरी मूंछें बहुत छोटी होती हैं जो आँख से आगे नहीं जातीं। इसके पेट के नीचे 66 से 71 किरणों वाला अत्यंत लंबा पंख होता है। यह रात के समय छोटी मछलियों का शिकार करती है और खाने में बहुत स्वादिष्ट मानी जाती है।

Field tip: Look in slow, deep river pools and connected oxbow lakes. An elongate, scaleless silvery catfish with very short maxillary barbels not reaching past the eye, an equal or shorter lower jaw, and an exceptionally long anal fin of over 65 rays, is Ompok pabo.

हिंदी: यह बिना तराशों वाली चपटी कैटफिश है। इसका निचला जबड़ा आगे नहीं निकला होता और इसकी मूंछें बहुत छोटी होती हैं जो आँख को पार नहीं करतीं। इसके पेट के नीचे बहुत लंबा पंख होता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Size as given by FishBase (Froese & Pauly 2025):

MeasurementRangeSource
Maximum length25 cmFroese & Pauly (2025), FishBase

Adult description:

Ompok pabo possesses an elongate, slender body that is strongly compressed laterally from behind the head to the caudal base (Hamilton 1822). The head is broad, depressed, and slightly shorter than one-fifth of the standard length (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The snout is blunt and rounded; eyes are small, subcutaneous, and situated just above the level of the mouth corner (Hamilton 1822). The mouth is wide and terminal; uniquely among Indian Ompok species, the lower jaw does not project beyond the upper jaw, being either subequal or slightly included beneath the snout (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Teeth are villiform, sharp, and arranged in broad bands on the jaws and in two separate patches on the vomer (Jayaram 2006).

Two pairs of barbels are present: the maxillary barbels are noticeably short and slender, extending backwards only to the hind margin of the eye or barely reaching the opercular edge; the mandibular barbels are minute, hair-like, and barely visible without magnification (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

The dorsal fin is minute, lacking an osseous spine, bearing only 5 soft rays (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). An adipose fin is completely absent (Jayaram 2006). The pectoral fins have a moderately strong, smooth or finely serrated spine and 14–15 soft rays. The anal fin is extraordinarily long, bearing 66 to 71 branched rays, representing the highest anal fin ray count among all Indian sheatfishes (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The caudal fin is deeply forked with equal pointed lobes (Hamilton 1822).

Living specimens are silvery-grey to olivaceous-brown on the dorsum, with gleaming brassy-yellow reflections on the flanks and white on the belly (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). A diffuse, dark dusky smudge is situated on the shoulder behind the gill cover (Hamilton 1822). The fins are pale yellowish-grey and translucent.

हिंदी: इसका शरीर लगभग 25 सेमी तक लंबा, बहुत चपटा और चिकना होता है। मुँह का निचला जबड़ा ऊपरी जबड़े के बराबर या थोड़ा छोटा होता है। मूंछें बहुत छोटी होती हैं और पेट के नीचे 66 से 71 किरणों वाला लंबा पंख होता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Pabo Catfish is a nocturnal and crepuscular benthopelagic predator adapted to standing and slow-flowing freshwater ecosystems (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). It inhabits perennial rivers, deep oxbow lakes (tals), sluggish canals, and large village tanks with muddy bottoms and submerged aquatic vegetation.

During the day, individuals seek refuge in dark, sheltered underwater microhabitats, resting among submerged tree stumps, undercut banks, or dense beds of Hydrilla and floating macrophytes (Jayaram 2006). At dusk, they move into shallow margins and open waters to hunt small fish and crustaceans (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Using their lateral line system and chemosensory cells on their lips and barbels, they detect the minute water displacements created by passing prey (Jayaram 2006). During seasonal monsoon floods, they migrate into inundated floodplains to forage and reproduce, returning to deep river pools as the dry season progresses.

हिंदी: यह रात के समय शिकार करने वाली कैटफिश है। दिन में यह पानी के नीचे जड़ों और पौधों के झुरमुट में विश्राम करती है और रात में बाहर निकलकर शिकार करती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Seasonal phenology in northern Indian alluvial river systems across the agricultural year (Talwar & Jhingran 1991; Jayaram 2006):

Spawning takes place during the southwest monsoon from June to August (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Rising water levels and strong monsoonal rainfalls induce courtship displays. Pairs spawn in shallow, vegetated floodwaters. Females release small, adhesive yellowish eggs that attach to submerged grass roots and aquatic plant stems. The eggs hatch after a few days into predatory larvae that absorb their yolk reserves before actively hunting rotifers, cladocerans, and midge larvae (Jayaram 2006).

हिंदी: बरसात के दिनों में जब तालों में नया पानी भरता है, यह घास में अंडे देती है। कुछ ही दिनों में अंडों से बच्चे निकल आते हैं और वे तेजी से तैरकर शिकार करने लगते हैं।

Diet and Feeding Behaviour आहार

Ompok pabo is an obligate carnivorous predator throughout its adult life (Talwar & Jhingran 1991):

Natural diet components:

  • Small indigenous carplets and minnows, including Salmostoma bacaila (Large Razorbelly Minnow), Rasbora daniconius (Slender Rasbora), and Chela cachius (Silver Hatchet Chela).
  • Freshwater prawns and shrimps (Macrobrachium spp.), seized and swallowed whole (Jayaram 2006).
  • Aquatic insects and larvae, including chironomid midges, water bugs, and mayfly nymphs (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).
  • Small benthic worms and bottom-dwelling organisms ingested from mud.
हिंदी: यह पूरी तरह मांसाहारी मछली है जो छोटी मछलियों (जैसे चलवा और चेला), पानी के झींगों और जलीय कीड़ों को खाती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Documented natural predators in northern Indian alluvial freshwater systems:

  • Riverine predatory fishes: Large predators like Wallago attu (Striped Catfish), Channa marulius (Great Snakehead), and Chitala chitala (Clown Knifefish) prey on sub-adult pabo.
  • Piscivorous birds: Night herons, pond herons, and egrets spear adults in shallow marsh margins.
  • River otters: Hunt schooling bottom fish along perennial river stretches.
  • Water snakes: Checkered keelbacks pursue them among shoreline weeds.
हिंदी: बोआल, सौर, चितल, बगुले और उदबिलाव इस मछली का शिकार करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Nutritional and market value: The Pabo Catfish is a prized, high-value freshwater food fish across northern and eastern India. Its flesh is white, tender, rich in beneficial fats and micronutrients, and virtually devoid of intramuscular bones. Because of its delicious taste and relative rarity compared to Ompok bimaculatus, it commands premium prices in local fish markets.

Floodplain ecosystem role: In seasonal monsoon floodplains and inundated rice fields, wild schools consume pest insects and forage fish, cycling nutrients without causing any damage to rice plants (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

Traditional: Consumed as an esteemed delicacy in traditional Awadhi cuisine, prized for its sweetness and digestibility.

Today: Harvested from wild rivers and oxbow wetlands; recognized as a priority candidate for conservation aquaculture.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Establishing induced breeding protocols for Ompok pabo in rural hatcheries to enhance wild stock conservation.

हिंदी: यह एक अत्यंत स्वादिष्ट और कीमती देसी मछली है। इसका मांस बहुत कोमल और कांटे रहित होता है। बाजारों में इसे बहुत ऊंचे दामों पर खरीदा जाता है।

The Pabo Catfish is expected across eastern Uttar Pradesh in perennial rivers, oxbow lakes (tals), and weed-fringed canal systems. Look in quiet river pools and deep weed beds from August through December. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to South Asia, recorded from India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and Myanmar (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

In India: Distributed throughout the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra river drainages, from foothill rivers to the plains (Menon 1999).

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded throughout the Ganges, Ghaghara, Rapti, and Gomti river networks and associated floodplain depressions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a resident native species in the Ghaghara, Kuwano, and Ami river corridors and major oxbow wetlands.

हिंदी: यह भारत, पाकिस्तान, नेपाल और बांग्लादेश की प्रमुख नदियों में पाई जाती है। उत्तर प्रदेश में घाघरा और कुआनो नदी प्रणालियों में यह उपस्थित है।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 as Silurus pabo in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches, page 153. Type locality: River Brahmaputra, near Goyalpara, Assam.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognized across its continental South Asian range.

Family and order placement. Order Siluriformes, family Siluridae. The genus Ompok Lacepède, 1803, comprises Asian sheatfishes characterized by an extremely reduced dorsal fin and an elongate anal fin.

Phylogenetic notes. Ompok pabo is distinguished from all other members of the genus Ompok by its terminal mouth with a non-projecting lower jaw, exceptionally short maxillary barbels, and extremely high anal fin ray count (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the global IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. It maintains widespread distributions, though wild populations are less common than O. bimaculatus.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Ompok pabo है और यह Siluridae परिवार की सदस्य है। प्रकृति में इसका वास नदियों में स्थित है।
