नीबू पैंसी / Lemon Pansy

Junonia lemonias (Linnaeus, 1758) — Nymphalidae

नीबू पैंसी — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खेतों, घास की जगहों और सड़क किनारों में पाई जाने वाली एक छोटी से मध्यम आकार की पीली-भूरी तितली है, जिसके पंखों पर बड़ी गहरी आँख जैसी लकीरें (eyespots) होती हैं। इसके पंखों का रंग पीला-नारंगी होता है जिन पर दो बड़े काले और नीले गोल दाग़े होते हैं जो आँखों की तरह दिखते हैं। यह तितली इचिनेकोलोआ और अन्य घास की पत्तियों खाती है। इसकी उड़ान तेज़ और कठोर होती है, और यह गर्मी में सक्रिय होती है। ये आँख जैसे दाग़े शिकारियों को डराने के लिए काम आते हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription

ColorationPale yellow-orange or cream-yellow ground color
EyespotsTwo large, prominent black eyespots with bright blue or purple iridescent centers on each wing
ForewingContains two eyespots; yellow background between spots
HindwingContains one large eyespot; occasionally smaller marginal eyespots
FlightSwift, erratic, and rapid; stays low over grassland

Field tip: Look for a small to medium-sized yellow-orange butterfly with large, striking black eyespots with blue centers on both wings flying over grassland and fallow fields. The eyespots are distinctive and readily identify the Lemon Pansy.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for adults in northern Indian plains):

MeasurementRange
Body length20–24 mm
Wingspan55–70 mm
Forewing length25–32 mm

Body: Slender, pale yellow-brown, with clubbed antennae.

Wings:

  • Dorsal (upper) surface: The ground color is pale yellow-orange or cream-yellow. The forewing contains two large, well-defined black eyespots with iridescent blue or purple centers, surrounded by pale yellow rings. The hindwing has one large eyespot, similar in structure.
  • Ventral (lower) surface: Paler, with eyespots similarly marked but less vivid.

Eyespot Function: The large eyespots serve as a startle defense mechanism (deflection). When a predator attacks, the eyespots draw the predator's attention to the wing margins, away from the vulnerable body and head.

Sexual Dimorphism: Males are brighter and more vivid than females, which are duller and slightly larger.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Swift grassland flyer with startle defense. The Lemon Pansy is a fast, erratic flyer that stays in open grasslands and fallow areas. When threatened or surprised, it rapidly opens its wings, flashing the large eyespots, which startles predators. Males are territorial and perch on low grass stems, rapidly chasing away competitors and pursuing females. They are strongly attracted to damp earth and mineral-rich mud.

Grass specialist. The caterpillar is a grass feeder, specializing on Echinochloa and related grass species, making it one of the most generalist butterflies in agricultural landscapes.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stage / SeasonEvent
EggLaid singly on grass stems; pale yellow; hatch in 3–5 days
Larval (Caterpillar)4–5 instars; mature caterpillar 18–22 mm long; greenish with darker stripes; development 12–16 days
PupaHanging chrysalis; green or brown; emerges in 8–11 days
AdultEmerges March to November; peak abundance June to October; lifespan 2–3 weeks

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Echinochloa species (barnyard grass), Paspalum, and other common agricultural and wild grasses.

Adult nectar sources:

  • Low flowering herbs, Lantana camara (Indian Lantana), wild composites, and flowering weeds.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Insectivorous birds and dragonflies are deterred by the eyespots.
  • Spiders: Jumping spiders and grass spiders.
  • Parasitoid Wasps: Small wasps parasitize caterpillars and pupae.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pollinator. The Lemon Pansy is a frequent visitor to flowering crops and grassland herbs, serving as a minor pollinator.

Grassland indicator. Its abundance indicates healthy grassland and fallow-land vegetation and is an indicator of habitat quality in agricultural landscapes.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia, Southeast Asia, and extending into East Asia and Australia.

In India: Distributed throughout the peninsula and the Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant resident in all grassland and agricultural districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all grass-dominated habitats throughout the year.

Field rule: A small to medium-sized pale yellow butterfly with large, prominent black eyespots with iridescent blue or purple centers on both wings, flying rapidly over grassland, is the Lemon Pansy.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Linnaeus described the Lemon Pansy as Papilio lemonias in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition. The type locality is Asia. The genus Junonia was later established for these pansy butterflies characterized by prominent eyespots.

Subspecies. The nominate subspecies J. l. lemonias occurs throughout the Indian subcontinent.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Nymphalidae, subfamily Nymphalinae.

Phylogenetic notes. Junonia is a genus of African and Asian nymphalid butterflies, characterized by prominent eyespots used in predator deterrence.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Common and widespread, under no conservation threat.
