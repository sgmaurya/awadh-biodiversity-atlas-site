लाल भेरेंडा / Bellyache Bush

Jatropha gossypiifolia L. — Euphorbiaceae

लाल भेरेंडा — एक विदेशी झाड़ी जिसकी पत्तियाँ लाल रंग में रंगी होती हैं, विशेषकर नई पत्तियाँ। इसमें सुंदर लाल फूल खिलते हैं, पर पूरा पौधा ज़हरीला है — विशेषकर बीज। भारत में कभी-कभी बाग़ों में लगाई जाती है, पर सावधानी से। इसके नाम "बेली एक बुश" का अर्थ यह है कि इसको खाने से पेट में दर्द होता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (cultivated plants in eastern India):

Stem and branches: Woody, smooth or sparsely hairy; milky acrid latex present.

Leaves: Alternate, palmately lobed with 3–5 lobes; petioles are long and reddish. Young leaves are prominently reddish or burgundy; older leaves become greenish. The leaf margins are serrulate.

Flowers: Small, bright red or dark red, unisexual (male and female on same plant), in terminal or axillary corymbs. Petals are red; stamens yellow.

Fruit: A three-lobed (tricoccous) capsule, initially green, ripening black; each lobe contains one seed.

Seeds: Black, smooth, oily, about 1 cm long. The seeds are oily.

Toxins: The entire plant contains curcin, a toxic protein related to the ricin of castor, and irritant diterpenes, concentrated in the seeds. Latex irritates skin and eyes.

हिंदी: पत्ती लाल-भूरी, हाथ जैसी बँटी। फूल लाल। तने में दूधिया रस। बीज काले, तेल से भरे, ज़हरीले।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Jatropha gossypiifolia is native to tropical America, but is now cultivated ornamentally in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. The plant is fast-growing, drought-tolerant, and tolerant of poor soils. The red pigments in the leaves (anthocyanins) are photosynthetic and provide some shade advantage. The plant is occasionally established in disturbed areas near human habitation.

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Bees and other insects visit the flowers.
  • Seed dispersal: Fruit splits; seeds fall near the plant or are dispersed by water.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf spots in humid seasons (minor).
  • The plant's toxins provide resistance to most generalist pests.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Caution: Toxicity

The entire plant, especially the seeds, is highly toxic. The seeds contain curcin, a toxic protein related to the ricin of castor. Ingestion of even a few seeds can be fatal. Ingestion causes severe burning in the throat, vomiting, bloody diarrhoea, abdominal pain, and in severe cases, seizures, kidney failure, and death. Handling the latex can cause skin dermatitis; contact with eyes causes severe irritation.

This plant has no safe household use. It should not be grown in homes with children or pet animals.

Ornamental value: The distinctive reddish foliage and bright red flowers make it attractive as an ornamental shrub in some tropical gardens.

Bellyache Bush is occasionally cultivated in gardens in Basti district, but is not widespread. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain. The plant is most visible during flowering (May–September). A first confirmed local record would be a photograph showing the distinctive red leaves and bright red flowers with place and date. Caution: Do not handle or ingest any part of this plant.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical America; cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

In India: Occasional cultivation in gardens; not naturalized.

In Uttar Pradesh: Rare; found only in cultivated gardens.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Very rare; found only where deliberately planted.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. The epithet gossypiifolia means "with cotton-like leaves," referring to the lobed leaf shape.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Malpighiales, family Euphorbiaceae (spurge family).

Phylogenetic notes. Jatropha gossypiifolia is closely related to the more widely cultivated Jatropha curcas (Physic Nut).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widely cultivated; not assessed for wild populations.
