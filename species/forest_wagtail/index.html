फॉरेस्ट वैगटेल / Forest Wagtail

Dendronanthus indicus (Gmelin, 1789) — Motacillidae

फॉरेस्ट वैगटेल — पूर्वी एशिया से सर्दियों में भारतीय उपमहाद्वीप के घने और छायादार वनों में आने वाला एक विशिष्ट, जमीन पर चलने वाला खंजन (वैगटेल) है। अन्य खंजनों के विपरीत, जो खुले पानी और मैदानों में अपनी पूंछ को ऊपर-नीचे हिलाते हैं, यह घने जंगलों और पुराने बागों के भीतर रहता है और अपनी पूंछ को अगल-बगल (दाएं-बाएं) हिलाता है। इसके सीने पर दोहरी काली पट्टी होती है—एक चौड़ा काला अर्धचंद्राकार पट्टा ऊपर और एक खंडित पट्टा नीचे। इसके पंखों पर चौड़ी पीली-सफेद पट्टियां होती हैं और यह जमीन के साथ-साथ पेड़ों की निचली डालियों पर भी आसानी से बैठता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
HabitSolitary or paired woodland wagtail of shaded forest floors and quiet trails
SizeSlender wagtail roughly the size of a [[white_browed_wagtail\White-browed Wagtail]]

Field tip: Look along shaded forest paths and stream banks. When walking ahead on a forest path, notice that it constantly sways its entire rear body and tail from side to side in a rhythmic swaying motion.

हिंदी:
यह छायादार रास्तों पर चलने वाला खंजन है जिसके सीने पर दो काली पट्टियां होती हैं।
इसकी सबसे खास बात यह है कि यह अपनी पूंछ को ऊपर-नीचे के बजाय अगल-बगल हिलाता है।
इसके पंखों पर चौड़ी सफेद-पीली धारियां होती हैं और यह पेड़ों की डालियों पर भी बैठता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1926):

MeasurementRange
Wing and TailWing 77 to 81 mm. ; tail 65 to 68 mm. (Baker 1926 as Dendronanthus indicus)

The Forest Wagtail is an unmistakable motacillid possessing several unique anatomical and plumage features. The upper plumage, crown, and mantle are uniform olive-brown, while the longer upper tail-coverts are darker blackish-brown.

A narrow yellowish-white supercilium extends from the eye to the nape (Baker 1926, p. 276). The wings are boldly patterned: the median and greater wing-coverts are black with broad yellowish-white tips, forming two conspicuous wing bars, and the flight feathers bear a broad yellowish-white central patch. The central tail feathers are olive-brown, while the outer pairs are extensively white.

The underparts are white, washed with pale sulfur-yellow. A dark brown lateral line runs down either side of the throat, joining a bold, solid black crescentic band across the upper breast; a second, narrower, often broken black band crosses the lower breast. The bill is dark horny-brown above and fleshy-white below. The irises are dark hazel. The legs and feet are purplish-flesh.

Vocalisations. A loud chirrup frequently repeated both when flying, running or perching (Baker 1926, p. 277).

हिंदी:
इसके पंखों की लंबाई 77 से 81 मिमी और पूंछ 65 से 68 मिमी होती है (बेकर 1926)।
इसके सीने पर दो काली धनुषाकार पट्टियां और पंखों पर चौड़ी सफेद धारियां होती हैं।
उड़ते, दौड़ते या बैठते समय यह लगातार 'चिरप' जैसी आवाज निकालता है (बेकर 1926)।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Dendronanthus indicus is an inhabitant of evergreen forest, open glades, forest paths and riversides, occasionally on the banks of rivers through open but well-wooded country (Baker 1926, p. 277). In Uttar Pradesh, published GBIF records in Lucknow occur in gardens and wooded groves.

It is not shy and will allow a close approach, alighting every hundred yards or so, running a few feet, flying on again and so on (Baker 1926, p. 277). Its signature behaviour is the rhythmic swaying of its tail and hindquarters continuously from side to side, a lateral movement distinct from the vertical pumping motion of typical Motacilla wagtails (Baker 1926, p. 277).

हिंदी:
यह पक्षी सदाबहार जंगलों, वन मार्गों, और नदियों के किनारों पर रहता है (बेकर 1926)।
यह इंसानों से बहुत नहीं डरता, कुछ दूरी पर उड़कर बैठता है और दौड़ता है (बेकर 1926)।
यह अपनी पूंछ और पिछले शरीर को निरंतर अगल-बगल (दाएं-बाएं) झुलाता है (बेकर 1926)।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

The Forest Wagtail is a non-breeding winter visitor to India (Baker 1926, p. 276); GBIF records in Uttar Pradesh fall between September and April. It does not breed in Uttar Pradesh.

Its breeding range covers eastern Siberia, northeastern China, Korea, and parts of the sub-Himalayan hills of northeastern India (Baker 1926, p. 276). It builds a neat cup nest of moss, fine roots, and leaves bound with cobwebs, placed on a horizontal tree branch (Baker 1926, p. 277). A small clutch of spotted greyish eggs is reared before migrating south for the winter.

हिंदी:
यह उत्तर प्रदेश में सर्दियों के दौरान सितंबर से अप्रैल तक प्रवास करता है।
यह यहां घोंसला नहीं बनाता; इसका प्रजनन काल गर्मियों में पूर्वी एशिया और चीन में होता है (बेकर 1926)।
वहां यह पेड़ों की डालियों पर मकड़ी के जालों से लिपटा सुंदर कटोरे जैसा घोंसला बनाता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

The Forest Wagtail feeds on invertebrates found in wooded habitats (Baker 1926, p. 277):

  • Small slugs and snails (Baker 1926, p. 277)
  • Tiny worms and all kinds of insects (Baker 1926, p. 277)

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in eastern Asia from southeastern Siberia to northern China and Korea; winters in South and Southeast Asia, from India and Sri Lanka to Indonesia.

In India: A winter visitor to India and Ceylon (Baker 1926, p. 276); published GBIF records in Uttar Pradesh occur from September to April.

In Uttar Pradesh: Documented by winter records in Lucknow, Lakhimpur Kheri, and Shravasti between September and April.

In Basti district / eastern UP: No published records from Basti or Ayodhya; known in the wider Awadh plain from records in Lucknow and Terai woodland.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Johann Friedrich Gmelin in 1789 in Systema Naturae (13th ed., vol. 1, pt. 2, p. 962), under the basionym Motacilla indica. The type locality is India.

Synonyms and historical names. Stuart Baker (1926, p. 276) treated the species under Dendronanthus indicus, noting its historic generic designation as Limonidromus.

Subspecies. Monotypic; no subspecies are recognized across its eastern Palearctic breeding range.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes (songbirds); family Motacillidae (wagtails and pipits); genus Dendronanthus.

Phylogenetic notes. Dendronanthus contains a single species, distinguished from the other wagtails by its tail, whose central pair of feathers is shorter than the lateral ones (Baker 1926, p. 275).

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC) by the IUCN Red List due to its immense breeding and wintering range and stable population.
