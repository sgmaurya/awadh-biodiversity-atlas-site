चाकी पर्चर व्याध पतंग / Chalky Percher

Brachydiplax farinosa Krüger, 1902 — Libellulidae

चाकी पर्चर व्याध पतंग — बारहमासी तालाबों, कमल के पोखरों और दलदलों में पाई जाने वाली एक छोटी और बहुत फुर्तीली असली व्याध पतंग (dragonfly) है। वयस्क नर का सीना और पेट सफेद चाक या हल्के नीले पाउडर (pruinescence) से ढका होता है, जिससे यह दूर से ही सफेद चमकता दिखता है। इसकी आँखें चमकीली हरी होती हैं और पिछले पंखों के आधार पर हल्का पीला धब्बा होता है। यह पानी के ऊपर उभरी टहनियों पर बैठकर छोटे कीड़ों का शिकार करती है।

Field tip: Look along sunny, vegetated ponds with abundant water lilies or lotus. A small dragonfly perched rigidly on the very tip of an exposed stick, its compact body completely coated in a pure chalky-white or light powdery-blue dust contrasting with brilliant green eyes, is the Chalky Percher.

हिंदी: कमल के तालाबों पर सूखी टहनियों के सिरे पर बैठने वाली इस छोटी पतंग का शरीर सफेद चाक के पाउडर जैसा दिखता है और इसकी आँखें गहरी हरी होती हैं।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given in the handbooks (Subramanian 2005; Fraser 1936):

Adult description:

Brachydiplax farinosa is a small, stoutly built libellulid dragonfly exhibiting dramatic developmental and sexual dimorphism (Fraser 1936). In teneral (young) males and females, the frons is metallic blue, the face is pale creamy-yellow, and the eyes are olive-brown (Subramanian 2005). The synthorax is initially dark metallic green or bronze-black, boldly marked with bright yellow stripes along the lateral sutures (Fraser 1936).

As the male matures, its entire body transforms: a dense, powdery, chalky-white to pale cerulean-blue pruinescence develops over the dorsal and lateral surfaces of the thorax and throughout abdominal segments 1 to 7 (Subramanian 2005). Terminal segments 8 to 10 remain predominantly black, bearing short, black, pointed anal appendages (Fraser 1936). The compound eyes become intense, glossy emerald-green (Subramanian 2005).

The adult female never develops chalky pruinescence (Fraser 1936). Her thorax retains its rich chocolate-brown and golden-yellow stripes, and her abdomen is black with broad, bright yellow paired dorsal spots on segments 1 through 8 (Subramanian 2005). In both sexes, the wings are broad, hyaline, and carry a distinct amber-yellow spot at the base of each hindwing, while the pterostigma is dark brownish-black (Fraser 1936).

Larval Stage निम्फ अवस्था

The aquatic naiad is short, stocky, and dark mottled brownish-grey, dwelling in muddy detritus and decaying leaf matter beneath floating lotus pads. The abdomen bears small dorsal hooks and short lateral spines, providing camouflage and defense against predatory fish.

हिंदी: नर का पेट 19 से 22 मिमी लंबा होता है। युवावस्था में यह पीला-काला होता है, लेकिन वयस्क होने पर सफेद पाउडर से ढक जाता है। मादा के शरीर पर पीली और काली धारियाँ बनी रहती हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Chalky Percher is a sun-loving, highly territorial dragonfly of stagnant freshwaters (Subramanian 2005). It prefers permanent village tanks, oxbow marshes, quiet river embayments, and irrigation reservoirs containing expansive beds of Nelumbo nucifera (Indian Lotus) and Nymphaea nouchali (Blue Water Lily) (Fraser 1936).

Adults are active throughout sunny hours (Subramanian 2005). Males select prominent vertical perches—such as dead reed stalks, protruding bamboo poles, or lotus seed pods—and defend a feeding and mating territory spanning several square metres. When perching, they adopt an obelisk posture under intense midday heat, raising the abdomen vertically to minimize sun exposure while drooping their wings forward (Subramanian 2005). Mating occurs rapidly on the wing or perched on shoreline bushes. Oviposition is exophytic: the female hovers low over open water amongst floating leaves, repeatedly dipping the tip of her abdomen to disperse eggs.

हिंदी: यह पतंग दिन भर तेज धूप में पानी के ऊपर निकली सूखी लकड़ियों पर बैठती है। दोपहर की गर्मी में यह अपने पेट को ऊपर की तरफ उठा लेती है ताकि शरीर पर कम धूप पड़े।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Females fly in hovering flight over calm water, tapping their abdomen against the surface film to shed hundreds of small eggs that settle into submerged vegetation. The eggs hatch after a few days into predatory nymphs. Naiad development lasts several weeks through multiple instars, feeding on aquatic insect larvae. At maturity, the nymph crawls up an emergent lotus stalk at sunrise to shed its exuvia and emerge as an adult dragonfly.

हिंदी: मादा पानी की सतह पर उड़ते हुए अंडे देती है। कुछ दिनों में अंडे फूटते हैं और पानी की तली में रहने वाला निम्फ कुछ हफ्तों बाद पौधे पर चढ़कर वयस्क पतंग में बदल जाता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Brachydiplax farinosa is an aerial and aquatic predator throughout its lifecycle (Subramanian 2005):

Adult diet:

  • Swarming wetland mosquitoes, including Culex quinquefasciatus (Southern House Mosquito) and Culex tritaeniorhynchus (Rice-field Mosquito).
  • Agricultural sap-feeders hovering around irrigation channels, such as Nilaparvata lugens (Brown Planthopper) and Nephotettix virescens (Green Leafhopper).
  • Non-biting midges (Chironomidae), small hoverflies, and tiny flying beetles.

Larval diet:

  • Mosquito larvae, bloodworms, small ephemeropteran nymphs, and aquatic oligochaetes.
हिंदी: यह पतंग हवा में उड़ने वाले मच्छरों, धान के फुदकों और छोटी मक्खियों को पकड़कर खाती है। इसका निम्फ पानी में रहने वाले मच्छरों के लार्वा को नष्ट करता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Natural predators documented in Gangetic plain freshwater ecosystems:

  • Predatory dragonflies: Larger anisopterans like Crocothemis servilia (Scarlet Skimmer) and Orthetrum sabina (Slender Skimmer) frequently prey upon perching perchers.
  • Spiders: Wetland orb-weavers (Araneidae) and fishing spiders ambush perching adults near the water surface.
  • Amphibians: Bullfrogs and skipper frogs (Euphlyctis cyanophlyctis Skittering Frog) catch low-flying adults.
  • Fish: Murrels and walking catfishes feed on bottom-dwelling nymphs in muddy tanks.
हिंदी: बड़ी शिकारी पतंगें, मकड़ियाँ और किनारे पर बैठे मेंढक इस सफेद पतंग को पकड़ लेते हैं। तालाब की मछलियाँ इसके निम्फ को खाती हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Natural insect pest suppression: The Chalky Percher is an active natural biological control agent in rural agricultural mosaics (Subramanian 2005). Because it hunts from exposed perches near water channels and rice paddies, it captures significant numbers of pest leafhoppers and planthoppers that damage cereal crops.

Public health benefit: Both adults and aquatic naiads feed heavily on culicine and anopheline mosquitoes, helping limit the vectors of malaria and encephalitis around rural villages (Subramanian 2005).

Traditional: No traditional medicinal, economic, or cultural uses are known.

Today: Valued by naturalists as a conspicuous, easily observed wetland bio-indicator.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Investigating the physical optical properties of chalky pruinescence in reflecting solar ultraviolet radiation.

हिंदी: यह पतंग फसलों को नुकसान पहुँचाने वाले कीड़ों और बीमारी फैलाने वाले मच्छरों को खाकर किसानों और ग्रामीणों को बहुत फायदा पहुँचाती है।

The Chalky Percher is expected across eastern Uttar Pradesh around perennial ponds, oxbow marshes, and irrigation reservoirs covered with lotus and water lilies. Look on the tips of protruding sticks and emergent vegetation in full sunshine from July through November. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. A photograph showing the chalky-white thorax, abdomen, and green eyes, submitted through the About page, would confirm the first local documentation for the district.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across the Oriental realm, occurring from India and Nepal through Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, southern China, and Indonesia (Fraser 1936).

In India: Recorded across peninsular India, the Western and Eastern Ghats, central India, the lower Himalayas, and eastward across the Gangetic plain to Assam and West Bengal (Prasad & Varshney 1995; Mitra 2002).

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded from wetland complexes, protected sanctuaries, and rural irrigation lakes across central and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a resident breeding species in perennial village tanks, oxbows, and canal backwaters.

हिंदी: यह भारत और दक्षिण-पूर्व एशिया में पाई जाती है। उत्तर प्रदेश के कमल वाले पुराने तालाबों और झीलों में यह आसानी से मिल सकती है।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Leopold Krüger in 1902 as Brachydiplax farinosa in Stettiner Entomologische Zeitung, 63, page 135. Type locality: "Sumatra" (Indonesia).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognized across its broad Indo-Malayan range.

Family and order placement. Order Odonata, suborder Anisoptera, family Libellulidae. The genus Brachydiplax Brauer, 1868, comprises small perching dragonflies characterized by compact bodies, dense male pruinescence, and broad wing bases.

Phylogenetic notes. Brachydiplax farinosa is closely related to B. sobrina and B. chalybea, distinguished by its intense chalky-white pruinescence and small amber hindwing basal markings (Subramanian 2005).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the global IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. It is a secure, widespread, and common species in wetlands throughout South and Southeast Asia.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Brachydiplax farinosa है और यह Libellulidae परिवार की सदस्य है। प्रकृति में इसका वास पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है।
