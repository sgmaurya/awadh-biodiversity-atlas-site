लेसर व्हाइटथ्रोट / Lesser Whitethroat

Curruca curruca (Linnaeus, 1758) — Sylviidae

लेसर व्हाइटथ्रोट — यह छोटी सर्दियों की चिड़िया मध्य एशिया से अक्टूबर में पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश आती है और अप्रैल तक रहती है। इसकी सबसे खास पहचान है काली कान की पट्टी (male में) और सफेद गले का धब्बा। यह झाड़ों और बाग-बगीचों में अकेली या छोटे झुंड में रहती है, तेजी से कूद-कूद कर कीड़े खोजती है।

Field tip: In scrubby habitat and garden margins in winter, look for a small grey-and-white warbler with a distinctive dark ear patch (male) hopping through low growth. The white throat and dark ear patch together are diagnostic. It moves quickly and sings a distinctive churring song.

Size: 12–13 cm long; wingspan 16–17 cm. Weight 8–15 g. A small, active warbler.

Plumage (male in winter): Crown grey; ear-coverts (lores) and cheek-stripe black or very dark grey, sharply contrasting with white throat and pale ear-covert borders. Back grey-brown; rump slightly warmer brown. Underparts white to pale cream; undertail-coverts white. Wings darker brown with paler feather edges. Tail graduated, dark brown with white outer rectrices.

Plumage (female): Duller overall. Crown brownish-grey; dark ear patch much less pronounced than in male. Throat and underparts cream-white. Lacks the sharp contrast of the male.

Bill: Small, pointed, dark.

Legs and feet: Pale; strong.

Eyes: Dark, small, alert.

Juveniles: Resemble females; duller still.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A loud, churring or rattling song, often delivered from exposed perches.; distinctive harsh quality.
  2. Call: Sharp chak or chat, often given as alarm.
  3. Contact calls: Soft whistle between pair members.
हिंदी: नर की तीव्र, कठोर "चर्र-चर्र" गान। तेज़ "चाट" आवाज़ जब चिंतित हो।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Gleaning forager: Lesser Whitethroat actively picks insects and small arthropods from foliage and branches, moving rapidly through dense vegetation. It feeds on:

  • Small insects (flies, gnats, small hymenoptera)
  • Spiders and small arthropods
  • Occasionally small berries in late winter

Social behaviour: Territorial during winter, but may form loose feeding flocks. Males are vocal and often visible, singing from exposed twigs.

Habitat preference: Scrub, thorny vegetation, garden margins, hedgerows, cultivation boundaries. Prefers semi-open country with scattered bushes.

Foraging technique: Hops and gleams along branches; explores under leaf cover; occasionally hovers to pick insects from foliage tips.

Predators: Small raptals (Eurasian Kestrel, Shrike), cats, snakes may take individuals.

Movement: Restless and active; males perform frequent sallies and song flights.

हिंदी: झाड़ों में कूद-कूद कर कीड़े खोजता है। गर्मियों में अकेला, सर्दियों में छोटे झुंड। तेजी से गलत और तेज आवाज़।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Absent during breeding season (April–August); returns to eastern UP from October and remains through April–May.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in late September–October; numbers peak in January–February; departure begins in March, with most gone by late April.

Courtship and breeding (elsewhere): Breeding grounds in central Asia. Males are territorial and vocal in spring before departure. Clutch of 4–5 eggs in a cup nest in dense shrubery.

Moult: Complete moult on breeding grounds before arrival; partial moult during winter stay.

Roosts: Roosting in dense shrubery or hedgerows; often solitary.

हिंदी: अक्टूबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। सर्दी में जोड़े बनते हैं। मध्य एशिया में घोंसला बनाता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Eurasian Kestrel, Merlin, Red-backed Shrike, Common Hoopoe (nest predator)
  • Mammalian predators: Domestic cat, weasel, occasionally mongoose
  • Nest parasites and eggs: Cuckoos may parasitize nests on breeding grounds
  • Parasites: Internal helminths and blood parasites
हिंदी: बाजें और बड़ी चिड़ियाँ शिकार करती हैं। घर की बिल्ली भी खतरा है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is prohibited.

Pest control: Consumes large numbers of small insects, gnats, and flies, providing natural pest control benefit in gardens.

Cultural relevance: Mentioned in classical literature as a winter visitor. In South Asia, known from historical ornithological records.

Scientific value: Population monitoring as an indicator of winter habitat quality; long-distance migration patterns important for understanding Central Asian flyways.

हिंदी: वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम 1972 के अनुसूची IV के अंतर्गत पूरी तरह संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from October to April, particularly in scrubland, gardens, and cultivation margins of Basti and Ayodhya districts. Published ornithological handbooks document this species in similar habitats across the Indo-Gangetic Plain in winter.

Habitat preference: Scrub, thorn forest, hedgerows, garden margins, cultivation boundaries with scattered vegetation.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from central Asian breeding grounds are settled.

Nearest published record: Birdlife International and regional ornithological surveys document this species in winter throughout the Indo-Gangetic region. Specific records from Uttar Pradesh wetland surveys include sightings in similar habitat types.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph or sound recording showing the bird clearly in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date), such as singing from a bush or gleaning in vegetation, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeding range extends from central Europe through the Caucasus, central Asia to western China. Winters in Africa and the Indian subcontinent. Two subspecies recognized.

In India: Distributed across the Indian subcontinent as a winter visitor (October–April). Occurs from the Himalayas south to the Deccan.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread in winter, particularly in cultivated areas, gardens, and scrubland of the plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor in scrub, hedgerows, and garden margins; expected on migration grounds rather than confirmed in local surveys.

Field rule: Lesser Whitethroat is a small, restless grey-and-white warbler with a black ear patch (male). If the bird is greenish with an eyebrow stripe, it is Hume's Leaf Warbler. If the bird is noticeably larger with rufous wing-coverts, it is Common Whitethroat (rare in eastern UP).

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae (10th edition), as Motacilla curruca. Type locality is Scandinavia (Sweden).

Subspecies. The nominate subspecies C. c. curruca (Linnaeus, 1758) is the form that winters in the Indian subcontinent, breeding from central Europe to the Altai Mountains.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Sylviidae (Old World warblers). The genus Curruca contains the whitethroats, recently split from Sylvia.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular work has placed Curruca as a distinct genus within the Sylviidae, separate from the larger Phylloscopus genus.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widely distributed across a large range. The species is adaptable to human-modified habitats. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
