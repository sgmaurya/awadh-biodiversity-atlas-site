सफेद सूंडी / White Grub

Holotrichia consanguinea Blanchard, 1851 — Scarabaeidae

सफेद सूंडी — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में गन्ने, मूंगफली और सब्जियों की फसलों की जड़ों को भारी नुकसान पहुंचाने वाला एक प्रमुख भूमिगत कीट (root pest) है। यह वास्तव में एक मटमैले रंग के भृंग (chafer beetle) का डिंभक (larva) है। इसका शरीर अर्ध-चंद्राकार या 'C' आकार (C-shaped body) का, मलाईदार सफेद (creamy-white) रंग का होता है, और इसका सिर सख्त व भूरे-लाल रंग का होता है। यह सूंडी जमीन के नीचे 2 से 10 सेंटीमीटर की गहराई में रहकर पौधों की जड़ों को खाती है, जिससे फसलें सूख जाती हैं। इसके वयस्क भृंग मानसून की पहली बारिश के बाद जून-जुलाई में जमीन से बाहर निकलते हैं और रात के समय नीम व बबूल के पेड़ों पर इकट्ठा होकर पत्तियां खाते हैं और संभोग करते हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
FormPlump, fleshy, C-shaped grub (larva) with three pairs of well-developed thoracic legs
ColourCreamy-white or translucent white body; the posterior end is swollen and dark (due to soil contents)
HeadProminent, hard, reddish-brown head capsule with strong, dark biting mandibles
Size20–35 mm in length; head capsule width 4.5–6.0 mm

BehaviourFound buried in agricultural soil near crop roots; coils tightly when exposed to light

Field tip: Dig 5–10 cm into the soil around a dying sugarcane or groundnut plant. If you find a plump, creamy-white C-shaped worm with a brown head and six legs near the front, it is a White Grub.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for late-instar grubs in sandy-loam soils of Basti):

MeasurementRange
Body length20–35 mm
Head capsule width4.5–6.0 mm

Larval body: The body is divided into a distinct head, three thoracic segments (each bearing a pair of legs), and ten abdominal segments. The skin is wrinkled and covered with sparse, stiff brown hairs. The dark digestive tract is visible through the translucent skin at the rear end.

Head capsule: The head is highly sclerotised (hardened), reddish-brown or dark yellow, and carries a pair of short, 4-segmented antennae. The mandibles are large, dark, and strong, designed for slicing through fibrous plant roots.

Thoracic legs: The legs are pale yellow, covered in fine brown hairs, and end in a small claw. They are used to crawl slowly through loose soil.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Subterranean root-eater.

  • Feeding habits: The grub moves horizontally through the soil, locate plant roots, and feed continuously. Early instars eat fine root hairs, while mature grubs consume the main taproots, severing the plant's nutrient supply.
  • Depth regulation: Moves vertically in the soil column based on moisture and temperature. In dry periods or winter, it burrows deeper (up to 30–50 cm) to seek moisture, returning to the surface (2–10 cm) during the warm monsoon.
  • Light sensitivity: Highly photophobic. When exposed to sunlight by ploughing, it curls into a tight ball and attempts to dig back into the soil.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Life Cycle of Holotrichia consanguinea

Holotrichia consanguinea has a single-generation annual life cycle (univoltine), closely tied to the monsoon calendar of eastern UP:

  1. Adult Emergence (June–July): The adult stage is a dark brown, oval-shaped chafer beetle (18–22 mm long). Following the first heavy monsoon shower in June, adult beetles emerge from the soil at dusk (around 7:30 PM) in millions. They swarm to nearby host trees (especially neem, ber, and babool) to feed on leaves, mate, and return to the soil before dawn.
  2. Egg-laying (July): Mated females burrow 10–15 cm into loose, sandy-loam agricultural soil and lay 20–40 white, oval eggs individually.
  3. Larval Phase (July–October): Eggs hatch in 8–10 days. The grubs pass through three growth stages (instars) over 8–10 weeks:
  • First Instar: Feeds on decaying organic matter and fine rootlets.
  • Second Instar: Begins feeding on crop roots.
  • Third Instar (August–September): The most destructive phase; consumes large taproots, causing crop death.
  1. Pupation (October–November): The mature grub burrows deeper (30–40 cm) into the soil, constructs an earthen chamber, and pupates.
  2. Overwintering: The pupa transforms into an adult beetle in 15–20 days. However, the adult remains dormant inside the subterranean earthen chamber through the winter and dry summer, waiting for the next monsoon rains to emerge.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Herbivorous root-feeder (Grub):

  • Crop hosts: Roots of sugarcane, groundnut (sarson relative crop), maize ([FLR-0054 Maize]), sorghum, pearl millet, and garden vegetables.
  • Weed hosts: Roots of various grasses and common weeds.

Herbivorous leaf-feeder (Adult Beetle):

  • Tree hosts: Leaves of Neem ([FLR-0009 Neem]), Ber ([FLR-0046 Indian Jujube]), and Babool ([FLR-0047 Babool]).

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Common Mynas ([BRD-0006 Common Myna]), Cattle Egrets ([BRD-0029 Cattle Egret]), and Crows ([BRD-0027 House Crow]) follow tractors during ploughing to prey on exposed grubs.
  • Mammals: Wild boars ([MAM-0005 Wild Boar]) and shrews ([MAM-0015 Asian House Shrew]) dig up grubs from soil.
  • Insect predators: Predatory ground beetles and centipedes ([MYR-0001 Indian Centipede]).
  • Fungal pathogens: Metarhizium anisopliae (green muscardine fungus) infects and kills grubs in damp soil.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Severe agricultural pest.

  • Economic damage: White grub is a major limiting factor for groundnut and sugarcane farming in Basti. A density of even 1–2 grubs per square metre can cause 80–100% crop loss in sandy soils, turning fields yellow and dry.
  • Traditional control: Farmers collect adult beetles by shaking host trees (neem/ber) at night during their emergence swarm, dropping them into water mixed with kerosene.
  • Ploughing control: Deep summer ploughing exposes overwintering pupae and beetles to solar heat and predatory birds, significantly reducing the pest load for the next season.
  • Biological control: Application of entomopathogenic nematodes (EPN) and neem cake into the soil helps suppress grub populations naturally.

Commonly found in the sandy loam soils of the Ghaghra River basin blocks ( Vikramjot and Vikrampur) in Basti district. Sighted in high numbers during July–August in groundnut and sugarcane fields. The adult beetles are a common sight swarming around streetlights on warm, humid evenings in June.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia, primarily endemic to India, Pakistan, and Nepal.

In India: Found throughout the northern plains, western semi-arid zones, and peninsular highlands. Highly abundant in states with sandy-loam soils.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant in all agricultural districts, particularly in sugarcane and groundnut belts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common resident in all rural farming blocks.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Émile Blanchard described the species as Holotrichia consanguinea in 1851 in Catalogue de la collection entomologique. Classe des insectes. Ordre des coléoptères (Vol. 1, p. 183). The type locality was Northern India. The genus name Holotrichia combines the Greek holos (entire) and thrix (hair), referring to the hairy body of the grubs and beetles. The specific name consanguinea is Latin for "related by blood" or "closely akin," describing its close morphological similarity to European melolonthids.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Coleoptera, suborder Polyphaga, superfamily Scarabaeoidea, family Scarabaeidae, subfamily Melolonthinae (cockchafers).

Phylogenetic notes. The subfamily Melolonthinae contains phytophagous scarab beetles whose larvae are soil-dwelling root feeders. The genus Holotrichia is highly diverse in the Oriental region, with species boundaries historically defined by male genitalia characteristics.

IUCN status rationale. Not assessed by the IUCN Red List. As a widely distributed pest species with large populations in agricultural landscapes, it is not of conservation concern.
