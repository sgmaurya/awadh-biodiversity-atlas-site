कैटेनेरिया / Nematode-parasitic Chytrid

Catenaria anguillulae Sorokin, 1876 — Catenariaceae

कैटेनेरिया — मिट्टी में रहने वाली अत्यंत सूक्ष्म फफूँद जो सूत्रकृमि (नेमेटोड) को खा जाती है। इसे नंगी आँखों से नहीं देख सकते; माइक्रोस्कोप से ही दिखती है। जब कृमि पानी की बूँदों में चलता है, तब इस फफूँद के छोटे-छोटे बीज (ज़ूस्पोर) उसके शरीर को छेद देते हैं। अंदर बढ़ते हैं और कृमि को पूरी तरह बर्बाद कर देते हैं। यह मिट्टी के कृमियों को नियंत्रित करने का एक प्राकृतिक उपाय है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
VisibilityNot visible to the naked eye; a microscope and a nematode culture are needed
Zoospore appearanceMotile, with a single posterior flagellum; swims for an hour or more and is drawn to the mouth, excretory pore and anus of a nematode (Deacon & Saxena 1997)
Infection siteInside nematode body cavity (hemocoel); visible as hyphal filaments and sporangia within host tissue
SporangiaRounded, formed in chains along the hypha inside the nematode — the chain is the Catenaria of the name (Couch 1945)
MyceliumFine hyphal network inside nematode; appears as thread-like structures
In cultureWhite to pale mycelium on minimal nutrient media; slow growth; not visible to unaided eye
In nematode cultureInfected nematodes appear swollen or discolored; hyperparasitized nematodes shrivel and die

Field tip: Catenaria anguillulae cannot be identified in the field without a microscope. Detection requires:

  1. Extract nematodes from soil (baiting or extraction techniques)
  2. Examine nematode cultures under the microscope
  3. Look for hyphal filaments and sporangia inside living or dead nematodes
  4. Observe zoospores if culture is kept moist and warm

This organism is identified by laboratory methods, not field observation.

Morphology आकारिकी

Fungal structures (microscopic):

StructureAppearance
ZoosporeOne posterior flagellum; swims in water films; settles and encysts on the nematode's cuticle, preferring the body openings (Deacon & Saxena 1997)
CystRounded; germinates with a germ tube that enters the host
AppressoriumSpecialized penetration structure; produces enzymes to breach nematode cuticle
MyceliumHyaline, unbranched to sparsely branched hyphae; grows through nematode body cavity (hemocoel)
SporangiaIn chains along the hypha inside the host, each releasing zoospores through an exit tube (Couch 1945)
RhizoidRoot-like anchoring structure; attaches thallus to substrate (nematode tissue or soil particle)
Cell wallChitin-based; characteristic of true fungi, distinguishing from oomycetes (water moulds)

Culture characteristics:

  • Grows slowly on nutrient-poor media; requires specific conditions
  • Produces mycelium and sporangia in moist culture
  • Often difficult to maintain in culture; requires live or recently dead nematodes

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Nematode parasite: Catenaria anguillulae is an endoparasitoid fungus, infecting multiple nematode taxa including root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne), cyst nematodes (Heterodera, Globodera), and free-living soil nematodes.

Infection mechanism:

  1. Zoospore production: Sporangia in soil or infected nematode tissue release motile zoospores
  2. Host encounter: Zoospores swim in soil water films and are attracted to the nematode's body openings (Deacon & Saxena 1997)
  3. Encystment: Upon contact with nematode cuticle, zoospore encysts
  4. Penetration: Appressorium grows and penetrates nematode cuticle using enzymatic degradation
  5. Colonization: Mycelium invades and grows through nematode hemocoel; consumes internal organs
  6. Sporangium formation: Thallus develops sporangia containing new zoospores
  7. Host lysis: Infected nematode dies; sporangia rupture, releasing zoospores for next infection cycle

Ecological role in soil:

  • Natural nematode population regulator; particularly significant for Meloidogyne control
  • In Indian rice fields it was among the fungi found on root galls of the rice root-knot nematode, more often in heavily galled fields (Singh, Jaiswal, Kumar & Kumar 2007)
  • More effective in moist soils with high nematode density
  • Contributes to suppressive soils (fields where pest nematode populations remain low despite crop susceptibility)

Biocontrol potential: Catenaria is a candidate biological control agent, particularly for root-knot nematodes in high-value crops.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Mycelial persistence: Persists in soil as mycelium or resting structures; colonizes nematode corpses and organic matter
  • Infection cycle: A zoospore settles on a nematode, encysts and enters; the hypha grows through the body
  • Inside host: The mycelium consumes the nematode's tissues
  • Sporangium maturation: Sporangia form in chains along the hypha and each releases zoospores through an exit tube (Couch 1945)
  • Zoospore release: Upon hydration or moisture increase, sporangia rupture; zoospores released
  • Dispersal: Zoospores swim only in water films, so the fungus moves with water and with its hosts; resting sporangia carry it through dry spells (Sparrow 1960)
  • Reinfection: Released zoospores seek new nematode hosts; cycle repeats in moist conditions
  • Dormancy: In dry soil, mycelium enters dormancy; resting structures persist until moisture returns

Seasonal activity: Most active during monsoon (June–October) when soil moisture is high; reduced activity in dry season but mycelium remains viable.

Associated Species and Decay Role संबंधित प्रजातियाँ और विघटन की भूमिका

Host range:

  • Meloidogyne species (root-knot nematodes) — primary target; multiple species parasitized
  • Heterodera and Globodera species (cyst nematodes)
  • Xiphinema dagger nematodes (Jaffee 1986)
  • Free-living soil nematodes

Related biocontrol agents in the atlas:

  • Trichoderma — soil fungus; antagonist of plant pathogens; often co-occurs with Catenaria
  • Beauveria — entomopathogen; different host range (insects vs. nematodes); different ecological niche

Interactions in soil: both are part of the community of a living soil; whether they help or hinder each other has not been studied.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Root-knot nematode problem in eastern UP:

Root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne species) are a significant pest of vegetables, pulses, and field crops in eastern UP. Farmers observe:

  • Stunted plants with poor growth
  • Root galling (small lumps on roots)
  • Wilting during drought

Biocontrol potential:

Catenaria anguillulae offers a natural approach to nematode management:

  • Mechanism: Parasitizes nematode adults and juveniles; reduces population density
  • Sustainability: Self-perpetuating in soil; no repeated application needed (unlike chemicals)
  • Compatibility: Safe for soil microorganisms; enhances natural soil suppression
  • Limitations: Field efficacy is variable; requires specific conditions (moisture, temperature, host availability)

Current status:

  • Commercial production of Catenaria products remains limited globally; most research is in laboratory or controlled settings
  • Farmer adoption is minimal; chemical nematicides remain dominant
  • Potential for future development as awareness of biological control increases

Recommended practice:

  1. Soil management: Increase organic matter (compost, crop residue); this enhances natural Catenaria populations
  2. Crop rotation: Include non-host crops (cereals, grasses) to reduce nematode pressure; allows Catenaria populations to build
  3. Resistant varieties: Choose nematode-resistant crop varieties where available (tomato, cotton, pulse varieties)
  4. Monitoring: Observe plant health and root symptoms; if stunting appears, dig roots to check for galling
  5. Research participation: If available locally, farmers can participate in trials of Catenaria-based products through agricultural research stations

Note on pesticides: No specific chemical control for Catenaria management is recommended here. Nematicide use, while effective short-term, kills soil microbes including Catenaria, reducing long-term soil health. Integrated approaches combining resistance, rotation, and biological control are more sustainable.

Catenaria anguillulae is expected in soil throughout eastern UP, particularly in fields with high nematode populations and moist conditions. It is most likely to be detected in monsoon-season samples (June–October) when soil moisture is high. Laboratory detection requires:

  • Soil nematode extraction (Baermann funnel or centrifugation)
  • Microscopic examination of nematode populations for parasitic hyphae and sporangia
  • Incubation of infected nematodes to observe zoospore release

Documentation of Catenaria in farmer fields would require collaboration with a soil microbiologist or agricultural research institution. Records are other people's published research.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread in temperate and tropical soils worldwide; recorded from Europe, Asia, Africa, and Americas.

In India: Recorded from agricultural soils, including the root galls of rice (Singh et al. 2007).

In Uttar Pradesh: GBIF holds records from the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: GBIF holds records from the core districts of this atlas; the fungus is expected wherever nematodes and moisture are.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Sorokin (1876) from the vinegar eelworm Anguillula, hence the name. The genus Catenaria holds a handful of species, most of them parasites of nematodes or other small animals.

Taxonomic history. Couch (1945) moved the genus from the Chytridiales to the Blastocladiales on the structure of its zoospores; molecular work now places the Blastocladiomycota as a phylum of its own among the early-diverging true fungi (James, Porter & Martin 2014). Older texts call it a "water mould", a name that belongs to the unrelated oomycetes.

Subspecies. No subspecies recognized; C. anguillulae is cosmopolitan.

Family and order placement. Phylum Blastocladiomycota, order Blastocladiales, family Catenariaceae.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. This is a widespread soil organism with no threatened status; abundance varies with environmental conditions and nematode availability.
