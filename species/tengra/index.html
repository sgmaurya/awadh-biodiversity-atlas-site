टेंगरा / Tengra Catfish

Mystus tengara (Hamilton, 1822) — Bagridae

टेंगरा — यह एक छोटी, तेंदुए-सी धारीदार देशी कैटफिश है जो नहरों, पोखरों और गड्ढों में मिलती है। इसकी लंबाई महज़ 5–8 सेंटीमीटर होती है, पर स्वाद अच्छा होता है। इसका शरीर काले-भूरे रंग का होता है, जिस पर सफ़ेद या हल्के धारियाँ होती हैं, जैसे तेंदुए की चमड़ी। इसके सिर से तीन दीर्घ तार निकले होते हैं — मुँह के चारों ओर के सेंसर। यह रात को ज़्यादा सक्रिय है, मिट्टी खोदता है और कीड़े, पानी की घास और छोटी चीजें खाता है। छोटी मछली होने के बाद भी यह काफ़ी मज़ेदार भोजन है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

BarbelsThree pairs: one on upper jaw (maxillary), two on lower jaw; long and sensory

Field tip: In clear water bodies, look for small, striped catfish with prominent barbels. The distinctive light bars or stripes on a darker body are immediately diagnostic. The three pairs of barbels (which may be as long as the body) are used to feel the bottom while foraging. Found in both flowing water (streams) and still water (ponds).

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: छोटी कैटफिश, तेंदुए जैसी धारियाँ। तीन जोड़ी तार, सफ़ेद धारियाँ। रेत-पत्थर में छिपी रहती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Body structure: Small, elongate catfish with a flattened dorsal profile. Skin is scaleless and covered with mucus, giving it a slippery texture. The body is laterally compressed posteriorly.

Head: Flattened, width greater than height. Eyes are small, positioned laterally and dorsally. The mouth is subterminal (slightly below the head), adapted for bottom feeding.

Barbels: Three pairs of barbels are sensory organs used for tactile and chemical detection: the maxillary pair (upper jaw) is long and moveable; the outer mental pair (outer lower jaw) and inner mental pair (inner lower jaw) are shorter. These barbels are covered with taste buds and are used to explore the sediment while feeding.

Fins: Dorsal fin small, with 6–7 rays; adipose fin present and prominent. Pectoral and anal fins are rounded and soft-rayed.

Coloration: The distinctive pattern of light stripes or bars running vertically along the body on a darker background is the key field character. The pattern may fade with age.

Sexual dimorphism: Sexual dimorphism is minimal; males may be slightly smaller and have more pronounced genital papilla.

हिंदी: छोटी, पतली, धारीदार। तीन जोड़ी तार, बिना तराज़ू। छोटी डोरल और एडिपोज़ फिन। रात को सक्रिय।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Benthic omnivore. Mystus tengara is a small benthic catfish that inhabits the bottom of freshwater habitats. It is primarily nocturnal, hiding under rocks, vegetation, and in muddy banks during the day and emerging at dusk to forage. The fish uses its barbels to probe the substrate, feeling for food items.

Feeding behaviour. The species feeds on a variety of food items: small invertebrates (chironomid larvae, ostracods, small snails), algae, plant detritus, and organic sediment. It has been observed feeding on aquatic insect larvae and small fish eggs.

Social behaviour. Mystus tengara is solitary or found in small groups; it shows no complex schooling behaviour but may aggregate in areas with high food availability or suitable habitat.

Habitat preference. The species is found in both flowing (streams and rivers) and still water (ponds, tanks) habitats. It prefers areas with rocky or vegetated substrates where cover is available.

Ecological role. In freshwater ecosystems, the species is a minor component of the benthic food web, feeding on small invertebrates and serving as prey for larger fish and piscivorous birds.

Reproduction. Details of reproduction in wild populations are poorly documented, but in captivity, the species spawns in response to monsoon-like conditions (simulated by temperature and water level changes). Spawning is preceded by courtship, and eggs are adhesive, laid on substrates.

Seasonal pattern in eastern UP:

  • June–August (monsoon): Spawning; rising water levels trigger reproduction
  • August–November: Growth of juveniles; abundance of food in floodplain habitats
  • December–May: Reduced reproductive activity; overwintering in deeper refugia
  • Year-round: Feeding and growth in suitable habitats

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Omnivorous diet:

  • Small aquatic invertebrates (chironomid larvae, ostracods, copepods)
  • Small snails and other molluscs
  • Algae and plant detritus
  • Fish eggs and larval fish
  • Organic sediment and mud

The species shows flexibility in diet and will consume whatever suitable food items are available in its habitat.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Larger fish species (catfish, carps)
  • Wading birds (kingfishers, herons)
  • Aquatic snakes
  • Fishing by humans

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food fish. Mystus tengara is a food fish of local importance in eastern UP. Though small, it is esteemed for its flavour and is often preferred in local cuisine. It is captured using small nets, rod and line, and traps in ponds, streams, and paddy fields. The price is modest compared to larger fish, but the species' abundance and ease of capture make it economically significant for subsistence fisheries.

Aquaculture potential. The species has been considered for small-scale aquaculture in ponds and tanks in South Asia, though it has not achieved the commercial status of larger carp species. Its small size limits market appeal, but its adaptability to pond conditions and ready market make it a candidate for polyculture systems (mixed with other species).

Indicator of ecosystem health. The presence of Mystus tengara in freshwater habitats indicates a healthy benthic ecosystem and suitable water quality conditions for small-bodied fish species.

Harmlessness. The species poses no threat to humans or agricultural systems; it is entirely beneficial as a food source and indicator of ecosystem health.

हिंदी: छोटी पर स्वादिष्ट मछली। गरीब परिवार इसे पकड़ते हैं और खाते हैं। तालाबों में पाली जा सकती है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indo-Gangetic plains from Afghanistan through northern India and Bangladesh.

In India: Widespread in rivers, streams, and ponds of northern India, particularly in the Ganges and Brahmaputra drainage basins.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in all freshwater habitats; common in rivers, streams, and ponds throughout the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Widespread and abundant in all freshwater habitats; one of the most common small catfish species.

Field rule: A small, striped catfish 5–8 cm long with three pairs of prominent barbels and distinctive vertical light bars or stripes on a darker body, found in ponds and streams, is Mystus tengara; microscopic examination of gill raker count and spine morphology confirms identification.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Hamilton in 1822 in his enumeration of Indian fishes (An Account of the Fishes Found in the Rivers and Lakes of India and Bangladesh). The species epithet tengara is derived from the local Bengali/Hindi name for the fish.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Siluriformes (catfishes), family Bagridae (bagrid catfishes), subfamily Bagrinae.

Phylogenetic notes. The genus Mystus is a diverse genus of small to medium catfishes distributed across South and Southeast Asia. Mystus tengara is one of the smaller members of the genus.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Widespread in suitable habitats throughout its range; no conservation concerns. Stable populations in natural habitats and some evidence of successful aquaculture.
