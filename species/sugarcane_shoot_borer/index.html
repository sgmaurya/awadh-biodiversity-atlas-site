गन्ने का तना छेदक / Sugarcane Early Shoot Borer

Chilo infuscatellus (Snellen, 1890) — Crambidae

गन्ने का तना छेदक — गन्ने के कोमल शूट के अंदर घुसकर रहने वाली एक छोटी सूंडी, जो तने को खोखला कर देती है और शूट सूख जाता है। कीटनाशक से सताया गया गन्ने का पुराना दुश्मन, जो अब पाँच सालों में एक बार प्रकोप ले आता है। बारिश के मौसम में शुरू होता है, गर्मियों में सो जाता है। गन्ने को अगर इस सूंडी ने संक्रमित कर दिया तो साल भर ठीक होने में लगता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Adult mothSmall, whitish to pale tan moth; wingspan 20–25 mm; wings with faint patterns or scales
Larva (sundi)Pale whitish or cream-coloured caterpillar, 12–15 mm; smooth, in the stem interior
Entry signSmall round hole (2–3 mm) on the shoot surface; fresh frass (sawdust-like) at entrance
Damage symptomShoot becomes yellow, wilts, and eventually dies from the top down ("dead heart")
Plant stage affectedYoung plants with 4–6 leaves (most vulnerable); less common in mature stools

Field tip: Soon after the monsoon planting, scout young sugarcane plants regularly. Look for shoots that have suddenly turned yellow from the top while the rest of the plant is green. Part the leaf sheath: if you find a small hole with sawdust-like frass, and a pale caterpillar inside, it is the shoot borer. The earlier the infestation is found, the easier it is to manage.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बारिश के बाद गन्ने के कोमल शूट देखो। एक शूट पीला पड़ गया, बाकी हरे हैं? पत्ती की म्यान को अलग करो। छोटा-सा छेद, उसके आसपास मिट्टी जैसा मल, अंदर सफ़ेद सूंडी। बस, ये है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Adult moth: Small, delicate moth with pale whitish, cream, or tan coloured wings. Forewings may show faint darker patterns or reticulation. Hindwings are pale. Body is covered with fine scales. Antennae are thread-like. Active during dawn and dusk.

Egg: Smooth, oval, pale, laid singly or in clusters on leaf surfaces, particularly on the abaxial (lower) surface of leaf sheaths of young plants.

Larval stage: Four to five instars. Larvae are pale whitish to cream-coloured, almost transparent, with a small brown head. Body is smooth and slightly wrinkled. The larva is cryptic, living inside the stem and burrow systems, making it difficult to detect without dissection.

हिंदी: छोटा, सफेद, पारदर्शी पतंगा। सूंडी भी सफेद, अंदर तने के। अंडे छोटे, पत्तियों पर।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A specialist borer that lives most of its life hidden within the sugarcane shoot. Newly hatched larvae move along the leaf surface and locate a susceptible point of entry near the leaf attachment. The larva burrows directly through the shoot, creating galleries within the stem. As the larva feeds, it consumes the internal tissues, and the affected shoot yellows and dies (the "dead heart" symptom). The larva pupates inside the damaged shoot or in adjacent plant tissue. In most of eastern UP, the insect is univoltine or bivoltine (one to two generations per year), with most activity during the monsoon and early post-monsoon period.

The insect's cryptic life history makes control difficult; preventive measures (clean seed cane, removal of damaged shoots) are more effective than curative sprays. Natural enemies include small parasitoid wasps and a few ground beetles, but these rarely provide complete control in fields with heavy infestations.

हिंदी: शूट के अंदर सारा समय। शूट में सुरंग खोदता है। शूट मर जाता है। सर्दी में सो जाता है, गर्मी शुरू होते ही ग़ायब। कीटनाशक से बचा रहता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Sugarcane Sugarcane: the primary and almost exclusive host
  • Wild sugarcane species (Saccharum spontaneum, Saccharum arundinaceum): occasionally infested
  • Maize and sorghum: very rarely infested; not a preferred host
  • No documented feeding on common agricultural weeds or other crops

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid wasps: Trichogramma species (egg parasitoids); Chelonus, Apanteles, and other larval parasitoids
  • Predators: ground beetles (Carabidae), spiders, some birds that probe into damaged shoots
  • Entomopathogenic nematodes: Steinernema and Heterorhabditis species under moist conditions
  • Natural predators of pupae: soil-dwelling beetles and centipedes

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Historically a major pest. The sugarcane early shoot borer was a destructive pest in the 1960s–1980s, but repeated broad-spectrum insecticide spraying caused its natural enemies to collapse, followed by a resurgence cycle. With the reduction in indiscriminate spraying, the pest has become less of a problem in many regions, though it remains present. Outbreaks still occur sporadically, especially in regions with poor field sanitation or high populations in the preceding season.

What works (Traditional, Today, Research direction):

  • Traditional: Removal and destruction of affected shoots (removal of "dead hearts" by hand or by pruning). Crop rotation to prevent buildup in the field. Flooding fields briefly to expose pupae to predators.
  • Today: Use of clean seed cane (cane without egg-bearing leaves from infested fields). Early detection and removal of infested shoots. Maintenance of ratoons only when pest pressure is low. If chemical control is needed, a selective spray (spinosad or neem) applied to young plants during the peak oviposition period (June–July). Deep summer ploughing to expose and kill overwintering pupae.
  • Research direction (a lead, not a result): Conservation of native parasitoids through minimizing insecticide use. Breeding for varietal tolerance (most modern varieties show some level of resistance). The village-level practice of removing damaged plants and replanting during the monsoon is effective and costs little.
हिंदी: पुराना दुश्मन, अब कम। मरे शूट तोड़ देना, स्वच्छ बीज गन्ना लगाना। गर्मी में जुताई गहरी करना। बारिश में छिड़काव सिर्फ़ नई पौध पर, जहाँ समस्या दिखे। ततैयों को बचाना अहम।

Expected in sugarcane fields throughout Basti district, particularly in areas with a history of heavy infestation or poor field sanitation. Incidence is highest in fields planted during the monsoon (June–July), when populations are active. A simple record of dead-heart shoots observed in a sugarcane field, with date and location, would be a valuable first local confirmation.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: India, Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia; particularly abundant in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

In India: All sugarcane-growing regions, especially in the Indo-Gangetic plain and the Deccan plateau.

In Uttar Pradesh: Throughout sugarcane-growing districts, with particular concentration in the eastern plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all sugarcane fields. Incidence varies seasonally, peaking during monsoon and early post-monsoon plantings.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many dead-heart shoots should be removed from an infested field to reduce population carryover to the next crop? / संक्रमित खेत से कितने मरे शूट निकालने से अगली फसल में जनसंख्या कम रहती है? — Compare removal intensity and monitor next season's infestation.
  2. What is the incidence of early shoot borer in sugarcane fields planted at different times during the monsoon? — Compare fields planted in June, July, and August.
  3. Which parasitoid wasps emerge from sugarcane shoots that contain living larvae collected from the field? — Collect infested shoots and rear them indoors.
  4. Does deep summer ploughing (ploughing at 20+ cm depth) reduce the overwintering pupae survival and the next season's shoot borer population? — Monitor paired ploughed and unploughed fields.
  5. How long does the larva take to complete its development in sugarcane shoots at different stem diameters? — Collect young and mature shoots, rear larvae indoors, and record development time.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Snellen in 1890 in Tijdschrift voor Entomologie 33: 183–184. The genus Chilo was erected by Zincken in 1818 and includes several crop-damaging stalk borers.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Crambidae (grass moths), subfamily Acentropinae.

Phylogenetic notes. Chilo infuscatellus is one of several Chilo species that attack sugarcane and wild grass stems. It is closely related to C. partellus, which attacks maize and sorghum.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Like most agricultural pests, it is not assessed by the IUCN Red List.
