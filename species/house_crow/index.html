कौआ / House Crow

Corvus splendens Vieillot, 1817 — Corvidae

कौआ (घरेलू कौआ) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के ग्रामीण और शहरी दोनों क्षेत्रों में पाया जाने वाला सबसे बुद्धिमान और पर्यावरण को गहराई से प्रभावित करने वाला निवासी पक्षी है। यह इंसानी बस्तियों के आसपास एक प्रमुख अपमार्जक (scavenger) के रूप में कार्य करता है जो कूड़े-कचरे को साफ़ करने में मदद करता है। इसकी मुख्य पहचान इसका स्लेटी-धूसर रंग का कॉलर (grey collar) है जो इसकी गर्दन और छाती पर होता है और इसके बाकी चमकीले काले शरीर से साफ़ तौर पर अलग दिखता है। यह एक अत्यंत चतुर अवसरवादी पक्षी है जो अन्य छोटे पक्षियों (जैसे पंडुक, दहियर और लटोरा) के घोंसले से अंडे और बच्चों को चुराकर खाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeMedium-sized bird (40–43 cm total length); slender and long-legged
ColourationSlate-grey neck, nape, and upper breast; rest of the body is glossy, jet-black
BillMedium-sized, stout, and black with a slight arch at the tip
ForeheadFlat forehead forming a smooth slope towards the base of the bill
BehaviourHighly vocal, inquisitive, bold, and quick to alarm the flock; hops and walks
CallHarsh, dry, and flat caw: kaaa-kaaa (shorter and flatter than Jungle Crow)

Field tip: Look near domestic dump yards or village tea stalls. They are highly active and will approach humans for food scraps, cocking their heads to observe before seizing food.

Plumage: The crown, forehead, throat, and breast are deep glossy black with blue and purple iridescence. The back of the head (nape), neck, and upper chest form a distinct, contrasting band of pale slate-grey (the collar). The rest of the body, including the wings, lower belly, and tail, is deep glossy black with strong metallic sheen.

Bare parts: The bill is heavy, black, and surrounded by stiff black rictal bristles at the base. The eyes are dark brown. The legs and feet are black.

Vocalisations

House Crows possess a wide range of vocal signals.

  1. Contact and Flight Call: A dry, harsh, flat caw: kaaa-kaaa, repeated at varying speeds.
  2. Alarm Call: A rapid, high-pitched kow-kow-kow, used to mobilize the flock to mob a predator.
  3. Rattle/Click Call: A strange metallic clicking or rattling sound, made during courtship or close social interactions.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Opportunistic omnivore. House Crows are critical scavengers in the Gangetic ecosystem.

  • Scavenger: They remove human refuse and animal carcasses, preventing disease outbreak.
  • Nest predator: They raid nests of other birds (such as [BRD-0025 Laughing Dove], Baya Weaver, and Oriental Magpie-Robin) to consume eggs and hatchlings.
  • Agricultural predator: They feed on crop seedlings, sown grains, and ripening fruits, but also consume crop-damaging insects.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Breeding season: June to August, coinciding with the hot pre-monsoon and monsoon.
  • Nesting: They build a robust nest in tall trees (neem, mango, or peepal), often near human homes.
  • Nest structure: A large cup of twigs, wire pieces, and hair, constructed high up in a tree fork.
  • Parasitism: Their nests are frequently parasitized by the Asian Koel (Eudynamys scolopaceus).
  • Clutch size: 3 to 5 pale blue-green eggs with dark brown speckles.
  • Incubation: 15–17 days, primarily by the female.
  • Fledging: Nestlings are fed insects and meat scraps; they fledge in about 21–28 days.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary food items:

  • Insects: Grasshoppers, locusts, termites, and beetles.
  • Small animals: Lizards, rodents, frogs, and fish.
  • Refuse: Leftover human food, grains, and kitchen waste.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Raptors: Large eagles and owls hunt them at night.
  • Asian Koel: Parasitizes nests, forcing crows to raise Koel chicks.
  • Reptiles: Large tree snakes (Ptyas mucosa) prey on nests.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Sanitation assistant. In rural Basti, they act as primary sanitation agents by consuming food waste and carcass leftovers. However, their high populations can depress the nesting success of other small insectivorous garden birds due to heavy nest predation.

They are highly intelligent and can remember individual human faces, especially those who throw stones at them.

Abundant throughout Basti district. They gather in large numbers around local vegetable and fish markets (sabzi mandi). They are observed nesting in tall mango (Mangifera indica) and neem (Azadirachta indica) trees on the edges of villages. They are also known for actively mobbing stray dogs, cats, and birds of prey.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Indian Subcontinent, Southern Iran, and Western Myanmar; introduced and established in parts of the Middle East, East Africa, and Europe.

In India: Widespread in plains and low hills up to 1,600 m, heavily associated with human settlements.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant resident state-wide.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Found in high numbers in all rural and urban settlements.

Field rule: Medium size, grey collar around the neck contrasting with glossy black plumage, flat forehead, and flat kaaa calls identify the House Crow.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Louis Jean Pierre Vieillot described the House Crow as Corvus splendens in 1817 in the Nouveau Dictionnaire d'Histoire Naturelle (Vol. 8, p. 44). The type locality was Bengal. The genus name Corvus is the Latin word for crow. The specific name splendens is Latin for "splendid" or "shining," referencing the glossy sheen of its black feathers.

Subspecies. Five subspecies are recognized. The subspecies found in UP is:

SubspeciesAuthorityRangeNotes
C. s. splendensVieillot, 1817India, Nepal, BangladeshUP subspecies; nominate form; characterized by a moderately grey collar

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Passeriformes, family Corvidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Corvus splendens is part of the core Asian crow lineage.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) due to its extremely large range, growing population, and strong association with human food sources.
