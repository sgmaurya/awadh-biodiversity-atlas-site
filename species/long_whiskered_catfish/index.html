आर / Long-whiskered Catfish

Sperata aor (Hamilton, 1822) — Bagridae

आर — बड़ी देसी कैटफिश, जिसकी लंबी दाढ़ी (बारबेल) होती है। नदियों में पाई जाती है, विशेषकर बरसात में। यह बड़ी मछली है और खाने के लिए अच्छी मानी जाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size35–60 cm (occasionally larger)
BodyElongate, slightly flattened; scaleless, slimy
HeadBroad, flattened; 4 long barbels (whiskers)
ColourSilvery-grey above, silvery-white below
FinsDorsal fin short; pectoral spines serrated
TailForked, grey with paler edges

Field tip: A large catfish with 4 long barbels (whiskers). Nocturnal, hiding in deep pools and under banks during the day. Caught in river fisheries and also found in flooded fields during monsoon. The longest barbels (especially outer pair) distinguish it from smaller catfish species.

हिंदी: बड़ी मछली, चार लंबी दाढ़ियाँ। रात में शिकार करती है। नदी में पाई जाती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Body: Elongate, anteriorly elevated, posteriorly compressed. Scaleless skin covered with mucus. The body is adapted for benthic life in rivers and moving water.

Head: Broad, flattened dorsoventrally. Eyes small, positioned dorsolaterally. Mouth terminal to subterminal; four barbels: one maxillary pair (long, reaching beyond gill slits) and one nasal pair (shorter).

Coloration: Silvery-grey above, lighter on the flanks, white or silvery below. Fins grey or blackish with paler edges.

Fins: Dorsal fin short with 1 spine and 6–7 rays. Pectoral fin with 1 spine (serrated, venomous) and 9–10 rays. Ventral fin with 8 rays. Anal fin long, with 14–16 rays. Adipose fin present.

Tail: Forked; caudal lobes rounded; grey with white outer edges.

हिंदी: लंबा शरीर, चपटा सिर। चार दाढ़ियाँ, ऊपरी जोड़ी बहुत लंबी। काँटा जहरीला होता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Nocturnal predator. The Long-whiskered Catfish is predominantly nocturnal, hiding in deep pools, under submerged logs, and in burrows during the day. At night, it emerges to hunt for fish, aquatic invertebrates, and decaying organic matter. Uses its long barbels as sensory organs to locate food in murky water.

Locally nomadic. This species exhibits local nomadism related to water levels. During monsoon flooding (June–August), the fish disperses into floodplain water bodies, marshes, and temporary lakes. As water recedes (September–November), fish concentrate in deeper, more permanent pools. This nomadism is driven by food availability and breeding opportunities in flooded areas.

Habitat preference: Prefers larger rivers with rocky or sandy substrates and moderate current. Tolerates turbid water and low oxygen but requires clean, flowing systems.

Feeding ecology: Omnivorous, consuming smaller fish, aquatic invertebrate larvae, decaying plant matter, and detritus. Opportunistic scavenger.

Gregarious? Not strongly gregarious but may gather in areas of food abundance (carcasses, decomposing matter).

Predators: Large catfish and snakeheads; humans (heavily fished). Eagles and cormorants may take small individuals in shallow water.

हिंदी: रात में शिकार करता है। बरसात में इधर-उधर घूमता है। बड़ी मछलियों को खाता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Aquatic insect larvae (chironomid, mayfly)
  • Crustaceans and molluscs
  • Decaying organic matter and plant detritus

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Fish: Large snakeheads, larger catfish
  • Birds: Cormorant, eagle, heron (for juveniles)
  • Humans: Extensively fished with hooks, traps, and nets

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food fish. One of the most important food fish in the region. Highly valued for its taste and texture; commands good prices in local fish markets. Sold fresh in bulk during post-monsoon months (September–November) when fish concentrate in pools.

Fishing methods. Caught using:

  • Hook and line (night fishing)
  • Cast nets
  • Fish traps and weirs
  • Gill nets

Nutritional value. Protein-rich; consumed fresh, dried, or smoked. Important for food security of rural communities.

Breeding habitat. Requires access to floodplain waters for reproduction. Conservation of floodplain connectivity is essential for recruitment.

Today. Abundance varies year to year depending on monsoon flooding and fishery pressure. Considered sustainable where floodplain flooding occurs regularly; threatened where river regulation prevents flooding.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The spawning habitat requirements and recruitment patterns of this species in relation to monsoon flooding and dam operation are not well-studied locally.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Long-whiskered Catfish should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

To be expected in the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and their tributaries throughout the region. Most abundant during and after the monsoon (June–October) when floodplain waters are filled. Contract into deep river pools during the dry season (March–May).

During monsoon, expected in all flooded fields, marshes, and temporary lakes. A net catch or photograph is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia; native to the Gangetic region and throughout the Brahmaputra system. Found from Afghanistan to Myanmar.

In India: Throughout the Ganga and Brahmaputra drainages; common in eastern and north-eastern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in rivers and floodplain water bodies.

In Basti / eastern UP: Expected in the Ghaghra, Rapti, and their tributaries; abundant in floodplain water bodies during monsoon.

Field rule: A large catfish (35–60 cm) with 4 barbels (whiskers), two of which are very long (reaching beyond gill slits), is a Long-whiskered Catfish. The long outer barbels are diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton (earlier known as Buchanan) in 1822 in Fishes of the Ganges as Pimelodus aor. Later transferred to Sperata by Swainson.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Siluriformes; family Bagridae (bagrid catfish). The genus Sperata contains large Asian catfish with long barbels and benthic habits.

Phylogenetic notes. Sperata aor is closely related to Sperata seenghala (Giant River Catfish); these two are sister species within the genus.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern globally. Widespread, abundant, and adaptable to a range of river habitats. No major threats in the core of its range, though local declines may occur where river regulation or pollution is severe.
