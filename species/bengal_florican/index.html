चरस / Bengal Florican

Houbaropsis bengalensis (Gmelin, 1789) — Otididae

चरस (बंगाल फ्लोरिकन) — भारतीय उपमहाद्वीप के तराई घास के मैदानों में पाया जाने वाला एक अत्यंत दुर्लभ और गंभीर रूप से संकटग्रस्त (Critically Endangered) तिलोर पक्षी है। यह उत्तर प्रदेश के दुधवा राष्ट्रीय उद्यान के खुले और ऊंचे कास-नरकुल के मैदानों में छिपकर रहता है। प्रजनन ऋतु (मार्च से जून) के दौरान नर पक्षी मादा को रिझाने के लिए घास से कई फीट ऊपर हवा में उछलकर फड़फड़ाता है, जो एक विहंगम दृश्य होता है। नर पक्षी का सिर, गर्दन और पेट गहरा मखमली काला और पंख चमकदार सफेद होते हैं, जबकि मादा रेतीली-भूरी होती है।

Field tip: Look over extensive, undisturbed tall grasslands in the Terai during early morning in spring. The adult male's jet-black body and dazzling white wing flashes during aerial leaping displays are unmistakable even at great distances across open grassland.

मादा रेतीले-भूरे रंग की होती है और सूखी घास में बिल्कुल दिखाई नहीं देती।
बसंत के मौसम में नर पक्षी घास के ऊपर ऊंची छलांग लगाकर मादा को आकर्षित करता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1929 as Houbaropsis bengalensis):

MeasurementRange
Wing338–347 mm (Baker 1929)

The Bengal Florican is a medium-sized bustard showing dramatic sexual dimorphism. In breeding males, the entire head, neck, elongated neck hackles, breast, and lower plumage are deep velvety black. The mantle and back are black, intricately barred and vermiculated with pale buff. The wing-coverts and all flight feathers except the outer two primaries are dazzling pure white.

Females are cryptically patterned throughout: the crown is dark brown with a buff median coronal streak, the upperparts are blackish-brown mottled and freckled with warm buff, and the underparts are sandy-buff with fine dark barring on the breast.

The bill is dusky-horn above and pale yellowish below. The iris is yellow. The long legs and feet are dull straw-yellow.

Vocalisations. Generally silent for most of the year. During the territorial leap display, displaying cocks produce a distinctive mechanical clapping sound with their wings, accompanied by a metallic croak or whistle transcribed as chik-chik-chik.

हिंदी:
इसके पंखों की लंबाई 338 से 347 मिमी होती है।
उड़ते समय नर के सफेद पंख और काला शरीर बहुत दूर से पहचाने जा सकते हैं।
छलांग लगाते समय यह अपने पंखों को तेजी से फड़फड़ाकर ताली जैसी आवाज निकालता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Houbaropsis bengalensis is a specialist inhabitant of early-successional, flood-maintained riverine grasslands. Outside the breeding season, it is exceptionally secretive and solitary, walking stealthily through dense grass tussocks and crouching flat against the ground when approached. It flies with rapid, direct wingbeats low over the grass canopy.

During the breeding season in March and April, territorial males exhibit an exploded lek breeding system. Each cock establishes a cleared display territory in short or recently burned grassland, performing spectacular courtship leaps where it launches high vertically into the air, hovering momentarily with frantic wingbeats and trailing legs before dropping back down.

हिंदी:
यह पक्षी बहुत शर्मीला होता है और ज्यादातर समय ऊंची घास में छिपा रहता है।
यह जमीन पर बहुत तेज चलता है और खतरा महसूस होने पर उड़ने के बजाय घास में बैठ जाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Baker (1929, p. 72–73) records that the Bengal Florican breeds almost entirely in March and April, though occasional eggs occur in February and second broods in June or July. No nest is made, the eggs being laid directly on the bare ground in the immense grasslands along the base of the Himalayas.

The female incubates the olive-green blotched eggs alone, leaving the scrape quietly when danger approaches and rearing the precocial chicks without male assistance.

हिंदी:
बेकर (1929) के अनुसार यह मुख्य रूप से मार्च और अप्रैल में प्रजनन करती है और हिमालय की तलहटी के घास के मैदानों में जमीन पर ही अंडे देती है। केवल मादा ही अंडों को सेती है और बच्चों की देखभाल करती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

The Bengal Florican is an omnivorous ground feeder that consumes both vegetative material and diverse grassland invertebrates:

  • Tender green shoots, seeds, and runner tips of native grasses (Saccharum, Imperata, and Cynodon)
  • Berries and seeds of wetland shrubs
  • Grasshoppers (Acrididae), crickets, and bush-crickets
  • Beetles, especially dung beetles (Scarabaeidae) and ground beetles
  • Ants, termites, spiders, and occasionally small frogs or skinks

Foraging occurs on foot, with birds deliberately plucking grass blades and snatching terrestrial insects from ground litter.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The Bengal Florican feeds on tender grass shoots, seeds, berries, and terrestrial insects in open grasslands (Baker 1929, p. 73). It is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Extremely fragmented distribution restricted to two disjunct populations: the alluvial grasslands of northern India and Nepal, and the seasonal floodplains around the Tonle Sap lake in Cambodia and southern Vietnam.

Regional status: Resident in tiny, isolated populations within protected Terai grasslands of Uttar Pradesh, with known historical records concentrated in the Dudhwa grasslands of Lakhimpur Kheri.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Johann Friedrich Gmelin in 1789 in the 13th edition of Systema Naturae (vol. 1, part 2, p. 724), under the basionym Otis bengalensis. Formerly treated under "Sypheotis bengalensis" by Blanford & Oates and Houbaropsis bengalensis by Baker (1929).

Subspecies. Two subspecies are recognized: the nominate H. b. bengalensis of northern India and Nepal, and H. b. blandini of Cambodia and southern Vietnam.

Family and order placement. Order Otidiformes; family Otididae (bustards and floricans).

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Critically Endangered (CR) by the IUCN Red List due to an extremely small and rapidly declining global population caused by widespread conversion of grassland savannas into intensive agriculture.
