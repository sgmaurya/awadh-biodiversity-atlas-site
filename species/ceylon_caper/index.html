सीलोन केपर / Ceylon Caper

Capparis zeylanica L. — Capparaceae

सीलोन केपर — पेड़ों और पुरानी दीवारों पर चढ़ने वाली एक विशाल, कांटेदार और बेहद आकर्षक फूलों वाली झाड़ी। इसे स्थानीय भाषा में 'गोविंद फल' या 'अर्दंडा' भी कहते हैं। वसंत में इस पर सफेद फूल खिलते हैं, जिनमें से ढेरों लंबे गुलाबी-जामुनी पुंकेसर बाहर निकलते हैं। तितलियों के लिए यह एक अत्यंत महत्वपूर्ण जीवनदायी पौधा है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
HabitRigid, climbing or scrambling thorny woody shrub clambering over trees
ThornsPaired, sharp, recurved stipular spines present at every leaf node
Young GrowthShoots and buds densely clothed in rich brick-red to rust-coloured tomentum
FlowersLarge, white turning dull pink, dominated by a spray of long purple stamens
FruitLarge, round to ellipsoid berry turning bright glossy reddish-purple when ripe

Field tip: Look in late spring along thorny hedgerows or roadside tree trunks for a rampant climber with hooked thorns and showy white-and-pink flowers featuring long radiating purple stamens.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: पेड़ों पर चढ़ने वाली कांटेदार बेल, मुड़े हुए तीखे कांटे, और ढेरों लंबे जामुनी धागों जैसे पुंकेसरों वाले सफेद फूल।

Morphology आकारिकी

Shrub description: Extensive, thorny, climbing or scandent woody shrub. Young twigs and shoots densely cloaked with rusty-brown or rufous flocculent tomentum, later becoming glabrous and olive-green. Stipular spines paired, sharp, recurved, hooked downwards, hooked bases swollen. Leaves alternate, elliptic, oblong or ovate-lanceolate, acute or obtuse with a sharp mucronate apex, rounded at base, coriaceous, shining above, petiolate. Flowers showy, solitary or two to three in serial supra-axillary rows along branches. Sepals four, concave, rusty-tomentose outside. Petals four, oblong, white when opening, quickly turning rose-pink or dull purplish. Stamens very numerous, long, radiating, filament bases white with upper halves brilliant crimson-purple. Fruit a globular or ovoid fleshy berry on a thick gynophore, ripening reddish-purple, containing multiple seeds embedded in white pulp.

हिंदी: यह पेड़ों पर चढ़ने वाली एक विशाल कांटेदार झाड़ी है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

  • Scrambling Habit: Uses pairs of downward-hooked stipular thorns to grapple onto tree branches, fences, and rocky banks.
  • Color Change in Flowers: Newly opened white petals turn rich purplish-pink within hours of opening, signalling floral age and pollination status to insect visitors.
  • Butterfly Magnet: Serves as one of the single most important larval food reservoirs for pierid butterflies in the Gangetic plain.
  • Frugivory and Dispersal: Ripe sweet-smelling purple fruits are eagerly consumed by bulbuls, barbets, bats, and tree geckos.
हिंदी: यह अपने मुड़े हुए कांटों से पेड़ों पर चढ़ती है। इसके फूल खिलने पर सफेद होते हैं और परागण के बाद गुलाबी हो जाते हैं। पक्षी इसके मीठे फल खाते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Spring Flowering: Buds burst into magnificent bloom from February through May, perfuming hedgerows in early summer heat.
  • Fruit Maturation: Fleshy green berries swell through the monsoon months, ripening to reddish-purple from September to November.
  • Foliage Retention: Evergreen to semi-deciduous, retaining dark leathery leaves throughout the intense dry pre-monsoon period.
  • Seed Germination: Seeds passed through bird guts germinate readily beneath mango tree canopies after first monsoon showers.
हिंदी: वसंत ऋतु में मार्च-अप्रैल में फूल आते हैं। बरसात में फल पकते हैं और पक्षियों द्वारा इनके बीज बिखेरे जाते हैं।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Primary Butterfly Host: Crucial breeding plant for the pioneer butterfly (Belenois aurota), common gull (Cepora nerissa), and great orange-tip (Hebomoia glaucippe).
  • Avian Consumers: Ripe pulp eaten by red-vented bulbuls and Asian koels.
  • Ant Associations: Extrafloral nectaries along young shoots attract aggressive ants that patrol foliage against non-pierid herbivores.
हिंदी: सफेद और पीली तितलियों की इल्लियां इसी के पत्तों पर पलती हैं। कोयल और बुलबुल इसके पके फल खाते हैं।

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Heavy Defoliation: Large clutches of pierid caterpillars can completely strip new spring shoots of all leaves within days.
  • Gall Midges: Cecidomyiid gall flies induce irregular woody swellings on vegetative stems and flower buds.
  • Sooty Mould: Follows infestations of scale insects on dense sheltered vines beneath mango trees.
हिंदी: तितलियों की इल्लियां कभी-कभी इसके सारे पत्ते खा जाती हैं, लेकिन पौधा जल्द ही नई पत्तियां निकाल लेता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

  • Living Protective Fence: Ideal thorny bio-fence along village orchards, impenetrable to cattle and nilgai.
  • Traditional Food and Pickling: Unripe green fruits ('Govind phal') are harvested in rural Awadh and pickled in mustard oil.
  • Folk Medicine: Roots and bark are used in folk medicine for soothing rheumatic joints and managing intermittent fevers.
हिंदी: ग्रामीण क्षेत्रों में इसके कच्चे फलों (गोविंद फल) का अचार डाला जाता है। यह नीलगाय और मवेशियों को खेतों में घुसने से रोकता है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native across South Asia and Southeast Asia, from India, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka eastwards to Indonesia and the Philippines.

In India: Common throughout deciduous forests, scrub zones, and plains from Punjab through Uttar Pradesh to southern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widely recorded across the Gangetic alluvial plain from Ayodhya, Varanasi, Lucknow, and Mirzapur.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected along ancient orchard margins, dry ravines of the Kuwana and Ghaghara, and old brick boundaries.

Field rule: A robust scrambling thorny climber with rusty young shoots and showy white flowers turning pink with a brush of purple stamens identifies Capparis zeylanica.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1762 as Capparis zeylanica in Species Plantarum, ed. 2, vol. 1, p. 720.

Subspecies. Monotypic across northern India; historically often confused with Capparis horrida L.f., which is a synonym.

Family and order placement. Belongs to the family Capparaceae (caper family) in the order Brassicales.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated by the IUCN; widespread, common, and resilient across anthropogenic scrub landscapes.
