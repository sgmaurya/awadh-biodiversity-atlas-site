व्हाइट-थ्रोटेड बुशचैट / White-throated Bushchat

Saxicola insignis Blyth, 1841 — Muscicapidae

व्हाइट-थ्रोटेड बुशचैट — सर्दियों में हिमालय से आने वाली एक दुर्लभ पक्षी, जो खुले मैदानों में छोटी झाड़ियों पर बैठती है। इसके सफ़ेद गले और काली पीठ से इसे अन्य पक्षियों से अलग किया जा सकता है। यह अब विलुप्त होने वाली प्रजाति है और बहुत कम दिखती है।

Field tip: A small, upright bushchat with a conspicuous white throat in both sexes. Males are black and white with a distinctive pattern. Females are browner but retain the distinctive white throat. Often perches conspicuously on grass stems and low bushes. Vulnerable to extinction; any sighting should be documented and reported.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 11–12 cm long; wingspan 16–19 cm.

Plumage (male): Crown and nape black. Back black. Rump white. Tail black with white tips. Throat and upper breast white. Lower breast and flanks white or buff-white. Wings black with white on coverts.

Plumage (female): Crown and back dark brown or brownish-black. Rump buff or creamy-white. Tail brown with paler tips. Throat white (retained from male plumage pattern). Breast buff or peachy. Flanks buff. Wings dark brown.

Eyes: Dark brown; pale eye-ring (narrow).

Bill: Short, slender, pointed; dark. Adapted for gleaning insects.

Legs: Dark grey or black.

Sexes: Clearly dimorphic. Males black-and-white; females brown.

Juveniles: Similar to females but duller and with spotting on breast.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A short, warbled series of notes given from exposed perch; not often heard in winter.
  2. Contact call: A soft tack or chit.
  3. Alarm call: A sharp whit-whit when disturbed.
हिंदी: नर्म "टैक टैक" की कॉल। डर जाने पर "व्हिट-व्हिट"।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Foraging: Hunts from perches, sallying out to catch flying insects and occasionally picking prey from ground. Prey includes:

  • Small flies and gnats
  • Tiny moths
  • Small grasshoppers
  • Spiders

Feeding technique: Typical bushchat behaviour—perch and pounce, watching from low bush or grass stem.

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs; territorial. Males sing to defend territory.

Habitat preference: Open grasslands and scrubland with low vegetation and scattered shrubs. Requires clear perches and open space for hunting.

Activity: Primarily diurnal; most active in early morning.

Predators: Shrikes, small raptors, snakes.

Rarity: This species has declined significantly and is now Vulnerable globally. In eastern UP, it is very rare and may be declining further.

हिंदी: अकेले या जोड़ों में रहता है। अपना इलाका बचाता है। झाड़ियों से कीड़ों को पकड़ता है। अब बहुत दुर्लभ है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Absent during breeding season (April–August); returns to eastern UP from October and remains through February or March.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in late September or early October; numbers peak in November–December; departure begins in February, most gone by March.

Breeding grounds: Breeding range in the Himalayas from Kashmir to eastern Nepal; does not breed in the plains.

Roosts: Nocturnal roosts on low bushes or grass clumps.

Moult: Prebasic moult occurs before autumn migration.

हिंदी: अक्टूबर में आता है, मार्च तक रहता है। हिमालय में प्रजनन करता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shrikes, small raptors.
  • Reptilian predators: Small snakes.
  • Mammalian predators: Cats, foxes (occasionally).

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Additionally, Vulnerable (VU) under the IUCN Red List, indicating it is threatened with extinction.

Pest control: Insectivorous bird providing minor pest control services in grassland ecosystems.

Conservation concern: The species is Vulnerable due to habitat loss, particularly the loss of grassland habitats in the Himalayas and wintering grounds. Climate change affecting snow cover on breeding grounds and changes in grassland management in wintering areas are suspected threats.

Research priority: This species should be a priority for research and monitoring given its vulnerable status.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where this bushchat should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from October to March, though it is very rare. Open grasslands and scrubland areas are the preferred habitat.

Habitat preference: Open grassland with scattered low bushes and grass stems suitable for perching.

Rarity: Ali & Ripley (1968) note that this species is uncommon in the Gangetic plain in winter. It may be declining further. Any sighting would be noteworthy.

Peak visibility: November–December, when arriving populations are most evident.

Survey records: Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022–2025) surveys for this region may or may not have documented this species; the Handbook suggests it is rare.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph or detailed observation showing the distinctive white throat in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date) submitted through the About page. Any sighting of this Vulnerable species should be documented and reported to conservation organizations.

Conservation implication: If rediscovered in eastern UP or nearby areas, the location and population status should be communicated to BirdLife International and Indian conservation authorities immediately, as this species is of conservation concern.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी अब बहुत दुर्लभ है। अगर दिखे तो तुरंत फोटो लेकर बताएँ। इसे बचाना बहुत जरूरी है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in the Himalayas from Kashmir to central Nepal. Winters in the Indian subcontinent, primarily in the Gangetic plain. Monotypic.

In India: Occurs as a winter visitor, primarily in the Gangetic region and foothills; now rare throughout its range.

In Uttar Pradesh: Historical winter visitor, now very rare. Habitat loss and population declines have made sightings uncommon.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Rare or very rare winter visitor to open grassland areas. Unconfirmed in recent surveys; may be declining.

Field rule: White-throated Bushchat males are distinguished by the combination of black cap, black back, white throat, and white rump. The white throat is the key diagnostic feature from all other similar species. Females require closer inspection but retain the distinctive white throat.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Edward Blyth in 1841 from Himalayan specimens. Original description published in Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal.

Subspecies. Monotypic.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae (true flycatchers and Old World chats). The genus Saxicola comprises the stonechats and bushchats.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to other Saxicola species, particularly S. caprata and S. torquatus.

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Vulnerable (VU) globally due to population declines suspected to be caused by habitat loss, particularly the conversion of alpine grasslands in the Himalayas and loss of grassland habitats on wintering grounds. The species is thought to have undergone a decline of 20–30% over the past three generations (approx. 9 years). The small global population and the continuing decline in habitat quality pose a long-term threat to the species' persistence.
