पाबदा कैटफिश मछली / Pabdah Catfish

Ompok pabda (Hamilton, 1822) — Siluridae

पाबदा कैटफिश मछली — मैदानी नदियों, तालों और दलदली झीलों में पाई जाने वाली एक अत्यंत स्वादिष्ट, कोमल और कीमती देसी कैटफिश (बटरफिश) है। इसका शरीर बिना तराशों (scales) वाला, चिकना और दोनों तरफ से चपटा होता है। इसके मुँह पर दो जोड़ी मूंछें होती हैं और इसके गलफड़े के ठीक पीछे एक गहरा काला धब्बा होता है। यह रात के समय सक्रिय होकर छोटी मछलियों और झींगों का शिकार करती है। ग्रामीण और शहरी बाजारों में यह बहुत महंगे दामों पर बिकती है।

Field tip: Look in slow-moving rivers and oxbow wetlands with muddy bottoms and weed beds. An elongate, scaleless silvery-brown catfish with an upturned lower jaw, an extremely long anal fin along the belly, and maxillary barbels reaching only to the pectoral fin base is Ompok pabda.

हिंदी: यह बिना छिलके वाली चपटी और चिकनी मछली है। इसका निचला जबड़ा आगे निकला होता है। गलफड़े के पीछे एक काला निशान और पेट के नीचे बहुत लंबा पंख होता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Size as given by FishBase (Froese & Pauly 2025):

MeasurementRangeSource
Maximum length30 cm TLFroese & Pauly (2025), FishBase

Adult description:

Ompok pabda possesses an elongate, compressed body that becomes increasingly thin towards the caudal peduncle (Hamilton 1822). The head is broad, depressed, and slightly shorter than one-fifth of the standard length (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The snout is rounded and obtuse; eyes are small and situated just above the angle of the mouth (Hamilton 1822). The mouth is wide, obliquely directed upward (superior), and the lower jaw projects strongly beyond the upper jaw (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Teeth in both jaws are villiform and arranged in broad bands (Jayaram 2006).

Two pairs of barbels are present: the maxillary barbels are slender, extending backwards past the eye to reach the middle or tip of the pectoral fin; the mandibular barbels are very small and barely reach the gill opening (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The pectoral fins are well developed, supported by a moderately strong spine that is serrated along its inner margin (Hamilton 1822). The pelvic fins are small. The dorsal fin is minute, lacking an osseous spine, bearing only 4 soft rays (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). An adipose fin is completely absent (Jayaram 2006). The anal fin is elongate, carrying 50 to 56 rays, running uninterrupted to near the caudal fin base (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The caudal fin is deeply forked with pointed lobes.

Coloration is shining silvery-yellow to olive-brown on the back, with irregular cloudy markings along the flanks (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). A prominent, dark blackish-violet oval blotch is situated directly behind the operculum above the pectoral fin base (Hamilton 1822).

हिंदी: इसका शरीर लगभग 30 सेमी तक लंबा, चपटा और बिना तराशों का होता है। मुंह ऊपर की ओर खुला होता है और मूंछें सीने के पंख तक पहुंचती हैं। पेट के नीचे का पंख बहुत लंबा होता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Pabdah Catfish is a nocturnal and crepuscular benthopelagic predator inhabiting quiet freshwater ecosystems (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). It prefers slow-flowing river glides, oxbow lakes, weed-rich canals, and deep perennial village ponds with muddy bottoms and submerged logs.

During daylight hours, adults remain quiescent, resting among submerged tree roots, undercut clay banks, or dense tangles of Hydrilla and water hyacinth (Jayaram 2006). As dusk falls, they emerge to hunt actively in open shallows and weed margins (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Using their sensory barbels and lateral line system, they detect water ripples produced by small schooling carplets and freshwater shrimps. During the monsoon flood season, they move onto inundated floodplains to forage and reproduce, retreating to deep river pools during the dry winter months (Jayaram 2006).

हिंदी: यह रात में सक्रिय होने वाली शिकारी मछली है। दिन में यह पानी के नीचे जड़ों और घास में छिपी रहती है और रात होते ही छोटी मछलियों और झींगों का शिकार करने निकलती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Seasonal phenology in northern Indian alluvial waters across the agricultural year (Talwar & Jhingran 1991; Jayaram 2006):

Spawning occurs during the peak monsoon rains from June to August (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Rising water levels and barometric pressure drops trigger breeding. Pairs perform courtship displays in shallow, vegetated floodwaters. Females deposit small, adhesive greenish-brown eggs that attach to submerged grass blades and roots. The eggs hatch after a few days into predatory larvae that absorb their yolk sacs before actively hunting zooplankton and insect larvae (Jayaram 2006).

हिंदी: मानसून की बारिश के दौरान यह मछली उथले पानी और जलमग्न घास में अंडे देती है। कुछ ही दिनों में अंडों से बच्चे निकलकर तेजी से शिकार करना शुरू कर देते हैं।

Diet and Feeding Behaviour आहार

Ompok pabda is a carnivorous predator throughout its adult life (Talwar & Jhingran 1991):

Natural diet components:

  • Small indigenous carplets and minnows, including Salmostoma bacaila (Large Razorbelly Minnow), Rasbora daniconius (Slender Rasbora), and Puntius sophore (Pool Barb).
  • Freshwater prawns and shrimps (Macrobrachium spp.), crushed with villiform teeth (Jayaram 2006).
  • Aquatic insects and larvae, including chironomid midges, water bugs, and dragonfly naiads (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).
  • Small benthic molluscs and oligochaete worms ingested from bottom silt.
हिंदी: यह एक मांसाहारी मछली है जो छोटी मछलियों (जैसे चलवा और पूल बार्ब), ताजे पानी के झींगों, जलीय कीड़ों और कीचड़ के कीड़ों को खाती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Documented natural predators in northern Indian alluvial freshwater systems:

  • Large predatory fishes: Major riverine predators like Wallago attu (Striped Catfish), Channa marulius (Great Snakehead), and Chitala chitala (Clown Knifefish) prey on sub-adult pabda.
  • Piscivorous birds: Night herons, pond herons, and cormorants hunt adults in shallow marsh margins.
  • Otters: Smooth-coated otters pursue adults in perennial river pools.
  • Water snakes: Checkered keelbacks prey on juveniles in weed-rich shallows.
हिंदी: बोआल, चितल और बड़ी सौर मछलियाँ, बगुले और उदबिलाव इस कोमल कैटफिश का शिकार करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

High commercial and culinary value: Ompok pabda (known as "Madhu Pabda" or "Butterfish") is regarded as one of the finest and most delicate freshwater food fishes in northern and eastern India. Its flesh is white, tender, virtually bone-free apart from the central spine, and rich in essential polyunsaturated fatty acids, vitamin A, and zinc. In urban and rural markets, wild-caught pabda commands premium prices, often two to three times that of major carps.

Paddy wetland fisheries: During seasonal monsoon floods, wild stocks colonize rice field ecosystems, where they feed on pest insects and small forage fish without harming the crop (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

Traditional: Revered as a therapeutic, highly digestible fish for convalescents and nursing mothers in Awadhi cuisine.

Today: Harvested from wild wetlands using gill nets and traps; targeted for captive breeding and commercial aquaculture.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Developing cost-effective live-feed protocols in local hatcheries to improve larval survival during induced breeding.

हिंदी: यह बहुत महंगी, स्वादिष्ट और सुपाच्य देसी मछली है। इसमें कांटे बहुत कम होते हैं और इसका माँस मक्खन जैसा कोमल होता है। बाजारों में इसकी कीमत बहुत अधिक होती है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to South Asia, recorded from India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and Myanmar (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

In India: Distributed throughout the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra river drainages, extending into the Mahanadi basin (Menon 1999).

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded throughout the Ganges, Ghaghara, Rapti, Gomti, and Sarda river networks and associated alluvial floodplain lakes.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a native resident in the Ghaghara, Kuwano, and Ami river corridors and major oxbow wetlands.

हिंदी: यह भारत, पाकिस्तान, नेपाल और बांग्लादेश की प्रमुख नदियों में पाई जाती है। उत्तर प्रदेश में घाघरा और कुआनो नदी के गहरे पानी में यह उपस्थित है।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 as Silurus pabda in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches, page 150. Type locality: Ponds and rivers of Bengal.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognized across its continental South Asian range.

Family and order placement. Order Siluriformes, family Siluridae. The genus Ompok Lacepède, 1803, comprises Asian sheatfishes characterized by an extremely reduced dorsal fin and an elongate anal fin.

Phylogenetic notes. Ompok pabda is closely allied to Ompok bimaculatus and Ompok pabo, distinguished from O. bimaculatus by its shorter maxillary barbels and higher anal fin ray count (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the global IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. Although still distributed across South Asian river basins, wild populations have experienced localized declines due to overharvesting.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Ompok pabda है और यह Siluridae परिवार की सदस्य है। प्रकृति में इसका वास नदियों और तालों में स्थित है।
