अंकरी / Common Vetch

Vicia sativa L. — Fabaceae

अंकरी (सर्दियों की बेल, जानवर घास, शीतकालीन सेम) — सर्दियों में गेहूँ के खेतों में और बंजर जमीन पर आपसे आप उगने वाली एक पतली बेल। पत्तियाँ पंख जैसी, बिल्कुल मटर जैसी। बैंगनी-गुलाबी फूल छोटे-छोटे। फली मटर की फली जैसी, पर छोटी। गाय-भैंसों के लिए बहुत अच्छा सर्दियों का चारा। खेत में अपने आप आने के बाद भी किसान इसे निकालते नहीं हैं, क्योंकि जानवरों को खिलाने के लिए यह सब्जी बहुत पोषक होती है।

Field tip: A climbing or trailing winter legume with feathery compound leaves (like pea leaves) and small purple-pink flowers. The slender, beaked pods are typical of vetches. Look for it on field margins and in wheat fields from November to March.

Morphology आकारिकी

Root system: Taproot with nitrogen-fixing nodules formed by symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria; extending 20–30 cm deep.

Stems: Herbaceous, angular with 4 ridges, slightly hairy or smooth, green, producing leaf-like tendrils from leaf bases.

Leaves: Alternate, compound, pinnate, typically with 5–8 small leaflets. Leaflets are linear to oblong, 2–4 cm, with entire margins. The terminal leaflet is modified into a tendril for climbing. The petiole is short.

Flowers: Papilionaceous, typically purple or pink, occasionally white or bicolored. Flowers are solitary or in pairs in leaf axils. Both self-pollination and insect pollination occur.

Pods: Linear, 3–6 cm long, slightly flattened, green when immature, drying to tan or brown. Each pod contains 4–10 round seeds.

Seeds: Round, glossy, 3–5 mm, variously colored — usually brown or black, sometimes mottled or speckled.

हिंदी: पतली बेल, पंख जैसी पत्तियाँ, तेंदुए। बैंगनी-गुलाबी फूल। फली छोटी, गोल दाने।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Associated crops: Infests Wheat, barley, and other winter cereals.
  • Associated weeds: Often found with Chickpea, Indian Mustard, and other winter crops.
  • Seed dispersal: Dispersed by harvesting machinery, water, and animal movement.

हिंदी: अक्टूबर-नवंबर में अंकुरण, दिसंबर-जनवरी में फूल, फरवरी-अप्रैल में फली और बीज।

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • No significant pests or diseases reported in most regions.
  • Occasionally affected by leaf spots or minor insect feeding; not usually serious.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Livestock forage: Vicia sativa is primarily valued as a winter forage crop and field margin herb for livestock feeding. The green herbage is palatable to cattle, buffalo, sheep, and goats. The plant is commonly collected in winter when fresh forage is scarce, dried, and stored for later use as hay. The nutritional quality is good, with moderate protein (15–18% on a dry-matter basis) and adequate energy content.

Volunteer crop: In crop rotation systems, vetch volunteers (self-seeded plants) in the field after a previous crop and provide free forage. The plants are often retained by farmers for this reason, becoming semi-cultivated.

Nitrogen fixation: As a legume, vetch fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root nodule symbiosis with Rhizobium bacteria. The plant improves soil nitrogen, benefiting subsequent crops. In some regions, vetch is grown as a green manure crop and ploughed back into the field.

Weed vs. resource: While technically a weed of winter cereal crops, Vicia sativa is commonly tolerated or even encouraged on field margins and in crop fields because its presence provides a free fodder resource. The distinction between "weed" and "useful plant" is thus context-dependent and depends on the farmer's priorities.

Hardiness: The plant thrives in cool-season conditions and can grow on marginal lands (poor soils, semi-arid regions) where more demanding crops struggle. It is well-suited to rainfed agriculture.

हिंदी: सर्दियों का सबसे अच्छा चारा। गायों-भैंसों को खूब पसंद। खेत में अपने आप आया तो किसान निकालते नहीं। नाइट्रोजन मिट्टी में बढ़ाता है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Mediterranean region and western Asia; now naturalized in temperate regions worldwide.

In India: Widespread in cooler and wetter parts of the north; particularly common in the Indo-Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in northern and central wheat-growing regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a spontaneous winter herb on field margins and in wheat fields.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How much fresh and dry-matter forage can be harvested from one square meter of ankri-infested field? — Measure harvests; weigh fresh and dried material.
  2. Do cattle and other livestock prefer ankri forage over other winter weeds, and what is the intake? — Offer to livestock; measure consumption.
  3. Can ankri seeds be collected and stored for sowing as a winter forage crop? — Harvest mature seeds; store; test germination in autumn.
  4. How much nitrogen does an ankri-infested field fix if it is ploughed back as green manure? — Measure soil nitrogen before and after ploughing.
  5. Does ankri grow on marginal or degraded soils where wheat grows poorly? — Compare growth and yield on different soil types.

Field rule: A trailing or climbing winter legume with feathery pinnate leaves ending in a tendril, small purple-pink papilionaceous flowers, and linear pods containing round seeds is ankri.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as a cultivated and naturalized plant. The genus name Vicia is the classical Latin name for vetch. The specific name sativa means "cultivated" or "sown."

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised, though some authorities recognize subspecies based on seed size and color.

Family and order placement. Fabaceae, order Fabales. The family includes all legumes.

Phylogenetic notes. Vicia sativa is closely related to other Vicia species of Mediterranean and Eurasian origin, particularly V. ervilia (bitter vetch) and V. angustifolia (narrow-leaved vetch).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Naturalized and locally common; not assessed for conservation status.
