मोला / Mola Carplet

Amblypharyngodon mola (Hamilton, 1822) — Cyprinidae

मोला — पूर्वी मैदानों की सबसे छोटी कार्प मछली, जो सूखे गड़ों और तालाबों में भी जीती है। चाँदी-सुनहरी, हल्की, और घास और सड़े पत्तों को खाती है। हर छोटे तालाब में सैकड़ों होती हैं और गाँव के खिचड़ी पकवान में इसका अंश हमेशा रहता है। अंडे सीधे घास पर देती है और फिर परवाह नहीं करती — लेकिन अगली बरसात में फिर हज़ारों हो जाती हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size5–10 cm, rarely to 12 cm
ShapeDeep-bodied, compressed laterally, with a rounded head and small mouth
ColourSilvery-golden above, white below; fins yellowish; a faint dark stripe down the lateral line
FinsDorsal and anal fins rounded; tail fin rounded and yellowish
MouthSmall, inferior, adapted for browsing
ScalesLarge, bright, and loosely set

Field tip: A tiny deep-bodied silvery-golden fish in a shallow pond or paddy with aquatic plants. It stays in weeds and avoids open water. The small inferior mouth and deep body distinguish it from the lean minnows; the small size from the larger carps. A net haul from a weed-filled pond brings dozens.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: तालाब की घास में छोटी, चौड़ी, चाँदी-सुनहरी मछली जो झुंड में होती है। मुँह नीचे की ओर, छोटा। जब जाल डालो तो दर्जनों आती हैं।

Morphology आकारिकी

MeasurementRange

Head and mouth: The head is blunt and rounded. The mouth is small and inferior (below), with no barbels, adapted for scraping and browsing. The pharyngeal teeth are blunt and mill-like for grinding plant material.

Body: Deep-bodied and laterally compressed; large cycloid scales set loosely. The lateral line is complete. The dorsal fin is positioned above the pelvic fins and is rounded; the anal fin is also rounded.

Fins and tail: All fins are rounded; the tail fin is fan-shaped and yellowish. The pectoral and pelvic fins are clear to yellowish.

हिंदी: सिर भोथरा, गोल। मुँह नीचे, छोटा, कोई दाढ़ी नहीं। गले के दाँत कुंद, पीसने वाले। शरीर गहरा, किनारों पर सपाट। बड़े, ढीले शल्क। सब पंख गोल, पीलापन लिए।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The fish of weed-choked ponds and margins. It lives in schools among aquatic vegetation, browsing on algae, fallen leaves, detritus and plant material; the ground-up teeth attest to its plant diet. It is found even in oxygen-poor water where other fish starve, because it gulps air at the surface when needed. With the monsoon it spreads from deeper ponds into flooded paddies and temporary swamps to breed.

It is food fish: easy to catch, good eating, and abundant enough that a small net in any village pond yields a basketful. Herons hunt it in the shallows; catfish and snakeheads in deeper water. In ponds where it is not harvested it can become so abundant that all are stunted, as with tilapia, though it does not grow to the same size.

हिंदी: घास वाले तालाब की झुंड वाली मछली। काई, गिरी पत्तियाँ, सड़ी चीज़ें खाती है और ख़राब पानी सहती है। बरसात में खेतों में जाकर प्रजनन करती है। पकड़ने में आसान, खाने में अच्छी। अगर नहीं पकड़ी जाए तो इतनी हो जाती है कि सब ठिगनी रह जाती हैं।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Water Silk and other algae.
  • Fallen leaves and detritus of aquatic plants.
  • Diatoms and the film on submerged surfaces.
  • Small organic particles and ostracoda.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Indian Pond Heron, Little Egret, White-throated Kingfisher, Pied Kingfisher, House Crow.
  • Fish: Stinging Catfish, Great Snakehead, Mozambique Tilapia, larger mola.
  • People: netted and eaten; the main catch from small ponds.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food. Abundant, easy to catch, and good eating: sold fresh in local markets and cooked whole or in curries. The flesh is soft and the bones are small; a small fish is eaten whole. A common source of cheap protein for rural families.

Aquaculture. Sometimes stocked in village ponds as a supplementary species with carp, although its proliferation can stunt other fish. Its tolerance of poor water makes it suitable for waste-water ponds.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: none recorded specifically for this species.

Today. Abundant in most ponds; a staple catch.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The basic life cycle and reproductive ecology in the region is not published; a record of breeding time, fecundity and growth rate from village ponds would establish a local baseline and allow monitoring of environmental change.

हिंदी: आसानी से पकड़ी जाती है, खाने में अच्छी, सस्ती। ग़रीब परिवारों का प्रोटीन का साधन। कहीं कहीं कार्प के साथ खेती में डाली जाती है, लेकिन ज़्यादा हो जाए तो सब ठिगनी हो जाती हैं। ख़राब पानी सहती है।

To be expected in every shallow, weed-choked pond and tank in the district. Abundant in flooded rice paddies during and immediately after monsoon. A photograph or a net catch from any village pond is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Indo-Gangetic plain from Pakistan through India to Bangladesh and Myanmar.

In India: Throughout the Ganga system and associated flood plains; widespread in the north-east and Bengal.

In Uttar Pradesh: Throughout the northern and eastern plains, particularly in paddy-growing areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all still-water habitats; abundant in monsoon.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many mola can be caught in one haul with a hand-net from a weed-filled village pond in July? / जुलाई में एक गाँव के तालाब से एक हाथ-जाल से कितनी मोला पकड़ी जा सकती है? — Net one corner of the pond for one minute.
  2. What is the size of the smallest breeding female, by measurement? — Examine females in breeding season.
  3. Which plants do the eggs stick to, and how many eggs are on each plant? — Look in flooded paddies in July and August.
  4. Do dense weed beds harbour more mola than sparse beds in the same pond? — Compare catches from weed-thick vs. weed-sparse areas.
  5. When do they move from deep ponds into flooded paddies, and when do they return? — Keep a daily count in a channel between a pond and a paddy field.

Field rule: A small deep-bodied silvery-golden fish with a tiny inferior mouth and rounded fins in aquatic weeds is the mola; the depth of body and small mouth distinguish it.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Hamilton in 1822 as Cyprinus mola in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches, p. 323, from the Ganga and other Bengali rivers. The specific epithet mola comes from the Bengali name.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Cyprinidae, the carp family; order Cypriniformes. The genus Amblypharyngodon (with blunt pharyngeal teeth) contains a few species of small South Asian carps with inferior mouths and plant-feeding habits.

Phylogenetic notes. Amblypharyngodon belongs to the cyprinid subfamilies of the Indian peninsula; its exact position among the basal cyprinids is debated.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Widespread and abundant in suitable habitats; no range contraction documented.
