गैंगेटिक गगाटा मछली / Gangetic Gagata

Gagata cenia (Hamilton, 1822) — Sisoridae

गैंगेटिक गगाटा मछली — बहती नदियों, रेतीले नालों और स्वच्छ नहरों में पाई जाने वाली एक छोटी, सुंदर और विशिष्ट देसी कैटफिश है। इसका शरीर बिना छिलके का, चिकना और हल्का पीला-चांदी जैसा होता है, जिस पर पीठ के ऊपर चार गहरी काली-भूरी पट्टियाँ (saddles) बनी होती हैं। इसका मुँह नीचे की ओर होता है और यह नदी की रेतीली तलहटी में चिपकी रहकर बहते पानी में भोजन तलाशती है। यह बहते पानी की स्वच्छता का एक प्राकृतिक सूचक है।

Field tip: Look along clean sandy shoals of flowing rivers. A small, bottom-hugging catfish with an inferior mouth, four bold dark saddles across a yellowish-silvery back, and dark marks on the dorsal and tail fin lobes, is the Gangetic Gagata.

हिंदी: रेतीली तलहटी पर रहने वाली यह छोटी मछली है। इसकी पीठ पर चार चौड़े काले-भूरे पट्टे बने होते हैं। इसका मुंह नीचे की ओर होता है और पीठ पर कांटा होता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Size as given by FishBase (Froese & Pauly 2025):

MeasurementRangeSource
Maximum length15 cm SLFroese & Pauly (2025), FishBase

Adult description:

Gagata cenia has an elongate body that is moderately compressed in the head region and strongly compressed towards the caudal peduncle (Hamilton 1822). The dorsal profile rises gently to the dorsal fin origin, while the ventral surface of the head and chest is flattened, facilitating adhesion to sandy riverbeds in strong currents (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The head is compressed and wedge-shaped; the snout is prominent, fleshy, and overhangs the mouth (Hamilton 1822). Eyes are situated high on the head, dorsolateral in position. The mouth is small, inferior, and transverse; the upper lip is thick and grooved (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Teeth are villiform, restricted to the jaws, and absent on the palate (Jayaram 2006).

Eight barbels are present: two nasal barbels that are extremely minute or rudimentary; two maxillary barbels with stiffened, dilated proximal bases that reach the pectoral fin base; and four mandibular barbels arranged in a nearly straight transverse row across the chin, with the outer pair reaching the gill opening (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

The dorsal fin is positioned well forward, armed with a strong, sharp spine that is smooth on its anterior face and finely serrated posteriorly, accompanied by 6 soft branched rays (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). A prominent, well-developed adipose fin is present along the rear half of the back (Jayaram 2006). The pectoral fins have a stout, internally serrated spine. The anal fin has 10 to 12 branched rays. The caudal fin is deeply forked with equal pointed lobes (Hamilton 1822).

Living specimens are yellowish-bronze to shining silver on the back, fading to white on the belly (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Four distinct dark brown or blackish saddle-like transverse bands cross the dorsum: one over the head, one behind the dorsal fin, one below the adipose fin, and one at the caudal base (Hamilton 1822). The dorsal fin has a dark blotch on its upper half, and each lobe of the caudal fin bears a dark longitudinal streak (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

हिंदी: इसका शरीर लगभग 15 सेमी तक लंबा, चिकना और बिना छिलके का होता है। सीना चपटा होता है जिससे यह बहते पानी में रेत पर जमी रहती है। पीठ पर चार काले पट्टे और कांटेदार पंख होते हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Gangetic Gagata is a benthic, rheophilic catfish strictly adapted to flowing riverine waterways (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). It inhabits perennial rivers, large sandy streams, and clear flowing canals with continuous water movement and clean sand-gravel substrates.

Adults rest directly on sandy riverbeds, using their flattened bellies and broad pectoral spines to maintain station against strong currents (Jayaram 2006). Small loose groups forage over shallow sandbars, probing into sand ripples with their stiffened maxillary barbels to locate buried midge larvae, caddisflies, and small worms (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The bold saddle pattern provides exceptional disruptive camouflage against sunlit, rippled sandbars (Jayaram 2006). They strictly avoid stagnant, mud-laden, or hypoxic waters, making them sensitive biological indicators of flowing river health.

हिंदी: यह मछली तेज बहते पानी और रेतीली तलहटी में रहना पसंद करती है। यह रेत पर चिपकी रहती है और अपनी मूंछों से रेत में छिपे कीड़ों को खोजती है। यह गंदे और रुके हुए पानी में नहीं रह सकती।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Seasonal phenology in northern Indian alluvial rivers across the agricultural year (Talwar & Jhingran 1991; Jayaram 2006):

Breeding coincides with the southwest monsoon rains (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Increased river velocity and high dissolved oxygen levels trigger spawning runs into sandy side channels. Mating takes place in clear flowing shallows over coarse sand and fine gravel. Females shed small, demersal, non-adhesive eggs that settle into sand crevices. The eggs hatch after a few days into tiny transparent larvae that develop rapidly among sand ripples (Jayaram 2006).

हिंदी: मानसून में जब नदियों में नया पानी बहता है, यह रेत के किनारों पर जाकर अंडे देती है। कुछ दिनों में अंडों से बच्चे निकलकर रेत के कणों के बीच छिपते हैं और बड़े होते हैं।

Diet and Feeding Behaviour आहार

Gagata cenia is an obligate benthic micro-predator and insectivore (Talwar & Jhingran 1991):

Natural diet components:

  • Aquatic insect larvae, including chironomid midges, caddisfly cases, and mayfly nymphs burrowed in sand (Jayaram 2006).
  • Small benthic crustaceans, including ostracods and tiny freshwater prawns (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).
  • Aquatic oligochaete worms extracted from sand ripples.
  • Microscopic benthic diatoms and fine organic biofilms ingested while probing sand.
हिंदी: यह तलहटी में रहने वाले कीड़ों, रेत के कीड़ों, मच्छरों के लार्वा और जलीय सूक्ष्म जीवों को खाकर अपना पेट भरती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Documented natural predators in northern Indian alluvial freshwater systems:

  • Riverine predatory fishes: Large catfishes like Bagarius bagarius (Goonch) and Wallago attu (Striped Catfish), and spiny eels like Mastacembelus armatus (Zig-zag Spiny Eel) hunt on sandy shoals.
  • Piscivorous birds: River terns, little egrets, and cormorants strike at catfishes in shallow sandbars.
  • Gharials and softshell turtles: Ambush bottom-dwelling sisorids in flowing channels.
  • River otters: Hunt schooling bottom fish along sandy river bends.
हिंदी: गूंज, बोआल और बाम जैसी बड़ी शिकारी मछलियाँ, बगुले, पनकौवे और घड़ियाल इस मछली का शिकार करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Nutritional value and artisanal fishery: Although small, Gagata cenia is harvested in considerable numbers by artisanal riverine fishers in the Ghaghara, Kuwano, and Rapti river basins using cast nets, fine seines, and bamboo funnel traps. It is prized by local consumers for its sweet, tender flesh and is eaten whole, fried or in curries, providing important animal protein, calcium, and phosphorus to fishing communities.

Bioindicator of river ecological health: Because Gagata cenia requires clean sand substrates, strong flow velocity, and high dissolved oxygen, its presence indicates a healthy, unpolluted river ecosystem free from excessive organic siltation (Jayaram 2006).

Traditional: Consumed as an everyday fresh river fish by riverside communities across eastern Uttar Pradesh.

Today: Sold fresh in rural fish markets (haats) along riverbanks as an affordable native food fish.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Utilizing Gagata cenia distribution as a bioindicator for monitoring riverbed sand mining impacts in the Ghaghara basin.

हिंदी: यह एक स्वादिष्ट और पौष्टिक छोटी नदीय मछली है। यह केवल साफ और बहते हुए पानी में ही जी सकती है, इसलिए इसकी उपस्थिति से पता चलता है कि नदी का पानी साफ और शुद्ध है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the northern Indian subcontinent, distributed across India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, and Myanmar (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

In India: Found throughout the Indus, Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Mahanadi river drainages, from foothill rivers to the plains (Menon 1999).

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded throughout the Ganges, Ghaghara, Rapti, and Ramganga river networks.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a resident native species in the Ghaghara and Kuwano river channels and major sandy tributaries.

हिंदी: यह भारत, पाकिस्तान, नेपाल, बांग्लादेश और म्यांमार की नदियों में पाई जाती है। उत्तर प्रदेश में घाघरा और कुआनो नदी के रेतीले हिस्सों में यह उपस्थित है।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 as Pimelodus cenia in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches, page 174. Type locality: Northern rivers of Bengal.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognized across its continental range.

Family and order placement. Order Siluriformes, family Sisoridae. The genus Gagata Bleeker, 1858, comprises small rheophilic torrent and river catfishes of South Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Gagata cenia is the type species of the genus Gagata. It is distinguished from allied sisorid genera by its compressed head, distinct dorsal saddles, and unique mandibular barbel arrangement (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the global IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. It maintains widespread populations in major river channels, though local habitats face pressure from river modification.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Gagata cenia है और यह Sisoridae परिवार की सदस्य है। प्रकृति में इसका वास नदियों में सुरक्षित है।
