सैफ़्रन-फ़ेस्ड ब्लू डार्ट / Saffron-faced Blue Dart

Pseudagrion rubriceps (Selys, 1876) — Coenagrionidae

सैफ़्रन-फ़ेस्ड ब्लू डार्ट — एक मध्यम आकार की सूतिका कीट जिसके नर का सिर और आगे का भाग (thorax) केसरी-नारंगी रंग का होता है और शरीर का बाकी हिस्सा नीला होता है। यह उथले, पौधों से भरे जलाशयों में पाई जाती है। अपने विशिष्ट दो-रंगीय सिर (orange face + blue body) से आसानी से पहचानी जा सकती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription

Field tip: The distinctive saffron-orange face and head combined with blue abdomen in males makes this damselfly unmistakable. Females are duller but retain the orange-yellow facial marking.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Body length32–37 mm
Hindwing length17–20 mm
Wing span36–40 mm

Adult male: Face and frons brilliant saffron-orange to golden-yellow. Eyes orange-red with yellow inner regions. Thorax predominantly blue-green with orange lateral patches. Abdomen bright blue with black bands at abdominal joints. This striking two-toned coloration (orange head + blue body) is diagnostic.

Adult female: Face and head orange-yellow like the male but less brilliant. Thorax greenish-yellow to brown-green. Abdomen greenish-yellow with darker markings.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Pseudagrion rubriceps is a medium-sized damselfly of clear, vegetation-rich freshwater wetlands. It is moderately sensitive to water pollution and requires healthy plant growth. The species prefers slow-moving water with good aquatic vegetation. Males are territorial and perch on emergent plants, pursuing rivals and hunting small flying insects. Females forage among vegetation.

The species is a moderate indicator of water quality; its presence suggests reasonably clean water and good vegetative habitat.

Larval Stage

Naiads are slender, greenish-brown, and found among submerged vegetation. Larval duration is approximately 10–12 weeks. Emergence occurs year-round, with seasonal peaks.

Adult (2–3 weeks)Territorial; oviposits in plant tissues
SeasonalityPresent year-round; activity highest spring and post-monsoon

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Adult prey:

  • Small midges and gnats
  • Mosquitoes
  • Tiny flies and aphids

Larval prey:

  • Mosquito larvae
  • Small crustaceans and aquatic worms

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Small warblers, flycatchers, and swallows
  • Fish: Small fish fry
  • Spiders: Tetragnathid and jumping spiders
  • Larger insects: Dragonfly naiads

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The Saffron-faced Blue Dart is not documented as a pest. It feeds on small flying insects, including mosquitoes, and may contribute to mosquito control in wetland and irrigated areas.

GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. The species is expected in vegetation-rich ponds and slow-moving water bodies throughout eastern UP, year-round. Look for the distinctive orange-faced, blue-bodied damselfly perching on floating plants. A photograph showing the saffron face and blue abdomen, with date and location, would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia; widespread from Pakistan and India to Myanmar, Thailand, and Indonesia.

In India: Scattered throughout wetland regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded in various wetlands.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in permanent and semi-permanent water bodies; unconfirmed locally.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Selys Longchamps described Pseudagrion rubriceps in 1876. Type locality: India. The species name refers to the reddish head (rubri = red; ceps = head).

Subspecies. Monotypic.

Family and order placement. Order Odonata, suborder Zygoptera, family Coenagrionidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Pseudagrion is a diverse, primarily Afrotropical genus with some Asian species. P. rubriceps is one of the few Asian representatives.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC). The species has a broad range and stable populations where suitable habitat persists.
