लालसर / Red-crested Pochard

Netta rufina (Pallas, 1773) — Anatidae

लालसर — एक बड़ी, रंगीन सर्दियों की डाइविंग बत्तख जो मध्य एशिया से अक्टूबर-नवंबर में आती है। नर के चमकीले लाल सिर और पीले पैरों से यह तुरंत पहचानी जाती है। यह गहरे पानी में गोता लगाकर खाना खोजती है। बड़े तालाबों और झीलों को पसंद करती है।

Field tip: A large diving duck with a chestnut-red head and bright yellow legs (males) — is a Red-crested Pochard. Females are harder to separate from other pochards but show the yellow legs and pale crown. Always associated with larger, deeper water bodies in winter.

Size: 53–57 cm long; wingspan 85–102 cm. Weight 900–1200 g (males heavier).

Plumage (male): Head and upper neck chestnut-red with slight crest at crown. Lower neck and upper back black. Lower back and rump black. Breast black. Flanks and belly white. Tail black. Wings: coverts darker; speculum (secondary feathers) white.

Plumage (female): Overall dull brown. Crown and face paler buff-brown. Upperparts darker brown. Underparts pale buff-brown. Less crest than male. Wing pattern similar to male.

Bill: Red or orange-red with black tip (males); duller grey-brown with red base (females). Broad, flattened, and slightly spatulate.

Legs and feet: Bright yellow or yellow-orange (both sexes); very long and placed far back on body (adaptation for diving).

Eyes: Red or orange-red (males); brown (females).

Sexes: Strongly dimorphic; males spectacularly colourful.

Vocalisations. Relatively quiet species:

  1. Drake call: A soft whistle, sometimes silent; given during courtship.
  2. Duck (female) call: Soft croaking and grunting.
हिंदी: बहुत शांत पक्षी। कम आवाज़ करता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Omnivorous diver: Red-crested Pochards are diving ducks that feed on:

  • Aquatic vegetation (seeds, shoots, tubers)
  • Aquatic invertebrates (aquatic insects, snails, small crustaceans)
  • Small fish and tadpoles
  • Aquatic vegetation from the bottom of lakes

Feeding behaviour: Skilled divers; dive 1–3 m to reach bottom vegetation and invertebrates. Swim low on water (heavy relative to dabbling ducks). Nocturnal and diurnal feeders.

Social behaviour: Moderately gregarious; form flocks. Males and females may segregate during some periods. Less tame than dabbling ducks.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosts on open water of large lakes and reservoirs; diurnal feeding and resting on deeper water.

Territorial: Non-territorial during winter; however, males may defend mates vigorously during courtship season.

Predators: Eagles, harriers, and wild dogs may take adults. Chicks vulnerable to corvids and aquatic predators in breeding areas.

हिंदी: शांत, गहरे पानी की बत्तख। गोता लगाकर खाना खोजता है। शर्मीली बत्तख।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives October–November from breeding grounds; departs April. Does not breed in India.

Breeding range: Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Mongolia, Caspian region) and southern Russia. Breeds March–July.

Migration: Breeds on large lakes and reservoirs of Central Asia; southward migration begins August–September. Arrives in Indian subcontinent October–November. Winter stay: November–March; most birds depart by April.

Wintering: Concentrated on large lakes and reservoirs with adequate depth and open water.

Moult: Complete moult before autumn migration; eclipse plumage acquired and maintained during wintering season.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Marsh Harrier, Peregrine Falcon
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, wild dog, fox, pike (fish predator)
  • At nest (breeding grounds): Corvids, weasels, martens, rats
  • Parasites: Waterfowl helminths, protozoa

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minimal crop damage: Red-crested Pochards prefer large water bodies and rarely visit agricultural fields; virtually no reported crop damage.

Habitat requirements: Dependent on large, deep freshwater lakes and reservoirs with good water quality and adequate vegetation. Sensitive to wetland degradation and pollution.

Hunting history: Hunted for food and sport in Europe and Asia; now protected in India under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Scientific interest: Used as indicator species for large-lake ecosystem health and hydrology.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Red-crested Pochards are expected at larger lakes and reservoirs in the Basti–Ayodhya region from October through April. Mishra et al. (2019) recorded this species at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary during October 2017–March 2018. This species is less common than dabbling ducks but occurs at deeper water bodies with adequate food supply. Published habitat and range information place this species in large lakes and reservoirs during winter months, with preference for the Ghaghra river system and adjoining larger water bodies.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Mongolia, Caspian region) and southern Russia; winters across the Mediterranean, Middle East, South Asia, and East Africa.

In India: Regular but local winter visitor to larger wetlands and reservoirs, particularly in northern and central regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to major lakes and reservoirs, with records from the Gangetic plain and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to larger water bodies including Bakhira Bird Sanctuary and adjoining lakes. Present October–April, with peak numbers December–February. Prefers deeper water bodies compared to dabbling ducks.

Field rule: A large diving duck with a chestnut-red head and bright yellow legs (males) — is a Red-crested Pochard. The yellow legs are diagnostic and unique among Indian pochards.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Netta rufina was described by Peter Simon Pallas in 1773 in Reise durch verschiedene Provinzen des Russischen Reichs, volume 2, page 710, as the "red-crested pochard" from Central Asia.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; all populations are homogeneous with no significant geographic variation.

Family and order placement. Order Anseriformes; family Anatidae; genus Netta contains the diving ducks (Pochard group). The genus contains three species, all Northern Hemisphere breeders.

Phylogenetic notes. Netta rufina is closely related to Aythya species (Common Pochard, Tufted Duck) and shares many ecological traits with them, though molecular evidence places Netta as a distinct lineage.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Global population is estimated at 300,000–500,000 birds, stable to increasing. The species has benefited from protection and management of key wintering wetlands across its range.
