यूरेशियन विजन / Eurasian Wigeon

Mareca penelope (Linnaeus, 1758) — Anatidae

यूरेशियन विजन — उत्तरी यूरोप से सर्दियों के लिए आने वाली एक मध्यम आकार की बत्तख, जो नर में सुंदर लाल-भूरे सिर और पीले माथे के लिए पहचानी जाती है। यह पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में तालाबों और नदियों के पास अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक मिलने की उम्मीद है। मादा का रंग भूरा होता है, लेकिन पंखों की पट्टी समान होती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 45–51 cm long; wingspan 80–98 cm. Weight 450–850 g (males heavier).

Plumage (male): Striking and distinctive. Head chestnut-rufous with buff-yellow or white crown (from eye backward). Neck chestnut. Back grey with fine vermiculation. Breast pale buff. Belly white. Stern (tail coverts) black. Wing coverts grey; speculum (inner secondary feathers) iridescent green and black.

Plumage (female): Rufous-brown head and neck (less bright than male's). Body brown with finer vermiculation than male. Underparts pale greyish-buff. Wing pattern similar to male; green speculum visible in flight.

Bill: Blue-grey with black tip; short, slender, and pointed.

Legs and feet: Grey or grey-brown; placed forward on body, typical of dabbling ducks.

Eyes: Dark; small and bright.

Sexes: Strongly dimorphic; females much duller than males.

Vocalisations. Vocal species; frequent calls:

  1. Drake call: A whistled wheeo or whee-oo, often given in flight.
  2. Duck (female) call: Soft quacking and grunting, less harsh than Mallard.
हिंदी: नर की तीक्ष्ण सीटी "व्हीयो"। मादा नरम क़वैक करती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Herbivorous dabbler: Eurasian Wigeons are highly specialised grazers, feeding on:

  • Aquatic grasses and sedges (primary diet)
  • Wetland plants and algae
  • Very few aquatic invertebrates (unlike other dabbling ducks)
  • Occasionally grain in agricultural fields

Feeding ecology: Primarily dabblers and up-enders; prefer shallow water where they can reach vegetation on bottom. Often graze on muddy margins. Notable for their ability to "steal" plant food dislodged by diving ducks.

Social behaviour: Highly gregarious in winter; form flocks, often in mixed-species groups. Males and females may separate during feeding periods.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosts on open water; diurnal rests in reed beds and shallow ponds.

Territorial: Non-territorial during winter; highly mobile within wintering range.

Predators: Eagles, harriers, and predatory mammals may take adults. Chicks vulnerable to corvids in breeding areas.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives October–November from breeding grounds; departs April–May. Does not breed in India.

Breeding range: Northern Europe and western Asia (Scandinavia, Russia, western Siberia). Breeds March–July.

Migration: Breeds in temperate and boreal wetlands; southward migration begins August–September. Arrives in Indian subcontinent October–November; remains through March; departure peaks April–May.

Wintering: Distributed across shallow wetlands and river systems; preference for nutrient-rich wetlands with abundant vegetation.

Moult: Complete moult before autumn departure; eclipse plumage acquired and maintained during wintering.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Golden Eagle, White-tailed Eagle, Marsh Harrier, Peregrine Falcon
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, wild dog, fox, hyena
  • At nest (breeding grounds): Corvids, weasels, martens
  • Parasites: Waterfowl helminths

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minimal crop damage: Eurasian Wigeons rarely feed in agricultural fields and cause negligible crop damage due to their strong preference for aquatic vegetation.

Herbivorous ecology: Their strict herbivory makes them less flexible than other dabbling ducks; they require wetlands with abundant vegetation.

Hunting history: Hunted in Europe and parts of Asia; now protected in India under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Ecological role: Play a role in aquatic plant control in wetlands, though impact is minimal in most systems.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Eurasian Wigeons are expected in the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems in eastern UP from October through April, based on their published range and habitat preferences. The nearest published record is Bakhira, across the district border from Basti, where Mishra et al. (2019) counted it through the winter of 2017–18 in smaller numbers than the other ducks, with a December peak; the Parvati Arga and Ayodhya surveys did not list it. The species' preference for vegetation-rich shallow wetlands means it should be looked for in reed-fringed ponds and margins of lakes during the winter season. A first record would be a photograph clearly showing the distinctive male head pattern (chestnut with buff crown) or female's rufous-brown head, with place, date, and GPS coordinates.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in northern Europe and western Asia (Scandinavia to western Siberia); winters across the Mediterranean, Middle East, South Asia, and East Africa.

In India: Widespread winter visitor across northern India, but with more limited distribution in central and southern regions. Records concentrated in the Indo-Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to major wetlands and river systems, with records from the Gangetic plain and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in winter (October–April) along the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems based on published range, though not yet recorded in local surveys. Should be looked for in vegetation-rich wetlands and shallow lakes.

Field rule: A dabbling duck with a chestnut-red head and buff crown (males) or rufous-brown head (females), with iridescent green wing patch in flight — is a Eurasian Wigeon. The head pattern of males is diagnostic and unmistakable.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Mareca penelope was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th Edition, volume 1, page 127, as Anas penelope. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; populations across the breeding range are homogeneous with no consistent geographic variation.

Family and order placement. Order Anseriformes; family Anatidae; genus Mareca contains dabbling ducks of northern origin with specialized feeding ecology.

Phylogenetic notes. Mareca penelope is closely related to the Gadwall (M. strepera) and American Wigeon (M. americana); molecular phylogenetics places them in a monophyletic clade.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Global population is very large (>5 million) and stable to increasing. The species has expanded its range southward into the Mediterranean and East Africa in recent decades, possibly in response to climate change.
