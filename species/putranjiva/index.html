पुत्रंजीवा / Putranjiva

Putranjiva roxburghii Wall. — Putranjivaceae

पुत्रंजीवा — पूरे भारत में पूजा जाने वाला एक पवित्र पेड़ है, खासकर हिंदु परिवारों में बच्चों के लिए शुभ माना जाता है (पुत्र = बेटा, जीव = जीवन देने वाला)। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में यह मंदिरों, तुलसी के बागों (बसेड़ों), और पुराने गाँवों के पवित्र वृक्षों के समूह में पाया जाता है। यह एक मध्यम आकार का सदाबहार पेड़ है जिसकी पत्तियाँ लंबी, सरल, चमकीली होती हैं। बसंत के फूल बहुत छोटे और नाज़ुक होते हैं। फल मांसल, गोल, छोटा, पहले हरा फिर गहरे रंग का होता है। भारतीय संस्कृति में इसे बहुत महत्व दिया जाता है और बच्चों का नामकरण इस पेड़ के नीचे किया जाता है।

Field tip: Look for a tall evergreen tree in temples and sacred groves with long glossy leaves and in May–June for small dark fleshy fruits. The tree is a landmark in village sacred spaces.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: लंबा, सदाबहार पेड़, लंबी चमकीली पत्तियाँ, छोटे सफेद-पीले फूल, छोटे गोल गहरे फल।

Morphology आकारिकी

Trunk and bark: Straight or slightly crooked, bark smooth and grey-brown on young trees, becoming darker and fissured with age. Wood hard, pale, durable.

Leaves: Alternate, simple, lanceolate to narrowly oblong, 8–20 cm long, 2.5–5 cm wide. Apex pointed, base tapered. Margin entire. Dark glossy green above, paler below. Pinnate venation prominent. Petiolate (leaf stalk 0.5–1.5 cm).

Flowers: Unisexual (male and female on same tree, but in different clusters). Small, 2–3 mm, greenish-yellow, borne in axillary clusters. Male flowers numerous, female flowers fewer. Calyx five-lobed, no petals. Pollinated by bees and wind.

Fruit: A drupe, round to slightly oblong, 1–1.5 cm diameter, fleshy, first green turning dark purple or black when ripe. Thin fleshy exocarp surrounds a hard stone containing a single seed. Aril present, sweet to taste.

हिंदी: सदाबहार, लंबी पत्तियाँ, बहुत छोटे फूल, छोटा गोल फल, पहले हरा फिर काला।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A native tree of the Indian subcontinent, found naturally in semi-evergreen and moist deciduous forests, and extensively cultivated in sacred groves and temples. In Basti, it is well-adapted and long-lived. Growth is moderate; trees reach maturity at 15–20 years and live for 50+ years. The tree is drought-tolerant once established and prefers well-drained soils.

Spring flowers attract bees and other pollinators. Ripe fruit is eaten by birds including Common Myna and Rose-ringed Parakeet, as well as by squirrels and bats. The tree provides good shade and is commonly planted in sacred spaces.

हिंदी: यह भारतीय जंगलों का अपना पेड़ है। मंदिरों और पवित्र बागों में बहुत महत्वपूर्ण। धीमी गति से बढ़ता है पर बहुत लंबी उम्र वाला।

Seeds germinate slowly in warm, moist conditions. Seedling growth is slow for first 2–3 years. Lifespan exceeds 100 years.

हिंदी: साल भर हरा रहता है। फरवरी-अप्रैल में फूल। मई-जुलाई में फल। बहुत धीमे बढ़ने वाला।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Bees and wasps on flowers; wind pollination also occurs.
  • Fruit dispersers: Common Myna, Rose-ringed Parakeet, Northern Palm Squirrel, Indian Flying Fox.
  • Companions: Often planted with Neem, Jamun, Mango in sacred groves and temples.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Relatively pest-free and disease-free.
  • Occasional leaf-eating caterpillars; rarely cause serious damage.
  • No major insect pests documented.
  • Leaf spot fungi in very wet years; improves naturally with drier weather.
  • Heart rot only in severely waterlogged soils.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Cultural and spiritual significance:

Putranjiva is deeply embedded in Hindu religious tradition. The tree is associated with fertility and childbirth, and is planted to pray for sons. In some regions, the naming ceremony of a child is performed under this tree. The vernacular name putrakara (son-maker) reflects this cultural significance.

Traditional use:

The fruit is eaten fresh, though not commonly in markets. The leaves and bark have been used in traditional Ayurvedic medicine, though documented uses are limited.

Horticultural significance: The tree is valued as a shade and ornamental tree in temple gardens, institutional grounds, and heritage orchards. Its slow growth, evergreen foliage, and cultural importance make it a landmark tree.

हिंदी: बहुत पवित्र पेड़ माना जाता है; बच्चों के जन्म पर पूजा जाता है। फल खा सकते हैं पर साधारण बाज़ार में नहीं मिलता। मंदिरों में बहुत महत्वपूर्ण।

Its presence is usually deliberate (planted for spiritual reasons) rather than natural. Any tree is worth recording with its location and condition.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Indian subcontinent from the Himalayan foothills to South India; also present in Sri Lanka and Bangladesh.

In India: Widespread in semi-evergreen and moist deciduous forests; extensively cultivated in temples and gardens throughout.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant in all forests; common in temples and sacred groves.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Native to forest areas; commonly planted in temples and villages.

Field rule: A tall evergreen tree with simple long glossy lanceolate leaves, tiny greenish flowers in spring, and small round dark fruits, planted deliberately in temples and sacred spaces, is putranjiva.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Nicolas Wallich in 1824 in Numerical List of Dried Specimens (no. 7871). The genus Putranjiva is derived from Sanskrit putrajīvā (son-giving plant), combining putra (son) and jīva (life-giver). The species name roxburghii honors William Roxburgh, a botanist.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Putranjivaceae (a monogeneric family with only Putranjiva as the genus), order Malpighiales.

Phylogenetic notes. Putranjiva roxburghii is the single species in its family. Recent molecular work suggests its closest relatives are in the Salicaceae.

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC). Abundant in natural forests and widely cultivated; stable across its range.
