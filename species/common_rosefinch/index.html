तूती / Common Rosefinch

Carpodacus erythrinus (Pallas, 1811) — Fringillidae

तूती — यह छोटी सर्दियों की चिड़िया सितंबर से अप्रैल तक पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के बागों, खेतों और खुली जगहों में रहती है। नर का रंग लाल या गुलाबी होता है जो सर्दियों में फीका पड़ जाता है। मादा भूरा रंग की होती है। यह बीजों को खाती है। जमीन पर और झाड़ों में झुंड में रहती है।

Field tip: In open cultivation and gardens in winter, look for small finches, males showing rosy or red plumage (duller in winter), females showing heavy streaking. The unstreaked belly distinguishes females from sparrows. Flocks are typical. The call is diagnostic.

Size: 14–16 cm long; wingspan 24–27 cm. Weight 19–28 g. A small, compact finch.

Male plumage (fresh in winter): Head, throat, upper breast, and rump pink or rosy-red. Red is the dominant color. Mantle and back dull brown-red. Lower breast and belly pink-buff. Wing feathers and tail dark with pink edges. Feather wear over winter causes red tips to become obscured by buff feather edges, making birds appear duller.

Male plumage (spring, before departure): Red becomes brighter as buff feather edges wear away, revealing the red base color.

Female plumage: Olive-brown to greyish-brown overall, heavily streaked with dark brown. Breast and underparts streaked; belly less streaked (more uniform) than breast. Rump and wing edges tinged red or pink. Supercilium (eyebrow stripe) indistinct.

Bill: Thick, conical, pale at base, dark at tip; adapted for seed-eating.

Legs and feet: Pale brown; strong.

Eyes: Dark; small.

Juveniles: Similar to females; duller overall.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A melodious warble.; not frequently heard in winter.
  2. Call: Whistled tu-it or tu-tu, sometimes in series.
  3. Contact calls: Soft chirps within feeding flocks.
हिंदी: नर की बोली सुरीली है। "तु-इट" या "तु-तु" कॉल। सर्दी में कम गाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Seed-eating specialist: Common Rosefinch feeds primarily on seeds, particularly in winter. It feeds on:

  • Seeds of grasses and herbs
  • Seeds of cultivated crops (millet, rape, sunflower)
  • Small seeds from shrubs and low vegetation
  • Occasionally insects (especially during breeding season)

Feeding behaviour: Forages on ground in loose flocks, hopping and gleaning seeds. Also picks seeds from standing vegetation, including grass heads and low shrubs.

Social behaviour: Highly gregarious, especially in winter. Forms flocks, often with other finches and sparrows.

Habitat preference: Open woodland, scrub, forest edge, cultivation margins, gardens with scattered trees. Prefers areas with available seed food.

Flock behaviour: Flocks move together; constantly vocalizing with soft calls.

Predators: Small raptors (Eurasian Kestrel, Merlin), cats, snakes may take individuals.

Movement: Within a flock, maintains loose cohesion but individuals move independently when feeding.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहती है। जमीन पर और झाड़ों पर बीज खोजती है। गौरैये के साथ भी रहती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Absent during breeding season (April–August); returns to eastern UP from September and remains through May.

Arrival and departure: First flocks arrive in August–September; numbers peak in December–February; departure begins in March–April, with most birds gone by May.

Breeding (elsewhere): Breeds in the Himalayas, central Asia, and eastern Europe. Nest is a cup in trees or shrubs. Clutch of 4–5 eggs.

Moult: Complete moult on breeding grounds before arrival. Pre-basic moult in winter results in fresh, bright red plumage visible by spring before departure.

Roosts: Roosting in trees and shrubs; flocks roost communally.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। पहाड़ों और एशिया में घोंसला बनाता है। बड़े झुंड में रहती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Eurasian Kestrel, Merlin, larger birds, snakes (to eggs/nestlings)
  • Mammalian predators: Domestic cat, weasel, occasionally mongoose
  • Parasites: Blood parasites, internal helminths, ectoparasites
हिंदी: बाजें शिकार करती हैं। बिल्ली भी खतरनाक है। झुंड में होने से सुरक्षा मिलती है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is prohibited.

Seed predation: Large flocks may consume significant amounts of cultivated crop seeds, particularly millet and rape. However, they also consume weed seeds, providing some pest control benefit.

Cultural relevance: Known from ornithological records. The red plumage makes males highly distinctive in classical bird guides.

Scientific value: Population monitoring important for understanding winter visitor impacts on agriculture and seed availability; flock dynamics illustrate collective foraging behavior.

हिंदी: वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम 1972 के अनुसूची IV के अंतर्गत पूरी तरह संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार निषिद्ध है।

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from September to May, particularly in open cultivation and gardens with scattered trees in Basti and Ayodhya districts. Published handbooks document this species as a regular winter visitor across northern India.

Habitat preference: Open woodland, scrub, forest edge, cultivation margins, gardens with scattered trees.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from Himalayan and Central Asian breeding grounds are settled.

Nearest published record: Handbook of the Birds of India and the Western Palearctic document this species as a winter visitor throughout the Indo-Gangetic Plain. Its flocks gather where seeding grasses and crops offer food.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date), such as a flock feeding in a field, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeding range in the Himalayas, central Asia, and eastern Europe east to Japan. Winters very widely across Asia, including throughout the Indian subcontinent, southeast Asia, and down to Indonesia.

In India: Distributed across the Indian subcontinent as a winter visitor (September–May). Very common and widespread.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in winter in cultivated areas and gardens of the plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular and common winter visitor in cultivation and gardens; expected on range grounds rather than confirmed in local surveys.

Field rule: Common Rosefinch males show rosy-red plumage (duller in winter); females are heavily streaked with unstreaked belly. If the bird shows a black bib and grey crown, it is a House Sparrow. If it is entirely red and tiny, it is a Red Munia.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Peter Simon Pallas in 1811 as Fringilla erythrina, based on specimens from the Caucasus.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognized; C. e. erythrinus winters widely in Asia.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Fringillidae (true finches). The genus Carpodacus contains the rosefinches, primarily Asian.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular work has placed rosefinches in the subfamily Carduelinae within Fringillidae, closely related to goldfinches and siskins.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and very widely distributed. The species is adaptable to human-modified habitats and winters across a vast area. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
