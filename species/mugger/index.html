मगर / Mugger Crocodile

Crocodylus palustris Lesson, 1831 — Crocodylidae

मगर — भारतीय नदियों का सबसे बड़ा और सबसे भयानक शिकारी, पर यह केवल और केवल नदियों में रहता है, विशेषकर बस्ती और अयोध्या जिलों की सीमा पर बहने वाली घाघरा और राप्ती नदी में। यह छोटी नावों, मवेशियों, और मनुष्यों के लिए ख़तरनाक हो सकता है। इसका पेट हरा-काला, जबड़े शक्तिशाली, दाँत बड़े। भारतीय वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम 1972 की अनुसूची I में पूरी तरह सुरक्षित। घाघरा और कटर्निहाट-गिरवा आबादी इस क्षेत्र के निकटतम ज्ञात रिकॉर्ड हैं।

Field tip: In the Ghaghra or Rapti rivers, a large broad-headed, heavily armored crocodile with a brownish-green back is a Mugger. Dangerous; approach only at a distance under expert guidance.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for Indian populations):

MeasurementRange
Total length3–4 m (males), 2–3 m (females)
Maximum recorded length5–6 m
Weight400–800 kg (males), 150–300 kg (females)

Head: Broad and massive, adapted for powerful jaw closure. The snout is broader and less V-shaped than in the saltwater crocodile (Crocodylus porosus). The 4th tooth of the lower jaw is prominent and visible when the mouth is closed—a key diagnostic character.

Body: Heavily built and robust. The dorsal surface is covered with thick keeled osteoderms (bony plates) arranged in regular rows. The scales are rough and provide excellent camouflage in turbid river water.

Colour: Olive-brown to dark brown dorsally; the ventral surface (belly and throat) is pale yellow or cream.

Limbs: Short, muscular, and positioned laterally (to the sides), allowing the body to rest on the ground between bursts of swimming or movement.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are considerably larger than females; males have thicker jaw muscles and broader heads.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

An ambush predator of rivers and large connected water bodies. It remains motionless in shallow water, with only its eyes and nostrils above the surface, waiting for prey. When a fish, frog, turtle, or small mammal approaches, the crocodile launches a rapid sideways strike with its powerful tail, then drags prey into deep water to drown and dismember it.

It basks regularly, hauling out onto banks in the morning to thermoregulate and to shed parasites and algae from its skin. Basking behaviour is most visible in the early morning and late afternoon.

Territorial and somewhat social, with multiple individuals tolerating each other in shared river stretches. Breeding occurs in the monsoon; females dig burrows in river banks and lay clutches of 15–30 eggs. Maternal care is limited but present—females have been observed guarding nests.

Lifespan is long; individuals may live 40+ years.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Adults: No natural predators. Large individuals are apex predators.

Eggs and hatchlings:

  • Monitor lizards
  • Jackals and feral dogs
  • Birds (crows, eagles)
  • Larger crocodiles (cannibalism)

Human persecution: Hunting (historically and illegally), habitat alteration.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Conservation status. The Mugger Crocodile is protected under Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, India — the highest protection level. It is classified as Vulnerable by the IUCN due to habitat loss, river damming, pollution, and historical hunting.

Danger to humans. Muggers attack humans who wade, swim, or cross rivers, and occasionally take livestock coming to water. In the Ghaghra region, local people are aware of the risk and take precautions.

Habitat requirement. The species requires perennial water bodies with suitable nesting habitat (riverbanks). Dams and barrages fragment populations; pollution degrades habitat.

Conflict mitigation. Crocodile sanctuaries and captive breeding programs in Uttar Pradesh have helped stabilise populations. Public education on avoiding risk (not wading at dusk/dawn, not crossing at known crocodile crossings) is essential.

Religious significance. The crocodile is recognized in Hindu tradition as the mount (vahana) of Ganga (the goddess of the river). Cultural respect for the species exists in some communities.

Today. Population recovery is occurring in protected stretches of the Ghaghra and adjoining rivers due to strict protection and captive breeding releases. The nearest known population to Basti district is in the Ghaghra–Rapti system and the Katarniaghat–Girwa population in the national record.

Range-restricted: The Mugger is expected only in or along the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems that border Basti and Ayodhya districts. It does not occur in small village tanks, irrigation channels, or in areas away from major river corridors. The nearest published records are from the Ghaghra surveys and the Katarniaghat–Girwa population of the Girwa river in the national survey records. Outside of these river systems, the species should not be expected in the district.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent — India, Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Bangladesh. Fragmented populations.

In India: Major populations persist in the Chambal, Son, Godavari, Krishna, and a few northern river systems. The Uttar Pradesh population is restricted to the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers.

In Uttar Pradesh: Primarily in the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems; historically more widespread, now critically fragmented.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected only in the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers that form the district borders. Rare and localized.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by René-Primevère Lesson in 1831 in Voyage Autour du Monde, Reptiles, p. 303. The epithet palustris refers to its habitat preference for marshes and swamps.

Subspecies. The Indian populations are considered the nominate subspecies Crocodylus palustris palustris. A Sri Lankan subspecies C. p. simus is sometimes recognized but validity is debated.

Family and order placement. Crocodylidae, the true crocodiles; order Crocodylia, class Reptilia.

Phylogenetic notes. Crocodylus is a genus of large-bodied apex crocodilians found on multiple continents. The mugger is sister to the American crocodile (Crocodylus acutus) in phylogenetic reconstructions.

IUCN status rationale. Vulnerable due to habitat loss (river damming, wetland drainage), pollution, and conflict with humans in agricultural areas. Population trends are mixed: some populations recovering due to protection; others declining.
