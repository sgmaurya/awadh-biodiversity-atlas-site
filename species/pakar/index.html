पाकड़ / White Fig

Ficus virens Aiton — Moraceae

पाकड़ — पेड़ जो कभी-कभी दीवार पर चढ़ते हुए शुरू होता है और फिर दीवार को ढक देता है। इसकी बहु-तने की आकृति (multiple stems) और हवाई जड़ें इसे अलग बनाती हैं। पत्तियाँ छोटी, चिकनी, हरी; फल नरम, मीठे, साल भर पकते हैं। पक्षियों और चमगादड़ों का प्रिय भोजन। कुछ गाँवों में इसे पवित्र माना जाता है और इसके नीचे पशुओं को बाँधा जाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall to medium tree, 5–12 m, or a climbing fig on walls and rocks; multi-stemmed
LeavesAlternate, simple, elliptic-lanceolate, 4–10 cm, glossy, margins entire
FruitsFig fruits (syconia) 1–2 cm across, borne in pairs at leaf axils on branch tips, white-green ripening to yellow, reddish or purple
Aerial rootsThin, hair-like, descending from upper branches to soil or rock surface, anchoring the plant
BarkSmooth, greyish, exuding white latex when injured

Field tip: Look for a tree with paired figs at the branch tips (not on the trunk like gular) and small, smooth, glossy leaves. The presence of hanging aerial roots is a sure sign of pakar.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: शाखाओं के सिरों पर जोड़ों में अंजीर (ट्रंक पर नहीं), छोटी चिकनी पत्तियाँ, हवाई जड़ें। यह दीवार पर भी चढ़ सकता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (northern Indian plains):

MeasurementRange
Height5–12 m

Leaf width1.5–5 cm

Trunk and bark: Single or multi-stemmed; bark smooth, light grey, exuding white latex. Wood pale, soft, not durable.

Leaves: Alternate, simple, elliptic to lanceolate, tapering to both ends, glossy above, entire margins, short petiole.

Inflorescence and fruit: Figs borne in axillary pairs on branch tips, greenish-white ripening to yellow, reddish or purplish. Obligately pollinated by fig wasps.

Aerial roots: Thin, filament-like, arising from upper branches and descending to rock, soil or masonry, functioning as supplementary roots and anchors.

हिंदी: छोटी, चिकनी, पूरी पत्तियाँ; शाखाओं के जोड़ों में अंजीर; हवाई जड़ें दीवार पर उतरती हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A tree of deciduous and dry deciduous woodland, but equally at home starting life on an old wall or rocky outcrop as a hemi-epiphyte (a plant that begins as a dependent but can live independently), dropping aerial roots that eventually reach soil and forming a multi-stemmed structure. The abundant, year-round fruiting makes it a keystone food source. It is grazed lightly by cattle and goats as a browse tree; the green figs are soft enough for young animals.

The tree is also associated with Hindu tradition and is planted deliberately near temples and water sources, where its aerial roots aid in slope stabilisation.

हिंदी: दीवार से शुरू हो सकता है और फिर आजाद पेड़ बन सकता है। साल भर फल देता है। गाय, भैंस का हल्का भोजन। मंदिरों के पास लगाया जाता है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Frugivores: Indian Flying Fox, mynas, parakeets, pigeons; bats and birds are the main seed dispersers.
  • Pollinators: Fig wasps.
  • Epiphyte habitat: Young seedlings of orchids and ferns germinate in the bark.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating beetles and caterpillars in the monsoon.
  • Aerial roots can eventually damage walls if not managed; not a serious concern.
  • Generally healthy and pest-resistant.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Fruit. Eaten fresh; the soft figs are sweet but less traded than gular fruit.
  • Fodder. The leaves are browsed by cattle, goats and sheep, especially when drought makes grass scarce.
  • Brake for erosion. The aerial roots stabilise loose slopes and embankments.
  • Sacred. Planted deliberately near temples and sacred pools.

Today. The fruit is eaten locally but not traded significantly. Its primary value is shade, shelter and sacred association.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The role of Ficus virens as a fodder and browse tree during drought has not been quantified for the plains; feeding trials would help assess its value.

हिंदी: फल मीठे, ताज़े खाए जाते हैं। सूखे में पशुओं का हरा भोजन। ढलानों को थामता है। देवता का पेड़।

To be expected in village groves, old fortifications, and temple grounds. Less common than gular but persistent where planted.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to South Asia from Pakistan through India to Sri Lanka and south-eastern Asia; wild in tropical deciduous and semi-evergreen forest.

In India: Widespread in the peninsula and the Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in cultivated settings; status in wild woodland uncertain.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in village groves and temple grounds; wild distribution unknown.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How fast do the aerial roots grow downward? — Mark a root and measure monthly over a year.
  2. When do most of the figs ripen? — Visit a tree monthly and estimate fruit count and ripeness.
  3. Which animals visit the tree most often, and at what time of day? — Watch early morning and evening.
  4. Do cows and goats prefer the leaves of pakar or neem? / गायें पाकड़ की पत्ती को नीम की पत्ती से ज़्यादा खाती हैं? — Offer both leaves to an animal and observe preference.
  5. Is there any pakar growing on an old fort, wall or building in your area? — Record its location and age.

Field rule: A fig with glossy, smooth, small leaves; figs in pairs at branch tips; and descending aerial roots anchoring the stem is pakar.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by William Aiton in Hortus Kewensis, 2nd ed. (1810: 467), based on cultivated plants grown at Kew Gardens. The epithet virens is Latin for "greenish" or "living", referring to the evergreen nature.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Moraceae, order Rosales. Ficus virens is a derived species within the large and diverse genus Ficus.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies place F. virens in the subgenus Urostigma, which includes the strangler figs of tropical forests.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread and common in cultivation.
