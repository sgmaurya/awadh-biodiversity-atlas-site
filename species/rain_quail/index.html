चीना बटेया / Rain Quail

Coturnix coromandelica (J.F.Gmelin, 1789) — Phasianidae

चीना बटेया — उत्तर भारत के खेतों और घास के मैदानों में रहने वाली एक छोटी बटेर। बेकर (1928) के अनुसार नर के सीने और ऊपरी पेट पर एक बड़ा गहरा काला धब्बा होता है, गले पर काली और सफेद धारियां होती हैं तथा पीठ भूरी व धारियों वाली होती है; मादा के सीने पर काला धब्बा नहीं होता। यह बारिश शुरू होने पर खेतों में घास के नीचे घोंसला बनाती है। इसकी संगीतमय आवाज 'व्हिट-व्हिट-व्हिट-व्हिट' लगातार गूंजती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1928):

MeasurementRange
BiometricsWing 83 to 92 mm. ; tail 30 to 32 mm. ; tarsus 25 to 26 mm. ; culmen about 13 mm. (Baker 1928)

The Rain Quail is a small, compact gallinaceous bird resembling the Common Quail but readily distinguished in male plumage by its bold black breast shield. Stuart Baker (1928, p. 375) described the adult male: "Upper plumage, wings and tail the same as in the Common Quail but generally rather richer and deeper in tint ; primaries and outer secondaries brown, edged pale but with no bars; head, chin and throat only differing from the Common Quail in having broad, more jet-black bars and the intervening spaces very pure white ; centre of breast and upper abdomen black ; flanks fulvous with broad streaks of velvety-black."

The female is somewhat smaller and lacks the black breast patch, being distinguished from the female Common Quail by unbarred primaries and smaller dimensions (Baker 1928, p. 376). Soft parts: iris hazel to deep brown; bill black in breeding season; legs and feet fleshy to fleshy-grey (Baker 1928, p. 375).

हिंदी:
बेकर (1928) के अनुसार इसका पंख 83 से 92 मिमी, पूंछ 30 से 32 मिमी, टार्सस 25 से 26 मिमी और कल्मेन लगभग 13 मिमी होता है। नर के सीने पर मखमली काला धब्बा होता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Stuart Baker (1928, p. 376) noted: "The Quail has generally been considered locally migratory but, if so, the migrations must be very local, possibly only movements from the driest areas with no cover to adjacent areas with some bush or other form of jungle in which the birds can seek protection from the sun in the hottest driest months."

Baker (1928, p. 376) described its voice: "In every way this little Quail resembles the Common Quail, but its note is a musical 'whit-whit-whit-whit,' constantly repeated."

हिंदी:
बेकर (1928) के अनुसार यदि यह प्रवास करती है तो यह बहुत स्थानीय हो सकता है, शायद गर्मियों में धूप से बचने के लिए झाड़ियों में जाना। इसकी संगीतमय आवाज 'व्हिट-व्हिट-व्हिट-व्हिट' लगातार सुनाई देती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stuart Baker (1928, p. 376) recorded: "The Rain-Quail breeds, as its name infers, after the break of the rains from June to October, though a few birds lay also in the month of April and early May."

Baker (1928, p. 376) noted that the nesting is like that of the Common Quail, the site sometimes in scrub and sometimes right in open fields under cover of a cactus-hedge or bush. The genus is monogamous; "four are frequently incubated whilst six to eight is the average, though they run up to eleven" (Baker 1928, p. 376).

हिंदी:
बेकर (1928) के अनुसार यह बारिश शुरू होने के बाद जून से अक्टूबर के बीच खेतों या कैक्टस की बाड़ के नीचे जमीन पर घोंसला बनाती है और औसतन छह से आठ अंडे देती है।

GBIF holds published records from these districts.

In eastern Uttar Pradesh, historical museum specimens include individuals collected in Sultanpur (1879 and one undated specimen in the Natural History Museum, London) and Varanasi (1847 specimen in Cambridge). GalliForm records document occurrences in Gorakhpur District and in Gonda District (1922 record, placed at district level). Gonda is a core district of the atlas region. Two GBIF records are labelled only 'Oudh', the whole historical province, and are placed at Faizabad: a specimen of December 1873 in the Natural History Museum, London, and an observation from between October 1857 and August 1860 ("Common during rainy season").

A first record would be a photograph of the bird with place and date, sent through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Stuart Baker (1928, p. 376) recorded the distribution: "Practically throughout Ceylon, where it is rare, India and Burma to the Shan States. Obtained at Simla, 7,000 ft., by Dodsworth."

In Uttar Pradesh, records are scattered across the Gangetic plain from Gonda, Gorakhpur, and Sultanpur to Varanasi.

हिंदी:
बेकर (1928) के अनुसार इसका वितरण भारत, बर्मा और सीलोन में है। उत्तर प्रदेश में इसके रिकॉर्ड गोंडा, गोरखपुर, सुल्तानपुर और वाराणसी से दर्ज हैं।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Johann Friedrich Gmelin in 1789 in Systema Naturae (13th ed., Vol. 1, pt. 2, p. 764), under the basionym Tetrao coromandelicus. The type locality is Coromandel Coast, India.

Synonyms and historical names. Stuart Baker (1928, p. 375) and Blanford and Oates retained the species in the genus Coturnix (Coturnix coromandelica).

Subspecies. Monotypic. Baker (1928, p. 376) recognized no subspecies across its range.

Family and order placement. Order Galliformes, family Phasianidae (partridges and quails), genus Coturnix.

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC) by the IUCN Red List (BirdLife International 2016).

हिंदी:
ग्मेलिन ने 1789 में कोरोमंडल तट से इसका वर्णन किया था। यह फैसिआनिडी (बटेर) कुल का सदस्य है और संकटमुक्त (LC) है।
