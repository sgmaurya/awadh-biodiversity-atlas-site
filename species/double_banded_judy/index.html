डबल-बैंडेड जूडी तितली / Double-banded Judy

Abisara bifasciata Moore, 1877 — Lycaenidae

डबल-बैंडेड जूडी तितली — घने जंगलों, बाँस के झुरमुटों और नम नालों के किनारे पाई जाने वाली एक अनोखी कत्थई तितली है। इसके अगले पंखों पर दो समानांतर हल्की पट्टियाँ होती हैं और पिछले पंख पर काले बिंदु होते हैं। यह पत्ती पर बैठकर अपने पंखों को आधा खोलती है और गोल-गोल घूमकर अजीब तरह का नाच दिखाती है। यह तेज धूप से बचती है और नम छाँव में ही खुश रहती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall to medium butterfly; wingspan 35–45 mm (Kehimkar 2008)
Upperside GroundDeep purple-brown or rich maroon in males; paler ochreous-brown in females (Wynter-Blyth 1957)
Forewing MarkingsTwo distinct, parallel pale lilac-white transverse discal bands (Evans 1932)
Hindwing ShapeAngled sharply at vein 4, bearing two small black subapical spots (Bingham 1905)
Underside PatternPale dull maroon-brown repeating the double pale bands and apical spots (Kehimkar 2008)
Unique BehaviourAlights on leaves with wings half-open and rapidly jerks or pivots in circles (Kunte 2000)
Flight StyleShort, jerky, skipping flight close to dark forest floor undergrowth (Wynter-Blyth 1957)

Field tip: Look along shaded bamboo brakes, overgrown ditches, and secluded ravine floors where light is dim. When this maroon-brown butterfly lands on a broad leaf, it half-opens its wings and spins around in a comical, jerky circular dance before settling.

हिंदी: नम और छायादार स्थानों में पत्ती पर बैठकर गोल-गोल घूमने की इसकी आदत बहुत अनोखी है। कत्थई पंखों पर दो सफेद-बैंगनी पट्टियाँ इसे तुरंत पहचान दिलाती हैं।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics recorded from adult specimens (Kehimkar 2008; Evans 1932):

MeasurementRangeSource
Wingspan35–45 mmKehimkar (2008)

Adult description: The Double-banded Judy exhibits marked sexual dimorphism and subtle seasonal variation between wet and dry broods (Bingham 1905). In males, the upperside is rich dark plum-purple or maroon-brown (Wynter-Blyth 1957). The forewing is crossed by two prominent, pale lilac-grey or buffish-white transverse fasciae: an inner discal band and an outer postdiscal band that run almost parallel across the wing (Evans 1932). In females, the ground colour is softer ochreous-brown or light fawn, and the two pale transverse bands are noticeably broader and brighter (Kehimkar 2008).

The hindwing upperside is distinctly angled or tailed at the termination of vein 4 (Bingham 1905). Near the apex of the hindwing lie two conspicuous jet-black spots encircled by pale rings, followed by faint submarginal lunules along the margin (Bingham 1905).

The underside mirrors the upperside markings in a softer, paler vinaceous-brown ground tone (Kehimkar 2008). Both transverse bands are cleanly defined, and the apical black ocelli on the hindwing are sharply bordered with metallic silvery-lilac scales (Bingham 1905). The antennae are slender and ringed with white; the palpi are small and pointed.

Larval Stage इल्ली अवस्था

The mature caterpillar is onisciform, flattened, light green, and cloaked in fine short downy pubescence (Kunte 2000). A dark green mid-dorsal vessel is flanked by obscure yellowish oblique dashes. The pupa is light green or brownish, flattened, and attached horizontally to the surface of a dried leaf with silk (Kehimkar 2008).

हिंदी: इसका शरीर मजबूत और पंखों का फैलाव 35 से 45 मिमी होता है। नर का रंग गहरा जामुनी-कत्थई और मादा का रंग हल्का बादामी होता है। दोनों के पंखों पर दो स्पष्ट पट्टियाँ होती हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Double-banded Judy is a creature of deep shade, inhabiting the understorey of moist deciduous tracts, riverine thickets, and overgrown bamboo clumps (Kehimkar 2008). It shuns bright sunlight and open windswept landscapes, confining its activity to the dense shrub layer within one metre of the forest floor (Wynter-Blyth 1957). Flight is brief, rapid, and erratic, rarely travelling more than a few yards at a time before ducking beneath another broad leaf (Kunte 2000).

Its most famous behavioural trait is its perching ritual: upon alighting on the upper surface of a leaf, it holds its wings partly spread at an angle of roughly forty-five degrees and immediately executes a series of abrupt, jerky rotations, pivoting in full circles or shifting 180 degrees back and forth (Kehimkar 2008). This jerky motion, combined with the false eyespots on the angled hindwing tails, is thought to confuse predatory spiders and birds regarding the butterfly's true head position (Kunte 2000). Adults occasionally mud-puddle singly on wet mud along secluded forest streams or sip honeydew on understorey shrubs (Kehimkar 2008).

हिंदी: यह तितली धूप से नफरत करती है और जमीन के पास झाड़ियों में ही उड़ती है। पत्ती पर बैठते ही यह 180 से 360 डिग्री गोल-गोल घूमती है, जिससे शिकारी इसकी आँख और पूँछ में धोखा खा जाते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

The female lays flattened, turban-shaped eggs singly on the underside of mature host leaves (Kunte 2000). The egg hatches in a few days. The slow-moving, slug-like caterpillar feeds on the lower leaf cuticle, leaving a transparent window of upper epidermis when young, and eating entire leaf margins as it matures (Kunte 2000). Larval development spans a few weeks, while pupation lasts about a week to ten days before the adult ecloses. Overlapping generations occur across the year.

हिंदी: सर्दी और पतझड़ के महीनों में यह तितली ज्यादा नजर आती है। मादा पत्तियों के नीचे एक-एक अंडा देती है। इल्ली धीरे-धीरे पत्तियां खाती है और कुछ हफ्तों में वयस्क तितली बनती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Documented larval host plants from published Indian lepidopteran accounts (Kunte 2000; Kehimkar 2008):

  • Ardisia solanacea (Wild Badi Kandi, family Primulaceae) — primary larval food plant in shaded riverine and nullah habitats (Kunte 2000).
  • Embelia tsjeriam-cottam (family Primulaceae) — widespread woody shrub of secondary scrub and forest margins.
  • Maesa indica (family Primulaceae) — understorey shrub documented as host in moist ravine tracts (Kehimkar 2008).

Adults feed sparingly on nectar from tiny woodland flowers, sap oozing from bruised vines, and mineral-laden moisture along wet forest nullahs (Kehimkar 2008).

हिंदी: इसके कैटरपिलर बड़िकांडी और बायबिडंग जैसे जंगली औषधीय पौधों की पत्तियां खाते हैं। वयस्क तितलियाँ बहुत कम फूलों पर जाती हैं और ज्यादातर नम मिट्टी से रस लेती हैं।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Common insectivorous predators of the Gangetic plain likely to take riodinid butterflies:

  • Spiders: Salticid jumping spiders and foliage-dwelling crab spiders (Thomisidae) are major ambush predators on understorey leaves.
  • Insects: Praying mantises and reduviid assassin bugs capture caterpillars and resting adults in dark thickets.
  • Birds: Ground-foraging and understorey birds, such as jungle babblers, tailorbirds, and white-browed fantails, hunt sluggish adults in leaf litter.
  • Parasitoids: Small braconid wasps and eulophid micro-wasps attack the exposed caterpillars.
हिंदी: झाड़ियों में रहने वाली कूदने वाली मकड़ियाँ, बगुले और दर्जी चिड़ियाँ इस तितली का शिकार करती हैं। इसकी इल्लियों पर छोटे परजीवी कीड़े अंडे दे देते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Agricultural status: The Double-banded Judy is entirely neutral to agriculture (Kunte 2000). None of its known larval host plants are cultivated crops or economically important orchard trees. It does not defoliate timber plantations or fruit trees.

Biodiversity indicator: Because of its strict reliance on humid microclimates, dense shade, and native understorey shrubs like Ardisia, the Double-banded Judy serves as a sensitive indicator of unfragmented riverine woodlots, traditional sacred groves, and dense bamboo sanctuaries that have escaped grazing pressure and undergrowth clearance (Kehimkar 2008).

Traditional: No traditional uses, folklore, or economic trades are associated with this species.

Today: Studied by entomologists exploring false-head wing patterns and predator-deflection pivoting behaviors.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Quantifying whether the rotational spinning display significantly reduces capture success by jumping spiders.

हिंदी: यह फसलों के लिए बिल्कुल सुरक्षित है और खेती को कोई नुकसान नहीं पहुँचाती। जहाँ यह तितली मिलती है, वह इस बात का सबूत है कि वहाँ की नम झाड़ियाँ और पुराने पेड़ सुरक्षित हैं।

The Double-banded Judy is expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh in shaded riverine nullahs, undisturbed bamboo groves, and dense overgrown orchards where understorey moisture persists through the dry season. Look on broad leaves within two feet of the ground in deep shade from October through March. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. A photograph with date and location, submitted through the About page, would confirm the first local documentation for the district.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South Asia and parts of Southeast Asia, including India, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, and the Andaman Islands (Bingham 1905).

In India: Found along the Western Ghats, central Indian hill ranges, the lower Himalayas from Himachal Pradesh to Arunachal Pradesh, and across the northern alluvial plains in relict forested riverine tracts (Evans 1932; Kehimkar 2008).

In Uttar Pradesh: Documented from shaded forested ravines, terai reserves, and moist relict woodlots across central and eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a resident in established village groves, protected orchard understoreys, and bamboo brakes bordering wetlands.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Frederic Moore in 1877 as Abisara bifasciata in Proceedings of the Zoological Society of London, Volume 1877, page 585, plate 58, figure 2. Type locality: South Andamans (Port Blair), India.

Subspecies. Several subspecies have been recognized across its range. The mainland northern Indian and Gangetic plain population is traditionally treated as subspecies suffusa Moore, 1882 (Evans 1932).

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Lycaenidae (placed in family Riodinidae by many authors, or treated as subfamily Riodininae). The genus Abisara C. & R. Felder, 1860, includes the Old World judies or metalmarks.

Phylogenetic notes. While modern morphological and molecular classifications frequently elevate Riodinidae to a distinct family, the GBIF taxonomic backbone currently groups Abisara within Lycaenidae. Abisara species are renowned for their specialized larval morphology and predator-deflecting adult locomotion (Bingham 1905).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. The species is secure and widespread across its broad South Asian distribution.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Abisara bifasciata है। इसे कई वैज्ञानिक Riodinidae परिवार में रखते हैं, जबकि कुछ इसे Lycaenidae में शामिल करते हैं। यह खतरे में नहीं है।
