कामलता / Cypress Vine

Ipomoea quamoclit L. — Convolvulaceae

कामलता (स्टार ग्लोरी, साइप्रेस वाइन) — अमेरिका की एक सुंदर बेल जिसे अब भारत के बाग़ों में सजावट के लिए उगाया जाता है। पत्तियाँ बहुत ही पतली और सूक्ष्म, साइप्रेस के पेड़ की पत्तियों जैसी (जिससे इसका नाम है)। फूल तारे जैसे, गहरे लाल, नारंगी या कभी सफेद। गर्मी में तेजी से बढ़ता है। आजकल खुद बढ़ता हुआ भी दिखता है।

Field tip: A graceful twining climber with feathery foliage that resembles cypress leaves and brilliant red (or orange) star-shaped flowers. Often cultivated on trellises and fences; increasingly escapes from gardens to hedgerows and field margins.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for plants in northern India gardens):

Stems: Slender, herbaceous, twining, smooth or slightly hairy, green, becoming woody at the base in older plants.

Leaves: Alternate, sessile or short-petioled, pinnately or bipinnately dissected into very fine linear segments, 3–8 cm long. The segments are 1–3 mm wide and thread-like, giving the entire plant a feathery, delicate appearance. This fine leaf structure is the most distinctive feature and gives the plant its common names.

Flowers: Solitary or in pairs in leaf axils, on pedicels 1–2 cm long. The corolla is funnel- or salver-shaped, 2–3 cm across, bright scarlet-red or orange (sometimes yellow or white). When fully opened, the flower appears star-shaped. The throat is often darker. Stamens and style project beyond the corolla. The flowers open in the morning and close by afternoon; they are visited by butterflies and bees.

Fruit: A globular, membranous capsule, 0.8–1 cm across, containing 4 round, small seeds.

हिंदी: पत्तियाँ सीधी और बहुत पतली, साइप्रेस जैसी। फूल तारे जैसे।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ipomoea quamoclit is a tropical American climber that is widely cultivated in India as an ornamental for its stunning flowers and delicate foliage. The plant grows rapidly during the warm season (May–November) and requires a support structure or trellis to climb. In gardens, it is often grown on fences, trellises, or arches. The plant does not compete seriously with crops but occasionally escapes from gardens to establish in hedgerows and waste ground.

The flowers are visited by butterflies, hummingbirds (in its native range), and small bees. The plant is self-compatible and produces viable seed readily. In India, the plant often self-seeds in the vicinity of gardens.

हिंदी: गर्मी में तेजी से बढ़ता है। बाग़ों में सजावट के लिए लगाया जाता है। बहुत सुंदर फूल।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Month / seasonEvent
May–JuneSowing of seeds; rapid germination and seedling establishment

August–OctoberProfuse flowering; continuous bloom if water is adequate

Seeds require heat to germinate and will not germinate below about 15°C. In eastern UP, the plant is an obligate warm-season annual and does not survive frost.

हिंदी: मई-जून में बीज, जुलाई-अगस्त में बेलें, सितंबर-अक्टूबर में फूल।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Garden companions: Often grown with other ornamental climbers such as Velvet Bean and Blue Morning Glory.
  • On trellises and arbours: Commonly trained on garden structures alongside Pointed Gourd and Ridge Gourd.
  • Pollinator support: The flowers attract butterflies and bees; a popular garden plant for supporting pollinators.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • No specific serious pests; occasionally attacked by spider mites in very dry conditions.
  • Powdery mildew may occur in humid conditions but is usually not serious.
  • Leaf spots and fungal infections are rare.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Ornamental use:

Ipomoea quamoclit is cultivated throughout India as an ornamental climber for its delicate foliage and brilliant flowers. The plant is popular for trellises, fences, arches, and decorative plantings in homestead gardens and parks. The flowers are short-lived but are produced continuously through the warm season, making the plant attractive from mid-summer through autumn.

Cultural significance:

The plant is sometimes grown for religious or ceremonial purposes in South Asian gardens, though it has no major recorded cultural role (unlike some other Ipomoea species).

No recorded traditional or medicinal use in India. The ornamental value is its main use.

Garden escape:

In some regions, the plant has escaped from cultivation and is naturalized in hedgerows and disturbed areas. It is not aggressive and does not displace native plants, but it can establish in fence rows and waste ground.

Today. The plant is an ornamental garden favourite and is increasingly available in seed catalogues and nurseries across India.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The potential for Ipomoea quamoclit to be used in butterfly gardens and pollinator conservation initiatives could be explored, as it is a proven butterfly-attracting plant.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical America (Mexico and Central America); widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

In India: Cultivated throughout India, more common in the south and east; naturalized in some regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in homestead gardens and parks; occasionally naturalized in hedgerows.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in gardens; GBIF records from the wider region.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum, p. 106. The epithet quamoclit is derived from the Aztec (Nahuatl) name for the plant.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Convolvulaceae, order Solanales. The genus Ipomoea holds about 600–700 species, mostly of tropical and subtropical origin. It includes some of the world's most widely cultivated ornamental climbers and the sweet potato.

Phylogenetic notes. Ipomoea quamoclit is closely related to other red-flowered Ipomoea species, particularly I. coccinea (Red Morning Glory), with which it sometimes hybridizes in cultivation.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Cultivated globally; not assessed.
