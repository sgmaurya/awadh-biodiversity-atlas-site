केला / Banana

Musa × paradisiaca L. — Musaceae

केला (केला का पेड़ / प्लैंटेन) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खेतों, बाड़ियों और बागों में बड़े पैमाने पर उगाई जाने वाली एक अत्यंत महत्वपूर्ण, विशालकाय जलीय और बहुवर्षीय शाकीय फसल (giant perennial herb) है। यह कोई सच्चा पेड़ नहीं है; इसका तना वास्तव में एक आभासी तना (pseudostem) है जो आपस में लिपटे हुए पत्तों के आवरणों (tightly packed leaf sheaths) से बना होता है। यह मूल रूप से दक्षिण-पूर्व एशिया का मूल निवासी है, और Musa paradisiaca इसके दो जंगली पूर्वजों (Musa acuminata और Musa balbisiana) के मेल से बना एक त्रिगुणित संकर (triploid hybrid) है। इसकी सबसे बड़ी पहचान इसके अत्यंत विशाल, चौड़े पत्ते (massive oblong leaves), इसके तने के शिखर से निकलने वाली लटकती हुई विशाल पुष्प-मंजरी (hanging purple inflorescence heart), और बीजरहित मीठे फलों के गुच्छे हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की संस्कृति और दैनिक जीवन में केले के पौधे का बहुत बड़ा महत्व है; इसके पत्तों का उपयोग धार्मिक भोज में पत्तल (plates) के रूप में, इसके फूलों (kela phool) का उपयोग सब्जी बनाने के लिए, और इसके तने के अंदरूनी भाग का उपयोग चिकित्सा में किया जाता है। भारत दुनिया का सबसे बड़ा केला उत्पादक देश है, और बस्ती जिले में मुख्य रूप से 'जी-9 कैवेंडिश' (G9 Cavendish variety) किस्म उगाई जाती है। पनामा रोग (Panama Disease) इसका सबसे घातक कवक रोग है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Plant habitGiant, erect, tree-like perennial herb up to 2–5 meters high, growing from an underground rhizome (corm)
PseudostemA thick, fleshy, green "trunk" made of overlapping leaf bases, containing no wood or bark
LeavesMassive, oblong, green (1.5–2.5 m long, 50–80 cm wide), spreading outwards, tearing easily in strong winds
InflorescenceA large, drooping spike with thick purple bracts enclosing clusters of small, tubular yellow flowers
FruitSeedless, elongated, curved green berries (10–20 cm) turning yellow when ripe, arranged in dense hands (bunch)

Field tip: Look in agricultural plots or village gardens. Search for tall, trunkless green plants with massive paddle-like leaves. Look at the center; a thick stem bends downward, bearing a heavy bunch of green bananas and ending in a large, heart-shaped purple flower bud at the tip.

Pseudostem: Made of tightly packed, concentric leaf sheaths containing air chambers that provide mechanical strength to support the heavy fruit bunch.

Foliage: Massive leaves, smooth, with a thick central midrib. The wind splits the leaf blades along their parallel veins, reducing wind resistance during summer storms.

Flowers: The spike grows up from the underground corm through the center of the pseudostem. Female flowers open first at the base (forming the fruits), followed by male flowers under purple scales at the tip.

Cultivation Calendar कृषि कैलेंडर

  • Propagation: Grown using young side shoots (sword suckers or puttee) weighing 1.5–2.0 kg, dug from the parent corm and planted in pits during June–July or February–March. Tissue culture plants are also widely used.
  • Crop Cycle: The bunch emerges 9–10 months after planting; fruits are harvested 3 months later when the ridges on the banana skin become rounded.
  • Ratooning: After harvest, the pseudostem is cut to the ground. A single selected sucker is allowed to grow to form the next crop (ratoon crop).

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Insect Pollinators: The sweet nectar inside the purple flower heart is visited by bats at night, and by sunbirds, honeybees, and wasps during the day.
  • Wildlife refuge: Small tree frogs and green lynx spiders often hide inside the leaf sheath joints.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Panama Disease (Fusarium Wilt): A deadly soil-borne fungal disease (Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense) that enters the roots, blocks water transport inside the pseudostem, causing leaves to yellow and collapse.
  • Banana Bunchy Top Virus: Transmitted by aphids, causing young leaves to become bunched, small, erect, and preventing fruit formation.
  • Pseudostem Weevil: The larvae bore into the pseudostem, creating tunnels that weaken the plant, causing it to break under wind pressure.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use पारंपरिक और नृवंशवनस्पति उपयोग

  1. Banana Leaf Plates (Pattal): The massive, clean leaves are harvested and used as natural, biodegradable plates to serve traditional meals during religious festivals, housewarmings, and weddings.
  2. Kela Phool Sabzi: The tender inner heart of the purple banana flower is chopped, soaked in saltwater to remove astringency, and cooked with potato and spices as a dry vegetable curry.
  3. Pseudostem Pith Juice: The tender white inner core of the cut pseudostem is crushed and the juice is drunk to dissolve kidney stones and manage urinary tract infections in traditional medicine.
  4. Ripe Fruit and Peel Compost: Ripe banana fruits are consumed as a high-potassium energy fruit, and the peels are dried and added to rose plant soil as a natural potassium booster.

A highly important cash crop grown in large plantations across the Basti district, especially in the Harraiya and Vikramjot blocks near the river plains. Farmers state that the "G-9" variety is highly valued for its large bunch weights and uniform yellow ripening. Traditional varieties like "Champa" and "Kanthali" are still maintained in backyards for local domestic consumption.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical Southeast Asia and Northern Australia; cultivated globally across all humid tropical and subtropical countries.

In India: Cultivated universally; India is the world's largest producer of bananas, with major hubs in Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Andhra Pradesh.

In Uttar Pradesh: Heavy cultivation in eastern river basins and canal-irrigated districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Widespread cash crop in riverine plains.

Field rule: Giant perennial herb with green pseudostem trunk, massive paddle-like leaves, and a large hanging purple inflorescence producing seedless curved yellow fruits, is the Banana.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus described the cultivated banana as Musa × paradisiaca in 1753 in his Species Plantarum (Vol. 2, p. 1043). The specific name paradisiaca is Latin for of paradise, referencing the early Christian belief that the banana was the "forbidden fruit" in the Garden of Eden. The multiplication sign (×) indicates its hybrid origin between Musa acuminata and Musa balbisiana.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised in the main index.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Zingiberales, family Musaceae (banana family), which contains only three genera: Musa, Ensete, and Musella.

Phylogenetic notes. Musa × paradisiaca represents a triploid hybrid (AAA, AAB, or ABB genome groups), where hybridization has led to sterile, seedless fruits that must be propagated vegetatively through suckers.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) by the IUCN Red List. It has a massive, secure global agricultural population.
