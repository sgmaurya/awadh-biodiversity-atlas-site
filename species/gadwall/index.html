गैडवॉल बत्तख / Gadwall

Mareca strepera (Linnaeus, 1758) — Anatidae

गैडवॉल बत्तख — ठंड के मौसम में यूरोप और साइबेरिया से आने वाली एक छोटी, भूरी-और-काली बत्तख, जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के तालाबों और नदियों में अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक पाई जाती है। नर का शरीर भूरा और काला मिश्रित होता है, मादा समान भूरी होती है। ये बत्तख उथले पानी में तालाब के पौधों को खाने के लिए प्रसिद्ध हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

SpeculumWhite in both sexes (diagnostic in flight)

Field tip: A dabbling duck with a male showing grey body and black breast — is a Gadwall. White speculum (wing patch) visible in flight is diagnostic. Females are trickier but show the white wing patch in flight. Often seen with other dabbling ducks.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 45–56 cm long; wingspan 84–100 cm. Weight 550–900 g (males slightly heavier).

Plumage (male): Grey body with fine vermiculation. Black breast and rump. White belly. Chestnut on head and neck (not glossy green). Wing coverts grey; speculum (inner secondary feathers) white and black. White upper tail coverts visible when folded.

Plumage (female): Mottled greyish-brown overall. Slightly paler on underparts. Head shows subtle markings. Wing pattern similar to male with white speculum visible in flight.

Bill: Orange-yellow with black tip; slender and pointed, suited for picking vegetation.

Legs and feet: Yellow-orange; placed forward on body, typical of dabbling ducks.

Eyes: Dark; small.

Sexes: Strongly dimorphic in breeding plumage; females much duller.

Vocalisations. Less vocal than other ducks:

  1. Drake call: A whistled burp or prrip, given during courtship.
  2. Duck (female) call: Soft quacking, less loud than Mallard.
हिंदी: बहुत शांत रहता है। कम आवाज़ करता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Omnivorous dabbler: Gadwalls feed on:

  • Aquatic plant seeds and shoots (particularly important in winter)
  • Aquatic vegetation and algae
  • Small aquatic invertebrates (insects, snails)
  • Grain and seeds from agricultural fields

Feeding behaviour: Primarily dabblers; upend in shallow water (rarely dive). Feed at water surface; wade in mud. Often "steal" food items dropped by diving ducks, leading to symbiotic relationship with other species.

Social behaviour: Form flocks in winter; less gregarious than some ducks but often in mixed-species flocks. Males may outnumber females in some wintering areas.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosts on open water; diurnal rest in reed beds or shallow ponds.

Predators: Eagles, harriers, and wild dogs may take adults. Chicks vulnerable to corvids and aquatic predators.

हिंदी: शांत, सावधान बत्तख। मिश्रित झुंड में रहती है। तालाब के पौधे खाती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives October–November from breeding grounds; departs March–April. Does not breed in India.

Breeding range: Northern Eurasia from Iceland and Britain eastward to Kamchatka; also northwestern North America. Breeds March–July.

Migration: Breeds in temperate and subarctic wetlands; southward migration begins August–September. First arrivals reach Indian subcontinent October–November; peak numbers January–February; departure begins March.

Wintering: Distributed across shallow wetlands and river systems of the Indo-Gangetic plain.

Moult: Complete moult before autumn migration; eclipse plumage acquired and replaced during stay at wintering grounds.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Golden Eagle, Marsh Harrier, Peregrine Falcon, Pike
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, wild dog, fox, wild boar (for chicks)
  • At nest (breeding grounds): Corvids, weasels, martens
हिंदी: बड़ी शिकारी चिड़ियाँ और जानवर इसका शिकार करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Gleaning behaviour: Small flocks may feed in rice and wheat stubble fields; generally cause negligible crop damage due to their small size and preference for aquatic vegetation.

Scientific interest: Gadwalls are used as "kleptoparasites" by researchers studying feeding ecology — they steal food items dropped or dislodged by diving ducks, providing insight into interspecific competition.

Hunting history: Hunted for food in Europe and Asia; now protected in India under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Gadwalls are expected at the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems in eastern UP from October through April. Mishra et al. (2019) recorded this species at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary during October 2017–March 2018. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025) recorded it at Parwati Arga from March 2024–February 2025, and Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022) recorded it in Ayodhya district wetlands. Published range and habitat information place this species in shallow wetlands and river margins during winter months.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds across northern Europe and Asia; winters across the Mediterranean, Middle East, South Asia, and East Africa.

In India: Regular winter visitor across northern and central regions, particularly along major river systems.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread winter visitor to lakes and river systems.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and shallow wetlands. Recorded at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary and Parwati Arga. Present October–April, with peak numbers December–February.

Field rule: A medium-sized dabbling duck with grey-and-black coloration (males) and white speculum visible in flight — is a Gadwall. In flight, the white wing patch is diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Mareca strepera was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th Edition, volume 1, page 126, as Anas strepera. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; all populations belong to a single species with cosmopolitan distribution.

Family and order placement. Order Anseriformes; family Anatidae; genus Mareca contains medium-sized dabbling ducks of northern origin.

Phylogenetic notes. Mareca strepera is closely related to the Eurasian Wigeon (M. penelope) and Green-winged Teal (Anas crecca); molecular studies place it within a clade of temperate-breeding dabbling ducks.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Global population is large and stable. The species occurs widely across the Northern Hemisphere and has adapted to human-modified wetlands. Protection and wetland conservation maintain populations.
