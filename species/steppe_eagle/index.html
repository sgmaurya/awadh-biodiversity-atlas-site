स्टेपी ईगल / Steppe Eagle

Aquila nipalensis Hodgson, 1833 — Accipitridae

स्टेपी ईगल — एक बड़ा भूरा और सुनहरा शिकारी पक्षी जो मंगोलिया, साइबेरिया और तिब्बत से अक्टूबर-नवंबर में आता है और मार्च तक रहता है। यह खुली घास और खेतों में धीमी गति से उड़ते हुए शिकार खोजता है। यह प्रजाति अब खतरे में है, क्योंकि संख्या तेज़ी से घट रही है।

Field tip: A very large eagle with dark brown plumage and paler nape and crown, soaring over open grasslands and cultivated fields — is a Steppe Eagle. Smaller and less brightly coloured than Golden Eagle. Hunts medium-sized prey (ground squirrels, hyraxes, small birds) by swooping from soaring height.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 66–81 cm long; wingspan 180–220 cm. Weight 1600–3200 g (females heavier than males).

Plumage: Highly variable. Generally dark brown overall. Crown, nape, and ear-coverts yellow-buff or golden (variable). Throat and upper breast golden-buff contrasting with darker lower breast and back. Flight feathers dark brown with barring. Tail dark brown, slightly rounded or square-tipped. Underparts of wings show variable barring.

Variation: Individual birds vary significantly in overall darkness and extent of golden coloration. Some birds appear uniformly dark brown; others show prominent golden nape and crown. Age (adults darker than immatures) and sex affect appearance.

Head: Relatively small and rounded. Eyes dark brown. Cere (base of bill) yellow or orange.

Bill: Large (48–58 mm), hooked, dark grey or blackish. Powerful and suited for tearing prey.

Legs and feet: Yellow or orange (variable); feathered to the toes (diagnostic of Aquila eagles). Large, strong claws for grasping prey.

Eyes: Dark brown iris; intense and piercing gaze.

Sexes: Females larger than males; otherwise plumage similar. Females may show more pronounced golden coloration.

Vocalisations. Vocalisations poorly studied in wintering birds:

  1. Contact call: A series of whistles and yaps, rarely heard in winter.
  2. Flight call: Short, sharp calls during territorial display (rare in winter).
हिंदी: बहुत कम आवाज़ करता है। ज्यादातर चुप रहता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Specialized predator: Steppe Eagles hunt a variety of medium-sized prey:

  • Ground squirrels and marmots (primary prey on breeding grounds)
  • Hyraxes and rock rabbits
  • Small birds (partridges, quail, game birds)
  • Small snakes and reptiles
  • Occasionally small mammals (voles, pikas)

Hunting behaviour: Primarily hunt from soaring height, scanning ground for movement below. Also hunt from perches. Swoop rapidly when prey detected. Prefer to hunt in early morning and late afternoon.

Social behaviour: Solitary in winter. Pairs during breeding season (not occurring in region). Highly territorial on breeding grounds.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosts in tall trees near hunting areas. Return to same roosting sites nightly.

Flight: Superb soarers; use thermal currents extensively. Flight is deliberate with slow wing beats, alternating with gliding. Silhouette distinctive: long broad wings, small head, medium tail.

Nomadic tendency: Wander widely within wintering range in search of prey concentrations.

Predators: Young may be taken by larger eagles (Golden Eagle, White-tailed Eagle). Humans historically hunted for sport.

हिंदी: अकेला शिकारी। घास और खेतों में शिकार खोजता है। धीमी गति से उड़ता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives October–November from breeding grounds; departs by March–April. Does not breed in India.

Breeding range: Kazakhstan, southern Siberia, Mongolia, Tibet, Himalayas (above 2000 m). Breeds March–August, with egg-laying March–April and fledging June–August.

Migration: Breeds on open grasslands and montane plateau; southward migration begins July–August. Travels through Central Asia and Middle East to winter in South Asia, Africa, and Middle East. Arrives in Indian subcontinent October–November; most depart by April.

Wintering: Distributed over open grasslands and cultivated areas; widely ranging. Solitary or in small loose aggregations.

Moult: Gradual moult during wintering; acquire fresh plumage before return to breeding grounds.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Golden Eagle, White-tailed Eagle (of young birds)
  • Mammalian predators: Jackals, wild dogs (of eggs and young on breeding grounds)
  • Human persecution: Poisoning and shooting on breeding grounds; habitat disturbance
हिंदी: बड़ी चिड़ियाँ इसका शिकार कर सकती हैं। आदमी द्वारा सताव।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Ecosystem service: Steppe Eagles provide pest control by consuming ground squirrels and rodents that damage crops and pastures. However, impact is minor in agricultural regions due to rarity.

Historical persecution: Historically persecuted in Europe and Asia due to perceived competition with humans for game birds (partridges, quail); this persecution continues in parts of range and is a major threat.

Cultural significance: Featured in Central Asian and Siberian cultures as a symbol of power and freedom. Nomadic peoples historically hunted with eagle falconry using this species.

Conservation crisis: The species is Endangered globally due to rapid population declines, estimated by BirdLife International's Red List assessment at about half over three generations, caused by habitat loss on breeding grounds, poisoning, persecution, electrocution on power lines, and lead ammunition.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Steppe Eagles are expected in the Basti–Ayodhya region in open grasslands and cultivated fields from October through March, based on published range and wintering habitat preferences. The nearest published record is from Ayodhya district itself, where Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022) listed it as a winter visitor to the unprotected wetlands they surveyed; the Bakhira and Parvati Arga surveys did not list it. The species prefers open grasslands and cultivated areas, so it should be looked for soaring over agricultural lands and grassland areas during winter months. A first record would be a photograph clearly showing the large brown plumage with golden nape and crown, with place, date, and GPS coordinates.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Kazakhstan, southern Siberia, Mongolia, Tibet, and Himalayas; winters across Asia (India, China, Southeast Asia), Africa, and Middle East.

In India: Regular but uncommon winter visitor to open grasslands and cultivated areas across northern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to open areas and grasslands, with records from eastern and central districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in winter (October–March) in open grasslands and cultivated areas based on published range, though not yet recorded in local surveys. Should be looked for soaring over agricultural lands and grassland habitat.

Field rule: A very large brown eagle with golden or buff crown and nape, soaring slowly over open grasslands with slow wing beats and broad wings — is a Steppe Eagle. Golden Eagle is larger and more uniformly brown.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Aquila nipalensis was described by Brian Houghton Hodgson in 1833 in Asiatick Researches 23: 403, from the Himalayas (Nepal). Type locality: Nepal.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; all populations belong to a single species.

Family and order placement. Order Cathartiformes; family Accipitridae (hawks, eagles, vultures, harriers, kites). The genus Aquila contains 12 species of "true eagles" (Booted Eagles).

Phylogenetic notes. Aquila nipalensis is closely related to the Tawny Eagle (A. rapax) and Golden Eagle (A. chrysaetos); molecular phylogenetics groups it with other large Aquila eagles.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Endangered (EN) due to rapid population declines. Estimated 50% decline over three generations caused by:

  • Habitat loss and degradation on breeding grounds (grassland conversion to agriculture)
  • Persecution (poisoning, shooting) on breeding and wintering grounds
  • Electrocution on uninsulated power lines
  • Lead ammunition in carrion (secondary poisoning)
  • Climate change affecting breeding productivity

Global population estimated at 20,000–50,000, down from historical levels. Recovery requires urgent protection on breeding grounds and across migration routes.

Conservation Status संरक्षण स्थिति

IUCN Status: Endangered (EN)

Wildlife Protection Act, 1972: Listed under Schedule I (Protected) of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. Hunting or harming this species is illegal in India.

Threats: Loss of breeding habitat; persecution; secondary poisoning from lead ammunition; electrocution on power lines; climate change.

Conservation actions needed:

  • Protection of breeding habitat in Central Asia and Himalayas
  • Control of persecution and poisoning
  • Conversion of power lines to insulated types in key areas
  • Promotion of non-lead ammunition
  • International cooperation for wintering ground protection
