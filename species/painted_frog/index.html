रंगीन मेंढक / Common Indian Kaloula

Kaloula taprobanica (Gunther, 1859) — Microhylidae

रंगीन मेंढक — गाँव के पानी के गड्ढों, धान के खेतों और नालियों में बरसात में दिखने वाला एक बहुत छोटा, गोल, रंगीन और अजीब दिखने वाला मेंढक। इसका सिर शरीर से छोटा, मुँह गोल-कुंद, और आँखें बहुत छोटी होती हैं — जो इसे अंधेरे में मिट्टी खोदने के लिए सहायक है। इसकी पीठ लाल-भूरी या नारंगी होती है जिस पर लाल और काली धारियाँ या धब्बे होते हैं, इसीलिए इसे 'रंगीन' कहते हैं। यह मेंढक दिन में गहराई में मिट्टी के बिलों में बैठा रहता है और रात को कीड़े खाता है। बरसात की रात में हज़ारों मेंढक गड्ढों में इकट्ठा होते हैं और नर की 'धप्-धप्-धप्' आवाज़ रात भर गूँजती है।

Field tip: A tiny, rotund, vividly colored (red-brown with dark bands) frog in rain pools in monsoon is a painted kaloula. Its call is a rapid clicking sound. It spends the day buried in soil and comes out at night.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: अत्यंत छोटा, गोल, लाल-भूरा, काली बैंडें। बहुत कम दिखता है, दिन में मिट्टी में, रात में बाहर। बरसात के गड्ढे में तेज़ धप्-धप् आवाज़।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (northern Indian populations):

Body: Globular and rotund, almost ball-like. The head is small and pointed, barely distinct from the body. The limbs are short and weak compared to other frogs, reflecting its burrowing lifestyle.

Coloration: The dorsal surface is typically reddish-brown or golden-brown, with bold dark red and black transverse bands or blotches. The belly is lighter, often pale yellow or cream. Sexual dimorphism in color is minimal.

Head and mouth: The snout is pointed, adapted for pushing through soil. The mouth is small and round. The eyes are very small and positioned dorsolaterally.

Skin texture: Smooth to granular, moist but not slimy like many frogs.

Vocal sac: Males possess a single vocal sac, prominently displayed when calling.

हिंदी: शरीर गोल, सिर छोटा-कुंद, पीठ लाल-भूरी गहरे बैंडों वाली। आँखें चमचमाती नहीं, बहुत छोटी। मुँह गोल, मिट्टी भेदने के लिए बना।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Fossorial and cryptic. The painted kaloula spends the dry months and most daylight hours buried in loose soil or leaf litter. Its small, pointed head and weak limbs are adaptations for burrowing rather than jumping.

Nocturnal forager. At night, it emerges to hunt small arthropods — ants, termites, beetles, and other invertebrates. It uses its small mouth to snap at minute prey.

Explosive breeder. With the onset of monsoon rains, the entire population moves to the nearest rain pool in a single night. Breeding aggregations are loud and massive. Females lay small egg clusters, and tadpoles hatch in a few days.

Defensive behavior. When threatened, the kaloula inflates its body, making itself appear larger and rounder than it actually is.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Adults:

  • Termites (especially during swarming)
  • Ants
  • Small beetles
  • Other small arthropods and insects

Tadpoles:

  • Algae
  • Plant detritus
  • Diatoms and organic matter on pool bottom

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Snakes: Banded Krait, Checkered Keelback, Indian Rat Snake hunt adult frogs
  • Birds: Indian Pond Heron, Spotted Owlet, House Crow on toadlets
  • Others: Bengal Monitor, Giant Water Bug on tadpoles
  • Dragonflies and aquatic beetles: predatory aquatic insects on tadpoles
  • Fish: may take tadpoles where fish are present

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest control. Painted kaloulas consume termites and ants, which are agricultural pests. Their presence in fields and gardens is beneficial.

Harmless. The species is completely harmless to humans. It does not bite or secrete toxins. The folk belief that frogs are toxic or harmful is false.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: None recorded for the region.

Today. Common in areas with suitable breeding pools. Population declines occur where rain pools are filled or drained, and where pesticides reduce invertebrate prey.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The breeding ecology of painted kaloulas is well described; a local question is habitat: whether the species is more abundant in rice-growing areas where breeding pools are common, compared to irrigated areas with fewer temporary pools.

हिंदी: दीमकें और चींटियों को खाता है — खेती में फ़ायदेमंद है। बिल्कुल हानिरहित। कोई जहर नहीं। गड्ढे भरने, कीटनाशक, और सूखे से घट रहा है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Painted kaloulas are expected in rice-paddy areas and wetland margins of Basti district during the monsoon season (June–October). They should be looked for in temporary rain pools, ditches, and wet grasslands at night, especially during rainfall when the breeding chorus is most active. The rapid "dhup-dhup" call is distinctive and audible across considerable distances. A first local record should include location (pool name or field name, block, village), date, time of night, and photograph or audio recording of the call. Please report any sighting to the atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South Asia — India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Nepal, Pakistan.

In India: Widespread in lowland wet areas, from the Himalayan foothills to the Deccan, wherever monsoon rainfall creates temporary breeding pools.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in rice-growing regions and wetland areas across the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in rice paddies and monsoon pools; distribution poorly documented locally.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many painted kaloulas arrive at a given pool on the first night of heavy rain? — Survey during June–July monsoon.
  2. What is the most common prey species in the diet of local kaloulas? — Examine stomach contents or observe feeding.
  3. How long does it take tadpoles to metamorphose in rain pools that dry versus those that persist? — Compare pool types.
  4. Do painted kaloulas call earlier in the evening or later at night in Basti? — Record calling activity patterns.
  5. Are there more painted kaloulas in rice-paddy areas than in non-agricultural wetlands? — Compare populations by habitat type.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Albert Gunther in 1859 in the Catalogue of the Batrachia Salientia in the Collection of the British Museum. The species name taprobanica refers to Taprobana, an ancient name for Sri Lanka, where the species was first collected.

Subspecies. Kaloula taprobanica taprobanica — monotypic; no subspecies are currently recognized.

Family and order placement. Microhylidae, a family of small, fossorial frogs with reduced mouth size, found across the tropics; order Anura.

Phylogenetic notes. Kaloula is a Southeast Asian genus of small, burrowing frogs. The painted frog represents the Indian distribution of this fossorial lineage.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern due to its wide range and stable population across suitable habitats. Declines occur locally where wetland breeding habitat is destroyed or contaminated with pesticides.
