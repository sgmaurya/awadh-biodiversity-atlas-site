ओरिएंटल टर्टल डव / Oriental Turtle Dove

Streptopelia orientalis (Latham, 1790) — Columbidae

ओरिएंटल टर्टल डव — छोटा कबूतर जो हिमालय और पूर्वी एशिया से सर्दियों में आता है। इसके पंखों पर काली धारियों वाली गर्दन का धब्बा इसे पहचान देता है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के गाँवों और खेतों में अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक देखे जाते हैं। बीज खाते हैं और नरम "कू-कू-कू-क" की आवाज़ करते हैं।

Field tip: A small, delicate dove with soft colouring. The key mark is the black and white striped patch on the side of the neck (characteristic of the genus Streptopelia). The spotted pattern on the wings and soft, melodious cooing call are also distinctive.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 24–26 cm long; wingspan 48–55 cm. Small dove.

Plumage: Head and nape soft grey-buff. Distinctive black and white striped patch on side of neck (present year-round, more prominent on males). Back olive-brown to grey. Wing-coverts buff with black centres creating a spotted pattern. Wings dark brown with buff fringes. Tail graduated, grey with darker edges. Underparts buff-grey.

Sexual dimorphism: Males may show slightly more prominent neck patch; otherwise similar.

Head: Soft rounded; orange-red orbital ring (around eye).

Bill: Short, thin; adapted for seed-eating.

Legs and feet: Red or purple-red; short.

Juveniles: Similar to adults but duller; neck patch less distinct.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Cooing call: A soft, mournful koo-koo-koo-kook or kook-koo-kook, given from perches.
  2. Contact call: Softer notes between individuals.
  3. Alarm: Sharp calls when alarmed.
हिंदी: नरम "कू-कू-कू-कूक" गीत। मार्मिक आवाज़। संपर्क कॉल कोमल।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Seed eater: Oriental Turtle Dove forages on the ground and from plants. It feeds on:

  • Seeds of grasses and weeds
  • Seeds of cultivated crops (wheat, rice, bajra)
  • Occasionally berries and fruits
  • Very rarely insects

Foraging: Walks on ground picking seeds; also feeds in trees. Drinks water daily; often visits water sources in morning and evening.

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs during breeding; forms small flocks in winter roosts.

Roosting: Roosts on trees and branches, often in groups.

Predators: Raptors (particularly Common Kestrel, Shikra). Chicks vulnerable to snakes and crows.

Flight: Swift, direct flight with rapid wing beats.

हिंदी: जमीन पर दाने खोजता है। अकेले या जोड़ों में सर्दियों में। पानी पीना पसंद करता है। झुंड में सोता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Breeding: Does not breed in eastern UP; breeds in the Himalayas and East Asia (April–August). Simple twig nest in tree. Clutch typically 2 eggs. May have 2–3 broods per season in breeding areas.

Moult: Prebasic moult occurs before arrival at wintering grounds (July–September).

Migration: Long-distance migrant; returns to same wintering areas in successive years.

हिंदी: अक्टूबर में आता है, मई तक रहता है। हिमालय में प्रजनन करता है। लंबी दूरी का प्रवासी। सर्दियों में मैदानों में।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Common Kestrel, Shikra, Snake Eagle, occasionally larger raptors
  • Mammalian predators: Snakes (chicks), rarely jackals
  • Nest predators: Snakes, crows, monitor lizards
  • Parasites: Parasitic flies, mites
हिंदी: शिकारी चिड़ियाँ इसका शिकार करती हैं। अंडों को सांपें और कौवे खतरे में डालते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: The Oriental Turtle Dove is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is prohibited in India.

Agricultural impact: May feed in wheat and rice fields post-harvest, consuming spilled grain, but damage is negligible.

Crop assistance: Consumes weed seeds, providing a minor benefit to agriculture.

Cultural relevance: Known to villagers and farmers; traditionally appreciated for its melodious cooing call.

Game species history: Historically hunted in some parts of Asia; no longer permitted in India.

Indicator species: Presence indicates open woodlands and cultivated areas with adequate cover.

हिंदी: यह विधि अधिनियम 1972 के तहत संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.

Winter range: Expected in eastern UP from October to May in open woodlands, cultivated areas, and village surroundings. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain. Recorded at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary (Mishra, Kumar & Kumar 2019). Particularly expected in areas with open cultivation and scattered tree cover.

Habitat preference: Open woodlands and forest edges; cultivated areas with adjacent tree cover; village gardens and parks.

Peak season: November–March, when numbers are highest from Himalayan populations.

Detection: Listen for its distinctive, mournful cooing call from trees in early morning and evening. Look for it on ground in open areas and fields.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph or detailed observation of the bird in eastern UP submitted through the About page, with location and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in the Himalayas and East Asia; winters across southern Asia. Highly migratory in northern populations.

In India: Winter visitor across northern and central India; resident in southern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor throughout the state in suitable open and cultivated habitats.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a winter visitor in cultivated areas and village surroundings; recorded at Bakhira Bird Sanctuary in published surveys.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Latham in 1790. The type locality is East Asia.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies; S. o. oxyura occurs in northern Asia and India.

Family and order placement. Order Columbiformes; family Columbidae (pigeons and doves). The genus Streptopelia contains turtle doves with characteristic neck patches.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to other Streptopelia species, particularly the Eurasian Collared Dove.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widespread. The species is adaptable to various habitats. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972.
