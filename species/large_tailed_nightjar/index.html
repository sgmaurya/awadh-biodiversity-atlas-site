लार्ज-टेल्ड नाइटजार / Large-tailed Nightjar

Caprimulgus macrurus Horsfield, 1821 — Caprimulgidae

लार्ज-टेल्ड नाइटजार — यह रात को सक्रिय चिड़िया बहुत कम दिखाई देती है क्योंकि यह दिन भर ज़मीन पर या पेड़ों की शाखाओं पर चुप बैठी रहती है। इसकी सबसे बड़ी पहचान इसकी विशिष्ट कूकना है — "चक-चक-चक" की आवाज़ जो रात को सुनाई देती है। इसकी पूंछ लंबी होती है, इसीलिए इसका नाम "लंबी पूंछ वाली" है। यह रात को पतंगों और अन्य कीड़ों का शिकार करता है।

Field tip: By day, look for a nightjar perched lengthwise (not across) on a branch or log, where its mottled plumage provides perfect camouflage. The distinctive long tail is visible. More easily found by listening for the characteristic loud chuck-chuck-chuck call at dusk and night from open areas with scattered trees. Very reluctant to fly unless closely approached.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange

Bill6–8 mm

Size: 31–35 cm long (with long tail); wingspan 52–62 cm. The long tail is distinctive.

Plumage: Cryptically mottled grey, buff, and brown overall. Upperparts darker, finely barred and streaked. Underparts paler, buff-brown, with fine barring. Plumage provides excellent camouflage against bark, dead leaves, and branches.

Head: Large, rounded; flattened. Eyes small, positioned for nocturnal vision; pupils large at night.

Bill: Short but with enormous gape (mouth opening) for capturing flying insects. Bristles surround the bill opening.

Tail: Very long (diagnostic feature), 145–175 mm, with pale tips on outer tail feathers visible in flight.

Wings: Long, narrow, pointed; adapted for swift, acrobatic flight. Primaries dark; coverts patterned.

Feet: Short, weak. Middle toe elongated with comb-like serration (typical of nightjars).

Sexes: Females slightly smaller; subtle plumage differences.

Vocalisations. Highly vocal, especially at dawn and dusk:

  1. Primary call: A loud, harsh chuck-chuck-chuck or chatter-chatter sequence, repeated rhythmically.
  2. Alarm: Nasal sounds when disturbed.
  3. Flight call: Quiet chirps.
हिंदी: रात को "चक-चक-चक" या "छत्त-छत्त" जैसी आवाज़।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Nocturnal specialist: Active from dusk to dawn. Hunts on the wing, capturing insects in flight. Feeds on:

  • Moths and butterflies
  • Flying beetles and flies
  • Grasshoppers and other aerial insects
  • Small aerial invertebrates

Hunting technique: Takes off from perch or from ground, flying in erratic patterns to snap up insects in flight. Uses wide gape to capture prey while flying.

Roosting by day: Sits motionless on ground, dead branch, or along log — never across a branch like most birds, but lengthwise. Cryptic plumage provides excellent camouflage. Very reluctant to fly during day.

Perching: Perches lengthwise (parallel to branch) rather than across (transverse). Rests with tail drooping.

Breeding: Monogamous; pairs may persist. Nests on ground with no nest structure — eggs laid directly on bare ground or leaf litter.

Territoriality: Maintains territory through loud calls.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round in suitable habitat. Some local seasonal movements in response to insect availability.

Breeding: Breeds February to August; peak April–June. Ground nester; no nest structure.

Eggs: Typically 2 buff or olive-brown eggs, cryptically marked.

Incubation: Approximately 20–22 days by both parents.

Chicks: Altricial; covered with camouflaged down. Brooded by parents.

Fledging: Young fledge at approximately 3–4 weeks.

Seasonal activity: More vocal and visible during breeding season; less conspicuous during non-breeding months.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Diurnal raptors: Shikra, Eurasian Kestrel
  • Snakes: Large snake species may predate nests
  • Monitor lizards: May consume eggs and chicks
  • Corvids: Crows opportunistically predate eggs

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest control: Important insectivore; feeds on agricultural and household insect pests including moths.

Protected status: All wild birds protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Schedule IV. Hunting is illegal.

Cultural significance: Known locally for its distinctive nocturnal call. Mentioned in folklore.

The Large-tailed Nightjar is expected as a resident of eastern Uttar Pradesh in open woodlands, cultivated areas with groves, and gardens with scattered trees. The species is best detected by its loud chuck-chuck-chuck call given at dusk and night. During daytime, scan tree branches and dead logs for a motionless bird perched lengthwise. The long tail is distinctive when visible. Most active vocally during breeding season (April–June). A first record would be a sighting of the bird or a clear audio recording of the distinctive call with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia, from India through Southeast Asia.

In India: Distributed throughout suitable habitat in the peninsula and Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Distributed throughout suitable habitat; present year-round.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident in open woodlands and cultivated areas. More vocal during breeding season.

Field rule: A large nightjar with a distinctly long tail, cryptic mottled plumage, found perched lengthwise on branches by day, and giving a loud chuck-chuck-chuck call at night is a Large-tailed Nightjar. The long tail is diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Thomas Horsfield in 1821 in Transactions of the Linnean Society as Caprimulgus macrurus. Type locality: Java.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies; C. m. macrurus occurs in India.

Family and order placement. Order Caprimulgiformes; family Caprimulgidae (nightjars). The genus Caprimulgus contains about 25 species of nightjars.

Phylogenetic notes. Large-tailed Nightjar belongs to the large Asian nightjar clade within Caprimulgus, characterized by larger size and long tails.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). The global population is large and stable. The species is adaptable to human-modified habitats and benefits from insect populations in agricultural areas.
