आम का गुठली घुन / Mango Stone Weevil

Sternochetus mangiferae (Fabricius, 1775) — Curculionidae

आम का गुठली घुन — आम की खूबसूरत गुठली के अंदर रहने वाला भूरा कीट जो अंदर-ही-अंदर खाता है। इतना अंदर कि जब तक आम पकता है, गुठली का सारा सफ़ेद भाग खो जाता है, और अंदर से सिर्फ़ कीट निकलता है। मई से जुलाई में आम के फूल खिलते हैं तो माँ कीटें छोटे फलों में अंडे देती हैं, और लार्वा जन्म से मौत तक गुठली के अंदर।

Field tip: Cut open a developing Mango suspected of infestation: the stone shows brown tunnels and frass inside. If the larva is present, the interior is hollowed. At harvest, an irregular hole in the stone from which a dark weevil has emerged is diagnostic.

Adult: Large, robust weevil. Snout is long, slightly curved. Elytra are deeply and coarsely punctate, with fine longitudinal costae between the puncture rows. Colour ranges from dull brown to dark brown, sometimes with reddish tints. Legs are brown, stout.

Larva: Cream-coloured to white, wrinkled, C-shaped, with a brown head. Five instars. Remains entirely within the stone, feeding on the endosperm tissue. By the final instar, the larva has consumed much of the stone's interior.

Egg: Laid individually in a small puncture made by the female, then sealed; remains inside the developing fruit.

हिंदी: बड़ा कीट, भूरा, लंबी सुँड। लार्वा क्रीम रंग, बिना पैर, गुठली में। अंडा गुठली में छिपा।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The mango stone weevil is a serious pest of Mango in all mango-growing regions from Pakistan to the Pacific. Adult females begin laying eggs as soon as the fruit is marble-sized (about 15–20 days after flowering), entering the developing fruit through the skin to lay eggs inside the stone. The larva develops entirely within the stone, feeding on the endosperm and destroying its nutritive value. Development takes 50–70 days in warm weather; the adult emerges just before or at harvest. Infested mangos may appear sound externally but are wholly unmarketable because the stone is destroyed and the kernel is lost. The pest is particularly damaging in areas with high humidity and warm temperatures (24–28°C), which favour rapid development.

The weevil is entirely dependent on Mango: it does not infest other fruit and cannot survive on non-mango hosts. Once a crop is infested (from immigrant females), populations build through the season, each fruit potentially harbouring one to three weevils.

हिंदी: आम पर ही, बाकी कहीं नहीं। छोटे आम में अंडा, गुठली में लार्वा, कटाई तक निकल आता है। गर्मी में तेज़।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Mango (Mangifera indica) — the exclusive host
  • Cannot develop on cashew, litchi, Guava, or other Sapindales or stone fruits

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid wasps: Centistes mangiferae (endoparasitoid; very rare in the field)
  • Predatory ants: Pheidole and Camponotus species feed on eggs and emerging weevils
  • Entomopathogenic fungi: Beauveria and Isaria under high humidity
  • Braconid parasitoids: some attack pupae inside the stone

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The loss. A major economic pest of Mango across the Indian subcontinent, Southeast Asia, and the Pacific. Damage can exceed 50% in susceptible varieties and unfavourable management. Every infested fruit is rendered wholly unmarketable for fresh export; only suitable for processing.

Today's management:

  • Resistant varieties: Some varieties (Alphonso, Langra in northern India) are less preferred by ovipositing females or have thicker stone walls that reduce larval survival. Variety choice is important.
  • Sanitation: Collection and destruction of fallen and infected fruit before the weevil emerges.
  • Insecticide spray: Broad-spectrum sprays (carbaryl, endosulfan, though latter is banned in many countries) applied at fruit-set and repeated 15–20 days later to kill ovipositing females. Timing is critical.
  • Bagging: Bagging developing fruit with non-woven cloth or plastic prevents oviposition; labour-intensive but effective for high-value export crops.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Natural enemy augmentation, though Centistes mangiferae is too rare to be practical. Host-plant resistance and its genetic basis. Monitoring systems for predicting peak oviposition.

हिंदी: प्रतिरोधी किस्में चुनो। गिरे आम को नष्ट करो। अंडा देते समय छिड़काव। मंहगे बागों में आम को कागज़ से लपेटो।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: All mango-growing regions. Native to the Indian subcontinent; now established in Southeast Asia, Hawaii, and the Pacific islands.

In India: Pan-Indian; all mango-growing states. More damaging in warmer regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in mango-growing areas; damage increases southeastward with temperature and humidity.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in home gardens and scattered Mango groves; frequency depends on variety and management.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. What is the earliest fruit size at which females successfully lay eggs? / सब से छोटे आम में भी माँ अंडा दे सकती है? — Measure fruit diameter when first eggs appear; infest fruits of varying sizes.
  2. Which Mango varieties in your village are infested most heavily? / कौन सी आम की किस्म में सब से ज़्यादा घुन आता है? — Inspect fruit from 3–4 varieties at harvest.
  3. How many weevils can develop in a single stone? — Dissect infested stones; count larvae at different stages.
  4. Does collection and destruction of infested fruit before harvest reduce the next season's population? — Treat half a grove; compare the following season.
  5. What proportion of eggs laid in very small fruit (finger-sized) are successful? / छोटे आम में अंडे कितने सफल होते हैं? — Mark fruit; follow to harvest.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Fabricius in 1775 in Systema Antliatorum as Curculio mangiferae. The genus Sternochetus was erected by Schönherr in 1838. Mangiferae refers to Mango (Mangifera).

Subspecies. No subspecies are currently recognised, although populations in different regions show some genetic variation.

Family and order placement. Order Coleoptera (beetles), family Curculionidae (weevils). The genus Sternochetus comprises stone-infesting weevils of Anacardiaceae, Sapindaceae, and other families.

Phylogenetic notes. Sternochetus mangiferae is a derived lineage within the genus, specialised on Mango alone. Related species infest cashew and guava in the same families.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a widespread agricultural pest, it is not assessed by the IUCN.
