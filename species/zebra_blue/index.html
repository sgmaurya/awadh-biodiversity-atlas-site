ज़ेब्रा ब्लू तितली / Zebra Blue

Leptotes plinius (Fabricius, 1793) — Lycaenidae

ज़ेब्रा ब्लू तितली — एक छोटी नीली तितली जिसके पंख के नीचे की ओर गहरी धारियों की एक विशिष्ट पैटर्न होता है। यह तितली पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के बागों और खेतों में पाई जाती है। इसका कैटरपिलर विभिन्न पौधों की कलियों पर भोजन करता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeVery small butterfly, wingspan 17-22 mm
Male colourBright metallic blue upperside; silvery-blue sheen
Female colourBrownish or greyish with blue patches at wing base
Underside patternPale with distinctive dark zigzag or wavy lines (zebra-like pattern)
Hindwing undersideConspicuous line pattern, often with post-discal spots
FlightRapid, erratic flight; stays close to vegetation

Field tip: A tiny blue butterfly with distinctive zigzag line pattern on the underside of wings. The pattern resembles zebra stripes, giving the butterfly its name. Look in gardens and grasslands near flowering plants.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बहुत छोटी नीली तितली, पंख के नीचे गहरी तिरछी धारियाँ। तेज़ उड़ान।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Wingspan17-22 mm
Forewing length8-11 mm
Body length7-9 mm

Wings:

  • Upperside (male): Bright metallic blue with silvery sheen; occasionally with dark margins.
  • Upperside (female): Dull brown or greyish with blue restricted to basal areas.
  • Underside: Pale grey or whitish with characteristic dark zigzag or wavy lines running across wings, often with a row of dark spots.

Body: Small, dark; antennae clubbed.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are bright metallic blue; females are dull brown with limited blue colouration, making them easily distinguishable.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stage / MonthEvent

EggLaid on or near flower buds; incubation 4-6 days
LarvaGreen or brown caterpillar, feeds on flower buds; 2-3 weeks
PupaSmall, greenish; formed on or near host plant; 5-10 days
Adult2-4 weeks lifespan; most active during monsoon months
हिंदी: साल भर पाई जाती है। कलियों पर हरा कैटरपिलर।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Flower buds of various plants
  • Garden herbs and flowering plants
  • Wild flowers

Adult food sources:

  • Nectar from flowers
  • Pollen

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Small birds: Flycatchers and warblers
  • Spiders: Web-building and jumping spiders
  • Parasitoid wasps: Parasitise caterpillars and pupae

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minor significance. The caterpillars feed on flower buds but cause negligible damage to crops or gardens. Not considered an agricultural pest.

Garden indicator. The presence of Zebra Blue butterflies in gardens indicates diverse flowering plants and a healthy garden ecosystem.

Today. Common in villages and gardens throughout the region where flowering plants are present, particularly during the monsoon and post-monsoon seasons.

हिंदी: बागों में आम, नुकसान नहीं।

To be expected year-round in gardens, grasslands, and cultivated areas throughout the region, with peak abundance during the monsoon. Look in gardens near flowering plants and in open areas with diverse flora. A photograph with date and location would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across tropical and subtropical Asia, from India to Southeast Asia.

In India: Distributed throughout the country in gardens and cultivated areas; common in most states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Found in gardens and cultivated areas throughout the state; common in villages and agricultural regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in gardens, grasslands, and field margins year-round, particularly during monsoon.

Field rule: A tiny blue butterfly with males bright metallic blue and females dull brown, distinguished by the bold zigzag or zebra-like line pattern on the pale underside, is the Zebra Blue.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Fabricius in 1793 in Entomologia Systematica suppl. Type locality: India.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognized across Asia. The subspecies found in India is Leptotes plinius plinius (Fabricius, 1793).

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Lycaenidae, subfamily Polyommatinae (blues). The genus Leptotes includes several species of small blues.

Phylogenetic notes. Leptotes species are highly adapted to gardens and cultivated areas, showing remarkable success in anthropogenic habitats.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed. Abundant and widespread; no conservation concerns.
