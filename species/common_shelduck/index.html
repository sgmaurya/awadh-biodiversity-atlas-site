कॉमन शेलडक / Common Shelduck

Tadorna tadorna (Linnaeus, 1758) — Anatidae

कॉमन शेलडक — ठंड के मौसम में आने वाली सबसे बड़ी हंस, सफेद और काली धारियों वाली, जिसका सिर अधेरी हरी चमकीली है। बड़ी झीलों और नदियों पर एकेले या जोड़ों में दिखती है। इसे "चकवा-चकवी" का जोड़ा कहा जाता है।

Field tip: A large, strikingly patterned duck with green head (male), white body, and chestnut chest band. Distinctive bright red bill and legs. Often rests on muddy banks or in shallow water margins. Much larger and brighter than other winter ducks.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (winter birds in eastern UP):

Size: 58–67 cm long; wingspan 100–115 cm. The largest duck in eastern UP in winter.

Plumage (male, winter): Glossy metallic green head and upper neck. Throat white. Breast and forebody chestnut or rufous-brown, forming a distinct breast band. Flanks and belly white or buff. Back black; rump and upperparts chestnut. Wings strikingly patterned black and white.

Plumage (female): Similar to male but duller overall; greenish tinge on head less glossy. Breast band less distinct.

Bill: Bright red or orange-red; slightly swollen at base with a small knob.

Legs and feet: Red or orange-red; relatively long, placed far back on body.

Eyes: Dark.

Sexes: Weakly dimorphic; males slightly larger and more brightly coloured.

Juveniles: Duller than adults; head less green; pale bill with dark tip.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Drake call: A soft, whistled whe-whe-whe, given in courtship and flight.
  2. Duck call: A harsh quacking, rarely heard in winter.
  3. Alarm call: Sharp, honking notes.
हिंदी: नर की सीटी जैसी आवाज़। बहुत कम गाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Dabbling and benthic forager. Common Shelducks forage at the water surface and in shallow water, dabbling and up-ending to reach aquatic vegetation. They also feed on land, grazing on grass and clover in meadows.

Social behaviour: Often seen in pairs or small groups of 2–10 birds; less gregarious than other wintering ducks. Pairs tend to remain together through the winter.

Habitat preference: Large, open water bodies with shallow margins and adjacent grassland. Muddy banks are preferred for resting.

Daily activity: Crepuscular (most active at dawn and dusk), but feed throughout the day. Nocturnal roosts on open water.

Flight: Strong fliers; fly with rapid, shallow wingbeats. In flight, display striking black and white wing pattern.

Predators: Large eagles and raptors; Peregrine Falcon. Ducklings vulnerable to crows, weasels, and jackals.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Historical hunting. Hunted extensively as a game bird across much of its winter range. In eastern UP, hunting is recorded in historical texts. Now protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Crop interaction. In some regions, flocks feed in stubble fields, consuming waste grain. May occasionally strip young crops, but damage is minimal.

Cultural significance: Named chakwa and chakvi in local speech, referring to the male and female respectively. Featured extensively in Indian classical poetry and folklore as a symbol of devoted partnership.

Scientific value: Important indicator of wetland quality and health; monitored in international wetland surveys.

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Common Shelduck should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from October to April, though records are less frequent than for other wintering ducks. Large lakes, reservoirs (such as Ghat Lakes near Balrampur), and the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems are suitable habitats.

Habitat preference: Large, open water bodies with shallow margins and adjacent grassland. Pairs favour quieter sections away from heavy disturbance.

Frequency: Irregular winter visitor; some years more abundant than others.

GBIF records: GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya.

What a first record would be: A photograph clearly showing the green head, red bill, and distinctive patterning in a specific location (GPS and date), submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Europe and Asia from the British Isles to Japan and Mongolia. Breeds in the Caspian and Mediterranean regions; winters across southern Asia to the Indian subcontinent.

In India: Distributed as a winter visitor (October–April) on major water bodies and wetlands, but more localized than other wintering ducks.

In Uttar Pradesh: Less common than Common Teal or Northern Shoveler; occurs on large lakes and reservoirs.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Irregular winter visitor to the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and major tanks; less frequent than other dabbling ducks.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae (10th edition), volume 1, page 122, as Anas tadorna. Type locality: northern Europe.

Subspecies. Monotypic — T. tadorna is the only recognised subspecies.

Family and order placement. Order Anseriformes; family Anatidae (dabbling ducks, diving ducks, shelducks, and geese). The genus Tadorna contains "shelducks" with distinctive large size and striking patterns.

Phylogenetic notes. Tadorna tadorna is closely related to other Tadorna species (e.g., T. ferruginea, Ruddy Shelduck). Molecularly placed as intermediate between dabbling ducks and diving ducks.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern globally. Populations are stable across a wide range. In India, protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Populations are monitored through international wetland surveys.
