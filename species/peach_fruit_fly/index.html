आड़ू की फल मक्खी / Peach Fruit Fly

Bactrocera zonata (Saunders, 1842) — Tephritidae

आड़ू की फल मक्खी — सफ़ेद धारियों वाली छोटी मक्खी जो आड़ू, नीबू, पपीता आदि फलों में घुसकर खराब करती है। इसके सफ़ेद लार्वे फल के अंदर पलते हैं और गूदा सड़ जाता है। ज़हरीली नहीं होती पर खेत में बहुत नुक़सान पहुँचाती है।

Field tip: Inspect developing stone fruits (peach, nectarine, plum), citrus (lemon), and guava for small entry holes with dark or oozing margins. Fruits may fall prematurely. Adult flies are small and fast; best seen in the field as they skim fruit surfaces.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: आड़ू या अन्य फलों पर सुई जैसा छेद, अंदर से सड़ा। छोटी धारीदार मक्खी, तेज़ उड़ने वाली।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Adult body length5–7 mm
Adult female ovipositor length2–3 mm
Larva length6–9 mm
Puparium length4–6 mm

Adult: Small fly, yellow-orange to brownish, with distinctive pale markings and bands on wings and body. Females larger than males. Wings have characteristic pattern of dark and pale areas. Eyes red; face yellow. Ovipositor of female is sharp, needle-like, used to puncture fruit skin.

Larva: Creamy white to pale yellow, cylindrical, smooth, pointed at anterior end. No distinct head capsule. Posterior end with two dark spiracles. Three instars in typical development.

Puparium: Brown, slightly flattened; formed within the fruit or in the soil.

हिंदी: पीली-भूरी धारीदार मक्खी, बहुत छोटी। लार्वा सफ़ेद, बेलनाकार, नरम।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Peach Fruit Fly is an aggressive frugivore, primarily of stone fruits but also attacking citrus, guava, and other fleshy fruits. Females are attracted to ripening fruits, which they puncture with their needle-like ovipositor to lay eggs. Larvae develop rapidly within the fruit, feeding on the pulp and introducing bacteria and fungi that cause rot and fermentation. The fly shows no diapause and multiple overlapping generations persist throughout the year in tropical regions, with peaks during the fruiting seasons (March–June for peaches and early season fruits; September–November for late-season crops). The species originated in tropical Asia (particularly on ber and other wild and semi-cultivated fruits) and has become a serious quarantine pest globally, spreading to Mediterranean and African regions. It is a highly invasive species where introduced.

In the Basti–Ayodhya region, the fly is present year-round on ber, wild jujube, and cultivated fruits; it is a significant pest wherever stone fruits, citrus, and guava are grown.

हिंदी: गर्मी और सर्दी में फलों को खराब करती है। पाँच-सात दिन में अंडे बड़े हो जाते हैं और फल गल जाता है। साल भर कई पीढ़ियाँ।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary hosts:

  • Peach (Prunus persica)
  • Nectarine (Prunus persica var. nectarina)
  • Plum (Prunus spp.)
  • Ber / Indian jujube (Ziziphus mauritiana)

Important secondary hosts:

  • Citrus (lemon, sweet orange)
  • Guava (Psidium guajava)
  • Mango (Mangifera indica)
  • Apple (Malus domestica)

The fly is highly polyphagous but prefers stone fruits and ber.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid wasps: Diachasmimorpha spp., Fopius spp. (imported biological control agents in many regions)
  • Braconid wasps: parasitise larvae and pupae
  • Flies: tachinid parasitic flies
  • Ground beetles and other predatory arthropods: consume pupae in soil
  • Fungi: entomopathogenic fungi in high-humidity conditions
  • Birds: consume pupae and emerging adults

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The Peach Fruit Fly is one of the most destructive fruit pests in warm climates, causing significant economic losses in stone fruit, citrus, and guava production globally. In eastern Uttar Pradesh, it is a serious pest wherever these crops are grown and represents a significant quarantine pest under plant health regulations in many countries.

Documented damage: Larvae develop inside the fruit, making it unmarketable. Infested fruits show fermentation, rot, and premature drop. Losses can be heavy in unprotected orchards.

Management (from published extension literature): Cultural practices include removal and destruction of infested fruit, sanitation of orchard floors, and barriers (netting) over developing fruit clusters. Insecticide use is widespread but increasingly subject to restrictions due to resistance and environmental concerns. Biological control with Diachasmimorpha species has been successful in Mediterranean and Middle Eastern regions.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical Asia (Indian subcontinent to Southeast Asia); now invasive in Mediterranean basin, Middle East, and Africa.

In India: Throughout India in fruit-growing regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in districts with stone fruit and citrus cultivation; widespread.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Present wherever these fruits are cultivated; particularly on ber, which is widely grown.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. In which months are peach or ber fruits most heavily infested in your area? / आपके गाँव में आड़ू या बेर फलों में इस मक्खी का प्रकोप किस महीने में सब से ज़्यादा होता है? — Inspect fruits weekly; record infestation rates.
  2. How many larvae can develop in a single fruit, and how long do they take? — Infest fruits experimentally; count larvae at harvest time.
  3. Which natural enemies parasitise or predate the larvae and pupae in local soil? — Rear pupae from infested fruit indoors; record emergences.
  4. Does netting of developing fruit reduce infestation? — Cover some fruit clusters; compare infestation rates.
  5. Which fruit types (ber, peach, citrus, guava) are most preferred for oviposition? — Offer female flies choice of fruits; observe and count oviposition attempts.

Field rule: Small yellow-orange fly with pale banded wings, puncturing developing stone fruits (peach, ber) to lay eggs; infested fruit shows fermentation and rot with visible larvae inside: Peach Fruit Fly (Bactrocera zonata).

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Saunders in 1842 in the Transactions of the Entomological Society of London as Urophora zonata based on specimens from the Indian subcontinent. The genus Bactrocera was erected by Macquart in 1835; the species was transferred to Bactrocera by later workers.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are formally recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Diptera (true flies), family Tephritidae (fruit flies). The family is large and includes many frugivorous and phytophagous species; Bactrocera is the largest and most economically important genus.

Phylogenetic notes. Bactrocera zonata is sister to Bactrocera dorsalis (Oriental Fruit Fly) and shares many ecological and behavioural traits. The two species have overlapping geographic ranges and similar host ranges, making field separation difficult.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a widespread, abundant, and invasive species, a formal conservation assessment is not warranted; the species is of concern as a pest rather than conservation status.
