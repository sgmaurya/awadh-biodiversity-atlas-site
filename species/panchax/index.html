पंचाक्स / Blue Panchax

Aplocheilus panchax Hamilton, 1822 — Poeciliidae

पंचाक्स — भारत की अपनी छोटी मछली, तालाब और नदी के किनारे। नर नीले रंग का, मादा हरा और गहरा। मच्छर के लार्वे खाती है — यही असली काम। गम्बूसिया अमेरिका से लाई गई, पर पंचाक्स पहले से यहाँ थी, और बेहतर भी है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size3–4 cm
ShapeSlender, laterally compressed, with a slightly upturned mouth
Colour, malesIridescent blue-green, darker on back; red spot on gill cover
Colour, femalesGreenish-brown; less colourful; darker bars or spots
FinsDorsal and anal fins set well back; tail rounded
DistinctiveUpturned mouth, surface-dwelling habit, colourful males in breeding season

Field tip: A small, colourful native fish of shallow water, ponds and rice field edges. Males are brilliant blue-green; females more drab. Look for them at the water surface hunting for mosquito larvae.

हिंदी: छोटी, रंगीन, पानी की सतह पर। नर नीला, मादा भूरा। मुँह ऊपर की ओर।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (field populations):

MeasurementRange
Standard length3–4.5 cm
Weight0.5–1.5 g
Dorsal fin rays12–14
Anal fin rays10–12
Sexual maturity8–12 weeks
Fecundity50–200 eggs per female

Coloration: Males display brilliant iridescent blue-green on the back and sides, with a distinctive red spot on the gill cover and red edges to the dorsal and anal fins. The display intensifies during breeding season and in the presence of females or rivals. Females are cryptic greenish-brown or olive, often with darker bars along the lateral line.

Mouth and feeding apparatus: Terminal, slightly upturned, with fine teeth; adapted for surface feeding on insects and larvae.

Fins: Dorsal and anal fins are set well back on the body; tail fin is rounded. All fins have subtle coloration and barring.

हिंदी: नर नीले-हरे, गिल पर लाल निशान, पंख लाल किनारों वाले। मादा भूरी, अंधकार पट्टियाँ। मुँह ऊपर की ओर, दाँत छोटे।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A surface-dwelling fish of shallow, still and slow-moving waters. Hunts mosquito larvae (Culex, Anopheles, Aedes), small insects and crustaceans at the water surface. Schools in weeds and shallow margins; group size increases in larger ponds. Males are territorial and establish small ranges during breeding, displaying bright colours and defending against rivals. The species tolerates poor water quality better than most fish, surviving in stagnant, vegetated ponds where dissolved oxygen is low; it can gulp air at the surface when needed.

The ecological role in village ponds is significant: a small population of panchax can suppress mosquito breeding over a wide area. Unlike Gambusia affinis, which was imported for the same purpose, panchax integrates into the native aquatic community and does not displace or outcompete native fish species.

हिंदी: सतह पर मच्छर के लार्वे का शिकार। झुंड में रहती है। नर आक्रामक, रंग दिखाते हैं। ख़राब पानी सहती है। गम्बूसिया की तरह देसी मछलियों को नष्ट नहीं करती।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Mosquito larvae: Culex, Anopheles, Aedes spp.; hundreds of larvae consumed per fish per day
  • Other insects: Small aquatic insects, insect pupae, small aquatic worms
  • Crustaceans: Copepods, ostracods, water fleas (Daphnia), small decapods
  • Eggs: Small fish eggs when available; scavenging behaviour

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Indian Pond Heron, Little Egret, White-throated Kingfisher
  • Fish: Stinging Catfish, Great Snakehead, larger panchax (cannibalism in crowded conditions)
  • Aquatic insects: Dragonfly and damselfly nymphs preying on juveniles and eggs

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Mosquito control: The primary ecological role. Feeds voraciously on mosquito larvae, particularly in shallow water and rice paddies. Unlike Gambusia, Aplocheilus panchax is native and integrated into the aquatic community; it has never caused documented ecological damage.

Comparison to Gambusia: Aplocheilus panchax is smaller, less competitive with native fish, and does not displace species as Gambusia does. The native fish is the original mosquito control agent; the imported Gambusia was an unnecessary and damaging introduction.

To be expected in shallow, weedy ponds, rice field margins and ditches throughout the district. The fish is most visible during monsoon months (June–September) when breeding activity peaks and males display their colours. A photograph or a net catch is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia, from India to Indonesia; native to the entire Indian peninsula.

In India: Throughout the Indian subcontinent from sea level to 1,500 m; particularly abundant in rice-growing regions of the north, north-east and centre.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in wetlands, ponds, rice fields and margins throughout the state; the eastern plains are core habitat.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all shallow water bodies with vegetation; likely abundant in monsoon.

Field rule: A small fish with an upturned mouth, metallic blue or green colouration in males, and a surface-hunting habit in shallow water and rice field margins = panchax.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches, p. 330, from the Ganges and Bengali rivers. The name panchax comes from a Telugu word; pancha means "five" (historically referring to dorsal fin ray count).

Subspecies. Aplocheilus panchax panchax (Hamilton, 1822) is the eastern Indian subspecies, distributed from the Ganges through the Indian peninsula. The genus Aplocheilus contains several species across the Indo-Pacific region.

Family and order placement. Poeciliidae (the livebearer family, though Aplocheilus is not viviparous like true poeciliids), order Cyprinodontiformes. Recent molecular studies have questioned the placement; some authorities now place it in a separate family Aplocheilidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Aplocheilus is a basal member of the Cyprinodontiformes and is thought to represent an early divergence within the group. The genus is closest to Asian killifish; the relationship to New World poeciliids is deeper but still poorly understood.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. The species is widespread, abundant and stable in its range. No range contraction or population decline is documented.
