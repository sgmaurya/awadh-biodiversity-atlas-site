खैर / Khair

Senegalia catechu (L.f.) C.H. Griscom — Fabaceae

खैर — सूखी और नम दोनों जमीन में पनपने वाला पेड़, जिसकी लकड़ी से काठ (कत्था) निकाली जाती है। काठ का इस्तेमाल पान की चूने की जगह होता है और कपड़े रंगने में। पत्तियाँ दोहरी पंखनुमा, फूल पीले, छोटे, सुगंधित। पेड़ नाइट्रोजन देता है, इसलिए खराब जमीन को सुधारता है। पेड़ से कीड़े काठी लाख भी पालते हैं।

Field tip: Look in open woodlands and along canal banks for a tree with very fine, fern-like leaves and spikes of small yellow fragrant flowers. The dark, very hard wood is distinctive.

Trunk and bark: Straight to crooked, dark grey, rough, with scattered spines on younger stems. Heartwood is very hard, dark brown to reddish, with a fragrant, spicy aroma.

Leaves: Alternate, bipinnate, the overall outline is feathery and fernlike. Leaflets are very small, entire.

Flowers: Small, pale yellow or greenish-yellow, highly fragrant, arranged in dense cylindrical spikes (catkins) at the branch tips.

Fruit: A flat, brown legume (pod) 10–15 cm long, woody, with one to three seeds.

Wood properties: Very hard, durable, and fragrant; used for tool handles and other items.

हिंदी: बहुत सूक्ष्म, दोहरी पंखनुमा पत्तियाँ। पीली, सुगंधित फूलों की बालियाँ। लकड़ी काली, बहुत कड़ी, सुगंधित।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार and पारिस्थितिकी

Khair is a tree of dry deciduous forests and scrub across northern India, the Deccan, and into southeast Asia. It is drought-hardy and fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules, enriching poor soils. The leaves are palatable to cattle and goats, and the tree is lopped for fodder in dry seasons. The flowers are visited by bees and produce fragrant honey. The tree regenerates readily from seed and coppices well after cutting.

The heartwood has long been harvested for the production of catechu (katha/kattha), an astringent extract used for tanning and dyeing, and also as a chewing ingredient mixed with betel leaf.

हिंदी: सूखी जमीन में भी जीवित रहता है। हवा से नाइट्रोजन खींचता है। पशुओं को पत्ती खिलाई जाती है। लकड़ी से काठ निकाली जाती है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Wild bees and Asian Honey Bee visit the fragrant flowers.
  • Fodder: Leaves eaten by cattle and goats.
  • Lac host: Lac Insect is reared on khair wood (and also kusum).

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Bark-boring beetles; wood-boring caterpillars in mature trees.
  • Leaf-eating caterpillars in the monsoon.
  • No significant fungal diseases in dry climates.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Catechu (katha/kattha) production. The heartwood is boiled and the extract reduced to a dark, astringent paste called catechu or cutch. This is mixed with betel leaf as a stimulant and digestive. The trade in khair catechu was historically significant across India and internationally.
  • Tanning and dyeing. Catechu is an astringent used to tan leather and dye textiles, particularly for browns and blacks. It is also used in the preparation of leather and as a mordant.
  • Traditional medicine. Listed in Ayurvedic texts as an astringent for mouth and throat complaints. The bark and heartwood are used for this purpose.
  • Timber. Very hard and durable; used for tool handles, small implements, and structural components.
  • Fuel. Dense wood produces good charcoal.

Today. The khair–katha trade continues in parts of India, though smaller than historically. Catechu is exported in quantity to countries of south and southeast Asia. Khair is also planted in agroforestry for its nitrogen-fixing properties and fodder value.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The chemistry of catechu extraction and the economics of the khair–katha supply chain in eastern UP would merit study.

हिंदी: खैर की लकड़ी को पकाकर काठ निकाला जाता है। काठ पान में चूना की जगह चबाया जाता है। कपड़े रंगने में इस्तेमाल होता है। दवा में गले के लिए। ईंधन भी अच्छा है। लाख पालने में होस्ट पेड़।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to dry deciduous forests from Pakistan through India to Myanmar and Thailand.

In India: Widespread in dry and semi-dry regions of the plains and plateau.

In Uttar Pradesh: Scattered in drier regions and along irrigated areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: May be present on wastelands and canal margins; status to be confirmed.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many khair trees are in your area, and do any of them show signs of catechu extraction (boiling pits)? — Walk wastelands and canal banks; document locations and girth measurements.
  2. Who in your village knows how to make katha, and are they still making it? / क्या कोई अब भी काठ बनाता है? — Interview elders about the khair–katha trade.
  3. How much heartwood can be extracted from a mature tree, and what is the yield of dried catechu? — If a tree is cut, measure the wood processed and final catechu weight.
  4. Which insects visit the yellow fragrant flowers in April? — Observe a flowering tree for 30 minutes at dawn and dusk.
  5. Does livestock actually browse the foliage in the dry season, and is it palatable? — Ask herders about feeding practices.

Field rule: A tree with very fine, feathery, doubly pinnate leaves, fragrant yellow flower spikes, and very hard dark wood, is khair.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Originally described as Acacia catechu by Willdenow in 1806. Transferred to Senegalia by Britton and Rose in 1928. The epithet catechu refers to catechu, the product of the heartwood.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Fabaceae, the legume family, in the order Fabales. Senegalia is the genus of spiny acacias, comprising about 100 species in tropical regions.

Phylogenetic notes. Senegalia catechu is closely related to other south Asian Senegalia species and shares their nitrogen-fixing and drought-tolerance traits.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in dry deciduous forests and cultivated settings.
