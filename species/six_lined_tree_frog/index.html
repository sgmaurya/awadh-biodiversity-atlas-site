सिक्स-लाइन्ड ट्री फ्रॉग / Six-lined Tree Frog

Polypedates taeniatus (Cope, 1862) — Rhacophoridae

सिक्स-लाइन्ड ट्री फ्रॉग — पेड़ों के पत्तों और टहनियों पर रहने वाली एक मध्यम आकार की, पूरी तरह बेज़हर मेंढक, जिसके शरीर पर छह गहरी सोने-रंग की पट्टियां होती हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के बाग-बगीचों और पेड़ों पर मानसून के मौसम में यह दिखाई देती है। रात को सक्रिय रहती है और हवा में छलांग लगाकर कीड़ों को पकड़ती है। इसकी तेज़ और ऊँची आवाज़ ("करैक-करैक") मानसून की रातों में सुनाई देती है।

Field tip: A medium-sized tree frog with distinctive longitudinal golden stripes. Found on leaves, branches, and in gardens during the monsoon. Makes loud croaking calls at night. Completely harmless.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for specimens from eastern UP):

MeasurementRange
Snout-vent length (SVL)40–55 mm
Weight8–15 g

Coloration: The dorsal surface is yellowish-brown or olive-green. The most distinctive feature is the presence of six longitudinal dark golden or dark brown stripes running from the head down to the hind limbs. The ventral surface is white or pale cream, sometimes with a faint longitudinal stripe.

Head: Moderately pointed snout. Eyes are large and protuberant with golden irises. Tympanum is distinct and moderately sized.

Limbs: Long and slender, adapted for arboreal life. Front limbs have four digits; hind limbs have five digits. All digits possess prominent adhesive discs (toe pads) at the tips, which allow the frog to grip smooth leaf surfaces and branches.

Webbing: Toes are moderately webbed, particularly in the hind limbs. The webbing is less extensive than in aquatic frogs, appropriate for an arboreal species.

Dorsal skin: Smooth to granular; lacks the prominent warty texture of terrestrial frogs.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Arboreal insectivore. Polypedates taeniatus is strictly nocturnal and completely adapted to life above ground on trees and shrubs.

  • Habitat preference: Inhabits deciduous and mixed forests, forest edges, gardens, orchards, and cultivated areas with substantial tree cover. Requires overhead vegetation (branches and leaves) for roosting and hunting.
  • Nocturnality: Strictly nocturnal; spends the day hidden in tree holes, under bark, in leaf axils, or among dense foliage. Emerges at dusk to hunt.
  • Foraging: Active hunter; moves through trees and shrubs using powerful hind limbs and adhesive toe pads. Hunts by sight, striking at flying insects and small arthropods.
  • Prey capture: Uses the tongue to capture insects in flight or at rest. Capable of snapping at flying insects in mid-air.
  • Climbing ability: Excellent climber; the adhesive toe discs allow it to hang from the underside of leaves and move across smooth vertical surfaces.
  • Vocalization: Males call from elevated perches in trees, producing loud, repetitive croaking calls ("carack-carack" or "krruk-krruk"), particularly during the monsoon breeding season.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Arboreal insectivore:

  • Primary prey: Flies, mosquitoes, small wasps, ants, small beetles
  • Hunting method: Visual stalking; rapid tongue projection; occasional aerial strikes at flying insects

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Predatory birds: Small owls and insectivorous birds hunt roosting frogs
  • Snakes: Small tree snakes (rat snakes, wolf snakes) prey on arboreal frogs
  • Spiders: Large spiders may catch roosting individuals
  • Parasites: Parasitic flies and leeches
  • Humans: Generally not hunted; habitat loss is the main threat

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Insect controller. Polypedates taeniatus provides pest control services in agricultural and garden areas by consuming flying insects, including agricultural pests. The species is harmless to humans and is generally tolerated in gardens and orchards. The loud mating calls during monsoon evenings are part of the natural soundscape and indicate healthy monsoon conditions. No agricultural damage is recorded.

Expected in deciduous forests, forest edges, gardens, and cultivated areas with substantial tree cover in the Basti–Ayodhya region during the monsoon season (June–October). GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain. The species is most visible during monsoon months when males are actively calling. Search in trees and shrubs at night with a headlamp, listening for distinctive croaking calls. Look on leaves, branches, and under bark for roosting frogs during the day. The first local record would be an audio recording of the distinctive call with place, date, and time, or a photograph showing the distinctive stripes with location and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widely distributed across South Asia from India and Sri Lanka to Myanmar, Thailand, and Malaysia.

In India: Found throughout the subcontinent in forested areas and cultivated regions with suitable vegetation.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident in forested areas and cultivated regions throughout the state; particularly common in monsoon-receiving areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in gardens, orchards, and forest areas with substantial tree cover; likely fairly common during monsoon months.

Field rule: A medium-sized tree frog with six distinct golden longitudinal stripes on the body is a Six-lined Tree Frog. The prominent adhesive toe discs and arboreal habits confirm identification.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Edward Drinker Cope in 1862 as Hyla taeniata based on Indian specimens. Type locality is India. It was later placed in the genus Polypedates (Günther, 1859).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Anura, family Rhacophoridae (Asian tree frogs). The genus Polypedates contains several large Asian tree frogs with prominent adhesive discs.

Phylogenetic notes. Polypedates taeniatus is closely related to P. leucomystax (Common Tree Frog) and represents a distinct lineage within the genus.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) globally because of its wide geographic distribution, large population size, and tolerance of human-modified habitats including gardens and cultivated areas. The species is not directly threatened, though pesticide use may affect insect food supply locally.
