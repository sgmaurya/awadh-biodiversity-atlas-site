बार्ड वुल्फ़ स्नेक / Barred Wolf Snake

Lycodon striatus (Shaw, 1802) — Colubridae

बार्ड वुल्फ़ स्नेक — एक छोटा, पूरी तरह से विषरहित साँप जो गाँवों और घरों में पाया जाता है। गहरे भूरे-काले रंग की, शरीर पर नियमित सफ़ेद-क्रीम धारियाँ जिनमें अंदर काले धब्बे होते हैं। रात के समय घरों की छिपकलियों का शिकार करता है। सर्पखंड (common krait) जैसा नहीं दिखता, विषरहित है।

Field tip: A small banded snake with a grid-like or bar pattern (white bands with black spots inside) found in houses at night. Non-venomous and harmless. Climbs well on walls and in rafters. Much smaller than the Common Krait; the barring pattern is diagnostic.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (northern Indian populations):

MeasurementRange
Total length40–70 cm
Tail length6–10 cm
Weight40–120 g

Dorsal pattern: Ground colour dark brown or blackish-brown. The distinctive pattern consists of regular, transverse yellowish-white or cream-coloured bands or bars that run across the entire body width. Inside each white band is a row of black spots or dots, creating a grid-like appearance. This barred pattern is diagnostic and distinguishes it from other wolf snakes.

Scales: Dorsal scales smooth, arranged in 15–17 rows at midbody. Scales are slightly keeled but not prominently. All dorsal scales are uniform in size; no row of enlarged vertebral scales.

Ventral surface: Pale yellow or white, occasionally with dark markings.

Head: Small, scarcely distinct from the neck, flattened. Eyes small and dark, with vertical pupils.

Dentition: Aglyphous (lacks fangs). Possesses enlarged, backward-curved, fang-like teeth at the front of both jaws. These "wolf-like" teeth are solid (not grooved) and are used to grip smooth-skinned prey like geckos and skinks.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Nocturnal climber and gecko specialist.

  • Climbing ability: Extremely agile; scales walls, wooden beams, and thatched roofs with ease. The flattened ventral scales aid in gripping rough surfaces.
  • Diurnal hiding: Hides in dark, tight spaces during the day — under boxes, behind cupboards, in wall cracks, inside roof tile piles.
  • Gecko specialist: Hunts geckos and skinks almost exclusively; feeds primarily on nocturnal house geckos on walls at night.
  • Defensive behavior: When cornered, hides head beneath coils and tries to escape. If handled or provoked, strikes repeatedly and bites readily, occasionally releasing foul-smelling musk from cloacal glands, but cannot inject venom.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Breeding: Occurs in late spring and early summer (April–June).
  • Reproduction: Oviparous (egg-laying).
  • Egg-laying: Females lay 5–11 small, elongated, white eggs in wall crevices, under wood piles, or in loose soil in June–July.
  • Hatching: Eggs hatch after 45–55 days. Hatchlings measure 12–15 cm; carry distinct barring and are fully independent.
  • Sexual maturity: Reached in 1–2 years.
  • Lifespan: 5–8 years recorded.
हिंदी: अप्रैल से जून तक प्रजनन; अंडे मई-जून में।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Carnivorous diet (gecko specialist):

  • Primary prey: House geckos (Northern House Gecko, Brooke's House Gecko) and other geckos
  • Secondary prey: Skinks, small lizards, occasionally small frogs

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Venomous snakes: The Common Krait is a major predator
  • Household predators: Cats and dogs readily kill wolf snakes
  • Birds of prey: Owls if caught in open; day-active raptors

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Natural gecko control. The Barred Wolf Snake hunts nocturnal geckos in homes, providing control of gecko populations. This helps prevent over-population of geckos in certain structures.

Harmless and beneficial. Non-venomous and completely harmless to humans. Bites cause no medical problems. Beneficial as a natural predator.

Protection status. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Cultural significance. Often feared but harmless. Better-known than Common Wolf Snake due to distinctive barring pattern.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Expected inside both traditional and modern houses in Basti district. Look for them during monsoon (July–September) when they climb walls seeking dry shelter and hunting geckos. The distinctive barred pattern makes identification straightforward. Most likely found on interior walls near ceiling or in roof spaces. The first local record should document location (house type, room, height), date, time, and any defensive behaviour observed. Send photographs with specific place name, GPS coordinates, and date to the atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia and Southeast Asia, from Pakistan through India to Nepal and Bangladesh.

In India: Found throughout the plains and hills up to 2,000 metres elevation.

In Uttar Pradesh: Extremely common resident in all districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Abundant in all blocks, particularly in human-dominated environments and agricultural areas.

Field rule: Any banded snake with a grid-like or barred pattern (white bands with black spots inside) found in a house is a harmless Barred Wolf Snake. If the bands are solid white without spots inside, and the head is solid black, it is a Common Krait and is dangerous.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. George Shaw described this species in 1802 in General Zoology or Systematic Natural History. Originally placed in Coluber; transferred to Lycodon by later authors. The epithet striatus refers to the striped or barred pattern. The genus Lycodon means "wolf-tooth" in Greek.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Squamata, family Colubridae, subfamily Colubrinae. Lycodon is a large genus of nocturnal colubrid snakes.

Phylogenetic notes. Lycodon striatus is part of a diverse Asian radiation of nocturnal, ophiophagous (gecko-eating) snakes. Molecular studies confirm its position within Colubridae.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern (LC). Extremely widespread, abundant, highly adaptable to human environments, and thriving in agricultural and urban areas. No conservation concerns.
