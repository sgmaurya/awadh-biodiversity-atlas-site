परवल / Pointed Gourd

Trichosanthes dioica Roxb. — Cucurbitaceae

परवल (पटोल, पेठा) — बसंत से सर्दी तक गर्मी में उगने वाली एक खीरे-तरबूज़ जैसी बेल, पर पूरी तरह अलग सब्जी। फल हरा, लंबा, नोकदार (इसीलिए नाम परवल — नुकीली सब्जी), डेढ़-दो इंच लंबा, अंदर से सफेद गूदा और काले बीज। बहुत कम देखभाल से आती है, वर्ष दर वर्ष एक ही जगह लगाई जा सकती है। गर्मी-बरसात में खूब फलती है, सब्जी बहुत हल्की और पौष्टिक होती है।

Field tip: A vigorous climbing vine with heart-shaped leaves, small white flowers, and characteristic pointed, dark green fruits with white stripes. Look for it on trellises in vegetable gardens from May to October.

Stems: Tender, not woody, angular, hairless or slightly hairy, green, producing branched tendrils from leaf axils.

Leaves: Alternate, simple or shallowly 3–5 lobed, heart-shaped at the base, margins toothed. Texture is smooth. Midrib is prominent.

Flowers: Regular, with 5 lobes, white or cream-colored, small. The plant is dioecious — male and female flowers are on separate plants. Male flowers are more numerous; female flowers are slightly larger and are followed by fruit development.

Fruits: Smooth, thin-walled, elongate-oval, pointed at both ends, deep green with white longitudinal stripes (giving a striped appearance). The flesh inside is white, moderately firm, with spongy texture. Seeds are flattened, dark brown, numerous.

Seeds: Smooth, flat, dark.

हिंदी: पतली बेल, दिल-जैसी पत्तियाँ, सफेद फूल, नुकीले, हरे-सफेद पट्टेदार फल। बीज काले, चपटे।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A tender annual or short-lived perennial climbing herb of South Asian origin, cultivated throughout the tropics for its edible fruits. Trichosanthes dioica is a warm-season plant that germinates readily from seed and grows quickly in summer. The plant requires a support structure (trellis, stake, or fence) for the tendrils to climb. The dioecious nature means that at least one male plant must be present for fruit set on female plants.

The plant is shallow-rooted and demands consistent soil moisture; it is sensitive to cold and dies back after the first frost. In ideal conditions (warm, moist, with good trellis support), a productive plant yields fruits continuously for 4–6 months.

हिंदी: गर्मियों की बेल। ठंड में मर जाती है। एक ही जगह कई साल लगाई जा सकती है (बहुआयु प्रकार)। तेली-पेली की ज़रूरत है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • On shared trellises: Often grown alongside Ivy Gourd, Ridge Gourd, and other climbing vegetables on the same support.
  • Garden companions: Grown in kitchen gardens (bari) with Onion, Potato, and herbs.
  • Pollinator support: Male flowers attract bees; female flowers require cross-pollination by bees for fruit set.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Fruit flies and squash bugs: Larvae can damage developing fruits, but damage is usually minor.
  • Powdery mildew: White fungal coating on leaves in humid conditions; spray with sulfur if severe.
  • Leaf spots: Various fungal pathogens; worse in wet years or where foliage stays wet.
  • Root rot: Waterlogging can cause root diseases, especially in clay soils; ensure good drainage.
  • Generally hardy: Compared to cucumbers and melons, parwal is relatively pest-resistant.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Vegetable use: The immature, tender green fruits are harvested when 4–6 weeks old and are cooked as a vegetable. The fruits are boiled and fried, often with turmeric and spices, producing a light, easily digestible dish. Parwal is considered particularly suitable for children and elderly people due to its mild flavor and soft texture.

Nutritional value: Parwal fruits are low in calories (about 20 kcal per 100 g), with moderate levels of vitamin C and fiber. The vegetable is recommended in traditional dietary practices for lightness and digestibility.

Cultivation advantages: Parwal requires minimal inputs beyond land, water, and a trellis structure. The plant can be propagated from seed or from tubers (underground swellings that form on the roots). Tuber-propagated plants bear fruit more quickly (3–4 weeks after planting) than seed-propagated ones (8–10 weeks). Many farmers save tubers from season to season, allowing the plant to persist on the same land for several years.

Common varieties: Landrace varieties are common in eastern UP, distinguished chiefly by fruit size and color intensity. Improved varieties from agricultural institutions include Pusa Sneha and others.

Seasonal availability: In eastern UP, parwal is in peak season from June to September when it is available fresh in all vegetable markets.

हिंदी: बहुत हल्की, पौष्टिक सब्जी। गर्मी-बरसात में बहुत फलती है। दर्जे (कंद) बचाकर साल दर साल लगाते हैं।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Believed to be native to northeastern India and Southeast Asia; now cultivated throughout tropical Asia.

In India: Widely cultivated in home gardens and commercial vegetable fields across the Indo-Gangetic plain, particularly in eastern and central regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in all vegetable-growing districts; especially common in eastern UP.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common as a cultivated vegetable in homestead gardens and vegetable plots.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by William Roxburgh in 1824 as a cultivated plant from eastern India (Bengal). The genus name Trichosanthes is from the Greek thrix (hair) and anthos (flower), referring to the fringed petals of the flowers. The specific name dioica refers to the dioecious nature of the plant.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Cucurbitaceae, order Cucurbitales. The family includes all gourds, melons, and cucumbers. Trichosanthes is a genus of about 20 species, mostly in South and Southeast Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Trichosanthes dioica is related to T. japonica and other Asian Trichosanthes species. The dioecious reproduction distinguishes it from most other Cucurbitaceae.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widely cultivated; not assessed for conservation status.
