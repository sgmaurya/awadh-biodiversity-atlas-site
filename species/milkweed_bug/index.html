मिल्कवीड बग / Milkweed Bug

Spilostethus hospes (Fabricius, 1794) — Lygaeidae

मिल्कवीड बग — लाल और काले रंग की सुंदर रिश करने वाली चीटी, जो मुख्य रूप से आक (कैलोट्रोपिस) के फूलों और बीजों को खाती है। इसका शरीर चपटा होता है और पंखें पूरी तरह विकसित होती हैं। यह कीट आक के रस को चूसकर रहती है। आक जहरीली होती है, पर यह कीट उसे खा लेती है। आक के बाग़ों और बेकार ज़मीन पर जहाँ आक उग आई हो, वहाँ अक्सर यह कीट ढेर लगा कर देखी जाती है।

WingsFully developed and functional; held flat over abdomen when at rest
AntennaeFour-segmented; relatively long
LegsRelatively long; adapted for walking and feeding on various plant parts

Field tip: On Calotropis (milkweed) plants in gardens and wastelands, look for small red and black bugs with fully developed wings. They often aggregate on flowers and seed pods, sometimes in large numbers. The aposematic (bright warning) coloration is distinctive — few other small bugs in eastern UP show this red and black pattern.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: छोटा कीट, लाल और काले रंग में। आक की पत्तियों और फूलों पर देखा जाता है। अक्सर झुंड में होता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Body length10–12 mm
Body width5–6 mm
Head width1.5–2 mm
Antennae4–5 mm

Colour pattern: The entire body is a striking combination of bright red (pronotum, legs, parts of abdomen) and black (head, portions of the wings — called the corium — and ventral surface). This aposematic (warning) coloration signals to predators that the insect may be unpalatable.

Head: Small, black, wedge-shaped. Eyes moderate-sized and placed laterally. Ocelli (simple eyes) present.

Antennae: Four-segmented (characteristic of Lygaeidae); relatively long and slender.

Pronotum: Reddish-orange or bright red; trapezoidal in shape; wider posteriorly than anteriorly. The anterior margin is straight to slightly convex.

Scutellum: Black or dark; triangular.

Wings (hemelytron — the modified forewing): Fully developed and functional. Anterior portion (corium) is dark (black or dark brown); posterior portion (membrane) lighter. Hindwings (alae) are clear and fully functional — the bug can fly.

Legs: Red or reddish-brown; relatively long and slender, adapted for moving across plants.

Abdomen: Dorsal (upper) surface partly red and partly black; ventral (lower) surface black.

Rostrum: Piercing-sucking mouthpart; composed of four segments forming a tubular structure; used to pierce plant tissues and extract juices.

हिंदी: लाल और काले रंग का। छोटा पर दिखने में बहुत सुंदर। पूरी तरह उड़ सकता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Milkweed Bug is a seed and fruit specialist, tightly associated with its host plant genus Calotropis (milkweed). The insect feeds on the nutritious seeds and developing fruit structures of the host plant.

Feeding behaviour: Uses its piercing-sucking rostrum to feed on plant tissues, including the developing seeds within the seed pod (follicle). Multiple bugs may feed on the same plant organ, creating areas of necrosis or tissue damage. The insect obtains both moisture and nutrition from the plant.

Host plant specialization: Spilostethus hospes is highly specialized on Calotropis species, particularly Calotropis gigantea and Calotropis procera. This specialization is unusual in Lygaeidae and has allowed the bug to exploit the toxic defences of the host. The bug is able to sequester or tolerate cardiac glycosides (the toxic compounds in milkweed), rendering it potentially toxic to its predators — an advantage conferred by the aposematic coloration.

Aggregation: Highly gregarious; often found in large numbers on a single plant, particularly during fruiting season. Aggregations of 20–50 or more individuals per plant are common.

Migration and dispersal: Adults can fly and move between plants. Dispersal typically occurs when host plants are depleted or during the dry season when fruit abundance decreases.

Seasonal activity: Year-round on perennial Calotropis plants, but abundance follows the flowering and fruiting phenology of the host. Peak abundance during seed-setting (typically monsoon and post-monsoon in eastern UP).

हिंदी: आक के फूल और बीजों को खाती है। झुंड में रहती है। लाल-काला रंग बताता है कि यह ज़हरीली है या स्वाद में बुरी है।

Seasonal pattern in eastern UP:

  • January–March: Cool season; populations reduce but remain present on host plants
  • April–June: Hot season; populations variable, dependent on host flowering
  • July–September: Monsoon; host flowering and fruiting; population increases
  • October–December: Post-monsoon; maximum fruit production and peak bug abundance
  • Year-round: On perennial host plants in gardens and wastelands
हिंदी: साल भर रहता है आक के पौधों पर। गर्मी के बाद बहुत होता है, जब आक के बीज लगते हैं।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary hosts:

  • Calotropis (Calotropis gigantea, Calotropis procera) — primary host
  • Other Asclepiadaceae (milkweed family members) where available

Host plant specialization: Highly specialized on Calotropis species. Occasionally recorded on other plants in captive conditions, but wild populations are virtually host-specific.

The bug feeds on:

  • Developing seeds within the seed pod
  • Nectar from flowers (occasionally)
  • Plant sap from various tissues
हिंदी: सिर्फ़ आक को खाता है। दूसरे पौधे नहीं।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid wasps: Some pentatomid and lygaeid parasitoid wasps are known to parasitize Spilostethus species (not specifically documented for S. hospes in India, but likely)
  • Predatory bugs: Larger reduviid (assassin) bugs and other predatory hemipterans may feed on eggs and nymphs
  • Spiders: Jumping spiders and other web-building spiders
  • Birds: Some birds (particularly bulbuls and other frugivores) may prey on bugs when aggregated
  • Entomopathogenic fungi: Beauveria and Metarhizium species may cause mortality under humid conditions

The aposematic coloration signals toxicity (or unpalatability) to potential predators, reducing predation pressure.

हिंदी: मकड़े, बड़े कीट इसका शिकार करते हैं। लेकिन लाल-काला रंग कई शिकारियों को दूर रखता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minor pest status: In regions where Calotropis is cultivated (not common in eastern UP) or where wild populations are abundant, the bug can cause noticeable damage to seed production by feeding on developing seeds. However, Calotropis is not a cultivated crop in eastern UP, and wild populations are not harvested, so practical damage is minimal.

No beneficial use documented: This species is not used for food, traditional medicine, or craft purposes in the region.

Ecological significance: The bug is an important component of milkweed plant-associated arthropod communities. It may serve as a prey item for birds and other insectivores when abundant.

Research interest: The ability of the bug to sequester or tolerate the toxic cardiac glycosides of milkweed is of interest to chemical ecology and toxicology researchers.

हिंदी: आक एक जंगली पौधा है, इसलिए यह कीट कोई नुक़सान नहीं करती। यह इकोसिस्टम का हिस्सा है।

Expected on Calotropis plants throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh, year-round where the host plant is present. In Basti district, Calotropis occurs in wastelands, roadsides, and abandoned gardens. The bug should be particularly abundant during the monsoon and post-monsoon seasons when Calotropis is flowering and fruiting.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Old World tropics and subtropics; native to Africa and southern Asia; now found across the tropics and subtropics of Africa, southern Asia, and the Americas.

In India: Widespread in grasslands, wastelands, and cultivated areas where Calotropis is present; recorded across most states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Likely present wherever Calotropis grows; particularly in eastern districts where wastelands are abundant.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on Calotropis in wastelands and abandoned gardens; exact frequency depends on the abundance of the host plant.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Fabricius in 1794 in Entomologia Systematica as Lygaeus hospes. The genus Spilostethus was assigned later.

Subspecies. No subspecies are formally recognised; the species is treated as monotypic.

Family and order placement. Order Hemiptera (true bugs); family Lygaeidae (seed bugs). Spilostethus is a genus of Old World seed bugs characterized by specialization on particular plant hosts.

Phylogenetic notes. The genus Spilostethus is widely distributed in the Old World with multiple species specialized on particular host plant genera. S. hospes shows the highest degree of host specialization within the genus, consistent with a long coevolutionary relationship with Calotropis.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. The species is widespread and abundant; no conservation assessment is warranted.
