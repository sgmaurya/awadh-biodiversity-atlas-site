कॉमन कार्प / Common Carp

Cyprinus carpio Linnaeus, 1758 — Cyprinidae

कॉमन कार्प — यूरोप की बड़ी मछली जो सौ साल पहले भारत लाई गई और सब तालाबों में है। सोने जैसी, भारी, तली में मुँह रखकर कीचड़ को गुलौज़ी हुई घास और तालाब के सब पौधों को बाहर निकाल देती है, जिससे पानी धुँआ उठा हुआ होता है। देसी रोहू और कतले की संतानों का खाना और घर खा जाती है, और जहाँ है, एक तालाब तबाह हो गया माना जाता है।

ColourGolden to olive-brown, lighter below; mirror carp variant has only a few large scales
MouthLarge, protrusible, facing downward; two pairs of barbels, one long
ScalesLarge, golden, tightly set; mirror and leather carp have varying scale patterns

Field tip: A large, golden-scaled, heavy-bodied fish with a downward mouth and barbels, digging and churning the bottom of any pond where it is present. The water is typically cloudy from its activity. Unlike the native reddish rohu, it is coppery-golden and much more turbid.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बड़ी, सोने जैसी, भारी, सिर चौड़ा, मुँह नीचे की ओर, दाढ़ी। तली को खोद-खोदकर पानी गंदा कर देती है। देसी रोहू लालपन लिए होती है और पानी को इतना गंदा नहीं करतीं।

Head and mouth: The head is broad and blunt. The mouth is large and protrusible, facing downward, adapted for bottom feeding. Two pairs of barbels, one pair maxillary (longer) and one pair maxillary, sense food in the mud.

Scales: Large, thick, and tightly arranged. Domesticated strains show variation: the fully-scaled mirror carp with normal scaling, the mirror carp with a few very large scales, and the leather carp with no scales.

Fins: The dorsal fin is long, with strong spines. The anal fin is also spiny. The tail is deeply forked. Pectoral and pelvic fins are broad.

हिंदी: सिर चौड़ा, भोथरा। मुँह बड़ा, नीचे की ओर, कीचड़ को खोजने के लिए। दो जोड़ी दाढ़ियाँ, बड़ी और छोटी। बड़े, कड़े, सोने जैसे शल्क। पेट काँटेदार। पूँछ गहरी V आकार की।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A bottom feeder and pond muddy. It uses its downward mouth and barbels to feel for invertebrates, seeds and detritus on the bottom, digging and uprooting aquatic plants as it does, which clouds the water and destroys the habitat. In doing this it removes the shelter and food sources of native fish. In warm water (above 15 °C) it feeds actively and grows fast, and a single female can produce hundreds of thousands of eggs. It is cold-tolerant and can survive winters in northern India. In ponds where it is not harvested it can become so numerous and large that it dominates the catch, and a pond managed for rohu and catla is effectively lost.

The only control once it is established is netting, draining, and liming the pond between crops. The government of India restricted culture in 1959; the ban has been widely ignored and the species is now in wild waters throughout the country.

हिंदी: तली में मुँह रखकर कीचड़ और पौधों को खोदती है, जिससे पानी धुँआ उठा रहता है। देसी मछलियों के घर और खाना बर्बाद करती है। ठंड सहती है और ज़्यादा हो सकती है। एक मादा लाख अंडे दे सकती है। बहुत हो जाए तो तालाब सुखाकर और चूने से उपचार करके ही निकाली जा सकती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Bottom organisms: chironomid larvae, ostracods, small molluscs.
  • Seeds and tubers of aquatic plants: Water Hyacinth, Hydrilla, roots of marsh plants.
  • Detritus and decaying plant matter.
  • Eggs and fry of native fish when available.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Large cormorants, Grey Heron, eagles (on young and small fish only; large carp are too heavy).
  • Fish: Large Great Snakehead and other predatory fish.
  • People: netted constantly; the only control mechanism.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The problem. In a pond managed for rohu and catla, common carp eats the native fish's fry and destroys their habitat by uprooting plants and clouding the water. The government of India prohibited culture in 1959; the ban was widely ignored and the species is now established in wild waters throughout the Gangetic plain, from which it cannot be removed.

The use. It is edible and the flesh is firm and good eating, though often muddy in taste if the pond water is poor. In Europe and China it is highly prized and farmed; in India it is cheap and eaten by those who cannot afford rohu.

Aquaculture. Farmed under licence in some states in enclosed systems to prevent escape, though the restriction is not universal.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: none; an arrival of the 20th century.

Today. Widespread in ponds, tanks and the Ganga system. Netting and marketing of common carp is a livelihood for fishermen in some areas.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The impact of common carp on native fish communities in the region is not quantified; a comparison of native fish diversity and abundance in carp-infested vs. carp-free ponds would provide evidence for management planning.

हिंदी: समस्या: रोहू-कतले का तालाब हो तो कार्प गंदगी और पौधों को नष्ट करके कार्प-रहित तालाब में बदल देता है। 1959 में खेती पर प्रतिबंध, जो किसी ने नहीं माना। उपयोग: मांस मज़बूत, खाने में ठीक, पर अक्सर कीचड़ का स्वाद। सस्ती मछली। यूरोप और चीन में बहुत कीमती।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to eastern Europe and western Asia; introduced to every continent except Antarctica for aquaculture.

In India: Throughout the country since the 1950s; most abundant in northern ponds and the Ganga system.

In Uttar Pradesh: Throughout; established in ponds, canals and the Ganga system.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in most still-water habitats and slow-moving channels.

Field rule: A golden-scaled, heavy, bottom-feeding fish with a downward mouth and long barbels, clouding the water as it digs, is the common carp; rohu and catla have terminal mouths and no barbels.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition, based on earlier descriptions. The epithet carpio comes from the French carpe.

Subspecies. The domesticated strains (mirror and leather carp) are variants of the wild European form, not distinct subspecies. Modern stocks are complex mixes of wild and domestic lineages.

Family and order placement. Cyprinidae, the carp family; order Cypriniformes. Cyprinus is monotypic.

Phylogenetic notes. Cyprinus carpio is among the basal cyprinids and is sister to a clade of Asian carps.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Widespread and extremely abundant; invasive in most parts of its introduced range.
