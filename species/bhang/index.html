भांग / Bhang

Cannabis sativa L. — Cannabaceae

भांग — यह पौधा हर देश में अलग क़ानून के तहत आता है। भारत में, उत्तर प्रदेश में, पत्तियों (भांग) को सरकार की अनुमति से बेचा जाता है। लेकिन फूल और राल (चरस या गांजा) ड्रग्स क़ानून के तहत अवैध हैं। यह सब होने के बावजूद, शिवरात्रि और होली पर पारंपरिक रूप से भांग का इस्तेमाल धार्मिक संदर्भ में किया जाता है। यह एक दस्तावेज़-आधारित विवरण है, सुझाव नहीं।

Field tip: In disturbed areas, look for a tall, aromatic herb with deeply palmately divided leaves. The plant has a distinctive musty-sweet smell when crushed. Male and female plants are easily distinguished by flower type.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: पत्तियाँ गहरी हरी, 5-7 भागों में बँटी, कँटेदार किनारे। तेज़ सुगंध। तना खुरदरा, कोणीय। फूल छोटे, हरे।

Stem and branches: Erect, hairy, angular, with prominent nodes.

Leaves: Alternate, palmately divided into 5–7 (rarely 3) narrow, linear-lanceolate leaflets with serrated margins. Leaves are bright green and strongly aromatic when crushed.

Flowers: Small, green, inconspicuous, unisexual (plants dioecious). Male flowers in elongated clusters (racemes or panicles); female flowers solitary or in clusters in leaf axils.

Fruit: An achene (small, hard, single-seeded fruit), smooth, with a distinctive reticulate (net-like) pattern.

Chemical composition: Contains cannabinoids, particularly delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and cannabidiol (CBD), concentrated in the resin of flowers and leaves. The highest concentrations are in the flowering plant and developing seeds.

हिंदी: तना खुरदरा, कोणीय। पत्ती गहरी हरी, 5-7 भागों में। फूल छोटे, हरे, नर और मादा अलग।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Cannabis is an annual herb native to Central Asia, now naturalized worldwide and cultivated in many regions. It is a pioneer colonizer of disturbed ground and wastelands, thriving in warm, well-drained soils. The plant has a short life cycle (4–5 months from seed to maturity). The flowers are pollinated by wind. The seeds are a food source for birds and animals.

The plant accumulates heavy metals from soil and has been investigated for phytoremediation (cleaning contaminated soils). Fiber production from the stem is an established agricultural use in some regions.

हिंदी: मध्य एशिया का पौधा है, अब पूरे दुनिया में है। बंजर जगह में जल्दी उग जाता है। छोटे जीवन चक्र, 4-5 महीने में पका बीज देता है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Wind pollination; insect visitation is incidental.
  • Seed dispersal: Birds and small mammals feed on the seeds.
  • Pioneer companions: Often grows with Patharchatta and other invasive weeds.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Stem-borers and caterpillars during vegetative growth.
  • Powdery mildew in humid conditions.
  • No significant diseases in well-drained soils.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

LEGAL STATUS IN UTTAR PRADESH — PLAINLY STATED:

Under Indian law and Uttar Pradesh state regulations:

  • Leaf (bhang): Permitted to be cultivated and sold under state excise licence. The leaf is processed into bhang (dried leaf or leaf paste) and is sold legally in authorized shops.
  • Flowers and resin (ganja and charas): Prohibited. The production, possession, sale, and use of cannabis flowers and resin are offences under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985. Conviction carries penalties including imprisonment and fines.

The legal distinction between the leaf (permitted under excise) and the resin or flowers (prohibited) is based on cannabinoid content and established by law.

Sacred and Cultural Use — Documented:

In Hindu tradition, bhang (the leaf) has documented use in religious contexts:

  • Shivratri: On the night dedicated to Shiva, bhang is prepared as a beverage and offered in temples and consumed in sacred contexts.
  • Holi: During the spring festival, bhang is a traditional ingredient in festive foods and beverages.
  • Other occasions: Bhang is used in temple rituals and religious gatherings.

This is documented cultural and religious practice.

Toxicity and Health Effects — Documented:

Bhang (cannabis) contains psychoactive compounds that affect the central nervous system. Effects vary with cannabinoid concentration, dose, and individual sensitivity. Acute effects include altered perception, memory impairment, and motor incoordination. Chronic use has been associated with cognitive effects, dependence, and mental health impacts in vulnerable individuals. Cannabis use during pregnancy and adolescence carries documented risks to fetal development and neurodevelopment.

The atlas describes and does not advise. The presence of bhang in eastern UP, its traditional cultural use, and its legal status are documented facts. Documentation is not endorsement, permission, or advice. Individual health decisions belong to individuals and their medical advisors. The atlas aims to provide accurate, evidence-based information about regional flora.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. The genus Cannabis and species sativa (meaning "cultivated") were named for the plant's long history of human use.

Subspecies. Classification is debated; some recognize subspecies (Cannabis sativa subsp. sativa and subsp. indica) based on morphology and cannabinoid content; others recognize Cannabis sativa as a single, highly variable species.

Family and order placement. Cannabaceae, a family also including hops, in the order Rosales.

Phylogenetic notes. Cannabis has a long history of cultivation and genetic modification by humans, resulting in substantial morphological and chemical variation. Modern genomic studies are clarifying relationships.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in cultivation and as a naturalized plant.
