चंदा / Indian Glassy Fish

Parambassis ranga (Hamilton, 1822) — Ambassidae

चंदा — सबसे खूबसूरत छोटी मछली, जिसका शरीर पारदर्शी (पारदर्शक) होता है, ऐसा लगता है कि शरीर शीशे का बना है। तालाबों और पोखरों में झुंड में रहती है। खाने में स्वादिष्ट होने के साथ-साथ सजावट के लिए भी महत्वपूर्ण है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size6–8 cm
BodyHighly compressed, transparent or translucent
HeadSmall, transparent; large eyes
ColourSilvery or translucent; iridescent stripe
FinsTranslucent with dark edges (dorsal and anal)
Body organsSlightly visible (heart, swim bladder) through transparent body
TailSmall, rounded

Field tip: Unmistakable due to its extraordinary transparency. Often the most beautiful fish in a pond, with a silvery or iridescent sheen. Feeds on zooplankton near the water surface. Often schooling; easiest to observe at dusk or in dim light when the transparency is most apparent.

हिंदी: बहुत खूबसूरत, पारदर्शी मछली। कांच जैसी लगती है। झुंड में रहती है। इसका दिल दिखता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (field populations):

MeasurementRange
Standard length60–80 mm
Weight3–8 g
Dorsal fin spines7–8
Dorsal fin rays10–11
Anal fin rays3 spines; 10–11 rays
Lateral line scales25–27

Body: Highly laterally compressed; disc-shaped. Remarkable transparency allows visibility of internal organs. Anterior profile rounded.

Head: Small, rounded, wholly transparent. Eyes large, positioned laterally; provide panoramic vision for detecting zooplankton and predators.

Transparency: The defining character. Body appears transparent or translucent; internal organs (heart, swim bladder, vertebral column) are slightly visible as silvery or whitish structures. The silvery swim bladder is particularly prominent.

Coloration: Predominantly silvery or translucent. A silvery-iridescent longitudinal stripe runs along the body, particularly prominent in lateral view. Fins are translucent or pale, with darker edges on dorsal and anal fins.

Fins: Dorsal fin with 7–8 spines and 10–11 rays; positioned posterior to the pelvic fins. Anal fin with 3 spines and 10–11 rays. Pectoral and ventral fins clear. Caudal fin small, rounded or slightly forked.

Scales: Minute, cycloid; barely visible.

हिंदी: पारदर्शी शरीर। दिल और तैराकी की थैली दिखती है। सिल्वरी रंग की पट्टी।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Schooling. Highly gregarious, forming schools that hover in the water column. Schools exhibit coordinated movement and are attracted to light sources (particularly at dusk).

Zooplankton feeder. Primary diet consists of small zooplankton (cladocerans, copepods, rotifers) captured by sight-feeding in the water column. Feeds actively at dusk and dawn when zooplankton availability is highest.

Water column dweller. Unlike many fish, Indian Glassy Fish spend most of their time in the water column rather than on the bottom. This pelagic habit is unusual among small fish in stagnant ponds.

Habitat preference: Found in a wide variety of freshwater habitats including ponds, tanks, slow rivers, marshes, and rice paddies. Tolerates stagnant water and variable oxygen levels, though prefers clear water where transparency gives a competitive advantage.

Predators: Small snakeheads, large insects, dragonfly larvae, cormorants, herons, and humans.

हिंदी: पानी के बीच में तैरता है। छोटे कीड़े खाता है। झुंड प्यार करता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Fish: Small snakeheads (Channa species), dragonfly larvae (aquatic insects)
  • Birds: Cormorant, heron, kingfisher
  • Humans: Trapped, netted, and collected for the aquarium trade

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food fish. Valued as a food fish despite its small size. Sold fresh in local markets. Often caught in bulk from ponds and rice paddies.

Aquarium trade. Important in the international aquarium hobby. Captive-bred forms are traded globally, though wild populations remain important for local subsistence.

Fishing methods. Caught using:

  • Small cast nets
  • Bamboo traps
  • Fine-mesh gill nets
  • Hand nets

Ecological role. As a zooplankton feeder, helps control mosquito larvae and other aquatic insect populations in ponds.

Today. Abundant in suitable habitats; populations appear stable.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The role of Indian Glassy Fish in controlling disease vectors (mosquito larvae) in village ponds, and the possibility of selective breeding for enhanced aquarium traits without losing wild populations, have not been systematically studied.

हिंदी: खाई जाती है और पालतू भी बनाई जाती है। मच्छर के बच्चों को खाती है। सुंदरता के लिए प्रसिद्ध है।

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Indian Glassy Fish should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

To be expected in all ponds, tanks, rice paddies, and slow-flowing water throughout the region. One of the most abundant small fish; found in almost any permanent water body with vegetation. Most visible at dusk when schools hover just below the surface. A net catch or observation is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia, native to the Gangetic region and throughout South Asia. Found from Afghanistan to Indonesia; widely introduced in the aquarium trade.

In India: Widespread in freshwater habitats throughout the country.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in ponds, tanks, and rivers throughout.

In Basti / eastern UP: Expected in all ponds and water bodies; likely the most abundant small fish in the region.

Field rule: A tiny (6–8 cm), transparent fish with large eyes, visible internal organs, and a silvery longitudinal stripe is unmistakably an Indian Glassy Fish.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Hamilton in 1822 in Fishes of the Ganges as Labyrinthus ranga. Later transferred to Parambassis by contemporary workers.

Subspecies. Monotypic — P. ranga is the only recognised subspecies.

Family and order placement. Order Perciformes; family Ambassidae (glass fish and jointing fish). The genus Parambassis contains small Asian fish with remarkable transparency.

Phylogenetic notes. P. ranga is phylogenetically distinct within Ambassidae; relationships are being clarified through molecular studies.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern globally. Widespread, abundant, and adaptable. No major threats in the core of its range. However, collection for the aquarium trade may impact local populations in some areas.
