Paddy Grasshopper / Rice Grasshopper

Oxya fuscovittata (Marschall, 1836) — Acrididae

धान टिड्डा — अगस्त में पूर्वी यूपी के किसी भी धान के खेत में जाइए — आपके क़दम पड़ते ही हवा में टिड्डे उड़ेंगे। प्रति वर्ग मीटर दसियों, कभी-कभी सैकड़ों। धान टिड्डा खुद कम महत्वपूर्ण है — उसकी तादाद उसे पूरी खरीफ खाद्य श्रृंखला में सबसे महत्वपूर्ण बनाती है।

Quick Identification शीघ्र पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeMedium-sized grasshopper; males 20–28 mm, females significantly larger at 28–35 mm
ColourGreen body with distinctive dark brown lateral stripes running from the eyes to the pronotum margins
WingsGreenish-brown forewings covering the abdomen, with translucent yellow-green hindwings visible in flight
HindlegsLong and powerful yellow-green legs with comb-like spines on the tibia, adapted for strong leaping
HabitatClosely associated with paddy fields, sugarcane fields, and adjacent tall grassy bunds

Field tip: Look for a medium-sized green grasshopper with a thick dark brown stripe running backward from each eye. When walking through a paddy field in Basti during the monsoon, any cloud of green grasshoppers leaping ahead of you is almost certainly this species.

Morphology आकृति विज्ञान

MeasurementRange
Body length (adult)20–35 mm
Wingspan38–54 mm
Forewing length18–28 mm

Size: Males 20–28 mm; females 28–35 mm. Female significantly larger — sexual dimorphism common in grasshoppers.

Head: Large compound eyes. Antennae shorter than body. Mandibles strong, for chewing grass blades.

Pronotum: Saddle-shaped, covering the thorax. Dark brown lateral stripe on each side — this stripe is the key field mark.

Wings: Tegmina (forewings) narrow, greenish-brown, covering the abdomen. Hindwings membranous, pale green to yellow-green, folded under tegmina at rest. In flight, hindwings create a flash of color.

Legs: Hindlegs long, powerful, with interlocking comb-like spines for jumping. Hindleg tibia yellow-green with brown spines.

Coloration: Green overall with brown lateral stripes. Color can vary slightly with moisture and temperature. Females often darker green.

Nymphs: Wingless; same general color pattern as adults but smaller, proportionally larger head. Pass through 5 nymphal instars.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Eggs: Laid in egg pods (clusters of 10–30 eggs in a frothy secretion) in moist soil on field bunds or field edges; June–July
  • Hatching: July–August, after monsoon rains
  • Nymphal development: 5 instars over 25–35 days
  • Adult emergence: August–October; peak adult density August–September coincides with peak paddy canopy
  • Mating and oviposition: September–October; eggs overwinter in soil
  • Die-off: Adults die with the first cold (November); population overwinters as eggs

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

As a herbivore: Feeds on paddy leaves — chewing cuts through the leaf blade, causing irregular defoliation. At high densities, significant leaf area loss. Also feeds on sugarcane, maize, sorghum, and wild grasses.

As prey — the most important role: The peak grasshopper density in August–September represents an enormous pulse of protein biomass — tens of thousands of kilograms per hectare across eastern UP's paddy landscape. This pulse is what drives the abundance of frogs, herons, drongos, and rollers during kharif season.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि और मानव संबंध

Pest impact: In eastern UP, Oxya grasshoppers can cause 5–15% leaf area loss in heavily infested fields, particularly in early kharif (August). However, paddy's compensatory growth means some defoliation is tolerated without significant yield loss. Severe outbreaks are less common than BPH or stem borer.

Chemical control: Organophosphate sprays are used in UP against hoppers in outbreak years. These sprays kill predatory insects (mantids, spiders, beneficial beetles) and reduce frog food, creating secondary pest outbreaks.

Biological control (natural): The extensive suite of natural predators listed above means that grasshopper populations rarely reach economically damaging levels in ecologically intact paddy systems. The key leverage is protecting frog and bird populations — particularly the Common Toad, Indian Pond Heron, and Indian Roller that are primary consumers.

Food use: Grasshoppers are a food source in many cultures globally. Not commonly consumed in eastern UP; however, poultry farmers in the region sometimes collect hoppers from paddy fields as feed.

In Basti district, paddy grasshoppers are omnipresent during the wet crop cycle. Observers walking along field bunds will note hundreds of these insects leaping into the air with a soft rustling sound. Local farmers generally tolerate small numbers and recognize their role in attracting insectivorous birds like mynas and drongos. Traditional agricultural practices in Basti often involve placing wooden perches in the fields to encourage drongos and rollers to hunt grasshoppers, acting as a natural pest management system.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South and Southwest Asia, including Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Myanmar.

In India: Widespread and highly common in the plains and river basins of all agricultural states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Extremely common and ubiquitous across all districts, particularly in rice-growing river valleys of the Ganges-Yamuna plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Highly abundant across all rice-growing blocks; populations peak dramatically during the monsoon crop cycle.

Range notes: No significant range changes documented.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. What is the grasshopper density in your paddy field at peak season (August)? / अगस्त में आपके धान के खेत में प्रति वर्ग मीटर कितने टिड्डे हैं? — Walk a 1-metre-wide transect; count every hopper that jumps. Repeat in 5 spots.
  2. Does the density differ between fields with heavy pesticide use and light use? / कीटनाशक वाले खेत और बिना कीटनाशक वाले खेत में टिड्डे कम या ज़्यादा? — Compare counts from both types. Also compare numbers of frogs and spiders.
  3. Which predator takes the most grasshoppers from your field? / कौन सा जानवर सबसे ज़्यादा टिड्डे खाता है? — Watch one 10 m² patch for 30 minutes; count predation events by species.
  4. Do grasshopper populations change with paddy growth stage? / धान की वृद्धि के साथ टिड्डे बढ़ते हैं? — Count at transplanting, tillering, panicle initiation, and grain filling stages. Which stage has most?
  5. What is the sex ratio of the grasshoppers in your field? / आपके खेत में नर और मादा टिड्डों का अनुपात क्या है? — Catch 20 hoppers; count males vs females (females larger). Does ratio change as season progresses?

Field rule: Green 20–35 mm grasshopper with brown lateral stripes + in paddy field + August–October = Oxya fuscovittata with high confidence. The only common green grasshopper of paddy fields in eastern UP.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Oxya fuscovittata was first described by August Friedrich de Marschall in 1836 as Gryllus fuscovittatus in Annalen des Wiener Museums der Naturgeschichte, 1: 211, pl. 18, f. 2. Type locality: "India orientalis". It was later transferred to the genus Oxya. The genus name Oxya is derived from the Greek oxys (sharp), referring to the ovipositor or vertex shape, and the species epithet fuscovittata is Latin for brown-banded.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Belongs to the order Orthoptera (grasshoppers, crickets), suborder Caelifera, family Acrididae (short-horned grasshoppers), subfamily Oxyinae. The genus Oxya is a major group of semi-aquatic and graminivorous grasshoppers containing about 50 species, many of which are pests of wetland rice.

Phylogenetic notes. Oxya fuscovittata belongs to a complex of rice grasshoppers. Hollis (1971) revised the genus, clarifying synonymies with species like Oxya turanica and Oxya oryzivora, which are now recognized as junior synonyms.

IUCN status rationale. This species has not been formally assessed by the IUCN Red List. It is extremely common and widespread in agricultural landscapes across its range, with stable populations that track rice cultivation.
