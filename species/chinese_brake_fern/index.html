ब्रेक फ़र्न / Chinese Brake Fern

Pteris vittata L. — Pteridaceae

ब्रेक फ़र्न — एक सुंदर, पतली पत्तियों वाली फर्न, जो नम, छायादार जगहों पर बढ़ती है। पत्तियाँ सीढ़ियों के पायदान की तरह एक सीध में लगी हुई होती हैं (इसीलिए "ladder brake" कहते हैं)। झरनों के पास, पत्थरों की दरारों में, पुरानी दीवारों पर पाई जाती है। सिंचाई के नाली में भी उग सकती है। बहुत सुंदर, घर के गमले में रखी जाती है, पर हवादार और नम जगह चाहिए।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
FrondsFeathery, delicate, 30–80 cm tall, bright green
LeafletsLinear or narrowly oblong, arranged alternately along the rachis, 3–8 cm long, 0.5–1.5 cm wide
ArrangementFronds pinnately compound; leaflets arranged in a ladder-like pattern along the rachis
RachisSlender, green to brownish; smooth or with fine hairs
SoriLinear strips along leaflet margins; covered by indusium
TexturePapery, delicate; not hairy

Field tip: A delicate fern with feathery, ladder-like fronds of narrowly arranged leaflets. Grows in shaded, moist habitats such as near streams, in rock crevices, or on damp walls. The linear sori are distinctive for the Pteridaceae family.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: नाज़ुक, सीढ़ीदार पत्तियाँ। नम, छायादार जगह में उगती है। पत्तियों के किनारे पर झुर्रीदार सोरस।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (cultivated and wild plants, tropical and subtropical regions):

FeatureMeasurement
Frond length30–80 cm
Frond width8–15 cm
Leaflet length3–8 cm
Leaflet width0.5–1.5 cm
Rachis diameter0.3–0.5 mm

Frond structure: Pinnately compound, lanceolate in outline. The main rachis (central stalk) is slender, green to brownish, smooth or minutely hairy. Pinnae (primary divisions) are narrow and linear, alternately arranged, creating a characteristic ladder-like appearance.

Leaflets (Pinnules): Linear to narrowly oblong, 3–8 cm long, 0.5–1.5 cm wide. Margins entire or slightly toothed. The texture is papery and delicate. Veining is prominent. The leaflets are arranged in a single row along the pinna rachis, at nearly right angles to the main axis.

Rhizome: Short, creeping to erect; covered with brown scales.

Sori: Linear strips (or "false indusia") along the leaflet margins, protected by a continuous indusium (the inrolled leaf margin). This is characteristic of the Pteridaceae family.

Spores: Monomorphic; produced in sporangia within the sori.

हिंदी: पत्तियाँ पतली, सीढ़ियों की तरह एक-एक लगी हुई। राइजोम काला, भूरी गोल छल्लियों से ढका।

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Shade-loving understory fern: Chinese Brake Fern is a plant of moist, shaded microhabitats. It typically grows in forest margins, ravines, stream banks, and rocky outcrops where humidity is consistently high.

Rock colonizer: The fern readily colonizes damp rock faces, crevices, and disturbed walls, trapping soil and organic matter and facilitating soil development.

Moisture indicator: Its presence indicates reliable soil moisture and, often, seepage. The species is sensitive to drought and is typically absent from dry zones.

Microhabitat creator: Dense fern populations create microsites for mosses, lichens, and other small plants, and provide shelter for small arthropods.

हिंदी: झरनों और नम छायादार जगहों पर उगती है। पानी का संकेत देती है। पत्थर पर मिट्टी जमा करती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Year-round growth: Fronds persist throughout the year; no dormant season in warm climates
  • Peak growth: Monsoon (July–October) and post-monsoon (October–November), when moisture is highest
  • Frond development: New fronds emerge continuously from the rhizome; growth is slow
  • Sori maturation: Sporangia mature throughout the year, with higher sporulation during monsoon
  • Spore release: Spores released when conditions are humid; passive wind dispersal
  • Gametophyte: Microscopic, heart-shaped, develops on moist surfaces
  • Lifespan: Individual fronds persist 6–12 months; rhizome is perennial

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Mosses and liverworts: Dense moss and liverwort communities commonly grow alongside Pteris vittata.
  • Other ferns: Ferns like Diplazium and Nephrolepis often occur in the same microhabitats.
  • Aquatic invertebrates: The moist microclimate supports soil invertebrates (nematodes, arthropods).

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Where it grows: A wild fern of old brick walls, wells, drains and shaded masonry across the plains of northern India (Dixit 1984; Fraser-Jenkins et al. 2017). It is also known for taking up arsenic from soil and water, a published line of research (Ma et al. 2001, Nature).

Habitat requirements: The plant requires:

  • High humidity
  • Shade or dappled sunlight
  • Consistently moist (but not waterlogged) soil
  • Good drainage

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Its uptake of arsenic has been studied for cleaning contaminated soil, a relevant lead for the arsenic-affected groundwater of the Gangetic plain.

Ecological value: In its natural habitat, the fern contributes to understory diversity and helps stabilize moist rocky and disturbed habitats.

हिंदी: सुंदर बेल गमले में लगाई जाती है। पर बहुत नाज़ुक है — नम, छायादार, हवादार जगह चाहिए। कोई पारंपरिक दवा या खान-पान का उपयोग नहीं।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Expected in Basti and Ayodhya districts at moist, shaded locations such as near springs, streams, damp walls, and forest margins, particularly in areas where humidity is consistently high. The fern may also appear in irrigation channels and seepage areas. It should be visible year-round but is most lush during the monsoon. A first confirmed local record would be a photograph showing the characteristic linear leaflets and ladder-like frond arrangement, with location details and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical Asia, from India and southern China through Southeast Asia to Australia.

In India: Distributed in the Western Ghats, Himalayas, northeastern India, and the Eastern Ghats; particularly common in moist mountainous regions and hill stations.

In Uttar Pradesh: Reported from the Himalayan foothill region; distribution in the plains is rare and localized.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Unlikely in lowland plains in wild habitats; possible only in specially maintained ponds, water gardens, or very moist, shaded artificial habitats.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum as Pteris vittata. The specific epithet vittata (Latin: "ribboned" or "striped") refers to the linear sori.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Pteridaceae, the brake fern family, order Polypodiales. The genus Pteris comprises about 250 species, mostly in tropical and subtropical regions.

Phylogenetic notes. Pteris vittata is one of the widespread Asian species of Pteris, adapted to moist tropical habitats.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in tropical Asia; not assessed for conservation status.
