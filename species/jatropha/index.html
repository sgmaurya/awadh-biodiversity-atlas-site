रतनजोत / Jatropha

Jatropha curcas L. — Euphorbiaceae

रतनजोत — एक विदेशी झाड़ी जो अब भारत के कई हिस्सों में रोपी जाती है। लाल-गुलाबी फूलों वाली, और गोल, सेम जैसे फलों वाली। इसके बीजों का तेल ईंधन (बायोडीजल) के लिए परीक्षण किया जा रहा है। लेकिन पूरा पौधा ज़हरीला है — बीज, तना, पत्ती सब में जहर है। इसे छूना हानिकारक नहीं है, पर खाना खतरनाक है। गाँव के किनारे बाड़ के रूप में लगाया जाता है।

Field tip: A shrub with large, hand-shaped lobed leaves and clusters of small red flowers. The green fruits split into three lobes when ripe. Do not touch the latex directly or ingest seeds. The smell of crushed leaves is distinctive.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: हाथ की उँगलियों जैसी बँटी पत्तियाँ, गुलाबी-लाल फूल, तीन भागों में बँटे हरे फल। पत्ती कड़वी, चिपचिपी लेटेक्स।

Stem and branches: Becoming woody with age; bark greyish-brown. The tissue is soft and contains a milky, acrid latex.

Leaves: Alternate, palmately lobed with 3–5 lobes, the lobes lanceolate and acute. Petioles are long, reddish or green. Leaves are deciduous in the dry season.

Flowers: Unisexual (male and female on the same plant). Petals are red, pink, or occasionally yellow. Flowers are small and arranged in terminal corymbs.

Fruit: A three-lobed (tricoccous) capsule, fleshy when green, becoming dry and hard as it ripens. Each lobe contains one seed.

Seed: Black, smooth, oily, with a white caruncle (seed appendage) at the hilum. The oil content is 27–40% by weight.

Toxins. The entire plant contains ricin, a highly toxic protein, present in highest concentration in the seeds. The latex contains phorbol esters, which are irritant to skin.

हिंदी: बड़ी, हाथ जैसी पत्तियाँ। तने में दूधिया रस। फल हरा, तीन में बँटा। बीज काला, तेल से भरा, ज़हरीला।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Jatropha is native to Mexico and Central America, but has been widely planted in tropical and subtropical regions as an oil crop. It tolerates poor soils and semi-arid conditions. The roots form symbiotic relationships with nitrogen-fixing bacteria, allowing the plant to improve soil fertility. The fruits eventually split open, scattering the hard seeds; some are taken by rodents and birds, though the toxins mean they rarely germinate after passing through an animal's digestive system. The latex irritates skin and eyes.

In India, jatropha has been promoted since the 2000s as a biofuel crop, though the economic viability remains uncertain.

हिंदी: मेक्सिको की झाड़ी, अब भारत में भी लगाई जाती है। खराब जमीन में भी उगती है। जड़ें मिट्टी को अच्छा करती हैं। फल पकने पर खुल जाता है, बीज फैल जाते हैं।

Seeds are recalcitrant and should be sown fresh. Seedlings grow rapidly. Plants may flower in the second year under good conditions. Lifespan 30–50 years.

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Various insects visit the flowers; pollination is not well documented.
  • Seed dispersal: Small mammals feed on fruit debris.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf spots and fungal diseases in humid seasons.
  • The plant's toxins provide some resistance to generalist pests.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Caution: Toxicity

The entire plant, especially the seeds, contains ricin and other toxic alkaloids. Seeds and leaves must not be eaten or used as a home remedy. Ingestion can cause vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, and in severe cases, death. Handling the latex can cause skin irritation and dermatitis; the latex is caustic to eyes. This is not a safe plant for a household with children.

Traditional and current uses:

  • Oil production. The seed oil has been used traditionally as a purgative (hence "Physic Nut") and as lamp oil. The oil is now being researched for biodiesel production.
  • Research focus. Jatropha is promoted in development projects as a biofuel crop, but the actual agronomic and economic viability in India remains under review. Several studies show that the crop is less profitable than initially promised and poses environmental risks if it spreads.

Today. Jatropha hedges are planted in some regions for boundary marking and as a windbreak. The seed oil is processed at a small scale for biodiesel experiments. However, large-scale cultivation programs have largely been abandoned in India.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Genetic improvement of yield, water-use efficiency, and oil quality is ongoing in agricultural research. Whether jatropha can be a sustainable biofuel is still an open question.

हिंदी: बीज और पत्ती ज़हरीली है — खाना घातक हो सकता है। पत्ती को पकड़ने से त्वचा खराब हो सकती है। बाड़ के लिए लगाई जाती है। तेल ईंधन के लिए परीक्षण हो रहा है, पर इसे सफल नहीं साबित हुआ है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to Mexico and Central America; now cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

In India: Planted in several states as a biofuel crop; not naturalized in the wild.

In Uttar Pradesh: Scattered cultivation in recent years; mostly confined to planted hedges.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Occasional in village gardens and field boundaries.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How much oil does a single shrub produce in a year, and what is the oil's quality? — Harvest fruits; extract oil; measure yield.
  2. Does jatropha spread from planted hedges into nearby fields, or does it stay within the hedge? — Map jatropha plants in and around villages over several years.
  3. How many insects are killed or repelled by the latex of jatropha compared to other shrubs? — Plant both jatropha and control shrubs; count insects.
  4. Can the seed oil be made safe for skin application by extraction and purification? — This should be done only by trained chemists with safety equipment.
  5. What happens to jatropha seeds if they are left under a hot sun for a month? — Collect seeds; store them; test for germination.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum, Vol. 2, p. 1006. The genus name Jatropha comes from Greek iatreia (medical treatment) and trophe (nutrition), referring to medicinal uses.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies currently recognised, though cultivation has produced local variants.

Family and order placement. Euphorbiaceae, the spurge family, in the order Malpighiales. The genus Jatropha holds about 170 species, mostly from Mexico and Africa.

Phylogenetic notes. Jatropha curcas is one of the most economically important species in the genus, though its native range is Mesoamerica, not Africa.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Globally widespread in cultivation; not assessed for wild populations.
