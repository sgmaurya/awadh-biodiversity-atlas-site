गंदम / Red-headed Bunting

Emberiza bruniceps J.F.Brandt, 1841 — Emberizidae

गंदम (रेड-हेडेड बंटिंग) — मध्य एशिया और कजाकिस्तान के शुष्क मैदानों से भारत के पश्चिमी और मध्य भागों में प्रवास करने वाला एक चमकदार और सामाजिक पक्षी है। यह अगस्त-अक्टूबर और मार्च-अप्रैल के दौरान गंगा के मैदानी इलाकों से होकर गुजरता है। उस समय यह बाजरा, ज्वार और गेहूं के खेतों, कटी हुई फसलों की डंठलों और बबूल की कंटीली झाड़ियों में बड़े-बड़े झुंडों में दाना चुगते हुए दिखाई देता है। नर पक्षी का सिर और गला तांबे जैसे लाल-भूरे रंग का और छाती व पेट चमकीले सुनहरे पीले रंग के होते हैं। इसे स्थानीय बोली में 'गंदम' (गेहूं जैसा रंग) भी कहा गया है।

Field tip: Look in ripening millet or wheat fields during migration. The adult male's fiery chestnut head contrasting with bright golden-yellow underparts is unmistakable; flocks rise together with rolling chirps to perch tightly along wires or thorn trees.

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1926 as Emberiza icterica):

MeasurementRange
Wing86–89 mm (Baker 1926)
Tail69–73 mm (Baker 1926)

The Red-headed Bunting is a sleek, long-winged bunting with a distinctly conical bill adapted for husking seeds. In breeding males, the forehead, crown, ear-coverts, chin, throat, and upper breast are rich coppery-chestnut or rufous-red. The mantle and back are yellowish-olive, prominently streaked with dark brown shaft stripes. The lower breast, belly, flanks, and undertail coverts are brilliant lemon-yellow.

In non-breeding males, the chestnut of the head is partially obscured by sandy-grey feather margins that wear off by spring. Females are much duller, with greyish-sandy upperparts finely streaked with dusky brown, a pale buffish-white throat, and dull pale yellow underparts.

The bill is stout and pale greyish-horn. The iris is dark hazel-brown. The legs and feet are fleshy-brown or yellowish-brown.

Vocalisations. The flight call is a soft, distinctive, rolling chirp or tweet transcribed as twit or chup, uttered continuously by flock members in flight. The song, delivered from an exposed perch on breeding grounds, is a short, cheerful metallic refrain.

इसकी मजबूत और नुकीली चोंच सख्त दानों को आसानी से फोड़ने के लिए बनी होती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Emberiza bruniceps is highly social throughout its annual cycle. During its transit across northern India, it travels in coordinated flocks ranging from a few dozen to several hundred individuals. The flocks forage intensively on the ground in open agricultural fields, stubble fields, and weed-choked fallows.

When alarmed, the entire flock lifts off simultaneously with a loud whirring of wings, wheeling together before perching in adjacent trees, hedges, or power lines. They roost communally at night in dense thorn thickets, reedbeds, or sugarcane brakes.

हिंदी:

किसी आहट पर पूरा झुंड एक साथ उड़कर पास के बबूल या बिजली के तारों पर बैठ जाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Baker (1926, p. 215–216) records that Emberiza icterica breeds mainly in May, with eggs taken from April to July, in Central Asia and neighbouring tracts, nesting in low bushes or weeds. In the cold weather it moves south into India, passing through northern and western plains into central districts before returning north in spring.

हिंदी:
बेकर (1926) के अनुसार यह मुख्य रूप से मई में (अप्रैल से जुलाई तक) मध्य एशिया के क्षेत्रों में झाड़ियों के बीच घोंसला बनाकर अंडे देती है। सर्दियों में यह प्रवास करके उत्तर और मध्य भारत के खुले मैदानों में आती है और वसंत में वापस लौट जाती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

The Red-headed Bunting is primarily granivorous outside the breeding season, feeding on a wide variety of cultivated cereal grains and wild seeds:

  • Seeds of Pennisetum glaucum (pearl millet / bajra) and Sorghum bicolor (jowar)
  • Grains of Triticum aestivum (wheat) gleaned from harvest stubble
  • Seeds of wild grasses such as Setaria, Echinochloa, and Digitaria
  • Weedy seeds of Chenopodium and Amaranthus
  • Small insects, beetles, and caterpillars taken opportunistically in late summer

Foraging occurs predominantly on the ground, where birds pick up fallen grains and systematically strip seeds from standing grass panicles.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The Red-headed Bunting feeds on cereal grains, wild grass seeds, and weedy seeds gleaned from open fields and harvest stubbles (Baker 1926, p. 215). It is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds from the Volga basin and southern Kazakhstan east to north-western China and south to northern Iran and Afghanistan. Winters extensively across central and western India, from Gujarat and Rajasthan across Maharashtra to the Deccan plateau.

Regional status: Passage migrant across Uttar Pradesh during post-monsoon and pre-breeding migratory movements.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Johann Friedrich von Brandt in 1841 in the Bulletin Scientifique publié par l'Académie Impériale des Sciences de Saint-Pétersbourg (vol. 9, no. 1, col. 14). The type locality is between the Caspian Sea and the Aral Sea. Treated as "Emberiza icterica" by Baker (1926).

Subspecies. Monotypic; no subspecies are currently recognized across its range.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Emberizidae (buntings).

Phylogenetic notes. Emberiza bruniceps forms a closely related species pair with the Black-headed Bunting (Emberiza melanocephala), with which it occasionally hybridizes along narrow zone of range overlap in northern Iran.

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC) by the IUCN Red List due to an extremely large range and a stable overall population size.
