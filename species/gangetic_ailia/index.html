गैंगेटिक आइलिया मछली / Gangetic Ailia

Ailia coila (Hamilton, 1822) — Schilbeidae

गैंगेटिक आइलिया मछली — बड़ी मैदानी नदियों, स्वच्छ धाराओं और गहरे तालों में पाई जाने वाली एक अत्यंत पतली, चपटी और कागज जैसी पारदर्शी देसी कैटफिश (काजोली/बांसपत्ता) है। इस मछली की सबसे अनूठी विशेषता यह है कि इसकी पीठ पर कांटेदार पंख (dorsal fin) बिल्कुल नहीं होता, केवल पीछे एक छोटा चर्बीदार पंख होता है। इसके मुँह पर आठ लंबी मूंछें होती हैं और इसके पेट के नीचे 58 से 75 किरणों वाला बहुत लंबा पंख होता है। यह खाने में बहुत स्वादिष्ट और कोमल मानी जाती है।

Field tip: Look in open, flowing waters of large rivers and major canals. A paper-thin, ribbon-like silvery-translucent catfish completely lacking a rayed dorsal fin, with eight long barbels and an exceptionally long anal fin along the belly, is the Gangetic Ailia.

हिंदी: यह कागज जैसी पतली, चपटी और पारदर्शी कैटफिश है। इसकी पीठ पर कोई मुख्य पंख नहीं होता। इसके मुंह पर आठ लंबी मूंछें और पेट के नीचे बहुत लंबा पंख होता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Adult description:

Ailia coila has a remarkably elongate, strongly compressed, and ribbon-like body (Hamilton 1822). The dorsal and ventral contours are nearly straight and parallel until tapering smoothly to the slender caudal peduncle (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The head is small, conical, and compressed, comprising about one-sixth of the standard length (Hamilton 1822). The snout is rounded and slightly overhangs the small subterminal mouth (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Teeth are villiform and arranged in narrow bands on the jaws and vomer (Jayaram 2006).

Eight slender barbels are present: two nasal barbels, two maxillary barbels, and four mandibular barbels (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). All eight barbels are remarkably elongate, each exceeding the length of the head, with the maxillary pair reaching backwards to the pectoral or pelvic fin origins (Hamilton 1822).

The rayed dorsal fin is completely absent, a unique anatomical trait distinguishing Ailia from all other Indian schilbeid genera (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). A small, delicate adipose fin is positioned near the posterior end of the back (Jayaram 2006). The pectoral fins are well developed, supported by a slender, sharp spine and 13–15 soft rays (Hamilton 1822). The pelvic fins are small. The anal fin is extraordinarily elongate, containing 58 to 75 branched rays, running along the posterior two-thirds of the ventral profile (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The caudal fin is deeply forked with equal pointed lobes.

Living specimens are glass-like, shimmering silvery with pale yellowish-green or brassy reflections along the back (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). The belly is pure gleaming white. The pectoral and caudal fins are pale translucent lemon-yellow, often tipped with dusky or blackish margins (Hamilton 1822).

हिंदी: इसका शरीर लगभग 30 सेमी तक लंबा, बहुत चपटा और चिकना होता है। पीठ पर कांटा या मुख्य पंख बिल्कुल नहीं होता, केवल पीछे एक छोटा चर्बी का पंख होता है। मुंह पर आठ लंबी मूंछें होती हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Gangetic Ailia is a pelagic, diurnal and crepuscular schooling catfish adapted to open, flowing waters (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). It frequents large perennial rivers, deep oxbow lakes (tals), and major canal systems with clear to moderately turbid water and sandy or muddy substrates.

Schools of dozens of individuals cruise actively in the middle and upper water layers (Jayaram 2006). The paper-thin, laterally compressed body offers minimal drag, allowing the fish to maintain station in flowing river currents with effortless, undulating movements (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Using their eight elongate sensory barbels trailing outward like antennae, they sweep the surrounding water to detect swimming micro-crustaceans, mosquito pupae, and insect larvae. When threatened, the school executes synchronized, rapid evasive maneuvers, flashing their silvery flanks before darting into deeper water (Jayaram 2006). They require well-oxygenated water and avoid stagnant, polluted pools (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

हिंदी: यह मछली नदियों के खुले और बहते पानी में बड़े झुंडों में तैरती है। अपनी आठ लंबी मूंछों की मदद से यह पानी में तैरते सूक्ष्म जीवों को खोजती है और साफ, बहते पानी में रहना पसंद करती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Seasonal phenology in northern Indian alluvial river systems across the agricultural year (Talwar & Jhingran 1991; Jayaram 2006):

Spawning takes place during the southwest monsoon from June to August (Talwar & Jhingran 1991). Increased current velocity and monsoonal flood pulses trigger spawning activity. Mating occurs in open, gently flowing shallows. Females release hundreds of small, non-adhesive eggs that drift in the water column before settling onto clean sand. The eggs hatch after a few days into delicate, transparent larvae that develop rapidly on microscopic rotifers and copepod nauplii (Jayaram 2006).

हिंदी: बरसात के समय जब नदियों में नया पानी बढ़ता है, यह रेतीले उथले पानी में अंडे देती है। कुछ दिनों में अंडों से बच्चे निकल आते हैं और वे तेजी से तैरते हुए बड़े होते हैं।

Diet and Feeding Behaviour आहार

Ailia coila is an active pelagic micro-carnivore and insectivore (Talwar & Jhingran 1991):

Natural diet components:

  • Planktonic micro-crustaceans, including cladocerans (Daphnia, Bosmina) and copepods (Jayaram 2006).
  • Aquatic insect larvae, including chironomid midges, caddisflies, and mayfly nymphs (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).
  • Mosquito wrigglers and pupae caught in the water column.
  • Small terrestrial insects trapped in the surface film.
  • Tiny fish fry and larvae seized opportunistically in midwater.
हिंदी: यह मांसाहारी मछली है जो पानी के प्लवक (zooplankton), कीड़े-मकोड़ों, मच्छरों के लार्वा और छोटी मछलियों के बच्चों को खाती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Documented natural predators in northern Indian alluvial freshwater systems:

  • Riverine predatory fishes: Catfishes like Wallago attu (Striped Catfish) and Bagarius bagarius (Goonch), and Channa striata (Snakehead Murrel) strike pelagic schools.
  • Piscivorous birds: River terns, cormorants, and little egrets prey on schools near the surface.
  • Freshwater garfish: Xenentodon cancila (Freshwater Garfish) pursues schools in open river shallows.
  • River otters and softshell turtles: Hunt schools in perennial river reaches.
हिंदी: बोआल, गूंज, सौर और सुइया मछली पानी के नीचे से इस पर हमला करती हैं। बगुले और पनकौवे इसे पानी से झपट लेते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

High commercial and food value: The Gangetic Ailia (commonly known as kajoli or banspata) is one of the most prized small indigenous food fishes in northern and eastern India. Captured in substantial quantities by commercial and artisanal fishers using cast nets, shore seines, and floating gill nets in the Ghaghara, Kuwano, and Rapti river basins. Its flesh is exceptionally delicate, white, sweet-tasting, and virtually boneless, commanding high market prices in urban and rural fish bazaars. It provides high-quality animal protein, vitamin A, calcium, and essential trace minerals to local diets.

Ecological river health indicator: Because Ailia coila requires flowing, well-oxygenated water and is vulnerable to severe hypoxic conditions and heavy chemical effluents, its presence signals good ecological integrity in riverine systems (Jayaram 2006).

Traditional: Considered a delicacy and an easily digestible fish for children and elderly individuals in traditional Awadhi households.

Today: Sold fresh in morning riverbank fish markets as a premier small catfish.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Assessing the impacts of seasonal barrage flow reductions on Ailia coila spawning migrations in the Ghaghara basin.

हिंदी: यह काजोली या बांसपत्ता के नाम से मशहूर बहुत कीमती और स्वादिष्ट देसी मछली है। इसका मांस बहुत कोमल और मीठा होता है। यह साफ पानी में ही रहती है और ग्रामीणों को उत्तम पोषण देती है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the northern Indian subcontinent, distributed across India, Pakistan, Nepal, and Bangladesh (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

In India: Found throughout the Indus, Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Mahanadi river basins, from foothill rivers to the plains (Menon 1999).

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded throughout the Ganges, Ghaghara, Rapti, and Gomti river networks and major canal systems.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a resident native species in the Ghaghara and Kuwano river channels and major oxbow wetlands.

हिंदी: यह भारत, पाकिस्तान, नेपाल और बांग्लादेश की प्रमुख नदियों में पाई जाती है। उत्तर प्रदेश में घाघरा और कुआनो नदी प्रणालियों में यह सामान्य रूप से उपस्थित है।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 as Malapterurus coila in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches, page 158. Type locality: Freshwater rivers of Bengal.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognized across its continental range.

Family and order placement. Order Siluriformes, family Schilbeidae (placed by some authorities in Ailiidae). The genus Ailia Gray, 1830, comprises dorsal-less pelagic Asian catfishes.

Phylogenetic notes. Ailia coila is the type species of the genus Ailia. It is phylogenetically distinguished within Schilbeidae by the total absence of the rayed dorsal fin and the retention of a small adipose fin (Talwar & Jhingran 1991).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the global IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. It maintains widespread populations in major river basins, though wild stocks face pressure from barrage construction and overharvesting.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Ailia coila है और यह Schilbeidae परिवार की सदस्य है। प्रकृति में इसका वास नदियों में स्थित है।
