नीली पट्टी मधुमक्खी / Blue-banded Bee

Amegilla Friese, 1897 — Apidae

नीली पट्टी मधुमक्खी — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खेतों, बागों और गली-मोहल्लों में फूलों पर उड़ने वाली एक बड़ी, शक्तिशाली, भूरे या काले रंग की मधुमक्खी, जिसके पेट पर नीली चमकीली पट्टियाँ होती हैं। ये अकेली (solitary) मधुमक्खियाँ हैं, घर नहीं बनातीं, और शहद नहीं इकट्ठा करतीं, लेकिन फूलों से परागण का काम बहुत तेजी से करती हैं। गर्मी की धूप में तेजी से उड़ती हैं और पंखों को तेजी से काँपाती हैं ताकि फूल से सारा पराग झड़ जाए। भारत के मैदानों में इसकी कई जातियाँ हैं, और सब को स्थानीय स्तर पर एक ही नाम से जाना जाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeMedium to large bee, body length 12-18 mm
ColourationBlack or dark brown body with 1-3 bright metallic blue bands on the abdomen
StripesBands are iridescent, bright cyan or turquoise blue, sharply contrasting with black
EyesLarge, dark compound eyes; no ocelli visible
BehaviourFast, direct flight; buzzes loudly while foraging on flowers
SolitaryNever in groups; each bee forages individually

Field tip: A sturdy bee buzzing rapidly on flowers, especially on warm sunny days. The bright blue bands on the black abdomen are unmistakable. Unlike honey bees, it does not crowd flowers; it visits alone, stays briefly, and moves on, shaking the flower vigorously.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बड़ी, काली मधुमक्खी, पेट पर नीली चमकीली पट्टियाँ। अकेली उड़ती है, भीड़ में नहीं। फूलों को जोरों से काँपाती है, बहुत जोर की गूँज सुनाई देती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for north Indian species):

MeasurementRange
Body length12-18 mm
Wingspan25-32 mm
Hind leg width3-4 mm

Body structure: The head is broad, with large dark compound eyes meeting at the top in males, widely separated in females. The thorax is robust and covered in long, branched hairs (setae) for pollen collection. The abdomen is somewhat flattened, bearing 1-3 brilliant iridescent blue bands (stripes) on a dark ground, with the intensity of blue varying by species. The legs are long and strong, the hind legs flattened into a scopal organ (pollen brush) for carrying pollen. Wings are translucent, with darker veins.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are smaller and have longer antennae; females are larger and stouter. The blue bands may differ between sexes in some species.

Species of the region. Multiple Amegilla species occur in north India, including Amegilla florea (Banded Bee), Amegilla zonata (Zoned Bee), Amegilla fasciata (Banded Carpenter Bee) and Amegilla patellata (Vihar Bee). Separation requires observation of exact band patterns, body size, and often dissection. This profile stops at the genus level.

हिंदी: सिर पर बड़ी आँखें, दोनों ओर की ओर मुड़ी हुई; शरीर पर लंबे बाल परागण के लिए; पेट पर नीली पट्टियाँ; पिछली टाँगें चौड़ी, परागकणों को सँजोने के लिए। उत्तर भारत में इसकी कई जातियाँ हैं — पहचान के लिए सूक्ष्मदर्शी चाहिए।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Fast foragers with a unique pollination technique.

  • Sonication: Amegilla bees use a specialized behaviour called "buzz pollination" (sonication). They hold onto a flower and vibrate their flight muscles at high frequency (up to 200 Hz), causing pollen to fall from the anthers. This is especially effective on flowers with tightly-sealed pollen (like tomato, brinjal and bell pepper).
  • Foraging pattern: Solitary, fast, energetic foragers that visit many flowers rapidly in search of nectar and pollen.
  • Nesting: Females are solitary, digging burrows in soft earth, in mud banks or under plant roots. Each female stocks her nest cell with a ball of pollen and lays a single egg.
  • Lifespan: Short-lived; adults live for a few weeks.
  • Territoriality: Males perch on flowers or vegetation and dart out to intercept passing females.
हिंदी: पंखों को इतना तेज काँपाते हैं कि परागकण टपकने लगते हैं — यह "buzz pollination" है। टमाटर और बैंगन जैसे फूलों पर यह सबसे असरदार है। अकेली मादा मिट्टी में बिल खोदती है, परागकणों की गेंद बनाती है, एक अंडा देती है, और चली जाती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Nectar and pollen sources:

  • Wide range of flowering plants: mustard flowers, vegetable flowers (brinjal, tomato, cucumber, pumpkin), wild composites, and roadside shrubs.
  • Particularly attracted to flowers with exposed nectar and abundant pollen.

Preferred flowers:

  • Radish, mustard, sesame, sunflower, cosmos, marigold, and wildflowers along field margins.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Robber flies (Asilidae): Aerial predators that pursue bees in flight.
  • Spiders: Jumping spiders and web-building spiders on flowers.
  • Wasps: Parasitoid wasps may lay eggs in burrows.
  • Honey bees: May rob pollen and nectar gathered by blue-banded bees.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Native crop pollinator — undervalued and uncommon in recognizion.

  • Vegetable pollination: Highly effective at pollinating brinjal, tomato, cucumber, pumpkin and other bee-dependent crops, especially in regions without managed honey bee hives.
  • Seed production: Critical for seed production in vegetable and oilseed crops where buzz pollination is required.
  • Non-migratory: Unlike imported honey bees, blue-banded bees stay local, providing stable, year-round pollination.

Today. Populations are under stress from pesticide use and habitat loss, yet remain widespread in agricultural areas where flowers bloom.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Comparing yield and fruit set in Basti vegetable gardens with and without blue-banded bee activity would quantify their agricultural value and justify habitat preservation.

हिंदी: बैंगन और टमाटर के फूलों पर हनीबी से भी अच्छी परागण। कीटनाशकों से हानि; परागी जानवरों के बीच सबसे अधिक नजरअंदाज़ किया जाता है।

To be expected on flowering crops and wayside flowers throughout Basti district from April to October, particularly during the summer months when flowers are abundant. Most active during warm sunny days. Look on flowers with dense pollen during morning and midday hours.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: The genus Amegilla is widespread across Africa, the Middle East, South and Southeast Asia, Australia and parts of the Pacific region. About 230 species recognized.

In India: Distributed throughout the country; particularly diverse and abundant in tropical and subtropical regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in agricultural areas and open habitats throughout the state, especially the Gangetic plain.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on flowering crops and wildflowers throughout the district during the warm season.

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. Which flowering plants do blue-banded bees visit most frequently in your garden? / आपके बाग़ में नीली पट्टी मधुमक्खी किस फूल पर सबसे ज़्यादा आती है? — Observe and record bee visits to each flower type for one hour.
  2. Can you find the nesting burrows of blue-banded bees in soft earth? / नरम मिट्टी में नीली पट्टी मधुमक्खी के बिल ढूंढ सकते हो? — Look on exposed earthen banks in May-June and mark any burrows.
  3. Do blue-banded bees visit tomato and brinjal flowers more or less than honey bees? — Compare visit rates to the same flowers.
  4. When do the new generation bees emerge from their burrows? — Mark a bank with bees and watch for new emergence holes.
  5. Can you hear the buzzing sound when a blue-banded bee is vibrating flowers? — Record the sound; compare it with a honey bee sound.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The genus was established by Friese in 1897 in Entomologische Zeitung, Stettin. The name derives from the Greek a (without) and megille (brush), a misnomer given the prominent pollen-collection brushes. This profile is at genus level; the species of north India remain incompletely documented.

Subspecies. Not applicable at genus level. Multiple species occur in northern India.

Family and order placement. Family Apidae, subfamily Apinae, tribe Anthophorini; order Hymenoptera (bees, wasps, ants).

Phylogenetic notes. Amegilla is part of the large apid tribe Anthophorini, which includes mining bees, carpenter bees and digger bees. The capacity for buzz pollination evolved multiple times independently in the family.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated at genus level. Individual species assessments are lacking for most Indian populations.
