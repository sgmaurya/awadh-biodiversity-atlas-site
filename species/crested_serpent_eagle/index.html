सर्प चील / Crested Serpent Eagle

Spilornis cheela (Latham, 1790) — Accipitridae

सर्प चील — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के साल के जंगलों, बाग-बगीचों और वृक्षों की घनी जगहों में पाई जाने वाली एक मध्यम आकार की चील, जो साँपों को अपना मुख्य भोजन बनाती है। इसके सिर पर एक सुंदर कलगी (crest) होती है, जो खतरे में खड़ी हो जाती है। इसके काले पंखों पर पीले रंग की धारियाँ और पीली आँखें इसे सरलता से पहचान देती हैं। यह धीरे-धीरे जंगल में उड़ता है, साँपों की तलाश में, और अपनी नुकीली पंजों से साँपों को पकड़ता है। इसकी विशेषता यह है कि यह छोटे साँपों (harmless snakes) को भी शिकार करता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

Field tip: A dark eagle with a distinctive erectile crest and bright yellow eyes, hunting low over woodlands. The yellow spots and barring on dark wings are diagnostic. Often seen perched in tall trees scanning the ground.

Size: 66–75 cm long; medium-sized eagle.

Plumage: Overall dark brown to dark slate-brown. Upperparts darker; underparts slightly paler with barring.

Head: Distinctive erectile crest of elongated feathers on crown. Dark brown with slight metallic sheen.

Eyes: Bright yellow-orange; very large and prominent; adapted for scanning ground for reptiles.

Wings: Dark brown with bright yellow spotting on primaries and secondaries; distinctive markings visible in flight and when perched.

Tail: Dark brown, crossed by yellow-buff bars; relatively long.

Cere and bill: Yellow-orange cere; robust, hooked black bill with yellow base. Adapted for tearing reptile flesh.

Legs and feet: Stout, bright yellow; powerful talons adapted for gripping reptiles.

Sexes: Slightly dimorphic; females larger than males.

Juveniles: Similar to adults but with more buffy underparts and less conspicuous eye-color.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Display call: Distinctive, prolonged pip-pip-pip-pip whistle, often given during flights in breeding territory.
  2. Contact call: Softer, repeated chi-chi-chi.
  3. Alarm call: Sharp kee-kee-kee when disturbed.
हिंदी: "पिप-पिप-पिप" आवाज़ करता है। गुस्से में "की-की-की" करता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Specialised serpent hunter: The Crested Serpent Eagle is highly specialised for hunting reptiles, particularly snakes. Primary prey includes:

  • Cobras, common kraits, rat snakes, vine snakes
  • Lizards (occasional)
  • Frogs and other amphibians (occasional)

Hunting method: Hunts by soaring low over woodlands, forests, and cultivation. Scans ground and tree canopy for reptiles. Pounces with powerful talon grip. Can carry snakes up to 1 m long.

Feeding: Often perches on high branches while eating prey, dismembering carefully.

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs. Maintain territories through year.

Flight: Relatively slow, flappy wing-beats; soars occasionally on thermals.

Roost: Roost in tall trees, often same perch night after night.

हिंदी: साँपों का शिकार करता है। पेड़ों में बैठकर जमीन को देखता है। धीरे-धीरे उड़ता है। अकेले या जोड़े में।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Year-round resident: Breeds in the region; no seasonal migration.

Breeding: March–July; peaks in May–June in Uttar Pradesh. Monogamous pairs.

Nest: Large stick nest built high in tall trees (sal, mango, neem); reused and enlarged annually.

Clutch: 1–2 eggs (usually 1); incubation 35–40 days.

Fledging: Single young leaves nest at 50–60 days. No second brood.

Territoriality: Pairs maintain territories year-round; display with characteristic whistled calls.

हिंदी: साल भर रहता है। मई-जून में अंडे देता है। एक ही अंडा देता है। 2 महीने में बच्चा उड़ जाता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Large eagles (unlikely; top predator), martial eagles (rare), flying dragons (may harass at nest)
  • At nest: Large snakes (paradoxically), crows, eagles
  • Mammalian predators: Unlikely due to size and habitat
  • Parasites: Avian parasites, blood parasites, intestinal parasites
हिंदी: शायद ही कोई शिकारी है। बड़ी चील इसका दुश्मन हो सकती है। कौवे इसके घोंसलों में परेशानी पैदा कर सकते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Snake control: Feeds on snakes, including venomous species. Provides incidental benefit by controlling snake populations near human habitation. However, killed venomous snakes are rarely a threat to humans compared to direct human activity (habitat destruction).

Persecution: Historically hunted due to misunderstanding; some persecution continues in areas where large snakes are feared.

Cultural significance: Featured in traditional stories and beliefs. Its hunting prowess is noted in folklore.

Habitat indicator: Presence indicates healthy woodland ecosystem with diverse reptile fauna.

हिंदी: यह चील वन्यजीव अधिनियम 1972 की अंतर्गत पूरी तरह संरक्षित है। साँपों को खाता है, इसलिए जंगल के संतुलन के लिए महत्वपूर्ण है। इसका शिकार करना या परेशान करना कानूनी रूप से गंभीर अपराध है।

Range: Expected in sal forests, wooded villages, and areas with tall trees throughout Basti–Ayodhya region. Requires suitable reptile populations and perching habitat.

Habitat preference: Tall, leafy trees (sal, mango, banyan) for both hunting and nesting. Open or semi-open areas for hunting, with nearby dense woodland for refuge.

Seasonality: Resident year-round; locally nomadic following reptile activity and breeding cycles.

Published records: GBIF holds published records from the Gangetic plains. Occurs in wetland surveys and forest bird surveys.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph clearly showing the bird's distinctive crest, yellow eyes, and wing markings, with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia from foothills to peninsular India. Multiple subspecies across range.

In India: Distributed throughout forested and wooded areas of India; more common in peninsular region.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident in northern and eastern districts with suitable sal forest and woodlands.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular resident in sal forests and wooded areas; locally distributed in areas with suitable reptile populations.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by John Latham in 1790 in Index Ornithologicus, vol. 1, p. 1, as Falco cheela. Type locality is India.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognised across the range:

  • S. c. cheela (Latham, 1790) — Indian subcontinent (occurs in UP)
  • S. c. asiaticus — Southeast Asia

Populations in Uttar Pradesh belong to S. c. cheela.

Family and order placement. Order Accipitriformes; family Accipitridae (eagles, hawks, vultures). The genus Spilornis contains the serpent eagles of Asia, specialised for reptile hunting.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to other crested serpent eagles across Asia. Represents a highly specialised hunting ecology within the eagle family.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations remain relatively widespread despite some habitat loss. Protected under Indian law, though persecution persists in some areas due to fear of snakes.
