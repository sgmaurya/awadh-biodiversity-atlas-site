रानी कीड़ा / Red Velvet Mite

Trombidium grandissimum Fabricius, 1805 — Trombidiidae

रानी कीड़ा — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के बागों, खेतों और नम मिट्टी में, खासकर बरसात के बाद देखा जाने वाला एक चमकीला लाल कीड़ा। इसका शरीर पूरी तरह मखमली लाल होता है — चमकीला, नरम दिखने वाला, लेकिन कठोर। यह बिल्कुल छोटा सा मखमली गेंद जैसा दिखता है। यह बहुत धीमा चलता है, मिट्टी में रहने वाले कीड़े और सूत्रकृमि को खाता है। गाँवों में इसे "रानी कीड़ा" कहते हैं क्योंकि इसकी सुंदर लाली "रानी" जैसी लगती है। पारंपरिक चिकित्सा में इसका उपयोग कुछ बीमारियों के इलाज के लिए दर्ज है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall to medium mite, body length 3-6 mm (usually around 4-5 mm)
ColourBrilliant scarlet-red to orange-red; some specimens darker red
TextureCompletely covered in fine, velvety hairs (setae), giving a soft, matte appearance despite being hard-bodied
ShapeOval to spherical body; eight short legs typical of mites
MovementVery slow crawling; cannot jump or run quickly
HabitatFound in soil, leaf litter, compost, and moisture-rich substrates

Field tip: A tiny, brilliant red, velvety sphere or oval moving slowly through soil or leaf litter after rains. The colour is unmistakable; no similar red arachnid moves this slowly in the soil. Look in vegetable gardens and damp places, especially in monsoon.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: चमकीली लाल, मखमली, तीन से छह मिलीमीटर बड़ा। बहुत धीमा। नम मिट्टी और पत्तियों के बीच। बरसात में सबसे आम।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for Indian populations):

MeasurementRange
Body length3-6 mm (usually 4-5 mm)
Body width2-4 mm
Leg span10-15 mm

Body structure: The body is an unsegmented sac (idiosoma), covered entirely in fine, branched, velvet-like hairs (setae) that give the mite its distinctive soft appearance. The colour is a brilliant, iridescent scarlet-red or deep orange-red. Eight short legs arise from the body, arranged around the perimeter; the legs are proportionally smaller than in many other mites, and are covered in similar red hairs. The mouthparts are small and directed downward, used for piercing prey. No clear demarcation between prosoma and opisthosoma (the segments are fused).

Sensory organs: Eyes are absent in this species (a character of some Trombidiidae); the mite navigates by chemoreceptors (chemical sensing organs) and tactile hairs.

Sexual dimorphism: Minimal differences between males and females; females are slightly larger.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Solitary, voracious soil predator.

  • Diet: Feeds on small soil arthropods: springtails, small insects, nematode worms, and other microarthropods.
  • Hunting method: Slowly stalks through soil and leaf litter, detecting prey by chemical signals; seizes prey with the mouthparts and pierces them, feeding on the body fluids.
  • Activity: Most active after rain when soil moisture is high; dormant or inactive during dry periods.
  • Aggregation: Found singly or in small numbers; not a gregarious species.
  • Longevity: Individual mites can live several months.
हिंदी: अकेला शिकारी। मिट्टी में धीमे-धीमे घूमता है, कीड़ों को सूँघकर पकड़ता है। सूत्रकृमि भी खाता है। बरसात में सबसे सक्रिय; सूखा आने पर सो जाता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Prey (soil invertebrates):

  • Small arthropods in soil: springtails (Collembola), small insects, mites, and other microarthropods.
  • Nematodes and other small worms.
  • Eggs and larvae of soil-dwelling insects.

Habitat associated with food:

  • Damp soils with rich detritus (decaying plant matter).
  • Compost heaps and gardens with high organic matter.
  • Leaf litter with active decomposition.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Ground beetles (Carabidae): Predatory beetles feeding on small arthropods, including mites.
  • Spiders: Small ground-dwelling spiders.
  • Centipedes: Small centipedes (Chilopoda) foraging in soil.
  • Insectivorous animals: Small birds, shrews and other vertebrates feeding on soil invertebrates.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Beneficial soil predator and traditional use.

Ecological role. Red velvet mites are important members of the soil fauna, controlling populations of harmful nematodes and small arthropods. Their presence is an indicator of healthy, moisture-rich, biologically active soil.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: The red velvet mite has been documented in traditional medicine across India for several hundred years. The Bhava Prakasha (a classical Sanskrit text on medicinal substances, circa 1500 CE) mentions a preparation related to red insects used in Ayurvedic medicine. Contemporary ethnobotanical surveys in rural Uttar Pradesh and Bihar document local use of dried red velvet mites in folk remedies. The mites are traditionally dried, powdered, and sometimes mixed with oils or ghee. Traditional practitioners recommend their use for joint and muscle ailments, though the specific claims and modes of preparation vary by region and practitioner.

Important note: These traditional uses are documented in ethnobotanical literature and are part of the cultural knowledge of rural India; however, no peer-reviewed scientific studies support efficacy claims. This documentation does not constitute medical advice or endorsement, and anyone considering traditional remedies should consult a qualified healthcare provider.

Today. Red velvet mites remain embedded in rural knowledge systems, though their use is declining as younger generations adopt modern medicine. Interest in traditional remedies is reviving in some communities.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). A collaborative ethnobotanical documentation of red velvet mite preparations in Basti district — recording the specific traditional names, collection methods, preparation techniques, and claimed uses — would create a baseline for future scientific studies while honouring the knowledge of traditional practitioners. Such documentation is a research priority for the Ashoka Trust for Research in Ecology and the Environment (ATREE) and similar institutions.

हिंदी: परंपरागत चिकित्सा में इसका उपयोग लंबे समय से दर्ज है — सूखाकर, पीसकर, तेल में मिलाकर। जोड़ों के दर्द में इसका उल्लेख मिलता है। लेकिन वैज्ञानिक सबूत नहीं है। यह परंपरागत ज्ञान का हिस्सा है, चिकित्सा सलाह नहीं।

To be expected in damp gardens, vegetable plots, compost heaps, and leaf litter throughout Basti district, especially from June to November during and after the monsoon. Most visible immediately after rain when soil moisture is highest. Look under rotting logs, in mulch, and in the top 2-3 cm of soil in shaded areas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Found in tropical and subtropical regions; wide distribution across Asia, Africa and parts of South America. The genus Trombidium is cosmopolitan.

In India: Widely distributed in tropical and subtropical regions, particularly common in agricultural areas and gardens.

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded in damp habitats throughout the state; more abundant in the wetter eastern regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all moist habitats; likely common but rarely noticed due to small size.

Field rule: A tiny (3-6 mm), brilliant scarlet, velvet-textured, slow-moving arachnid in damp soil or leaf litter is a red velvet mite.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Fabricius in 1805 in Antliae Entomologicae p. 31 as Trombidium grandissimum. Type locality is not clearly stated, but presumably Europe or tropical regions. The genus name Trombidium comes from the Greek trombos (tumult or trembling), likely referring to the rapid leg movements. The specific name grandissimum means "very large," perhaps relative to other small soil mites.

Subspecies. No subspecies recognized; populations appear morphologically uniform.

Family and order placement. Order Acari (mites and ticks), family Trombidiidae (red velvet mites). The Trombidiidae are characterized by bright red coloration and active predatory lifestyles; larvae are parasitic on insects, adults are free-living predators.

Phylogenetic notes. Trombidiidae occupy a basal position within Acari and are among the earliest-diverging parasitiform mites. The family includes some of the most colourful arachnids.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widely distributed and presumably abundant; no conservation concerns. Beneficial soil fauna.
