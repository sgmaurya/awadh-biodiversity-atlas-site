कॉफ़ी बी हॉकमॉथ / Coffee Bee Hawkmoth

Cephonodes hylas (Linnaeus, 1771) — Sphingidae

कॉफ़ी बी हॉकमॉथ — यह सबसे छोटी हॉकमॉथ है, बस मधुमक्खी के जैसी दिखती है। इसके पंख काले-भूरे, पारदर्शी, पंखों की नोक और पूँछ काली होती हैं। दिन के समय फूलों पर मँडराती है, हूँ-हूँ करती है। पतंगा हालांकि रात में सक्रिय होती है, पर अक्सर दिन में भी देखी जाती है। कैटरपिलर हरा होता है। इसे पहचानना आसान है क्योंकि यह दिन के समय फूलों पर काम करती है, जैसे मधुमक्खी। भारत के सभी बागों में आने वाली सुंदर पतंगा है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmallest hawkmoth; wingspan 35–45 mm; resembles a bumblebee
ColourBlack or dark brown body; wings pale at base, dark at tips; abdomen with yellow or orange bands
WingsNarrow; base pale or whitish; apex (tip) dark brown or black; transparent areas visible
BodyBee-like in shape; wings held differently from other hawkmoths; fuzzy appearance
ProboscisLong, thin, coiled when at rest
FlightHovering at flowers; active in daylight as well as evening

Field tip: The smallest hawkmoth, easily mistaken for a large bumblebee because of its compact body, bee-like shape, and daytime flower-visiting behavior. Look for the characteristic dark wing tips and pale base; the moth hovers at flowers and produces an audible humming sound.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बहुत छोटी, मधुमक्खी जैसी दिखने वाली हॉकमॉथ। पंखों की नोक काली, बाकी पारदर्शी। दिन के समय फूलों पर होवर करती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for Indian populations):

MeasurementRange
Body length18–22 mm
Wingspan35–45 mm
Forewing length16–20 mm
Proboscis length20–25 mm

Wings and body:

  • Forewing: Pale buff or whitish at the base, becoming progressively darker towards the apex (wing tip), which is dark brown or black. The pattern creates a gradient effect.
  • Hindwing: Pale or whitish, sometimes with dusky tips.
  • Body: Compact, bee-like, dark brown or black, often with yellow or red-orange bands on the abdomen, giving it a distinctive wasp-like or bee-like appearance. The thorax is densely scaled, appearing fuzzy.

Proboscis: Long, thin, coiled tube, 20–25 mm, which unrolls to feed from flowers. When at rest, coiled tightly under the head.

Sexual dimorphism: Minimal; males and females are similar in appearance, though males may have slightly longer antennae.

Adaptations: Bee-like fuzzy body; daytime activity; strong hovering capabilities.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Coffee (Coffea arabica, C. canephora)
  • Gardenia (Gardenia spp.)
  • Ixora (Ixora spp.)
  • Related Rubiaceae species
  • Various ornamental shrubs

Adult nectar sources:

  • Coffee flowers
  • Gardenia, jasmine, datura
  • Various cultivated ornamental flowers
  • Both daytime and night-blooming flowers

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Spiders: Web-building spiders may catch hovering adults.
  • Parasitoid wasps: Small wasps parasitize caterpillars.
  • Predatory insects: Ground beetles and other predators feed on larvae.
  • Birds: Insectivorous birds may catch adults, though the bee-like appearance may provide some protection.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minor pest status: Caterpillars may feed on coffee plants and ornamental shrubs, but economic damage is usually minimal. The species is more important as a pollinator.

Pollination: Adult moths pollinate coffee flowers and other plants while feeding. Coffee pollination by insects is important in some regions.

Ornamental interest: Highly valued by naturalists and enthusiasts for its unusual daytime activity and bee-like appearance. Often photographed and observed in gardens.

Today. Common in gardens, coffee plantations, and ornamental areas wherever suitable host plants are present.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Daytime vs. nighttime activity patterns; pollination ecology of coffee flowers; host-plant specificity and larval performance; population dynamics in eastern Uttar Pradesh.

हिंदी: कॉफ़ी के फूलों को परागित करता है। दिन के समय फूलों पर काम करता है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Expected in gardens and ornamental plantings throughout Basti district where suitable host plants such as Gardenia and Ixora are present. The moth is active both in daylight and at dusk during warm months (April–November). It hovers at flowers with an audible humming sound and is likely to be encountered in ornamental gardens and parks.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across tropical Asia, including India, Myanmar, Thailand, Indonesia, and extending to Japan and the Pacific Islands.

In India: Distributed throughout the country, particularly in gardens and plantations with suitable host plants.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in gardens and cultivated areas with coffee, gardenia, or related plants, though not as widespread as in coffee-growing regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in ornamental gardens and areas with suitable host plants.

  2. Which flowers are most frequently visited for nectar: coffee, gardenia, or ixora? — Grow different plants; record visitation rates.

  4. What is the larval host-plant preference when multiple suitable plants are available? — Grow different host plants together; count eggs and larvae.
  5. Can the sound frequency of wing-beating be used to identify this species in the field? — Record audio of hovering moths; compare frequencies.

Field rule: The smallest hawkmoth; compact, bee-like body; dark-tipped pale wings; hovers at flowers with an audible hum; active in daylight; found on coffee, gardenia, or ixora plants — the Coffee Bee Hawkmoth.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1771 in Systema Naturae, 12th edition. Type locality is Asia (India). The genus name Cephonodes comes from Greek, meaning "head bearer" or "head marked"; the specific epithet hylas refers to a figure in Greek mythology.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised, though geographic variation occurs.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Sphingidae. Cephonodes hylas represents the smallest and most specialized nectar-feeding member of the Old World sphingids.

Phylogenetic notes. Cephonodes is a monotypic genus within the Sphingidae, with no close living relatives, representing a unique evolutionary specialization for small-bodied hovering at flowers.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed. Abundant and widespread; no conservation concerns.
