ग्रीन सैंडपाइपर / Green Sandpiper

Tringa ochropus (Linnaeus, 1758) — Scolopacidae

ग्रीन सैंडपाइपर — यह छोटी वाडर चिड़िया है जो उत्तरी यूरेशिया से सर्दियों में भारत आती है। इसकी पीठ जैतूनी-भूरी है और पेट सफेद है — जिससे यह दूसरी शैंकों से अलग दिखती है। छोटे पोखरों, सड़क के किनारे के नालों और पानी की धाराओं में अकेले या छोटे समूहों में दिखाई देती है। उड़ने के समय इसके अंडरविंग काले होते हैं।

Field tip: A small dark-backed wader with white belly and dark underwings visible in flight, usually found at small water bodies — is a Green Sandpiper. The dark back and dark underwing (visible in flight) are diagnostic. Solitary or in small groups.

Size: 21–24 cm long; wingspan 40–48 cm. Weight 50–85 g. A small wader, distinctly smaller than Redshank or Greenshank.

Plumage: Distinctive. Upperparts dark olive-brown or greenish-brown, heavily spotted with white. Head and neck buff-brown with streaking. Breast buff-white with dark spotting; belly white. Rump white but with dark barring on sides (diagnostic). Upper tail coverts show strong barring.

Legs and feet: Dark greenish or blackish; relatively short compared to larger shanks.

Bill: Dark with slight pale base; straight, thin, and pointed.

Wings: Upper wing coverts dark. Flight feathers dark. Underwing dark — this is a key diagnostic feature visible in flight.

Sexes: Females slightly larger; little sexual dimorphism.

Vocalisations. Two main call types:

  1. Flight call: A sharp, loud tlewit or chee-wee, given when flushed.
  2. Alarm call: Sharp chip calls given on ground.
हिंदी: तीव्र सीटी जैसी आवाज़। उड़ान में जोर की कूकना।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Solitary forager: Green Sandpipers are typically solitary or in very small groups, unlike the gregarious Redshanks. Feed at the edges of small water bodies.

Feeding: Primarily insectivorous:

  • Aquatic insect larvae (chironomids, mayfly nymphs)
  • Small aquatic invertebrates
  • Occasionally small fish and crustaceans

Feeding behaviour: Methodical, picking from water surface or probing in soft mud. Also glean on exposed banks.

Habitat preference: Prefers small, shallow water bodies with muddy margins — village tanks, irrigation ditches, small reservoirs, stream edges. Less common on large river systems than other shanks.

Flight behaviour: When flushed, often gives loud alarm calls and flies in characteristic erratic pattern, showing dark underwings.

Alert: Very wary; first to flush from small ponds.

हिंदी: छोटे पोखरों में अकेले रहते हैं। डर से तेज़ कूकना करते हुए उड़ते हैं। काले पंख दिखाई देते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP from September, with main arrival October. Departs February–April, with most birds gone by April.

Breeding range: Boreal regions of northern Eurasia from Scandinavia to Siberia. Breeds April–August. Unusual among Tringa species in that it nests in abandoned tree nests (old thrush or curlew nests) rather than on the ground.

Migration: Long-distance migrant. Diurnal and nocturnal migration.

Wintering: Solitary on individual small water bodies. Often shows site fidelity, returning to same water body year after year.

Moult: Complete moult occurs before arrival; breeding plumage acquired on wintering grounds.

Timing in UP: Early arrival September; main arrival October–November; peak December–February; departure February–April.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आना शुरू करते हैं, अप्रैल तक चले जाते हैं। छोटे पोखरों पर अकेले रहते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Listed under Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Ecological value: Important insectivore at small water bodies; helps control aquatic insects including mosquito larvae.

Wetland health indicator: Species present at healthy small wetlands with good water quality.

Cultural significance: Less well-known than larger shanks; noted in bird survey literature.

The Green Sandpiper is a winter visitor to Ayodhya district. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022) recorded this species in their survey of unprotected wetlands of Ayodhya district, including sites like Samda Jheel and Sirsa Jheel. The species is expected at small water bodies throughout eastern UP — village tanks, irrigation channels, and stream margins. Unlike Common Redshank, Green Sandpiper is typically solitary or in pairs and prefers smaller water bodies rather than large rivers. Peak occurrence should be October–March. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in northern Eurasia from Scandinavia to Siberia; winters from Mediterranean region to southern Africa and Asia.

In India: Widespread winter visitor, particularly on wetlands and river systems; more common in northern and central India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Regular winter visitor throughout the state, particularly common in eastern and central districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to small water bodies and wetland margins. Expected September–April, with peak occurrence October–March. Less common than larger shanks but regularly found at suitable habitats.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae (10th edition), page 145, as Scolopax ochropus. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. Monotypic — T. ochropus is the only recognised subspecies.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Scolopacidae. Genus Tringa contains eight species of shanks and teals.

Phylogenetic notes. Tringa ochropus is sister to T. glareola (Wood Sandpiper), forming a clade distinct from larger Tringa species. It is the only Tringa species that nests in trees rather than on the ground.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. The global population is large (around 200,000–300,000 individuals) and stable. The species is adaptable to various small wetland types, including human-made habitats like irrigation channels.
