सालविनिया / Giant Salvinia

Salvinia molesta D.S.Mitchell — Salviniaceae

सालविनिया — अफ्रीका की एक छोटी तितली जैसी जलीय फर्न जो तालाब की सतह पर तैरती है, लेकिन दक्षिण अमेरिका के मूल सालविनिया की चचेरी बहन है, इसका विशाल रूप। 1950 के दशक में अफ्रीका के मेनू डैम में यह पहली बार दिखी और तीन साल में पूरे डैम को ढक गई — "करीबा वीड" नाम पड़ा। अब दुनिया की सबसे हानिकारक जलीय खरपतवारों में से है। भारत में 2000 के दशक से दिखने लगी है।

Field tip: Look for floating oval fronds in pairs, bright green above, fuzzy to the touch, with distinctive feathery brown structures underneath. Unlike water hyacinth (which has swollen stalks) or water lettuce (which is velvety). Forms dense, choking mats in still water.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: पानी की सतह पर तितली जैसी, जोड़ी-जोड़ी में पत्तियाँ, हरी ऊपर से, नीचे से भूरी-लाल, बालों जैसी। तेज़ी से बढ़ता है, गाढ़ी चादर बन जाता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (invasive populations):

MeasurementRange
Frond length (upper)2–5 cm, occasionally to 8 cm

Mat thickness5–15 cm
Doubling time12–16 days at 25–30 °C

Structure: Salvinia molesta is a true fern (Pteridophyte), not an angiosperm. It lacks true roots. Instead, one of the three leaves in each whorl is modified into submerged, hair-like segments that function as roots and anchor the floating fronds.

Upper fronds: Bright green, sessile, oval or obovate, 2–5 cm long, with distinctive papillae (small bumps) on the surface giving a finely textured appearance. This is the key diagnostic feature distinguishing it from S. auriculata (smooth fronds).

Lower fronds: Highly divided and branched, dark brown or rust-coloured, feathery, 4–10 cm long. Function as anchors and nutrient absorbers.

Reproduction: Asexual, by fragmentation of stolons. The species is sterile (triploid); sexual reproduction does not occur in invasive populations.

हिंदी: फर्न है, जड़ें नहीं हैं। ऊपरी पत्तियाँ हरी, खुरदुरी; निचली पत्तियाँ भूरी, बालों जैसी। टूटकर फैलता है, अंडे नहीं बनाता।

Ecological Impact पारिस्थितिक प्रभाव

The damage cascade in Salvinia molesta infestations:

  1. Rapid mat formation: Under optimal conditions (20–30 °C, still water with nutrients), a single plant can produce 1,000+ descendants in 3 months. Mats become impenetrable.
  1. Light blocking: Dense mats block photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) from reaching submerged vegetation. Subaquatic plants die; photosynthesis ceases below the mat.
  1. Oxygen depletion: Decaying biomass below the mat consumes dissolved oxygen. Fish kills follow, particularly of rohu (Labeo rohita), catla (Catla catla) and air-breathing catfish.
  1. Displacement of native species: Lotus (Nelumbo nucifera), water lily (Nymphaea), Nymphoides and sedges retreat to mat edges or disappear entirely.
  1. Waterbird habitat loss: Open-water species like herons, egrets, ducks and cranes lose wading and foraging habitat. The mat is too thin to walk on and too dense to wade through.
  1. Obstruction of waterways: Clogs irrigation channels, reduces water flow, blocks boat and net movement in rivers and larger water bodies.
  1. Mosquito breeding: Still, oxygen-poor water under mats is ideal for Culex larvae, increasing malaria and dengue risk — ironically, despite being introduced for mosquito control in some regions.

What it does not fully destroy: The dense mat does provide some temporary habitat for small fish fry and aquatic invertebrates hiding from predators, but this is outweighed by overall habitat loss.

Comparison to water hyacinth: Both are invasive and form dense mats, but Salvinia molesta grows faster and is lighter, forming looser but more rapidly expanding mats. It is harder to control mechanically because it fragments easily.

हिंदी: मानसून में विस्फोट — तीन महीने में हज़ार गुना बढ़ता है। प्रकाश रोकता है → जलीय पौधे मरते हैं → ऑक्सीजन खत्म होती है → मछलियाँ मरती हैं। कमल गायब हो जाता है। नावें नहीं चल सकतीं। जलकुंभी से तेज़ बढ़ता है और हल्का होता है।

Salvinia molesta is asexual. The invasive population spreads entirely by vegetative fragmentation. A single frond can regenerate into a new colony.

हिंदी: अप्रैल से शुरुआत; मानसून में भीषण विस्फोट; अक्टूबर तक चरम; सर्दी में धीमा पर नहीं मरता।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Problems:

  • Clogs irrigation canals and reduces water flow for agriculture
  • Reduces fish production in aquaculture ponds and in natural water bodies used for fishing
  • Blocks boat and net movement, restricting access to fishing and water transport
  • Creates anoxic conditions, killing valuable fish species
  • Potential for rapid spread through fragmentation (pieces carried on machinery, animals, clothing)

Control methods:

  • Manual removal: Labor-intensive; must be repeated; effective only for small, isolated infestations
  • Biological control: The weevil Cyrtobagous salviniae (introduced from South America) is highly host-specific and effective; available through biocontrol programmes in India
  • Chemical control: Herbicides (glyphosate, diquat); effective but non-target effects and cost limit use in village ponds
  • Prevention: Screening of water intakes; rapid quarantine and removal of fronds found in new locations

Uses (limited):

  • In some regions, dried Salvinia has been tested for compost, but decomposition is slow and nutrient value is low
  • Potential in biofilters for wastewater, but not established in India
हिंदी: सिंचाई नहर रोकता है, मछली उत्पादन गिराता है। यांत्रिक हटाना मेहनत-भरा, बार-बार करना पड़ता है। विशेष घुन (Cyrtobagous) से जैविक नियंत्रण संभव है — भारत में उपलब्ध है।

अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Salvinia molesta has not yet been widely documented in Basti district but is expected in still waters — ponds, tanks, irrigation canals and slow-moving sections of rivers — if it has arrived, which it may have. Warm-season growth is most rapid. A single frond with the characteristic papillose texture and feathery lower fronds is sufficient for a confirmed record; a photograph with date and location should be reported immediately to the atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to southeastern Brazil; first recorded in Africa (Kariba Dam, Zambia) in 1952; now invasive in tropical and subtropical wetlands across Africa, Asia, Pacific and Australia.

In India: First recorded in Kerala (2000); since 2005–2010, reported from Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal and Uttar Pradesh. Range expanding.

In Uttar Pradesh: Status uncertain; reports suggest presence in eastern and central UP, but extent not fully mapped.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in still waters; may have arrived but is not yet widely recognised.

Field rule: Floating, oval, vivid green fronds in pairs with a fuzzy texture and feathery brown structures underneath = giant salvinia. Frond papillae and exponential growth are key.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. First described scientifically by Mitchell (1972) from Kariba Dam material as Salvinia molesta, distinguished from the cultivated Salvinia auriculata by frond morphology (papillae) and growth rate.

Subspecies. Salvinia molesta is a sterile triploid derivative of an unknown diploid progenitor, likely an accidental hybrid. It reproduces only asexually.

Family and order placement. Salviniaceae, order Salviniales. A monogeneric family of floating ferns, containing about 10 species, most native to the Neotropics and Africa.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular analyses suggest Salvinia molesta originated from hybridization within the Salvinia auriculata complex in southern Brazil. The sterility and explosive growth of the invasive form suggest hybrid vigour and release from sexual reproduction constraints.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed, but recognised as one of the world's 100 worst invasive species by the IUCN Invasive Species Specialist Group.
