ऐशी ड्रोंगो / Ashy Drongo

Dicrurus leucophaeus (Vieillot, 1817) — Dicruridae

ऐशी ड्रोंगो — सर्दी के मौसम में दक्षिण से आने वाली लंबी दो-फाँक पूँछ वाली धूसर-काली चिड़िया, जो खेतों के किनारे, बागों और गाँवों में दिखती है। यह उड़ते कीटों को हवा में पकड़ती है और हिम्मत से बड़े पक्षियों को भी भगा देती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

Field tip: Paler grey overall than Black Drongo, with a long forked tail. Winter visitor appearing in northern Indian forests and agricultural areas. Look for it on overhead wires and branch tips, especially in mixed deciduous and scrub forest habitat from September to April.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें:
काली कोतवाल की तुलना में हल्का-धूसरा। पूँछ लंबी और दो-फाँक। सर्दियों में तार और डालों पर बैठता है। उड़ते कीटों को हवा में पकड़ता है।

Size: 25–28 cm long; wingspan 35–40 cm. Lighter and smaller than Black Drongo.

Plumage: Overall ash-grey; head and back grey; underparts paler grey-white with white on throat and belly. Wings darker grey-black. Undertail-coverts white. Juveniles duller grey.

Tail: Long, deeply forked; outer feathers extend considerably beyond central pair; dark grey feathers.

Bill: Black, straight, pointed, slightly hooked at tip; bristles around base to aid in catching small insects.

Legs and feet: Black, relatively short.

Eyes: Dark brown.

Sexes: Similar in plumage; no major sexual dimorphism.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact and alarm call: Harsh ti-ti or chik-chik notes.
  2. Song: Pleasant series of warbles and whistles from exposed perch; less varied than Black Drongo.
  3. Alarm mimicry: Can mimic calls of other birds, especially raptors.
हिंदी: सिर और पीठ धूसरे। पेट सफेद-धूसरा। पूँछ लंबी, काली, दो-फाँक। चोंच काली और मजबूत। आवाज़ तीक्ष्ण और कठोर।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Feeding ecology: Solitary or in loose pairs. Aerial insectivore that hawkflies from exposed perches. Catches insects in mid-air with quick sallies, then returns to perch. Feeds on:

  • Flying insects (flies, bees, wasps, beetles, termites)
  • Grasshoppers and crickets
  • Occasionally small lizards
  • Insects flushed from vegetation or grass

Habitat use: Prefers open or semi-open country with scattered trees. Commonly seen on overhead wires, fence posts, and dead branches in agricultural areas, forest clearings, and village gardens.

Winter behaviour: Arrives in small waves; often solitary or paired, unlike some migratory drongo species. Can be seen moving through open scrub forest and cultivated land.

Aggressive behaviour: Fearless; will dive-bomb and harass larger birds, especially raptors and crows, though less vigorous than Black Drongo.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosting in dense foliage of trees; communal roosts less evident than in resident species.

हिंदी: अकेले या जोड़े में रहता है। आसमान में उड़ते कीटों को पकड़ता है। तार और सूखी डालों पर बैठता है। बड़े पक्षियों को भी सताता है, लेकिन काली कोतवाल जितना आक्रामक नहीं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP in September–October from breeding grounds in peninsular and central India. Numbers peak in January–February. Departs by March–April.

Breeding: Does not breed in eastern UP; breeding occurs in dry deciduous forests of peninsular India (April–June).

Moult: Complete moult occurs before arrival at wintering grounds.

Activity: Diurnal; feeds throughout the day, with peaks at dawn and dusk.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Larger raptors (Shikra, sparrowhawks), jungle crows
  • Mammalian predators: Cat, wild boar (nestlings)
  • Parasites: Helminths, ectoparasites
हिंदी: बड़ी चिड़ियों के शिकार के लिए अतिसंवेदनशील। चिल्लाते और भगाते समय ही शिकारी से बचता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: The Ashy Drongo is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Schedule IV. All wild birds listed in the act are protected; hunting and trapping are prohibited.

Pest control: Valuable in agricultural areas during winter months because it consumes many insects harmful to crops, including flies, beetles, and grasshoppers.

Cultural significance: Mentioned in classical Sanskrit literature on birds. Its fierce, defiant attitude toward larger predators has made it a symbol of courage in some folk traditions.

Scientific value: Winter visitor populations provide data on migration patterns and adaptation to seasonal habitat use; studied for understanding Central Asian migration corridors.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी विधि अधिनियम 1972 की सुरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है। सर्दियों में खेतों के कीटों को खाकर किसानों की मदद करता है।

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Ashy Drongo should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

GBIF holds published records from these districts during the winter months. The species is expected in open and semi-open habitats with scattered trees throughout the region during winter months.

Habitat requirements: Open forest margins, agricultural fields with scattered trees, village gardens and compound trees, roadside trees and wires.

Seasonal presence: Expected September through April, with peak abundance January–February.

Nearest published records: Winter surveys in adjacent districts document regular occurrence; Ali & Ripley (1968) note presence in eastern Uttar Pradesh during winter.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent, breeding in peninsular India and wintering in central and northern regions. Two subspecies recognized; D. l. leucophaeus occurs in India.

In India: Winter visitor throughout the subcontinent; resident and breeding in peninsular dry deciduous forests.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to eastern districts; occurs from September to April in open and agricultural habitats.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor in open forest margins and cultivated land with scattered trees, particularly along riverine corridors.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Vieillot in 1817 as Edolius leucophaeus from peninsular India. Later placed in genus Dicrurus. Type locality is southern India.

Subspecies. Monotypic or with minimal subspecific variation recognized; D. l. leucophaeus occurs across India.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Dicruridae (drongos). The genus Dicrurus includes ten species across Africa and Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Sister species to Black Drongo (D. macrocercus) based on morphology and genetics; both belong to the larger Asian drongo radiation.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Population trends stable; widespread and adaptable to human-modified habitats. Not threatened by hunting in India due to protected status.
