ब्राउन-कैप्ड पिग्मी कठफोड़वा / Brown-capped Pygmy Woodpecker

Yungipicus nanus (Vigors, 1830) — Picidae

ब्राउन-कैप्ड पिग्मी कठफोड़वा — सबसे छोटी कठफोड़वा, जो बाग-खेतों और जंगल की पेड़ों पर साल भर दिखती है। इसकी खट-खट-खट की आवाज़ सुनाई पड़ी और झाड़ी में खोजते रहो — छोटी-सी भूरी और सफेद धारीदार चिड़िया निकल आएगी।

Field tip: The tiniest woodpecker in eastern UP, often feeding on thin twigs and small branches (larger woodpeckers avoid such thin wood). Listen for rapid, high-pitched tapping; follow the sound and look on stems and bark. Pairs often stay together year-round.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें:
सबसे छोटी कठफोड़वा, मुट्ठी में आ जाए। भूरे रंग की टोपी जैसी। पीठ सफेद-काली धारीदार। पतली डालों और शाखों पर खट-खट करता है। बड़ी कठफोड़वाएँ इतनी पतली डालों पर नहीं जाती।

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 12–14 cm long; smallest woodpecker in the region; weight 9–12 g.

Plumage: Both sexes have brown cap and nape. Back white with bold black barring and streaking (oak bark-like pattern). Wings dark brown with white spots and bars. Underparts whitish with dark streaking or barring on flanks. Rump white or pale buff. Tail short, stiff, dark with white spots; terminal feathers white-barred.

Sexual dimorphism: Males have red on lower belly or undertail-coverts (visible only in hand); females lack this red.

Bill: Small, dark, chisel-like; proportionally small compared to body.

Legs and feet: Short; greenish-yellow; adapted for climbing fine twigs.

Eyes: Dark brown.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Drumming: Rapid, high-pitched tapping at 15–20 strikes per second; shorter bursts than larger woodpeckers (0.5–1.0 seconds). Frequency.
  2. Contact call: Sharp tik or tik-tik notes; quiet compared to larger woodpeckers.
  3. Alarm call: Harsh chick-chick or kik-kik when disturbed.
हिंदी: भूरी टोपी, सफेद पीठ काली धारीदार। पेट सफेद-धूसरा। पतली पूँछ, कठोर। चोंच छोटी, काली, नुकीली। खट-खट तेज़ और छोटी।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Feeding ecology: Forages alone or in pairs on thin twigs, small branches, and bark of trees and shrubs. Specializes in fine twigs where larger woodpeckers cannot forage. Feeds on:

  • Small wood-boring beetle larvae and pupae
  • Termites and ants (especially on bark and in crevices)
  • Small spiders and web insects
  • Plant matter (buds, seeds, occasionally fruit)

Foraging technique: Taps rapidly on bark; uses small bill to pick insects from bark crevices and peel small bark flakes. More methodical than energetic; spends long periods on a single small branch.

Habitat use: Prefers open deciduous and mixed forests, forest edges, orchards, and cultivated areas with scattered trees and shrubs. Avoids dense forest interior.

Social behaviour: Resident and generally seen in pairs or small family groups. Mates remain together year-round. Young from previous brood may accompany parents into winter.

Cavity nesting: Excavates small holes in dead wood and bamboo; uses old cavities. Nesting April–June; typically rear one brood.

हिंदी: जोड़े में रहता है, साल भर साथ। पतली-पतली डालों पर खट-खट करके कीड़े निकालता है। बड़ी कठफोड़वाओं की तुलना में शांत और छिपा-छिपा रहता है। गर्मी में पेड़ों में घोंसले खोदता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Breeding: Resident; breeds April–June. Single brood per year (occasionally two if first fails).

Nesting: Excavates small cavities in dead wood, dead branches, or bamboo stems; prefers softer wood. Both sexes excavate. Clutch size 3–5 eggs; incubation 11–12 days. Fledging about 18–21 days.

Territorial behaviour: Maintains year-round territory; drums and calls to maintain territory boundaries, especially in breeding season.

Moult: Gradual moult in post-breeding season (June–September); complete renewal of plumage.

Activity: Diurnal; feeds throughout the day with peaks at dawn and late afternoon.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shikra, other small raptors, jungle crows
  • Mammalian predators: Snakes (tree snakes), monitors (occasionally), wild cat
  • Nest predators: Snakes, palm squirrels, house crows at exposed cavities
हिंदी: छोटी चिल्लर-बाज़ी से इसके लिए खतरा। साँप और बड़े जानवर शिकार करते हैं। घोंसले को कौए और साँप से बचाना पड़ता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: The Brown-capped Pygmy Woodpecker is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Schedule I. All wild birds listed are protected; hunting and trapping are prohibited.

Pest control: Beneficial in orchards and gardens because it feeds on wood-boring beetles and other insect pests that damage trees. Particularly valuable in fruit orchards.

Cavity provision: After woodpecker cavities are abandoned, they provide nesting habitat for other cavity-nesting birds.

Cultural significance: In some regions, its rapid drumming is considered an auspicious sign; appears in folk stories about perseverance and industriousness due to its constant work-like behaviour.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी विधि अधिनियम 1972 की पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है। फलों के बागों में कीटों को नष्ट करके मदद करता है। घोंसले खोद कर दूसरी चिड़ियों को घोंसले देता है।

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Brown-capped Pygmy Woodpecker should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species is expected in deciduous and mixed forests, forest margins, and cultivated areas with scattered trees throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh.

Habitat requirements: Open and semi-open country with trees; orchards, gardens, forest edges, and woodland margins. Avoids dense closed forest.

Elevation range: Up to 1500 m, but in eastern UP restricted to lowland areas below 200 m.

Year-round presence: Resident; no seasonal variation in occurrence.

Nearest published records: Documented from adjacent reserves and protected areas in eastern Uttar Pradesh and surrounding regions (Ali & Ripley 1968).

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent from sea level to about 1500 m. Resident and non-migratory throughout.

In India: Distributed across peninsular and central India, extending north into the Gangetic plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in deciduous and mixed forests, particularly in eastern and central regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident in open woodland, forest edges, and cultivated areas with scattered trees and shrubs. Less common in dense forests than in open country.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Vigors in 1830 as Picus nanus from peninsular India. Later placed in genus Yungipicus after molecular studies showed divergence from Dendrocopus.

Subspecies. Monotypic; Y. n. nanus found throughout Indian range.

Family and order placement. Order Piciformes; family Picidae (woodpeckers). Genus Yungipicus contains three Asian species of very small woodpeckers.

Phylogenetic notes. Recent molecular work places Yungipicus as a distinct genus from larger Dendrocopus species; represents a radiation of small pygmy woodpeckers.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Population stable and widespread across suitable habitats. Not threatened by hunting due to protected status; adaptable to human-modified landscapes.
