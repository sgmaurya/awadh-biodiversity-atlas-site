थिरथिरा / Black Redstart

Phoenicurus ochruros (S.G. Gmelin, 1774) — Muscicapidae

थिरथिरा — यह एक सुंदर चिड़िया है जो मध्य एशिया के पहाड़ों से सर्दियों में भारत आती है। नर सम्पूर्ण काली होती है, केवल पूँछ का आधार और रंप नारंगी-लाल होते हैं। मादा भूरी-काली होती है। ये चिड़िया खुली, सूखी जगहों पर रहती है — उजाड़ खंडहरों, खेतों के किनारों पर, और गाँवों के आस-पास। यह सक्रियता से उड़ती है और हवा में कीड़ों को पकड़ती है।

Field tip: A small black (male) or dark grey-brown (female) bird with a striking orange-red tail and rump, on rocky areas or cleared ground during winter — is a Black Redstart. The orange-red tail base is diagnostic. Males are striking; females duller but still show the orange-red tail.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 13–14 cm long; wingspan 20–26 cm. Weight 12–18 g. A small songbird.

Plumage (male): Striking. Entire body, head, and wings black or charcoal-black. Tail and rump orange-red or chestnut-orange, the tail extending to the body (diagnostic feature). Under-tail coverts also orange-red. Wings show some grey-brown feather edges.

Plumage (female): Much duller. Head and body dark grey-brown or charcoal-brown. Underparts slightly paler grey-brown. Wings darker grey. Tail and rump orange-red like male, though sometimes paler.

Legs and feet: Thin, dark brown or black.

Bill: Thin, pointed, black. Typical flycatcher bill for catching insects in air.

Eyes: Dark iris; alert expression.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: Scratchy, warbling song with harsh notes.
  2. Call: Sharp tee or tick-tick-tick call, given repeatedly.
  3. Alarm: Loud, harsh chur-r-r-r when disturbed.
हिंदी: कठोर "टिक-टिक" की आवाज़। गाना खुरदरा होता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Flycatcher and gleaner: Black Redstarts are primarily flycatchers, sallying out from a perch to catch insects in mid-air. Also glean insects from ground and vegetation.

Feeding: Entirely insectivorous:

  • Flying insects caught mid-air (flycatcher style)
  • Small insects gleaned from ground and rocks
  • Occasionally berries in late winter

Feeding behaviour: Active hunter, typically using "sally-and-return" technique from an exposed perch. Spend much time watching from perches before attacking prey.

Habitat preference: Prefer open, rocky, or cleared areas. Avoid dense vegetation. Often on disturbed ground (ruins, cleared fields) where insects are exposed.

Territorial: Establish individual wintering territories on open areas. Males often sing from exposed perches or ruins.

Alert behaviour: Very active and obvious on perches; give sharp calls frequently.

Flight: Active, bouncing flight typical of redstarts. Orange-red tail flashes are obvious.

हिंदी: खुली जगह के ऊँचे डंठल पर बैठता है। उड़कर कीड़े पकड़ता है। नारंगी पूँछ दिखाई देती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP from October, with main arrival November. Departs February–April, with most birds gone by April.

Breeding range: Central Asia, Caucasus, and nearby mountainous regions from Turkey to Central Asia. Breeds April–August in rocky areas and cliff faces.

Migration: Medium-distance migrant. Diurnal migrant.

Wintering: Solitary on individual open areas. Returns to same wintering territories year after year.

Moult: Complete moult occurs before arrival; breeding plumage acquired on wintering grounds.

Timing in UP: Early arrival October (vanguard); main arrival November; peak December–March; departure February–April.

  • Mammalian predators: Mongoose, wild cat, feral cat

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Listed under Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Ecological value: Important insectivore in open areas; helps control flying insects.

Wetland health indicator: Species present on open areas near wetlands indicates good habitat diversity.

Cultural significance: Known locally by the name "thirithra". Valued for its striking appearance.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी विधि अधिनियम 1972 के अंतर्गत संरक्षित है। इसका काला-नारंगी रंग विशेषता है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Black Redstart is expected to winter in the eastern UP region based on its known range and wintering distribution in northern India. The species breeds in Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, and adjacent areas) and winters from the Middle East through Central Asia to northern India. However, it is not recorded by the three published surveys (Mishra et al. 2019, Yashmita-Ulman & Singh 2022, 2025) from Bakhira, Parvati Arga, or Ayodhya district wetlands. The species prefers rocky, open, or cleared areas rather than wetlands, so it may occur in suitable habitats (ruins, open fields, cleared areas) near villages and towns in eastern UP rather than on wetlands. Peak occurrence should be November–March if present. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Central Asia and adjacent mountains; winters from Middle East through Central Asia to northern India and occasionally beyond.

In India: Rare and irregular winter visitor, primarily in northern regions (Himalayas, northern plains). Widespread records are sparse.

In Uttar Pradesh: Rare winter visitor, primarily to northern and central districts. Most records are from higher-altitude areas or disturbed habitats.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as very rare or irregular winter visitor to open, rocky, or cleared areas. No records from the three local surveys. October–April if present.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by S.G. Gmelin in 1774 in Reise durch Russland zur Untersuchung der drei Natur-Reiche (volume 1) as Motacilla ochruros. Type locality: Russia.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies are recognised across the range:

  • P. o. ochruros — Central Asia
  • P. o. rufiventris — Western breeding populations
  • P. o. phoenicuroides — Eastern Asia

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae (thrushes and chats). Genus Phoenicurus contains the redstarts (11 species).

Phylogenetic notes. Phoenicurus ochruros is closely related to P. phoenicurus (Common Redstart), sharing many behavioural traits.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. The global population is large and stable. The species is adaptable to various open habitats and is not globally threatened.
