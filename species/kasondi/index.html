कसौंदी / Coffee Senna

Senna occidentalis (L.) Link — Fabaceae

कसौंदी (सन्नई, कफ़ी सन्ना) — एक खतरनाक खरपतवार जो पशु-चारागाहों में अक्सर मिलता है। लंबा पौधा, पीले फूल, चपटी फलियाँ जिनमें विषैले बीज होते हैं। गाय-भैंस के लिए ख़तरनाक, खासकर अगर बीज या कच्ची फलियाँ खाएँ तो पेट की समस्या और लकवा तक हो सकता है। तथापि, पारंपरिक चिकित्सा में इसे दस्त लाने वाली, कीड़े मारने वाली, और सूजन कम करने वाली दवाई के रूप में जाना जाता है। इसका तेल भी कभी-कभी त्वचा रोगों में लगाया जाता है।

Field tip: Look for an erect, somewhat bushy herb with yellow flowers and flat bean-pods. The compound leaves are pinnate with many leaflets. Crush a leaf; the plant has a distinctive unpleasant smell. Hazard: Seeds and unripe pods are toxic to cattle and buffalo — livestock illness or death can result from eating leaves or seeds. Do not allow livestock to graze freely on fields with mature plants.

Habit: Erect, branched annual herb or short-lived subshrub; 60–150 cm; stems angular, greenish, sometimes with purplish tinge.

Leaves: Alternate, even-pinnate; rachis 15–25 cm, with 6–10 pairs of opposite leaflets. Stipules small, deciduous. Leaflets oblong-lanceolate, 2–5 cm × 0.8–1.5 cm, smooth margin, slightly asymmetrical base, often showing reddish veining or tinge.

Inflorescence: Axillary and terminal racemes; flowers in pairs or loose clusters.

Flower: 4 sepals and 5 petals; petals bright golden-yellow, the upper petal smaller and often reflexed; 10 stamens, 6–8 fertile.

Pod (legume): Linear, flattened, 8–15 cm × 6–10 mm, hairless, yellowish-green when young, ripening to yellowish-brown; contains 8–12 seeds; pods somewhat constricted between seeds.

Seeds: Flattened, reddish-brown, 4–6 mm, hard seed coat; contain toxic alkaloids and glycosides; seeds remain toxic even after drying.

हिंदी: पत्ती में 6-10 जोड़ी पत्तियों की सजी। पीले फूल। फली लंबी, चपटी। बीज लाल-भूरे, विषैले।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Senna occidentalis is a warm-season annual pioneer of disturbed, compacted soils. It germinates rapidly after monsoon rains and reaches flowering size in 10–12 weeks. The plant thrives in poor, alkaline, and even saline soils, making it common in usar patches and canal command areas. Growth is vigorous and the plant produces abundant seeds.

Livestock toxicity — a significant hazard: The seeds and unripe pods contain toxic alkaloids (sennosides) and other glycosides that cause acute enteritis (gastroenteritis), tremor, depression, and in severe cases, death in cattle and buffalo. Sheep and goats are somewhat more resistant but still susceptible. Toxicity is highest in seeds and unripe pods; mature dried pods and senescent leaves are less toxic. Livestock deaths from senna seed consumption have been documented across North India.

The plant is avoided by most herbivores owing to its musty odour and bitter taste. Flowers are visited by bees and butterflies. Seeds are dispersed by water (pods float) and by accidental ingestion by livestock or farm workers.

हिंदी: गरम-गर्म ज़मीन पर उगता है। पशु के लिए जहरीला, खासकर बीज और कच्ची फली। गाय-भैंस की मौत भी इससे हो सकती है। फूल में मधुमक्खियाँ आती हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि और मानव संबंध

Livestock toxicity — critical hazard: The seeds and unripe pods of Senna occidentalis contain alkaloid and glycoside compounds, primarily sennosides A, B, C and anthranoid compounds. These cause acute toxicity in ruminants. Symptoms include anorexia, salivation, diarrhoea (often bloody), depression, tremor, and in high doses, death within 24–48 hours.Dried mature pods are less toxic but should not be fed to livestock.

Prevention: Fields known to have senna should be inspected in September–October as pods ripen. Plants should be manually removed before seeds mature. Livestock should not be allowed to graze freely on fields with senna. Hay or fodder from senna-infested fields should not be fed to cattle or sheep.

Traditional medicinal use: The leaves, and especially the pods, are used in folk and traditional medicine as a laxative and cathartic. The plant is recorded in Ayurvedic texts (Bhava Prakasha, Dhanvantari Nighantu) as a laxative (virechana). Uses include:

  • Pod decoction as a mild laxative for treating constipation
  • Leaf paste applied topically for skin infections and boils
  • Seed oil traditionally used for treating inflammatory skin conditions
  • Traditional use for anthelmintic (worm-killing) effect, though not scientifically validated

Weed status: A problem weed in irrigated fields and pastures; easily managed by hand-pulling of young plants. No herbicide resistance recorded.

Expected on field margins, wasteland, and canal banks throughout Basti district during the monsoon and post-monsoon (June to January). Any livestock illness or death suspected from senna seed consumption should be reported immediately to a veterinarian and to the atlas project. Fields should be checked in September–October as pods ripen. Roadsides and disturbed ground are places worth checking. The bright yellow flowers and flat pods are easily recognized. The first local record would be a photograph with date and location, sent to the atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical Africa and Americas; widely naturalized in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide; particularly abundant in the Indo-Gangetic plain.

In India: Widespread throughout the country, especially common in irrigated regions; recorded from sea level to 1,200 m.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common across agricultural districts and wastelands.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on disturbed ground and field margins; not yet confirmed by the project.

  1. What is the local incidence of livestock poisoning by coffee senna seeds in your area? / आपके क्षेत्र में कसौंदी के बीज से पशु मरते हैं? — Interview 5–10 farmers and herders; document any poisoning cases, symptoms, and outcomes.
  2. At what plant stage (seedling, young plant, flowering, mature seed) is coffee senna toxicity to cattle greatest? — Consult veterinary or toxicology records; document stages.
  3. How long do buried coffee senna seeds remain toxic after 6 months, 12 months of soil storage? — Test seed viability and toxicity after storage periods.
  4. Is there traditional knowledge of detecting senna poisoning in cattle, and what is the folk remedy? / कसौंदी जहर का पारंपरिक इलाज क्या है? — Interview local herders, farmers, and healers.
  5. How many seeds does a single mature senna plant produce in one season? — Count pods on 10 plants; count seeds per pod; calculate average.

Field rule: An erect herb with pinnate leaves (6–10 leaflet pairs), bright yellow flowers, and flat linear pods containing reddish-brown seeds is coffee senna. Seeds and unripe pods are toxic to cattle — do not allow livestock to graze on mature plants.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Originally described as Cassia occidentalis by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. Later transferred to the genus Senna by Christoph Friedrich Otto Link in 1822. The genus name Senna is derived from the Arabic name for the plant; the specific epithet occidentalis refers to the American (western) origin of the species.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Fabales, family Fabaceae (pea and bean family), subfamily Caesalpinioideae. The genus Senna includes about 50 species, many used medicinally.

Phylogenetic notes. Senna occidentalis is sister to Senna tora and other tropical senna species. The genus Senna is well-supported within Fabaceae by molecular phylogenetics.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread and abundant; invasive in some regions; no conservation concerns.
