छोटी कुर्री / Little Tern

Sternula albifrons (Pallas, 1764) — Laridae

छोटी कुर्री — यह सबसे छोटी tern है जो भारतीय नदियों में रहती है। इसका शरीर सफ़ेद होता है और पीठ धूसर होती है। सिर पर काली टोपी है और चोंच पीली होती है। यह बहुत तेज़ी से उड़ती है, पानी के ऊपर मंडराती है, और अचानक पानी में छलाँग लगाकर छोटी मछलियाँ पकड़ती है। नदियों की बालू पर समूहों में रहती है।

Field tip: The smallest tern in eastern UP, easily identified by its tiny size, white body, grey back, and black cap with yellow bill. Watch for rapid hovering above water before sudden plunges to catch small fish. Often in small flocks on river sandbars and margins. Much smaller than the River Tern and with a shorter, straighter bill.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 22–24 cm long; wingspan 48–56 cm. The smallest tern in the region.

Plumage: Head black cap, sharply demarcated from white face and underparts. White throat and underparts. Back and scapulars pale grey. Rump white. Wings grey above, white below (pale underwing). Tail white, lightly forked.

Head: Black cap from crown to nape; white forehead and face. Eyes dark.

Bill: Yellow or orange-yellow with black tip. Short, pointed. Used for capturing small fish.

Legs: Very short, yellow to orange-yellow. Feet webbed.

Wings: Pointed, grey above, for swift, acrobatic flight.

Tail: Lightly forked; much less deeply forked than River Tern.

Sexes: Similar; females may be slightly larger.

Juveniles: Cap duller; white forehead more extensive; orange on bill less developed.

Vocalisations

  1. Call: High-pitched kip-kip or pit-pit, given in flight and when hovering.
  2. Alarm: Sharp, loud calls when disturbed or defending territory.
  3. Contact: Softer calls between adults and with young.
हिंदी: तेज़ "किप-किप" या "पिट-पिट" आवाज़। बहुत शोर मचाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Specialist fish-eater: Feeds exclusively on very small fish:

  • Small cyprinids
  • Other tiny river fish
  • Occasionally aquatic insects

Feeding technique: Hunts by flying above water, hovering at height, and plunge-diving into the water to capture small fish. Dives are more vertical than River Tern. Makes repeated dives when fish are abundant.

Social behaviour: Often in small groups or loose flocks. Colonial or semi-colonial during breeding season. Less gregarious than some tern species.

Roosting: Roosts on sandbars and river margins; often in small groups.

Habitat specialist: Strictly dependent on rivers with suitable fish stocks and sandbars. Requires clear or relatively clear water.

हिंदी: बहुत छोटी मछलियाँ खाता है। तेज़ी से पानी में छलाँग लगाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round on suitable river systems; some local movements with water levels.

Breeding: February to September; peak April–August. Nests on sandbars; 2–3 eggs.

Eggs: Buff or olive eggs with dark markings.

Incubation: Approximately 18–22 days by both parents.

Chicks: Semi-precocial; leave nest at 2–3 weeks but remain dependent.

Fledging: Young become independent at 3–4 weeks.

Roosting: On sandbars and river margins; often communal.

Water level dependency: Breeding tied to water levels; flooding may inundate nests.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Raptors: Eagles, falcons
  • Snakes: Aquatic and semi-aquatic snakes
  • Monitor lizards: May predate eggs and chicks
  • Crows: Corvids depredate eggs
  • Habitat loss: Loss of suitable sandbars

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: Listed under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. All wild birds are protected.

Ecosystem role: As a specialist fish-eater, the Little Tern plays a role in fish population dynamics.

Habitat conservation: The species is dependent on healthy river systems with good fish stocks and suitable nesting habitat.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Little Tern is expected as a resident on the Ghaghra, Rapti, and Saryu rivers of eastern Uttar Pradesh throughout the year. Look for tiny white terns hovering over the water and plunge-diving for fish on river sandbars and margins. Much smaller than the River Tern and with a shorter, finer bill. Often in small groups. Most active during breeding season (April–June). A first record would require a clear photograph showing the tiny size, black cap, yellow bill, and white body, with place and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread in Africa, Asia, and Europe. Breeds in suitable habitats; winters in southern regions.

In India: Distributed on major river systems; particularly abundant on the Ganges and associated rivers.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present on major rivers including the Ganges, Gomti, Ghaghra, and Rapti.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on the Ghaghra, Rapti, and associated river systems. Resident year-round on suitable habitat.

Field rule: The tiniest tern species in eastern UP, with white plumage, black cap, yellow bill, and light tail fork, plunge-diving for very small fish, is a Little Tern. Size difference from River Tern is immediately obvious.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Peter Simon Pallas in 1764 in Zoographia Rosso-Asiatica as Sterna albifrons. Type locality: southern Russia.

Subspecies. Five subspecies recognised across range; S. a. gurkyi occurs in India.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Laridae (gulls and terns). The genus Sternula contains the small terns.

Phylogenetic notes. Little Tern is part of the genus Sternula, characterized by very small size among terns.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). The global population is large. Some regional declines due to habitat loss and disturbance.
