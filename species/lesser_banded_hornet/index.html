लेसर बैंडेड हॉर्नेट / Lesser Banded Hornet

Vespa affinis (Linnaeus, 1764) — Vespidae

लेसर बैंडेड हॉर्नेट — इसकी ततैया से छोटी है, पर ज़्यादा आक्रामक होती है। पीले और काले पतली धारियाँ होती हैं। घरों की दीवार, पेड़ों पर, घोंसलें बनाती है। इसका दंश बहुत दर्दनाक है। मई से सितंबर तक सक्रिय होती है। भारत में सर्वत्र पाई जाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeWorker 15–20 mm; queen 20–25 mm
ColourYellow and black stripes; thinner bands than V. orientalis
BodyRobust, smooth; relatively hairless
HeadYellow on face; large eyes
WingsClear, held parallel to body at rest
StingVisible, smooth, can sting multiple times
NestPapery, exposed or partially enclosed, grey-brown
BehaviourMore aggressive than V. orientalis; quicker to sting

Field tip: Slightly smaller than Oriental Hornet but more aggressive. Yellow face with fine black banding on body. Often nests under building eaves or in tree cavities. Very defensive of the nest; will chase intruders for considerable distances. The sting is very painful and more likely to be delivered than from V. orientalis.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: ततैया से छोटी पर ज़्यादा गुस्से वाली। पतली पीली-काली धारियाँ। बहुत जल्दी दंश मार देती है। घोंसली से दूर भी पीछा करेगी।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Worker body length15–20 mm
Queen body length20–25 mm
Wingspan35–45 mm

Worker: Smaller than Vespa orientalis. Head is relatively large with yellow face and dark occiput. Thorax is robust and mostly dark. Abdomen is banded with yellow and black, but the yellow bands are narrower and less prominent than in V. orientalis. Legs are dark. Wings are clear. The sting is smooth, allowing repeated stinging.

Queen: Similar in colour pattern to workers but larger.

Male: Winged, dark, produced late in the season.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A social hornet found throughout tropical Asia. Colonies are founded by overwintered queens in spring. Mature colonies contain 100–300 workers. The species is strongly carnivorous, hunting flies, moths, and other insects. More aggressive than V. orientalis and more likely to deliver multiple stings if the nest is threatened.

The species nests in cavities (tree holes, roof spaces) or builds open nests on branches or eaves. Nests are often smaller and more exposed than those of V. orientalis. The species is particularly abundant in cultivated and disturbed areas.

Workers are very active hunters and can be observed catching insects in flight. The species also visits flowers for nectar and feeds on fermented fruit. The sting venom is potent and the species' aggressive nature makes it a significant hazard to people.

हिंदी: ततैया से ज़्यादा आक्रामक है। सामने आ जाओ तो तुरंत दंश मार देगी। खेत में अच्छी चीज़ है, बहुत कीड़े खाती है, पर घर के पास खतरनाक है।

Annual cycle:

  • Spring (April–May): Queens emerge and establish new nests
  • Summer (June–September): Worker production peaks
  • Autumn (October): Males and new queens are produced
  • Winter (November–March): Old colonies die; new queens overwinter

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Prey:

  • Flies: houseflies, blowflies, robber flies, mosquitoes
  • Other insects: moths, small bees, beetles, caterpillars
  • Spiders: occasionally

Other food sources:

  • Nectar and plant juices
  • Fermented fruit
  • Honeydew

Feeding: Adults feed mainly on nectar and juices. Larvae are fed whole insects or insect parts. A colony may hunt many hundreds of insects per day.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid flies and wasps: Attack pupae
  • Birds: Some species prey on adults
  • Ants: Raid nests for pupae
  • Larger hornets: Displace this species from nesting sites

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Beneficial predator and stinging hazard. The Lesser Banded Hornet is a voracious predator of flies and other insects, including many agricultural pests. A single colony can consume large numbers of insects per day, providing valuable pest control. However, the species is a significant stinging hazard, more dangerous than Vespa orientalis because of its greater aggression and tendency to deliver multiple stings.

Stinging hazard: The sting is very painful and can cause local swelling and redness lasting several hours to days. Multiple stings or allergic reactions can be medically serious. Deaths from stings are rare but can occur. People should avoid approaching nests and seek immediate medical attention for severe reactions.

Nest management: Non-lethal nest removal by professionals is preferable to chemical control. Preventing nest establishment by blocking likely nesting sites is the most practical approach.

Expected on buildings and trees throughout Basti district and eastern UP from April to October. A specimen or photograph with date and location would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical Asia from India to Java and China.

In India: Widespread in all states; very common in gardens and cultivated areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common throughout; particularly abundant in eastern regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on buildings and trees throughout the year, most active April to October.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. How does the aggressiveness of Vespa affinis differ from Vespa orientalis, and what factors trigger attacking behaviour? — Behavioral observations; measure response distance to nest disturbance.
  2. What is the predatory efficiency of this species on specific crop pests (flies, moths)? — Collect and identify prey items; quantify predation rates.
  3. Can deployment of pheromone baits or deterrent compounds reduce nest establishment in residential areas? — Test deterrents; measure efficacy.
  4. How does colony size and female production vary with food availability and nest location? — Monitor colonies; quantify brood and worker production.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1764 in the Systema Naturae (12th edn.) as Vespa affinis.

Subspecies. Monotypic; no subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Hymenoptera, family Vespidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to Vespa orientalis and other Asian hornets.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated.
