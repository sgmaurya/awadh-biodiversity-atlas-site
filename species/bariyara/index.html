बरियारा / Common Wireweed

Sida acuta Burm. f. — Malvaceae

बरियारा — यह हर खेत की मेड़, हर गली के किनारे दिखता है। पूर्वी यूपी का सबसे साधारण खरपतवार जिसे ज़्यादातर किसान जानते ही नहीं कि इसका नाम क्या है। पतली डंडियों पर पीले फूल, बीज गोल और कड़े — कपड़ों से चिपकने वाले नहीं, पर मवेशियों के पेट को खराब कर सकते हैं। मधुमक्खियाँ इसके फूलों पर आती हैं और शहद का रंग पीलापन लिए होता है। पुरानी चिकित्सा में दस्त बंद करने के लिए इस्तेमाल होता था।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FlowersSmall (8–12 mm diameter), pale yellow with five petals; borne singly in leaf axils

Field tip: A slender annual weed with pale yellow flowers and wiry branched stems found on roadsides and field edges year-round. The round, slightly spiky seed capsule distinguishes it from other Sida species. When young it is easily pulled; when mature the stem is tough and wire-like.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Stem and Branching: Wiry, slender, moderately branched; green, turning reddish with age. The epidermis is smooth or covered with fine appressed hairs.

Leaves: Alternate, stipulate (stipules linear, 3–5 mm). Blade ovate-lanceolate, acute, base cuneate, margins crenate-serrate. Texture somewhat rough (scabrous) with a network of prominent veins.

Inflorescence: Flowers solitary in leaf axils, pedicels 0.5–1.5 cm, each subtended by a single bracteole.

Flower: Sepals 5, free, broadly ovate, green; petals 5, pale yellow, obovate, clawed; stamens numerous in a central column; style 5-lobed.

Fruit: Schizocarp separating into 5 mericarps (single-seeded units), each 6–8 mm long, slightly angled on dorsal surface, with short spines or bristles at apex and along margins.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Pioneer species: Sida acuta germinates rapidly in disturbed soil. It is one of the first plants to colonize newly tilled fields, roadside cuts, and waste ground. Its shallow root system makes it vulnerable to drought but allows rapid exploitation of monsoon rains.

Pollination: Flowers are pollinated by small generalist bees, especially honeybees (Apis mellifera and Apis cerana). The pale yellow flowers offer abundant pollen. Nectar production is moderate. Flowers open in the early morning and close by afternoon.

Seed dispersal: Seeds are released passively when the schizocarp dries and splits. Some seeds are dispersed by water during monsoon floods. The slightly bristly surface may facilitate short-distance mechanical attachment to livestock fur, but true zoochory is limited.

Weed competition: In fields with adequate moisture, Sida acuta competes with crops for light and nitrogen. However, its relatively modest height and slow vegetative growth mean it causes less crop damage than aggressive weeds like Parthenium hysterophorus.

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Honeybee plants: Visited by Apis mellifera and Apis cerana for pollen; contributes nectar to village apiary honey
  • Co-occurrence: Often found with Congress Grass, Dhatura, and Bathua on roadsides and waste ground
  • Livestock forage: Young tender shoots browsed by goats and sheep; mature plants are woody and less palatable

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Weed status: Sida acuta is a minor field weed of limited economic importance in eastern UP. Hand-weeding and mechanical cultivation remove it easily. No herbicide resistance reported.

Traditional medicine: The whole plant is used in Ayurvedic and folk medicine. According to Kirtikar & Basu (1935), the leaves and roots are employed for treating diarrhoea and dysentery, and the root decoction is used for cough and bronchitis. No modern pharmacological validation cited here.

Bee forage: In areas with village honey production, the presence of Sida acuta on roadsides contributes additional pollen for honeybees during the critical post-monsoon season (August–October) when many cultivated crops have completed flowering.

Sida acuta is to be expected year-round on roadsides, field margins, and waste ground throughout Basti district. Peak growth and flowering occur July through October. Any disturbed area near villages is a place worth checking. The first local record would be a photograph showing the pale yellow flowers and round seed heads, with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread throughout tropical and subtropical regions of Asia, Africa, and the Americas; native to the Indian subcontinent.

In India: Found throughout the country, from coastal plains to foothills up to about 1,500 m elevation.

In Uttar Pradesh: Ubiquitous across all districts in disturbed habitats.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common to very common on roadsides, field margins, and waste ground.

Field rule: A slender annual weed (30–90 cm) with pale yellow flowers and round, slightly bristly seed capsules splitting into five parts, found on roadsides and field edges, is Sida acuta. The wiry stems are the source of the common name "wireweed."

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Sida acuta was described by Nicolaas Laurens Burman in 1768 in Flora Indica, p. 147. Type locality: India. The genus name Sida is derived from the Greek sidé, a plant name used by ancient writers. The epithet acuta means "sharp-pointed," referring to the leaf apex.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Malvales, family Malvaceae (the mallow family), subfamily Sida. The genus Sida contains about 150 species, mostly tropical.

Phylogenetic notes. Sida acuta is closely related to S. rhombifolia and S. cordifolia, all three being common weeds in Asian agricultural systems.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. It has a stable, expanding population across its native range and introduced areas, with no conservation threats.
