रीठा / Soapnut

Sapindus mukorossi Gaertn. — Sapindaceae

रीठा — वह पेड़ जिसके फल से साबुन बनने से पहले कपड़े और बाल धुलते थे। पीले-भूरे, चिकने, मटर से बड़े फल पानी में रगड़ने पर झाग देते हैं। आज भी ऊनी और रेशमी कपड़े, सोने के गहने और बाल धोने में रीठा काम आता है। पेड़ ऊँचा, छायादार, और गाँव के बाग़ों में कहीं-कहीं बचा हुआ है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeDeciduous tree 10–20 m, straight trunk, rounded crown
LeavesPinnate, 30–45 cm, with 5–10 pairs of lance-shaped leaflets 7–15 cm; no terminal leaflet
FlowersSmall, greenish-white, in large branched clusters at branch tips, May–June
FruitRound drupe 1.5–2.5 cm, fleshy, yellow turning brown and wrinkled when dry; one, sometimes two or three lobes
SeedOne hard, round, black seed per lobe, 1 cm
BarkGrey-brown, flaking in thin scales on old trees
The testRub a ripe fruit in a little water: it lathers

Field tip: Look in old homestead groves and along canal roads for a tall tree with long feathery leaves and, from October, bunches of sticky yellow-brown "berries" that fall and dry to a wrinkled brown. The lather test settles it. In eastern UP it is planted rather than wild, and old trees are becoming rare.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: लंबा पेड़, पंखनुमा लंबी पत्तियाँ, और सर्दी में पीले-भूरे चिपचिपे फल जो सूखकर झुर्रीदार हो जाते हैं। फल को पानी में रगड़िए — झाग आया तो रीठा है।

Trunk and bark: Straight, with a grey to dark brown bark that becomes scaly with age. Wood pale yellow, hard but not durable.

Leaves: Alternate, even-pinnate, the rachis slightly winged in young leaves. Leaflets opposite or nearly so, entire, glossy above, slightly hairy beneath, tapering to a point.

Flowers: Polygamous: male and bisexual flowers on the same tree. Each 3–5 mm, five petals, in terminal panicles 20–30 cm long. Pollinated mainly by bees and flies.

Fruit: A one- to three-lobed drupe. The pericarp (the fleshy wall) is translucent yellow when ripe and contains 10–12% saponins by dry weight, the source of the lather. The seed is black, globose, very hard, and will not germinate well unless its coat is scarified.

हिंदी: पत्ती एक डंठल पर 5 से 10 जोड़ी पत्तियों की बनी होती है, सिरे पर कोई पत्ती नहीं। फल का गूदा ही साबुन है; बीज काला, गोल, बहुत कड़ा।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A tree of the sub-Himalayan tract and the outer hills that was carried into the plains by people, for its fruit. It tolerates poor and even slightly alkaline soils, which suits the usar patches of the district, and is drought-hardy once established. Growth is moderate; fruiting begins at 9–10 years and a mature tree may give 30–50 kg of fruit a season.

Its early-summer flowers are a nectar source when little else is in bloom, worked by Asian Honey Bee and other bees and by flies. The fruit pulp is bitter and soapy, yet Indian Flying Fox and some birds take it and drop the hard seeds some distance from the parent, which is how scattered wild-looking trees appear along canals and groves.

हिंदी: रीठा पहाड़ की तलहटी का पेड़ है जिसे लोग मैदानों में फल के लिए ले आए। खराब और ऊसर ज़मीन पर भी चल जाता है। गर्मी की शुरुआत में इसके फूल मधुमक्खियों के लिए रस का सहारा होते हैं।

हिंदी: मई-जून में फूल, सर्दी में फल। बीज से पेड़ 9-10 साल में फल देने लगता है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Bees: Asian Honey Bee, Giant Honey Bee and Dwarf Honey Bee on the flowers.
  • Fruit bats: Indian Flying Fox carries fruit from the tree.
  • Companions in the grove: planted beside Mango, Jamun and Neem in the older homestead gardens of the district.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating caterpillars and defoliating beetles in the monsoon, rarely serious on a mature tree.
  • Powdery mildew on nursery seedlings in humid weather.
  • Termites (Mound-building Termite) attack young plants and dead wood; seedlings need protection in their first two years.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Washing. The dried pericarp, soaked or boiled, lathers and cleans. It was the standard wash for wool, silk and fine cottons, for gold and silver ornaments, and for hair, across northern India well into the twentieth century, and is still sold in every bazaar.
  • Hair care. A decoction of the fruit as a hair wash is recorded in the classical pharmacopoeias and remains in use; shampoo makers now sell "reetha" products on the strength of it.
  • Traditional medicine. The fruit is listed as an emetic and expectorant and for skin complaints in Kirtikar & Basu and in Chopra's glossary. The saponins are irritant to the eyes and gut; the fruit is not eaten.
  • Insecticide and fish poison. Saponin-rich fruit water has been used against lice and, in the hills, to stupefy fish in pools — a practice now illegal.
  • Timber. Pale, hard, used for oil presses and cart parts, but not durable.

Today. Soapnut is traded as a "natural detergent" in India and abroad, and small-scale soap and shampoo makers use it. For a self-help group in Basti it is one of the few local plants whose product can be sold with no processing beyond drying.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Sapindus saponins are studied as biosurfactants for washing textiles and for cleaning contaminated soil; published work exists at laboratory scale. Whether a village-scale soapnut industry pays depends on fruit supply, and the trees have been disappearing from the plains as people stopped planting them.

हिंदी: रीठा धोने का फल है — ऊन, रेशम, गहने और बाल। बाज़ार में अब भी बिकता है। खाया नहीं जाता; आँख और पेट के लिए नुकसानदेह है। गाँव में नए पेड़ लगने बंद हो गए हैं, इसलिए पुराने पेड़ कीमती हैं।

अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

A planted tree of the plains rather than a wild one. To be expected as single old trees in homestead groves, near temples, and in older roadside and canal plantations; unlikely in open farmland. Any tree found is worth recording with its approximate age, because new planting has nearly stopped.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the sub-Himalayan belt from Pakistan through northern India and Nepal to Bhutan and into China; cultivated across tropical and subtropical Asia.

In India: Wild in the Shivaliks and outer Himalaya up to about 1,500 m; planted throughout the Indo-Gangetic plain and peninsular India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Natural stands in the tarai and bhabar; planted elsewhere.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Scattered planted trees; none wild.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many reetha trees are left in your village, and how old are they? / आपके गाँव में कितने रीठे के पेड़ बचे हैं, और कितने पुराने? — Walk the groves with an older resident; map each tree.
  2. Does anyone still wash with reetha, and for what? / क्या कोई अब भी रीठे से धोता है, और क्या? — Ask ten households; record who, what, and why they stopped.
  3. Which insects visit the flowers in May? — Thirty minutes at a flowering tree, morning and evening.
  4. Do bats or birds take the fruit? — Look for fruit and seeds under trees of other species nearby.
  5. How much does a tree yield, and what would the dried fruit sell for locally? / एक पेड़ कितना फल देता है, और सूखा रीठा गाँव में किस भाव बिकेगा? — Weigh the harvest from one tree; ask in the bazaar.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Joseph Gaertner in 1788 in De Fructibus et Seminibus Plantarum 1: 342. The epithet mukorossi comes from the Japanese name of the tree, mukuroji.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Sapindaceae, the soapberry family, in the order Sapindales, alongside litchi and longan. The genus Sapindus ("Indian soap") holds about a dozen species across the warm regions of the world, all with saponin-rich fruit.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular work places Sapindus among the core Sapindaceae; the Asian species form a group distinct from the American soapberries.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in cultivation, but wild sub-Himalayan populations are poorly surveyed.
