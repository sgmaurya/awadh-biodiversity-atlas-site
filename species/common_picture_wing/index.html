कॉमन पिक्चर विंग / Common Picture Wing

Rhyothemis variegata (Linnaeus, 1763) — Libellulidae

कॉमन पिक्चर विंग — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के तालाबों, नहरों और दलदली इलाकों में पाई जाने वाली एक छोटी-मंझोली, रंग-बिरंगे पंखों वाली व्याधपतंग है। इसके पंखों पर काली और सफ़ेद या भूरी पट्टियाँ और धब्बे होते हैं जो चित्र की तरह दिखते हैं। इसका शरीर आमतौर पर लाल या पीले रंग का होता है। यह व्याधपतंग अपने इलाके की सुरक्षा बहुत करती है और दूसरी व्याधपतंगों को अपने क्षेत्र से भगा देती है। यह पानी के पास की घास और पौधों पर बैठी रहती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall to medium dragonfly (body length 28–35 mm; wingspan 42–55 mm)
Wing PatternDistinctive dark markings (black or brown) with white or pale patches forming a patterned appearance; each wing has clearly defined blotches
Body ColourMale: bright red or golden-yellow abdomen; Female: duller yellow-brown or tan
BehaviourPerches on vegetation; highly territorial and active
HabitatShallow, vegetation-rich water bodies

Field tip: Look for a small dragonfly with distinctly patterned wings (dark markings with light patches) perching low on grass or reeds near shallow water. The patterned wings are diagnostic and separate it from other red or yellow dragonflies in the region.

Adult description: The male has a bright red or golden-yellow body with a relatively slender abdomen. The wings are the most distinctive feature, with dark brown or black markings forming bands and patches against pale or whitish patches. The female is duller, with a yellowish-tan or greenish-brown body and similar wing patterning but less contrasted. Both sexes have a broad head with large compound eyes positioned at the top.

Vocalisations

Odonates do not possess vocal organs and cannot produce true vocalisations.

  • Sound production: Wing-whirring sounds are produced during territorial interactions and mating displays. Wing-beat frequency is approximately 25–30 Hz.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Common Picture Wing is a small but aggressive territorial predator. Males establish territories along vegetation-rich pond margins and aggressively defend them from other dragonflies. They are most active during bright, sunny periods and are weak fliers compared to larger dragonflies, remaining close to vegetation and water. This species is particularly abundant in shallow, overgrown water bodies with dense emergent vegetation, where the larvae can find abundant prey and shelter. The species is a generalist with respect to water quality, tolerating moderately polluted waters.

Larval (Naiad) Stage

The larval stage is aquatic and predatory, living among aquatic vegetation.

  • Body form: The naiad is compact and flattened, with small lateral spines and a brownish coloration matching muddy substrates and plant debris.
  • Habitat and duration: Larvae inhabit the substrate around aquatic plants in shallow water. The larval stage lasts approximately 6–8 weeks.
  • Emergence: Larvae crawl up vegetation at night to emerge as adults, typically from June to September.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Egg: Laid by the female dipping her abdomen into water among vegetation.
  • Larva: Present in water year-round; feeds aggressively on small aquatic invertebrates.
  • Emergence: Peak from June to September during the monsoon.
  • Adult lifespan: Approximately 3–5 weeks.
  • Seasonality: Present year-round with peak abundance during the wet season.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Prey (adults):

  • Small mosquitoes and midges
  • Tiny mayflies
  • Small flies and gnats

Prey (larvae):

  • Mosquito larvae
  • Small aquatic insects and crustaceans
  • Midge larvae

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Insectivorous birds including warblers and flycatchers.
  • Amphibians: Small frogs and tree frogs.
  • Insects: Larger dragonflies and robber flies.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Beneficial insect. The Common Picture Wing contributes to natural mosquito and midge control in agricultural areas. Its presence in ponds and irrigation channels is an indicator of habitat quality and water conditions suitable for rice cultivation.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

This species should be expected throughout Basti district in shallow, vegetation-rich water bodies such as farm ponds, canal margins with tall grass, and marshy areas. Look for small dragonflies with patterned wings (dark markings against pale patches) perching on grass stems and reeds. Most active during sunny mornings and afternoons. Present year-round, most abundant during the monsoon and post-monsoon months (June to November). The first local record would be a photograph with place and date; send it to the atlas contact on the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia and Southeast Asia, from India and Sri Lanka through Myanmar, Thailand, and Indonesia.

In India: Distributed throughout plains and foothills in all regions with suitable wetland habitat.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present state-wide in districts with marsh and pond habitats.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all wetland and agricultural areas with standing water and emergent vegetation.

  2. What is the success rate of female egg-laying in dense vegetation versus sparsely vegetated water? — Count eggs laid in different vegetation densities.
  3. Do Picture Wings show preference for certain aquatic plant species when selecting perching and mating sites? / क्या पिक्चर विंग व्याधपतंगें निश्चित जलीय पौधों पर बैठना पसंद करती हैं? — Observe and record perching preferences on different plants.
  4. How does water quality (pH, turbidity, temperature) affect larval development rates? — Measure development time in different water types.
  5. What percentage of emerging adults successfully complete wing expansion and first flight in dense vegetation versus open areas? — Monitor emergence sites and success rates.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Linnaeus described the Common Picture Wing as Libellula variegata in 1763 in the twelfth edition of Systema Naturae (p. 911). The type locality is Java. The genus Rhyothemis was established by Hübner in 1818, meaning "flow-spirit" or "river-spirit" in Greek. The species name variegata refers to the variegated or patterned wings.

Subspecies. Two subspecies are recognized:

SubspeciesAuthorityRangeNotes
R. v. variegata(Linnaeus, 1763)South and Southeast AsiaUP subspecies; nominate form
R. v. indicaAsahina, 1974Andaman IslandsInsular subspecies

Family and order placement. Order Odonata, family Libellulidae.

Phylogenetic notes. Rhyothemis variegata belongs to a group of small territorial dragonflies with patterned wings, showing closer relationships to Southeast Asian species.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC). Common and widespread with stable populations across its range.
