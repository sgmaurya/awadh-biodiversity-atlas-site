पिग्मी डार्टलेट / Pygmy Dartlet

Agriocnemis pygmaea (Rambur, 1842) — Coenagrionidae

पिग्मी डार्टलेट — अपने परिवार की सबसे छोटी प्रजाति, जिसका शरीर मात्र 18–22 मिमी लंबा होता है। नर का रंग चमकीला नीला और काला, मादा मलिन हरा-पीला होती है। यह उथले जलाशयों और दलदलों के किनारों पर पाई जाती है, जहाँ पानी धीमी गति से बहता हो या रुका हो। छोटे पानी के पौधों के बीच यह अपना जीवन व्यतीत करती है। मच्छर और छोटे कीटों को पकड़कर खाती है।

Field tip: The Pygmy Dartlet is the smallest damselfly of the region. Look for a tiny, bright blue needle-like insect (if male) hovering weakly over shallow pond margins or vegetation mats. Its diminutive size is the key distinguishing character.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange

Hindwing length10–14 mm

Adult male: Head and thorax brilliant metallic blue with black lateral stripes. Eyes small and widely separated. Abdomen elongate, mostly blue with black bands at abdominal joints. Wings transparent, with blue tinting in some cells. Body extremely delicate and light.

Adult female: Head and thorax dull olive-green to yellowish-brown with darker markings. Abdomen similar coloration with dark patterns. Two colour forms exist (andromorph and heteromorph).

Damselfly key characters: Wings held folded parallel to the body at rest (unlike dragonflies with wings spread); eyes widely separated on sides of head (not meeting at top); body extremely slender.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Agriocnemis pygmaea is a small, sedentary damselfly of shallow, vegetation-rich wetlands. It is highly specialized for slow-moving or stagnant water with dense submerged and floating plants. The species feeds on tiny insects near the water surface and maintains weak, fluttering flight typical of coenagrionid damselflies. Males perch on vegetation and intercept small flying insects, while females remain among the plants searching for oviposition sites.

The species is a wetland indicator; its presence suggests permanent or semi-permanent water bodies with good vegetative cover. It is sensitive to water pollution and habitat loss (clearing of water plants). In the absence of vegetation, the species does not occur.

Larval Stage

Naiads are slender, greenish-brown, and adapted to slow-moving water. They live among submerged aquatic plants and feed on small aquatic organisms. Larval duration is approximately 8–10 weeks. Emergence occurs year-round, with peaks in early morning hours on vegetation at the water's edge.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Adult prey:

  • Small midges and gnats
  • Tiny mosquitoes
  • Small aphids
  • Minute leafhoppers

Larval prey:

  • Mosquito larvae
  • Small crustaceans (Daphnia and copepods)
  • Tiny aquatic worms

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Small warblers, flycatchers, and swallows
  • Fish: Fry of small fish species
  • Spiders: Tetragnathid spiders on vegetation
  • Larger insects: Dragonfly naiads; robber flies

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

The Pygmy Dartlet is not a documented pest. It feeds on small insects and may assist in controlling mosquito populations in wetlands and irrigated areas. It serves as an indicator of clean water and thriving wetland vegetation, valuable for assessing wetland health. Its presence indicates that the water body has not been heavily polluted.

GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. The species is expected on shallow ponds and marshes with dense vegetation throughout eastern UP, year-round. Look for the tiny, bright blue damselfly hovering weakly over vegetation mats in early morning. A photograph showing the diminutive size and blue-and-black coloration, with date and location, would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia, from Pakistan and India to Myanmar, Thailand, and Indonesia.

In India: Widespread in plains and foothill regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Scattered records in wetland systems.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on vegetation-rich marshes and shallow ponds; likely distributed but unconfirmed locally.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Rambur described Agriocnemis pygmaea in 1842 in Histoire Naturelle des Insectes. Névroptères. Type locality: South Asia. The genus Agriocnemis was erected by Selys Longchamps in 1876 for the smallest coenagrionids.

Subspecies. Agriocnemis pygmaea pygmaea is the nominate subspecies occurring in South Asia.

Family and order placement. Order Odonata (dragonflies and damselflies), suborder Zygoptera (damselflies), family Coenagrionidae (pond damselflies). Coenagrionidae is the largest damselfly family and contains exclusively small damselflies.

Phylogenetic notes. Agriocnemis is a genus of primarily African origin, with a few Asian species. A. pygmaea represents one of the smallest successful damselfly body plans.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC). The species has a broad range and stable populations where suitable habitat remains.
