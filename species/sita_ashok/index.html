सीता अशोक / Ashoka

Saraca asoca (Roxb.) Willd. — Fabaceae

सीता अशोक — हिंदू धर्म में सबसे पवित्र पेड़ों में से एक, जिसको रामायण में सीता का पेड़ माना जाता है। गाँवों और मंदिरों के बाग़ों में लगाया जाता है। पत्तियाँ पंखनुमा, नई पत्तियाँ सुंदर लाल-गुलाबी। फूल नारंगी-लाल, सुगंधित। छायादार और सजावट का पेड़ माना जाता है। परंपरागत दवाई में भी इस्तेमाल होता है।

The testLook for the distinctive young pink leaves and cauliflorous orange flowers

Field tip: In temple gardens in the cool season, look for a tree with brilliant pink-red new foliage and clusters of small orange-red flowers directly on the trunk and thick branches. The sight is unmistakable.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: नई पत्तियाँ गुलाबी-नारंगी। तने और शाखाओं पर सीधे नारंगी-लाल फूलों के गुच्छे। फूल सुगंधित। मंदिरों में सबसे महत्वपूर्ण पेड़।

Trunk and bark: Straight to slightly curved, dark grey, smooth when young, becoming fissured with age. Wood pale, hard.

Leaves: Alternate, abruptly pinnate, with 6–10 pairs of opposite leaflets, no terminal leaflet. Young growth is notably colourful, ranging from red to pink to copper before turning glossy green. Leaflets are elliptic to obovate, entire.

Flowers: Small, bright orange-yellow or orange-red, fragrant, with five sepals and five petals, arranged in dense, many-flowered clusters directly on the trunk and older branches (cauliflory), a feature shared with gular and a few other trees.

Fruit: A flat, elongated follicle (pod) 15–25 cm long, dark brown, woody, containing several large, hard seeds.

हिंदी: नई पत्तियाँ लाल-गुलाबी, फिर हरी। पंखनुमा पत्ती। तने पर सीधे नारंगी फूलों के गुच्छे। फल लंबी, सपाट फली।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ashoka is a tree of tropical and semi-evergreen forests of south and central India, now cultivated across gardens and temple grounds in warmer parts of India. It grows best in warm, moist soils and benefits from regular watering in cultivation. The cauliflorous flowers appear in the cool season and are visited by insects and birds. The large seeds in the mature fruit pods are not readily dispersed; the tree relies on deliberate human planting for its spread beyond its native range.

The tree is valued primarily for its ornamental foliage and flowers, and its sacred status. It is relatively slow-growing and long-lived.

हिंदी: दक्षिण और मध्य भारत का पेड़ है, पर अब उत्तर में भी मंदिरों में पाया जाता है। गर्म और नम जमीन पसंद करता है। ठंड में फूल आते हैं।

हिंदी: अक्टूबर-मार्च में नई पत्तियाँ और फूल। गर्मी में फल पकते हैं। 8-10 साल में फूल आता है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinators: Insects and some birds visit the flowers.
  • Seed dispersal: Large seeds are generally not dispersed by wildlife; human planting is the primary mode.
  • Temple companions: planted in association with other sacred species like False Ashoka and Neem.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating beetles and caterpillars in the monsoon; rarely serious on established trees.
  • Scale insects on branches; aphids on new growth.
  • No significant fungal diseases in warm, well-drained cultivation.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Sacred and Cultural Use (documented):

  • Sacred tree. In Hindu mythology, the ashoka is the tree under which Sita is said to have sheltered during her captivity in Lanka. It is considered one of the five sacred trees (panch vriksha) and is planted in virtually every major temple in India.
  • Ceremonial use. Flowers are offered in worship; leaves are used in rituals. The tree itself is an object of veneration.

Traditional Medicine (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Listed in Ayurvedic texts (Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita) for menstrual complaints and as an astringent for various conditions.
  • Used in traditional formulations, though modern clinical trials are limited.

Today. Grown primarily as an ornamental and sacred tree in temple gardens and educated households across India. The market for the plant is small but steady.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The cultivation history and temple ecology of ashoka plantations would benefit from systematic documentation.

हिंदी: सबसे पवित्र पेड़ है। रामायण में सीता का पेड़ माना जाता है। पूजा के फूल चढ़ते हैं। दवाई में परंपरागत इस्तेमाल है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the deciduous and semi-evergreen forests of the Western Ghats, central India, and parts of northeast India; cultivated throughout tropical and warm subtropical Asia.

In India: Sacred groves and temple gardens throughout the subcontinent; wild populations in the Ghats and central Indian forests.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in temple grounds and gardens, particularly in older sacred groves.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Occasional in temple gardens.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. When exactly do the new leaves turn pink, and how long does the pink colour last? — Observe a tree weekly from October onward.
  2. Which insects visit the cauliflorous flowers, and at what time of day are they most active? — Watch a flowering tree for 30 minutes, multiple times daily.
  3. How long is the flower scent perceptible through the day, and from how far away? — Note the fragrance at different distances and times.
  4. Is the ashoka planted only in major temples, or in small shrines and household compounds too? / क्या यह सिर्फ़ बड़े मंदिरों में है या घरों में भी लगाया जाता है? — Walk the village and record every tree.
  5. How many ashoka trees are in your nearest temple, and can you estimate their age? — Document each tree with location and girth measurements.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by William Roxburgh in 1832 in Flora Indica. The genus name Saraca comes from Sanskrit. The epithet asoca is from the Sanskrit vernacular name.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Fabaceae, the legume or pea family, in the order Fabales. The genus Saraca holds about 15 species in tropical Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Saraca asoca is the type species of the genus, closely related to other south Asian Saraca species.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in cultivation; wild populations in the Ghats and central forests are inadequately surveyed.
