छोटा टिड्डा मेंढक / Nilphamari Narrow-mouthed Frog

Microhyla nilphamariensis Howlader, Nair, Gopalan & Merilä, 2015 — Microhylidae

छोटा टिड्डा मेंढक — बरसात की पहली रात में गाँव के धान के खेतों और पानी के गड्ढों में लाख-दो लाख अंग-अंग मेंढक सुर मिलाकर जो चिड़-चिड़-चिड़ करते हैं, वह ये सब छोटे टिड्डा मेंढक होते हैं। ये इतने छोटे होते हैं कि इन्हें हथेली पर रखो तो एक बूँद जैसे दिखते हैं — 12 मिलीमीटर मतलब एक सेंटीमीटर से भी कम! इनके मुँह इतने छोटे होते हैं कि सिर्फ़ चींटियों और सूक्ष्म कीड़ों को निगल सकते हैं। रंग साधारण भूरा या हरा-भूरा, कोई अलग निशान नहीं — इसीलिए इन्हें घास में खोज पाना बहुत मुश्किल है। जहाँ भी पानी और गीली घास है, वहीं हज़ारों की तादाद में चिड़-चिड़-चिड़-चिड़ करते हैं। कीटनाशक और जलभराव की कमी से इनकी तादाद घट रही है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

ColorationPlain brown, olive-brown, or greyish-green; no prominent markings
MouthExtremely small and round, adapted for small arthropods
EyesVery small, round, positioned high on head

CallRapid, high-pitched "chirp-chirp-chirp" or "ki-ki-ki" in massive choruses

Field tip: An extremely small, plain-colored frog (12–18 mm) that is nearly invisible in grass. Best found by listening for the rapid chirping chorus in monsoon rain pools at night. The call is much faster and higher-pitched than larger frogs.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: अत्यंत छोटा, मटर का दाना जितना। रंग सादा भूरा या हरा, कोई पैटर्न नहीं। मुँह बहुत-बहुत छोटा। तेज़ चिड़-चिड़ आवाज़ हज़ारों की। घास में छिपा।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (northern Indian populations):

MeasurementRange
Snout–vent length (SVL), male12–14 mm
Snout–vent length (SVL), female14–18 mm
Body width8–12 mm
Tadpole, full grown12–15 mm

Body: Extremely small and rotund, with the head barely distinct from the body. The limbs are short and relatively weak. The tympanum (eardrum) is not visible externally.

Coloration: The dorsal surface is plain brown, olive-green, or yellowish-brown, usually without markings or with only very faint dorsal lines. The ventral surface is slightly lighter. There is no sexual dichromatism.

Head and mouth: The snout is very small and pointed. The mouth is minute and round — a specialization for feeding on small arthropods like ants, small beetles, and tiny grasshoppers.

Eyes: Very small and round, positioned high on the sides of the head.

Vocal sac: Males possess a prominent single vocal sac, which greatly expands during calling.

हिंदी: शरीर गोल, पीठ साधारण भूरी या हरी — कोई निशान नहीं। मुँह छोटी बूँद जैसा। आँखें मिलीमीटर के हिसाब से। नर गले के नीचे हवा की थैली।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Cryptic and fossorial. The chorus frog spends most of the year hidden among grass, leaf litter, and soil. Its tiny size and plain coloration provide excellent camouflage.

Explosive congregation. With the first heavy rains of the monsoon, these frogs move to breeding pools en masse. A single pool may contain thousands of calling males, creating a deafening chorus.

Feeding specialist. The tiny mouth is specialized for consuming small arthropods — primarily ants, small beetles, termites, and other minute invertebrates. This feeding specialization is unique among Indian frogs.

Rapid development. Tadpoles are similarly small and develop rapidly, completing metamorphosis in 2–3 weeks in temporary pools that dry quickly.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Adults:

  • Ants (major dietary component)
  • Small beetles
  • Tiny grasshoppers
  • Termites
  • Other small arthropods (small enough to fit the minute mouth)

Tadpoles:

  • Algae and diatoms
  • Detritus on pool bottom
  • Filter-feeding on suspended particles

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Snakes: Banded Krait, Checkered Keelback, grass snake hunt adult frogs
  • Birds: Indian Pond Heron, Spotted Owlet, White Wagtail take toadlets
  • Others: Bengal Monitor, dragonfly nymphs and aquatic beetles prey on tadpoles
  • Large frogs: Indian Bull Frog may opportunistically consume chorus frogs

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest control. Despite their tiny size, chorus frogs consume vast quantities of ants, small beetles, and other crop pests. Their presence in rice paddies is beneficial.

Harmless. The species is completely harmless to humans and poses no threat to any human activity.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: None recorded for the region.

Today. Common in rice-growing areas where breeding pools are available. Declines occur due to wetland destruction, pesticide application (which eliminates both the frogs and their small-arthropod prey), and pool filling.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The breeding ecology and call production of chorus frogs are well described; a local research question is pesticide impact: whether chorus frog populations are higher in pesticide-free rice paddies compared to conventionally sprayed fields.

हिंदी: चीटियों और छोटे कीड़ों को खाते हैं — हज़ारों की संख्या में। खेती में बेहद फ़ायदेमंद। पूरी तरह हानिरहित। कीटनाशक, जलभराव, और गड्ढे भरने से नष्ट हो रहे हैं।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Chorus frogs are expected in rice-paddy areas and monsoon pools throughout Basti district during the breeding season (June–October). They are best found by listening for their rapid "chirp-chirp-chirp" chorus in the evening and at night during rainfall. Individual frogs are nearly impossible to spot without significant searching due to their minute size and cryptic coloration, but the chorus is often loud enough to be heard from a distance. A first local record should include location (field or pool name, block), date, time of night, audio recording of the call, and a photograph showing the frog's tiny size on a fingertip. Please report any sighting to the atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia — India, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, and Southeast Asia.

In India: Widely distributed in lowland wet areas, from the Himalayan foothills to the Deccan, particularly in rice-growing regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in monsoon-pool habitats throughout the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in rice paddies and monsoon pools; distribution poorly documented locally.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. How many chorus frogs are calling in a given pool on the first night of heavy rain? — Record calling intensity in June–July.
  2. What is the most common prey species in the diet of chorus frogs? — Examine stomach contents under a magnifying glass.
  3. How fast do tadpoles develop compared to larger frog species in the same pool? — Compare development rates.
  4. Are chorus frog populations higher in pesticide-free paddies than in sprayed fields? — Compare sightings and call intensity.
  5. What is the ratio of males to females in a breeding pool at a given time? — Monitor sex ratios during breeding.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Howlader, Nair, Gopalan and Merilä in 2015 in PLoS ONE, from Nilphamari in northern Bangladesh, after molecular work split it from Microhyla ornata; most older Indian records of "M. ornata" from the Gangetic plain belong here. The species name nilphamariensis refers to Nilphamari, a region in Bangladesh where the species was first collected.

Subspecies. Microhyla nilphamariensis nilphamariensis — monotypic; no subspecies are currently recognized.

Family and order placement. Microhylidae, a family of small, fossorial, and cryptic frogs with specialized diets; order Anura.

Phylogenetic notes. Microhyla is a genus of Southeast Asian microhylids, with multiple species in India. The chorus frog's minute size and small-arthropod specialization are typical of the genus.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern due to its wide range and stable populations in suitable monsoon-pool habitats. Declines occur locally where wetlands are filled and pesticides contaminate breeding pools.
