काई / Star Jelly

Nostoc commune Vaucher ex Bornet & Flahault — Nostocaceae

ज़मीन की काई — बारिश के बाद आँगन, लॉन या खेत की नंगी ज़मीन पर अचानक उग आई हरी-जैतूनी, जेली जैसी, झुर्रीदार परतें, जो धूप में सूखकर काली पपड़ी बन जाती हैं और अगली बारिश में फिर फूल जाती हैं। यह न पौधा है, न शैवाल — एक जीवाणु है जो हवा से नाइट्रोजन खींचकर मिट्टी को खाद देता है, धान के खेत में चुपचाप। पुरानी कहानियों में इसे "तारों का गिरा हुआ टुकड़ा" कहा गया।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
WetRubbery, gelatinous sheets or lobed blobs 1–10 cm across, olive-green to blue-green or brownish, lying loose on soil
DryShrinks to a thin, brittle, black or dark brown crust that looks like scraps of burnt paper
FeelFirm jelly, slippery, slightly elastic; no roots, no leaves, no smell
Under a lensMade of countless beaded threads in jelly; a hand lens shows the granular look
WhereBare damp soil in the open: courtyards, path edges, lawns, bunds, field surfaces
WhenAppears within hours of rain and vanishes into crust within a day or two of sun

Field tip: The morning after a shower, look at any bare patch of a courtyard or bund for olive-green jelly that was not there yesterday. Nothing else appears so fast. Pick a piece up: it is rubbery and whole, unlike the slime of true algae on a pond.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बारिश के अगले दिन आँगन या मेड़ की नंगी मिट्टी पर जैतूनी-हरी, रबड़ जैसी, झुर्रीदार परतें जो कल नहीं थीं; धूप में काली पपड़ी। उठाइए — पूरी की पूरी उठ जाती है, तालाब की चिकनी काई की तरह नहीं।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (temperate and tropical material):

MeasurementRange
Colony diameter1–10 cm, sheets to 30 cm
Colony thickness, wet1–5 mm
Cell diameter4–6 µm
Heterocyst diameter6–7 µm
Filamenthundreds of cells, coiled

Structure: A colony is a sheath of firm jelly (a polysaccharide) packed with bent, beaded filaments of round cells. At intervals along each filament sit larger, pale, thick-walled cells, the heterocysts, which are the nitrogen-fixing factories, kept oxygen-free inside their walls. Resting cells (akinetes) let the colony survive drying.

Pigments: Chlorophyll a with the blue-green phycocyanin and, in sun-exposed colonies, a brown sunscreen pigment (scytonemin) in the sheath, which gives the dark colour.

Drying and reviving: The colony loses nearly all its water and can lie dormant for years; within an hour of wetting it swells and photosynthesis restarts. Herbarium specimens over a century old have revived.

हिंदी: जेली के खोल में मनकों जैसी कोशिकाओं के धागे; बीच-बीच में बड़ी, पीली, मोटी दीवार वाली कोशिकाएँ (हेटरोसिस्ट) जो नाइट्रोजन बाँधती हैं। धूप से बचने के लिए खोल में भूरा रंग। सूखकर सालों पड़ी रह सकती है और भीगते ही घंटे भर में जी उठती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Among the oldest kinds of life on earth and among the most useful. Nostoc and its relatives fix atmospheric nitrogen into forms plants can use, which is why flooded paddy fields that receive no fertiliser still yield: a living blue-green film on the water and mud adds nitrogen every season, and Nostoc is a regular part of it. Indian agronomists have inoculated rice fields with blue-green algae for that reason since the 1950s.

On dry land the colonies are the first thing to colonise bare, trodden, nutrient-poor soil, binding the surface against erosion and feeding snails, springtails and mites. They tolerate heat, drought, frost and ultraviolet that kill almost everything else.

हिंदी: पृथ्वी के सबसे पुराने जीवों में से, और सबसे उपयोगी में से। धान के पानी भरे खेत में यह और इसके जैसे नील-हरित जीवाणु हवा की नाइट्रोजन मिट्टी में डालते हैं — इसीलिए बिना खाद के भी धान होता है; भारत में 1950 के दशक से खेतों में ये जानबूझकर डाले जाते रहे हैं। सूखी ज़मीन पर ये नंगी मिट्टी को बाँधते हैं और घोंघों और मिट्टी के कीड़ों का भोजन बनते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Month / seasonEvent
June–JulyCrusts swell with the first rains; growth and division of filaments
July–OctoberColonies grow, fragment and spread across wet ground and paddy surfaces
November–MayDormant crusts; brief revival after winter showers
Any wettingRevives within an hour

Reproduces by fragmentation of colonies and by hormogonia, short motile lengths of filament that glide away to found new colonies.

हिंदी: बारिश में फूलकर बढ़ती है, टूटकर फैलती है; सूखे में पपड़ी बनकर सोई रहती है; जब भी भीगे, घंटे भर में फिर ज़िंदा।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • The crop it feeds: Paddy, alongside Water Fern, whose leaves house a related nitrogen-fixer.
  • Grazers: small snails such as the young of Freshwater Apple Snail, springtails, mites.
  • Ground companions: Doob Grass seedlings and mosses that follow it onto bare soil.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Grazed by snails and soil arthropods; attacked by fungi and viruses specific to cyanobacteria. Nothing a person would notice.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Rice fertility. The real use, though unseen: blue-green algal nitrogen fixation in paddy has been measured at tens of kilograms of nitrogen per hectare per season, and "algalisation" with Nostoc, Anabaena and relatives is an established, if under-used, practice in Indian rice.
  • Food. Nostoc commune is eaten in China, Japan, Java and the Andes as a vegetable and in soups; the related Nostoc flagelliforme ("hair vegetable") is a Chinese delicacy. Not a recorded food in Uttar Pradesh.
  • Folklore. In Europe the jelly that "fell with the rain" was star jelly, the remains of a shooting star; in India it is simply kai and ignored.
  • Soil crusts. Cyanobacterial crusts protect bare and degraded land from erosion and are used in dryland restoration.

Caution. Some Nostoc strains contain BMAA and microcystin-type toxins; wild colonies should not be eaten without identification and the traditional preparation.

Today. Not used in the district except, unknowingly, in every paddy field.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Cyanobacterial biofertiliser for rice is a mature Indian research line (the ICAR Blue-Green Algae programme); the current interest is in strains that also tolerate herbicides and in cyanobacterial crusts for reclaiming eroded and sodic land. A farmer can test the first part in a single field.

हिंदी: असली उपयोग दिखता नहीं: धान के पानी में नील-हरित जीवाणु हर मौसम हवा से नाइट्रोजन खींचकर मिट्टी में डालते हैं; इन्हें खेत में डालना एक जाना-माना पर कम अपनाया गया तरीका है। चीन-जापान में यह सब्ज़ी है, यहाँ नहीं। कुछ किस्मों में ज़हर हो सकता है, इसलिए बिना पहचान के न खाएँ। मिट्टी की पपड़ी बनाकर कटाव रोकती है।

To be expected on bare damp ground across the district after any rain, in courtyards, on bunds, on brick paths and on the drying surface of paddy fields. A photograph of the jelly the morning after a shower is a confirmed record; a dried crust is not, since several things look alike dry.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Worldwide, from the tropics to Antarctica.

In India: Throughout, in every wet season.

In Uttar Pradesh: Everywhere; the rice belt especially.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on every wet bare surface.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many hours after rain does the jelly appear? / बारिश के कितने घंटे बाद जेली दिखती है? — Check a known patch every two hours.
  2. How many days of sun turn it back to crust? — Keep watching.
  3. Does a dried crust kept in a jar for a year revive with water? — Try it.
  4. Is there a blue-green film on the flooded paddy field, and when is it thickest? / धान के खेत के पानी पर हरी-नीली परत है क्या, और कब सबसे मोटी? — Look weekly from transplanting to flowering.
  5. What eats it? — Turn a wet colony over and see what is underneath.

Field rule: Olive-green rubbery jelly lying loose on bare soil the morning after rain, gone to black crust in the sun, is Nostoc.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Named by Jean-Pierre Vaucher in 1803 (Histoire des Conferves d'Eau Douce, p. 222) and validly published by Bornet & Flahault in 1888 (Annales des Sciences Naturelles, Botanique, sér. 7, 7: 203). "Nostoc" is a word coined by Paracelsus; commune, "common".

Subspecies. Several forms and varieties have been named; molecular work shows Nostoc commune as currently understood to be a complex of several lineages.

Family and order placement. Not a plant at all but a cyanobacterium: kingdom Bacteria, phylum Cyanobacteria, order Nostocales, family Nostocaceae. Kept under "algae" in this atlas, as in field practice, because it is found and studied with them.

Phylogenetic notes. Cyanobacteria invented oxygen-producing photosynthesis over two billion years ago, and the chloroplasts of every plant descend from a captured cyanobacterium; Nostoc lineages also live inside liverworts, cycads and lichens as nitrogen-fixing partners.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated; micro-organisms are not assessed.
