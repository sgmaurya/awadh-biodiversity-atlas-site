अंतरी चूहा / Indian Gerbil

Tatera indica (Hardwicke, 1807) — Muridae

अंतरी चूहा — सूखी और बंजर जमीन की आम चूहा, जो घास के दाने, फसलें, और सब्ज़ियों को खाकर किसानों का बहुत नुकसान करता है। नाम 'अंतरी' सायद 'एंट्री' यानी घर में घुसता है — इसीलिए पड़ा। पूँछ लंबी, पतली, धारियों वाली। कान बड़े, आँखें गहरी और चमकीली। शरीर हल्के भूरे रंग का, पर पीठ पर गहरे भूरे रंग की एक पट्टी चलती है। दिन में गहरे बिलों में सोता है, रात को खेतों और अनाज भंडारों में निकल आता है। एक बिल से सैकड़ों मीटर दूर तक अपना साम्राज्य बसा लेता है। बहुत तेज़ दौड़ता है, कूदता है, और मिट्टी के बिल खोदने में माहिर है। साँपों, उल्लुओं, बिल्लियों और लोमड़ियों का खाना है।

Field tip: A large, tawny or sandy-colored mouse with a long thin tail, large ears, and a dark stripe along its back in arid fields or near grain stores. Most active at dusk and dawn. Look for burrows with a scattered mound of soil around the entrance.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (northern Indian populations):

Body: Relatively large for a rodent, with a robust frame adapted for running and jumping. The fur is soft and dense.

Coloration: The dorsal surface is greyish-brown, buffy-brown, or sandy brown. A prominent dark brown or blackish longitudinal stripe runs along the spine. The ventral surface (belly) is white, cream, or pale grey. The tail is long, thin, dark brown or blackish, and finely haired.

Limbs: The hind limbs are particularly strong and long, adapted for kangaroo-like jumping. The forelimbs are shorter. All feet have five toes with small claws.

Head: The head is rounded. The ears are moderately large and somewhat translucent, pinkish-brown in color. The eyes are large and prominent, reflecting the species' nocturnal habit. The snout is pointed.

Dentition: Two pairs of continuously growing upper incisors; molars with complex grinding surfaces for processing hard seeds.

हिंदी: मजबूत शरीर, हल्का-भूरा रंग, पीठ पर काली पट्टी। पूँछ लंबी, पतली, काली। कान गुलाबी-भूरे। आँखें बड़ी। पिछली टाँगें लंबी, उछलने के लिए।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Nocturnal forager. The Indian gerbil is primarily nocturnal, emerging from its burrows at dusk to forage in fields and grain stores. It is an excellent runner and jumper, capable of bounding considerable distances when threatened.

Burrower. Creates complex burrow systems in dry soil, loose sand, and abandoned rodent tunnels. Burrow entrances are often marked by small mounds of excavated soil. A single animal may maintain multiple burrows within its home range.

Granivore and seed-hoarder. Feeds primarily on grain, seeds, and crop material. It stores food in its burrows for consumption during lean seasons, and inadvertently disperses large seeds far from parent plants.

Social structure. Largely solitary or in pairs, though multiple individuals may inhabit nearby burrows. Territorial; males defend areas against other males.

Reproduction. Breeds year-round, with peaks in spring and fall. Females give birth to litters of 2–8 young after a gestation of about 24 days. Multiple litters per year are possible.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Major agricultural pest. The Indian gerbil is one of the most significant agricultural rodent pests in India, responsible for substantial crop losses annually. A single gerbil may consume 15–30 grams of grain per day, and a population of thousands can devastate a grain field or storage facility.

Damage profile. Losses occur both in the field (growing crops) and in post-harvest grain stores. The rodent also contaminates grain with feces and urine, reducing its quality and creating a disease hazard.

Plague vector and pathogen reservoir. While not currently a major plague vector in India, the gerbil is known to carry bacterial, viral, and parasitic pathogens that can affect livestock and humans.

Control methods. Traditional methods include trap-and-kill, poisoning, and habitat modification. However, gerbil populations are resilient, and sustainable control requires integrated pest management combining habitat management, cultural practices (proper grain storage), and controlled use of rodenticides.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: The species is hunted for food in some regions, though not commonly in eastern UP.

Today. Populations fluctuate seasonally and in response to crop conditions. Rodent damage is a persistent problem in agricultural areas, with losses increasing during years of good harvest (more food for rodents) and in improperly stored grain.

Indian gerbils are expected in dry agricultural fields, grain stores, and wastelands throughout Basti district. They are most visible at dusk and dawn when they emerge to forage, or when grain is being threshed and stored. Burrows with sandy mounds around the entrance are a good indicator of activity. A first local record should include location (field name or block), date, time of day, habitat type (field crop, grain store, or wasteland), and a photograph. Please report any sighting to the atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South Asia — India, Pakistan, Nepal. Widely distributed across the Indian subcontinent.

In India: Found from the foothills of the Himalayas to the Deccan, particularly in dry and semi-arid zones. Present in most states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread in agricultural areas and dry regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in dry fields, wastelands, and grain stores; common in agricultural areas.

Field rule: A tawny-brown mouse (15–20 cm) with a long thin tail, large ears, large dark eyes, and a dark stripe along its back in arid fields or grain stores is an Indian gerbil. It runs and bounds rapidly; most visible at dusk and dawn.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Thomas Hardwicke in 1807 in Transactions of the Linnean Society. The genus Tatera contains several African and Asian gerbil species; indica was named for its Indian distribution.

Subspecies. Tatera indica indica — the nominate subspecies found in India. Monotypic; no subspecies are currently recognized.

Family and order placement. Muridae, the mice and rats; order Rodentia.

Phylogenetic notes. Tatera is a genus of Old World gerbils specialized for arid and semi-arid habitats. The Indian gerbil is the easternmost representative of the genus in its natural range.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern due to its wide range, stable population, and ability to thrive in human-modified habitats. No major conservation threats exist.
