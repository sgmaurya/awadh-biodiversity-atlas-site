तुरुमती / Red-necked Falcon

Falco chicquera Daudin, 1800 — Falconidae

तुरुमती — यह छोटा और मजबूत बाज़ खुले मैदानों में शिकार करता है। इसका नाम इसकी लाल गर्दन से आता है। यह तेज़ उड़ने वाली चिड़िया है और अचानक गोता लगाकर छोटी चिड़ियों या तोते पकड़ता है। इसके पंख नीले-काले होते हैं और पेट सफ़ेद होता है। सिर पर काली मूँछ जैसी धारियाँ होती हैं। ये आमतौर पर खेतों के ऊपर उड़ती हैं और अपनी तेज़ आवाज़ निकालती हैं।

Field tip: A small, compact falcon with distinctive rufous cap and nape, blue-grey back, and white underparts with fine barring. Easily identified by the combination of rufous head and blue-grey back. Often seen soaring over open grasslands and cultivated areas. Powerful flier; hunts by making swift stoops from height. Watch for the characteristic rapid, powerful wingbeats and sudden dives.

Size: 25–28 cm long; wingspan 53–62 cm. A compact, muscular falcon.

Plumage: Head rufous or red-tinged crown and nape — the diagnostic feature. Black "moustache" marks extending from lores past eye. Back and wings slate-blue or blue-grey. Rump blue-grey. Tail blue-grey with darker bands. Breast whitish to buff. Belly white. Flanks with fine rufous barring.

Head: Rufous or red cap and nape (diagnostic); black moustache marks.

Eyes: Dark, intense; forward-facing raptor eyes.

Bill: Short, powerful, hooked; used for tearing prey.

Feet: Yellow or greenish-yellow. Powerful talons for gripping small birds.

Wings: Slate-blue, pointed; built for speed and agility.

Tail: Relatively short, square-cut, blue-grey with dark bands.

Sexes: Females larger than males; similar plumage.

Vocalisations. Vocal, especially around nest:

  1. Call: Loud, harsh screeching or kee-kee-kee, given in flight and perched.
  2. Alarm: Sharp, rapid calls when disturbed or defending territory.
  3. Contact: Softer calls between mates.
हिंदी: तेज़ "की-की-की" या शरारती कूकना। बहुत बोलती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Aerial predator: Hunts small birds in flight. Preferred prey includes:

  • Pigeons and doves (especially young and small species)
  • Small parrots
  • Mynas and starlings
  • Small songbirds
  • Occasionally insects

Hunting technique: Soars at height, hunting by sight. Makes swift, controlled stoops (dives) from height to catch prey in mid-air or flush birds from ground cover. Pursues prey with great agility.

Flight: Swift, agile flight; capable of rapid turns and dives. Strong, direct flier.

Perching: Often perches on exposed vantage points (trees, poles, wires) to scan for prey.

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs. Territorial during breeding season.

Habitat: Prefers open grasslands and scrubland with scattered trees for hunting and perching.

हिंदी: खुले आकाश में छोटी चिड़ियों का शिकार करता है। तेज़ी से गोता लगाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round in suitable habitat; some local seasonal movements with prey availability.

Breeding: February to June; peak March–May. Nests in cavities, cliff ledges, or abandoned nests of other raptors.

Eggs: Typically 2–4 reddish-brown or buff eggs with darker markings.

Incubation: Approximately 28–32 days by both parents.

Chicks: Altricial; brooded by parents. Fed on small birds brought by parents.

Fledging: Young fledge at approximately 5–6 weeks.

Post-fledging: Young remain with parents for several weeks after fledging.

Seasonal activity: More vocal and active during breeding season.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Listed in the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. All wild birds are protected; hunting is illegal.

Ecological role: Controls small bird populations; role in ecosystem dynamics.

Declining trend: The species is listed as Near Threatened (NT) globally due to declines in some regions. Causes include:

  • Pesticide use (affecting prey populations)
  • Habitat loss and degradation
  • Direct persecution (rare)
  • Electrocution on power lines

The Red-necked Falcon is expected as a resident of open grasslands and cultivated areas of eastern Uttar Pradesh throughout the year. Look for this small falcon soaring over grasslands and scrubland, hunting from height. The distinctive rufous cap and nape, combined with the blue-grey back, are diagnostic. Often seen perching on trees or poles scanning for small birds. Listen for the loud, harsh calls. The species hunts primarily on the wing by making swift stoops to capture small birds. A first record would require a clear photograph showing the rufous cap, black moustache marks, blue-grey back, and white underparts, with place and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent and parts of Southeast Asia. Resident to partially migratory.

In India: Distributed throughout suitable habitat in the peninsula and plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Distributed in suitable grassland and scrubland habitat; present year-round.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident in open grasslands and cultivated areas. Present throughout the year.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by François-Marie Daudin in 1800 in Traité Élémentaire et Complet d'Ornithologie as Falco chicquera. Type locality: India.

Subspecies. Two subspecies; F. c. chicquera occurs in India.

Family and order placement. Order Falconiformes; family Falconidae (falcons and caracaras). The genus Falco contains the "true" falcons.

Phylogenetic notes. Red-necked Falcon belongs to the Falco genus and is related to other small falcons including kestrels.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Near Threatened (NT). The global population shows declines in some regions due to habitat loss, pesticide use, and human persecution in some areas. The species is dependent on open grassland habitat.
