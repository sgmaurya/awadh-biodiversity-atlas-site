डेंडुआ / Gangetic Scissortail

Rasbora rasbora (Hamilton, 1822) — Cyprinidae

डेंडुआ — छोटी चाँदी रंग की मछली जो गंगा प्रणाली की नदियों में पाई जाती है। पूँछ की दो पत्तियाँ काली धारियों से सजी होती हैं, जिससे यह कैंची जैसी दिखती है। झुंड में तैरती है, जलीय पौधों को खाती है, पकवान में स्वादिष्ट।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size10–15 cm
BodySlender, laterally compressed; streamlined
ColorationSilvery-grey body with darker back; sides faintly marked
TailForked, with black or dark bands on both lobes (scissor-like pattern)
ScalesSmall, cycloid; body covered uniformly
FinsDorsal and anal fins small; caudal fin deeply forked

Field tip: A small silvery fish found in schools in slow-moving water. The distinctive forked tail with dark bands resembles scissors. Found in monsoon-fed ponds and river margins in eastern UP. The small size and scissor-tail pattern are diagnostic.

हिंदी: छोटी चाँदी मछली; पूँछ पर काली धारियाँ; झुंड में तैरती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (field populations):

MeasurementRange
Standard length10–15 cm
Weight15–35 g
Dorsal fin rays8–10
Anal fin rays8–10

Body structure: Slender, fusiform, laterally compressed. Streamlined for fast swimming in open water. Eyes moderately large; mouth small, terminal.

Coloration: Dorsal surface dark grey or greenish-brown; sides silvery with faint longitudinal stripes. Belly white or silvery. Caudal lobes with black or dark brown band on the outer margin of each lobe.

Fins: Dorsal fin small, located behind the pelvic fins. Anal fin small. Caudal fin deeply forked, the scissor-like pattern is highly distinctive.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A schooling fish that feeds primarily on algae, plant matter, and small aquatic invertebrates. Lives in slow-moving waters and monsoon-fed ponds; prefers water bodies with abundant aquatic vegetation. Forms large, synchronized schools; darting and turning in unison when alarmed. Tolerates a wide range of water conditions. Feeds by browsing on algae-covered rocks and vegetation.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Herons, cormorants, and kingfishers
  • People: Hand-netted or trapped for food

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food. An important food fish in local fisheries; sold fresh at markets. Small but palatable; often cooked whole or in mixed fish preparations. Nutritious and protein-rich.

Protection status. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Aquaculture. Used as a subsidiary fish in polyculture ponds for its algae-eating ability, helping to maintain water quality and reduce algal blooms.

Today. Abundant in suitable habitat during monsoon but less common in dry season ponds. Declining in some areas due to habitat loss and water pollution.

हिंदी: छोटी पकवान वाली मछली; झुंड में मिलती है। विधि अधिनियम 1972 के अंतर्गत संरक्षित है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. To be expected in monsoon-fed ponds and river margins throughout the district, particularly in areas with aquatic vegetation. Most abundant July–September. A photograph or net catch with place and date is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Gangetic system from Pakistan through northern India to Bangladesh and Bhutan.

In India: Throughout the Ganga and Brahmaputra systems and associated wetlands.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in rivers and tanks of the eastern plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in ponds and river margins; more abundant in monsoon-fed water bodies.

Field rule: A small silvery minnow with a deeply forked tail bearing black bands on both lobes, found in schools in open water, is a Gangetic Scissortail.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches. Originally placed in Cyprinus; transferred to Rasbora by later taxonomists. The genus name Rasbora comes from its local name.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Cyprinidae, the carp and minnow family; order Cypriniformes. Rasbora is a genus of small Asian minnows.

Phylogenetic notes. Rasbora rasbora belongs to a group of Asian cyprinids adapted to fast-flowing and monsoon-fed waters.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Widespread and abundant in suitable freshwater habitat across the Gangetic system; stable in range.
