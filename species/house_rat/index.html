घर का चूहा / House Rat

Rattus rattus (Linnaeus, 1758) — Muridae

घर का चूहा (या काला चूहा) — हर घर, हर खलिहान, हर अनाज की मांड में रहने वाला सबसे आम चूहा, जो रात भर अनाज खाता है और बीमारी फैलाता है। काली-भूरी पीठ, सफ़ेद या धूसर पेट, और लंबी नंगी पूँछ जो शरीर की लंबाई से बड़ी होती है। चिकने बाल, छोटे कान, तेज़ दाँत, और तेज़ नाक से खतरे को सूँघ लेता है। रात को गोदामों में दौड़ता है, खाने के दाने खोता है, अनाज को बर्बाद करता है, और अपने मल और पेशाब से खतरनाक बीमारियाँ फैलाता है (प्लेग, लेप्टोस्पाइरोसिस, साल्मोनेला)। खेतों में गन्ने की जड़ें काटता है। प्रत्येक परिवार साल में औसतन 50 किलो अनाज खो देता है इसी चूहे के कारण। पर यह चूहा Barn Owl और नेवला Small Indian Mongoose का सबसे महत्वपूर्ण भोजन भी है।

Field tip: A dark-furred rat with a tail as long as its body, found in grain stores and climbing rafters at night. The long naked tail and white belly distinguish it from the bandicoot rat, which has a shorter tail and coarse fur. Look for droppings and gnaw marks on wooden structures.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: काली-भूरी पीठ, पेट सफ़ेद, लंबी नंगी पूँछ जो शरीर से बड़ी। चिकने बाल। खलिहान और गोदाम में रात को दिखता है। घुँस से छोटा, पूँछ लंबी।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for local populations):

MeasurementRange
Head-body length17–24 cm
Tail length18–25 cm
Weight75–230 g

Body: Slender and graceful compared to the more robust bandicoot rat. The dorsal fur is dark grey-brown, sometimes black; the ventral fur is white, cream, or buff. Fur is smooth and glossy.

Head: Pointed, with a relatively narrow skull. Whiskers are long, sensitive, and used to navigate in complete darkness. The ears are medium-sized, nearly hairless, and prominent.

Tail: Long, naked (covered with scales, not fur), and prehensile for climbing; always longer than the head-body length—a key character separating it from the bandicoot rat (Bandicota bengalensis), which has a shorter tail.

Incisors: Large, bright yellow to orange, and continuously growing. Gnaw marks in food stores are diagnostic.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are slightly larger than females.

हिंदी: पतला शरीर, लंबी नंगी पूँछ, चिकने बाल, तेज़ दाँत।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

An obligate commmensal of humans; most populations live entirely in and around human dwellings, grain stores, and agricultural areas. Nocturnal, emerging after dusk to forage. Shy and alert; a startled house rat bolts immediately to the nearest hiding place.

An agile climber using its long tail for balance, able to scale vertical ropes and wires, traverse rafters, and access grain stored in elevated granaries. Excellent sense of smell and hearing; it detects changes in its environment and avoids new traps for several days (neophobia—fear of new things).

Omnivorous scavenger, but prefers cereal grains, pulses, and oilseeds. Caches food in burrows or hidden chambers. The amount of grain taken is substantial: a single rat may consume 20–30 g per day, but waste (gnawing, fouling) can be 5–10 times the amount eaten.

Prolific breeder: females can produce 6–8 litters per year with 6–12 young per litter.

Disease vectors: The house rat is a reservoir and vector for multiple serious human diseases: plague (Yersinia pestis), leptospirosis, salmonellosis, typhus, and hantavirus. Fleas associated with rats transmit plague; rat urine contaminates water and food.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Cereal grains (wheat, rice, maize)
  • Pulses and oilseeds
  • Groundnuts and legumes
  • Sugarcane (gnaws the stalk)
  • Roots and tubers
  • Plant seeds (wild and cultivated)
  • Insects, lizard eggs, and bird eggs
  • Any food scraps in human dwellings

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds of prey: Barn Owl, Spotted Owlet, Black Kite
  • Snakes: Indian Rat Snake, Common Wolf Snake, Indian Cobra
  • Mammals: Small Indian Mongoose, small Indian civet, wild cat, feral dog
  • Snakes and other predators: House centipede; very large huntsman spiders
  • People: Killed with traps, poisons, and manual methods

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Damage: The house rat is one of the most economically significant agricultural pests in eastern Uttar Pradesh. In grain stores the losses are substantial; in the field, damage to standing crops, newly planted seeds, and root crops is severe. Sugarcane farmers report significant loss from gnawed stalks.

Disease risk: Far more important than the direct food loss is the disease vector role. Plague is now rare but persists as a zoonotic disease in wild rodent populations; any outbreak in the district would involve house rats as vectors. Leptospirosis from rat urine is a serious risk in areas with poor sanitation.

Control: Farmers use traps (traditional and spring traps), rodenticides (poison baits), and habitat management (removing shelter). However, the single most effective control is maintaining populations of the Barn Owl and Small Indian Mongoose — natural predators that hunt rats continuously.

Today. The house rat persists universally. Control depends on community-level effort and maintaining healthy predator populations. Individual household trapping is necessary but insufficient unless coordinated across villages.

Research direction: Understanding which barn owl and snake populations remain in Basti and supporting their protection would be more effective and sustainable than individual poison efforts.

Universal in all blocks of Basti district. Present in every grain store, in house rafters, in agricultural warehouses, and in field margins. Most active at night; seen occasionally during day in places where they feel secure.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Originally native to Southeast Asia; now found worldwide as a commensal, following humans and ship traffic to every continent except Antarctica.

In India: Universal throughout the country, from the plains to 2,000+ m elevation in settlements.

In Uttar Pradesh: Universal across all districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Universal; present in all blocks and all habitations.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. How many rats are caught in traps in a standard grain store during a two-month period, and what is the sex ratio? / एक मानक खलिहान में दो महीने में कितने चूहे पकड़े जाते हैं? — Set standard traps and monitor daily.
  2. What percentage of stored grain is lost to rats annually in a village, and what is the monetary value? / गाँव में सालाना कितना अनाज चूहों के कारण खो जाता है? — Weigh grain before and after storage.
  3. How many barn owls are still nesting in the district, and where do they hunt? / पूरे जिले में कितने शाही उल्लू ईंट के गोदामों में घोंसला बनाते हैं? — Survey known barn owl roost sites.
  4. Do villages with active mongoose populations have lower rat populations than those without? — Survey rat signs (droppings, gnaw marks) in villages with and without mongooses.
  5. What are the main entry points for house rats into grain stores, and how can they be sealed? — Inspect a store and propose modifications.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition, as Mus rattus. The epithet rattus is from an Old English root for "rat."

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies are recognized across the range; the Indian populations are usually assigned to Rattus rattus rattus, the nominate form.

Family and order placement. Muridae, the family of mice and rats (the largest mammalian family); order Rodentia, class Mammalia.

Phylogenetic notes. Rattus rattus is part of the Rattus genus, which diversified in Southeast Asia. The house rat represents one of the earliest rodent commensals with humans, possibly following agriculture from its origin point.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Extremely widespread, abundant, and adaptable. No conservation concern; if anything, control is sought rather than protection.
