शिकाकाई / Shikakai

Senegalia rugata (Lam.) Britton & Rose — Fabaceae

शिकाकाई — काँटेदार, चढ़ने वाली झाड़ी जिसकी सूखी फलियाँ पीसकर बाल धोने का सबसे पुराना "शैम्पू" बनता है। पत्तियाँ इमली जैसी बारीक, डालियाँ मुड़े हुए काँटों से भरी, फूल सफ़ेद-पीले गोल गुच्छे, और फलियाँ मोटी, झुर्रीदार, भूरी। दक्षिण भारत का पौधा, पर उत्तर में बाड़ों और बाग़ों में लगाया जाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
HabitScrambling, thorny shrub or climber, 3–5 m, sprawling over hedges and trees
PricklesShort, hooked, scattered along the stems and leaf stalks; they catch cloth and skin
LeavesTwice-pinnate, 10–20 cm, with 5–7 pairs of pinnae and many tiny leaflets 5–10 mm; a gland on the leaf stalk
FlowersCream to pale yellow, in round fluffy heads 1 cm across, several on a stalk, March–May
PodsFleshy, thick, 7–12 × 2–3 cm, constricted between seeds, wrinkled and brown when dry
Seeds6–10 per pod, dark, flattened, 8 mm
The testA dry pod broken in water froths slightly

Field tip: On a hedge or an old garden wall, a feathery-leaved climber whose stems are armed with small hooked prickles and whose pods look like fat, wrinkled, brown fingers. In eastern UP it is a planted or escaped plant, not a wild one, and the pods, sold in the bazaar as shikakai, are the surer sign of it than the plant.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बाड़ पर चढ़ी काँटेदार बेल; पत्तियाँ इमली जैसी बारीक; फूल गोल रुई जैसे; फलियाँ मोटी, टेढ़ी, झुर्रीदार। सूखी फली को पानी में तोड़िए तो हल्का झाग आता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (Indian plants, cultivated and wild):

MeasurementRange
Stem length3–5 m, occasionally more as a climber
Leaf length10–20 cm
Leaflet5–10 × 1.5–3 mm
Flower head diameter8–12 mm
Pod7–12 × 2–3 cm
Seed7–9 mm

Stems: Woody, angled when young, grey-brown, armed with short recurved prickles that allow the plant to scramble through other vegetation.

Leaves: Bipinnate, with a raised gland on the petiole and often on the rachis between the upper pinnae, which is a useful character against other acacias. Leaflets numerous, linear-oblong, closing somewhat at night.

Flowers: In globose heads of many small five-parted flowers with long stamens that give the fluffy look. Fragrant, bee-pollinated.

Pods: Straight or slightly curved, thick and somewhat fleshy, becoming leathery and wrinkled; the pod wall holds the saponins. Pods remain on the plant for weeks.

हिंदी: तना काँटेदार और सहारे पर चढ़ने वाला; पत्ती के डंठल पर एक छोटी गाँठ (ग्रंथि) होती है जो दूसरी बबूल-जैसी बेलों से अलग करती है। फली का छिलका ही साबुन है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A plant of the deciduous forests and scrub of peninsular and south-eastern India and south-east Asia, long grown further north for its pods. It climbs by its prickles, fixes nitrogen through root nodules like other legumes, and makes a dense, protective tangle in a hedge that small birds such as Ashy Prinia and Common Tailorbird nest in. The spring flower heads draw Asian Honey Bee and other bees.

It is hardy in heat and drought once established and tolerates poor soil, but it does not stand waterlogging, so in the district it belongs to raised homestead land and hedge lines, not to the paddy flats.

हिंदी: यह दक्षिण भारत के सूखे जंगलों की बेल है जो उत्तर में बाड़ में लगाई जाती है। जड़ों में नाइट्रोजन बाँधती है, घनी काँटेदार बाड़ बनाती है जिसमें छोटी चिड़ियाँ घोंसला बनाती हैं। पानी भरा खेत इसे नहीं सुहाता।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Month / seasonEvent

From seed, first pods in three to four years. Seeds germinate well after soaking.

हिंदी: बसंत में फूल, बरसात में फली बनती है, सर्दी में पकती है। बीज से तीन-चार साल में फली आने लगती है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Bees: Asian Honey Bee, Dwarf Honey Bee on the spring flowers.
  • Hedge birds: Ashy Prinia, Common Tailorbird and House Sparrow use the thorny tangle for cover and nesting.
  • Hedge neighbours: often planted with Indian Jujube, Babool and Lantana in a living fence.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-feeding caterpillars and leaf-rolling moth larvae in the monsoon.
  • Pod borers reduce the harvest in some years.
  • Powdery mildew on new growth in humid, still weather. Generally a tough plant with few serious problems.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Hair wash. The dried pods, ground to a powder and soaked, are the classical Indian hair cleanser: they lather mildly, do not strip oil, and were used across the subcontinent long before soap. Often combined with Indian Gooseberry and reetha. This is the main trade in the plant.
  • Washing silk and wool. As with reetha, the saponins clean delicate fibres without harshness.
  • Traditional medicine. The pods are recorded as a laxative and for skin complaints in Kirtikar & Basu; the young leaves are eaten as a sour vegetable and chutney in the south. Pod water is irritant to the eyes.
  • Hedge. Its prickles and dense growth make a stock-proof living fence.
  • Nitrogen. As a legume it improves the soil along the boundary where it grows.

Today. Shikakai powder and shikakai shampoos are a steady trade in India; most pods come from Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Maharashtra, where the plant is harvested from scrub and small plantations.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Shikakai saponins are studied as mild surfactants and foaming agents in cosmetics; laboratory work exists. The question for a Basti self-help group is agronomic, not chemical: does the plant fruit reliably in this climate on hedge lines, and what does a kilogram of dried pods fetch against the southern supply?

हिंदी: शिकाकाई बाल धोने की फली है — आँवला और रीठे के साथ। रेशम और ऊन भी इससे धुलते हैं। काँटों की वजह से यह अच्छी बाड़ भी है और फलीदार होने से ज़मीन सुधारती है। बाज़ार का ज़्यादातर शिकाकाई दक्षिण से आता है; यहाँ यह कितना फल देगी, यह देखने की बात है।

Not a native of the Gangetic plain. To be expected, if at all, as a planted hedge or garden climber on homestead land, or as an escape on a grove margin. The dried pods in the bazaar are certain; the living plant in the district is the thing to look for and record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Tropical Asia from India and Sri Lanka through Myanmar to Indo-China, southern China and Malesia.

In India: Wild in the deciduous forests and scrub of the peninsula, the Eastern Ghats and the north-east; cultivated elsewhere.

In Uttar Pradesh: Grown here and there in gardens and hedges; not recorded as wild.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected only as a planted or escaped plant.

Field rule: A prickly feathery-leaved climber with cream flower heads and thick wrinkled brown pods that froth in water is shikakai; the thin papery pods of its wild cousin do not.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Lamarck in 1783 as Mimosa rugata in the Encyclopédie Méthodique, Botanique 1: 20. Long known as Acacia concinna (Willd.) DC., the name under which it appears in the Indian floras and the trade. Transferred to Senegalia by Britton & Rose (1928, North American Flora 23: 120) when the climbing, prickled acacias were separated from the true Acacia.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Fabaceae, subfamily Caesalpinioideae, mimosoid clade; order Fabales. Senegalia holds the prickled, non-spiny former acacias of the Old and New World tropics, about 200 species.

Phylogenetic notes. The split of Acacia into several genera (Acacia, Vachellia, Senegalia and others) followed molecular evidence that the old genus was not a natural group; Senegalia rugata sits with the Asian climbing species.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in the wild and in cultivation.
