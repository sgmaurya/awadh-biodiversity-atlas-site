कॉमन पोचार्ड / Common Pochard

Aythya ferina (Linnaeus, 1758) — Anatidae

कॉमन पोचार्ड — सर्दियों में यूरोप और साइबेरिया से आने वाली एक मध्यम आकार की डाइविंग बत्तख, जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के बड़े तालाबों में अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक देखी जाती है। नर के लाल सिर और भूरी पीठ से यह पहचानी जाती है। यह गोता लगाकर पानी के पौधे खाती है।

Field tip: A medium-sized diving duck with a chestnut-red head (males) — is likely a Common Pochard. The grey back and black breast distinguish it from Red-crested Pochard. Females show brown head and white eye-ring. Less common than other winter-visiting ducks.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 42–49 cm long; wingspan 72–86 cm. Weight 700–1000 g (males heavier).

Plumage (male): Head and neck chestnut-red to deep red. Upper back darker brown-black merging to grey on back and rump. Breast and stern black. Flanks and belly pale grey or greyish-white. Wing coverts grey; speculum grey and white.

Plumage (female): Head and neck brown (duller than male). Upperparts brownish-grey. Underparts pale buff. Distinctive white eye-ring and pale lore (space between bill and eye) visible. Wings show greyish speculum.

Bill: Black with conspicuous pale grey or white band in centre (diagnostic feature). Slightly hooked at tip.

Legs and feet: Grey or olive-grey; short; placed far back on body (adaptation for diving).

Eyes: Male has yellow or pale iris; female has dark iris; both have white eye-ring in some lights.

Sexes: Strongly dimorphic; males spectacularly colourful; females much duller.

Vocalisations. Relatively quiet species:

  1. Drake call: A soft, weak wheeze during courtship.
  2. Duck (female) call: Soft growling and grunting.
हिंदी: बहुत शांत पक्षी। कम आवाज़ करता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Omnivorous diver: Common Pochards are diving ducks that feed on:

  • Aquatic vegetation (seeds, shoots, tubers from bottom)
  • Aquatic invertebrates (aquatic insects, snails, small crustaceans)
  • Occasionally small fish and tadpoles

Feeding behaviour: Skilled divers; dive 1–4 m to reach bottom sediments and vegetation. Swim low on water. Primarily nocturnal feeders, with some daytime feeding.

Social behaviour: Gregarious in winter; form flocks, often in mixed-species groups with other diving ducks. Males may dominate females at roosting sites.

Roosting: Nocturnal roosts on open water of large lakes and reservoirs; diurnal feeding on deeper water or resting in reed beds.

Territorial: Non-territorial during winter; however, males defend mates during courtship season (late winter to early spring).

Predators: Eagles, harriers, and large predatory fish (pike) may take adults. Chicks vulnerable to corvids and aquatic predators.

हिंदी: गहरे पानी की, शांत बत्तख। झुंड में रहती है। गोता लगाकर खाना खोजती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives October–November from breeding grounds; departs March–April. Does not breed in India.

Breeding range: Northern Europe (Britain, Scandinavia) to western Siberia. Breeds March–July at elevation 0–1000 m.

Migration: Breeds on temperate freshwater lakes; southward migration begins August–September. Arrives in Indian subcontinent October–November; remains through February; departure peaks March–April.

Wintering: Distributed on larger lakes and reservoirs; less common in small ponds than dabbling ducks.

Moult: Complete moult before autumn migration; eclipse plumage acquired and maintained during winter stay.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Marsh Harrier, Peregrine Falcon
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, wild dog, fox, wild cat
  • At nest (breeding grounds): Corvids, weasels, martens, rats
  • Parasites: Waterfowl helminths, protozoa, lice

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

No crop damage: Common Pochards are specialist divers confined to large water bodies and never visit agricultural fields; no reported crop damage.

Habitat quality indicator: Used as an indicator species for freshwater lake ecosystem health; sensitivity to water quality makes them valuable bioindicators.

Hunting: Historically hunted in Europe; now protected in India under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Conservation status: Species is Vulnerable globally due to loss of breeding habitat in Europe (wetland drainage and pollution) and loss of wintering habitat in Africa and South Asia.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Common Pochards are expected at the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and larger water bodies in eastern UP from October through April. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025) recorded this species at Parwati Arga Bird Sanctuary from March 2024–February 2025. Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2022) recorded it in Ayodhya district wetlands. This species is less common than many other winter-visiting ducks and requires larger water bodies with good water quality and adequate vegetation.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds across temperate Europe and western Asia; winters across the Mediterranean, Middle East, South Asia, and East Africa.

In India: Regular winter visitor to larger wetlands and reservoirs, particularly in northern regions. Less common than other winter ducks.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to major lakes and reservoirs, with records from eastern districts and the Gangetic plain.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to larger water bodies including Parwati Arga and Ayodhya wetlands. Present October–April, with peak numbers January–February. Prefers deeper water bodies and larger systems.

Field rule: A medium-sized diving duck with chestnut-red head and grey back (males), or brown head with pale eye-ring (females), with distinctive grey-banded black bill — is a Common Pochard.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Aythya ferina was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th Edition, volume 1, page 128, as Anas ferina. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; all populations are homogeneous without significant geographic variation.

Family and order placement. Order Anseriformes; family Anatidae; genus Aythya contains the diving ducks (pochards). The genus contains 12 species globally.

Phylogenetic notes. Aythya ferina is closely related to other Aythya species (Tufted Duck, Ferruginous Duck, Ring-necked Duck). Molecular phylogenetics confirms monophyly of the genus.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Vulnerable (VU) due to significant population declines across breeding grounds (Europe and western Asia) caused by habitat loss, wetland pollution, and climate change. Global population estimated at 800,000–1.2 million, down from higher historical levels.
