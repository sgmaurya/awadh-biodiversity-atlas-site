रत्ती / Rosary Pea

Abrus precatorius L. — Fabaceae

रत्ती — एक बेल जिसके बीज लाल-काले होते हैं और इन्हें गहने, माला, और तराज़ू का बाट बनाया जाता है। भारतीय तराज़ुओं का पुराना वज़न का मानदंड ही रत्ती है। पत्तियाँ ईमली जैसी बारीक, फूल छोटे, पीले या गुलाबी, फलियाँ हल्की हरी रंग की। बीजों की चमकीली लालपन और सफेद धब्बे को देखते हुए इसे चेतावनी से संभालते हैं, क्योंकि ये जहरीले होते हैं।

Field tip: A thin-stemmed climber with tiny mimosa-like leaves and small pale yellow flowers. The ripe pods contain hard, shiny, red-and-black beads that feel slippery and smooth. These seeds are unmistakable.

Morphology आकारिकी

Stems: Slender, angular, woody at the base with age, twining spontaneously around support. Hairless or sparsely hairy.

Leaves: Compound, pinnate, with a thin rachis. Leaflets are small, oblong, hairless or sparsely hairy, closing partially at night (nyctinasty).

Flowers: Small, papilionaceous, pale yellow with hints of pink or purple. Borne in short axillary racemes. Self-pollinating and bee-visited.

Pods: Linear, slightly curved, hairy, narrowed at both ends. Wall is thin and papery. Each pod holds 4–5 seeds that are remarkably hard and dense.

Seeds: The defining feature — smooth, glossy, hard as stone, bright scarlet-red with a blackish patch around the hilum. Weight is remarkably uniform: 1.75 g per seed, which is the basis of the ratti as a traditional unit of weight.

हिंदी: इमली जैसी पत्तियाँ, छोटे पीले फूल। बीज लाल-काले, चमकीले, कठोर, गहने में लगते हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A pantropical legume, native to tropical Asia and now distributed globally. In India it is common in hedges, fallow land, and forest edges from sea level to 2000 m. It is a pioneer colonizer of disturbed ground, fixing nitrogen through its rhizobial association and enriching poor soils. The plant is evergreen to semi-deciduous, tolerant of drought once established, and capable of re-sprouting from a woody base if cut back. It twines around supporting vegetation without tendrils, reaching 2–3 m in length.

The small flowers are visited by bees and other small insects. The hard seeds, being extremely resistant to predation and decay, are dispersed primarily by humans (for ornament and trade) and rarely eaten by wild animals.

हिंदी: यह बेल ज़ंगली जगहों और बाड़ों में उगती है, बहुत कड़ी होती है। बीज इतने कठोर होते हैं कि जानवर खाते नहीं।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Hedge companions: Often found alongside Shikakai, Lantana, and wild grasses on unused land.
  • Bees and insects: Asian Honey Bee and other small insects visit the flowers.
  • Human association: Sacred in temples and worn as protective amulets in many Hindu and Ayurvedic traditions.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating caterpillars: Occasional damage from lepidopteran larvae.
  • Pod borers: Sometimes attack developing seeds.
  • Generally hardy: A robust plant with few serious problems; the hard seed coating protects seeds from most insects.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and cultural use.

Traditional. The seeds have been used for millennia in India as prayer beads (mala) in Hindu and Buddhist traditions. A mala typically contains 108 seeds, though 54-bead versions also exist. The plant's Sanskrit name, raktika, means "red," referring to the seed color. In Ayurvedic medicine, the root and seed are recorded in classical texts as warming (ushna) and used in decoctions for joint pain, cough, and fever. The Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita include it in various formulations. This is not medical advice — traditional uses are documented here for ethnobotanical record.

Weighing standard. For centuries, the ratti was the standard unit of weight in Indian bazaars. The weight of a single ratti seed (approximately 1.75 g) was used to weigh gold, spices, and medicines. This practice was formalized in Mughal administrative records and continued through the British period, until the metric system was adopted.

Sacred use. The seeds are strung into protective necklaces (tabeez) by Hindus and Muslims. In some traditions they are worn as amulets against illness.

Today. The seed trade continues; ratti seeds are sold in bead shops, temple supply stores, and online for making malas and jewelry. Some herbalists still use the root in traditional formulations.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Abrin, a toxin from the seeds, is being studied in cancer research and immunotherapy, but as a pure biochemical extract, not as a plant extract. Anyone encountering the plant should know: seeds are toxic if swallowed, and should never be consumed or ground.

हिंदी: रत्ती की माला 108 दानों की बनती है। तराज़ुओं में इसी का वज़न होता था। पर बीज जहरीले होते हैं — मुँह में नहीं डालते।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to India and now distributed throughout the tropics and subtropics globally, including the Americas, Africa, and Australia.

In India: Common throughout the peninsula and in the plains up to 2000 m elevation. Particularly common in central and southern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Found in hedges and waste ground throughout the state; more common in eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: To be expected in hedges and fallow land. The seeds are traded in local markets.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum, based on specimens from India. The genus Abrus contains about 15 species, mostly tropical.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised, although minor morphological variation exists across its range.

Family and order placement. Fabaceae, subfamily Faboideae, tribe Phaseoleae. Closely related to Vigna, Phaseolus and other legumes.

Phylogenetic notes. Abrus precatorius is the type genus for Abrus and sits within the diverse phaseoloid legumes. Its Old World tropical origin and pantropical distribution suggest early human-mediated dispersal.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widely distributed and not threatened.
