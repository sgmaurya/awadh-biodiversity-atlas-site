रेड-ब्रेस्टेड फ़्लाईकैचर / Red-breasted Flycatcher

Ficedula parva (Bechstein, 1792) — Muscicapidae

रेड-ब्रेस्टेड फ़्लाईकैचर — यह छोटी सर्दी में आने वाली चिड़िया सितंबर से अप्रैल तक पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में हिमालय और मध्य एशिया से आती है। नर का सफेद माथा, काली पीठ और लाल पेट इसे तुरंत पहचान देता है। मादा अधिक भूरी होती है किंतु लाल पेट सभी पक्षियों में दिखता है। यह पेड़ों पर बैठकर हवा से कीड़े पकड़ता है।

Field tip: A small winter flycatcher with distinctive red-orange underparts (both sexes). Males show a bold black head and white forehead; females are greyer but retain the red breast. Hunts insects by sallying from a perch and catching them in mid-air. Often restless and active.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length110–120 mm

Size: 11–12 cm long; weighs 8–11 g. One of the smallest Ficedula flycatchers.

Plumage (male, breeding): Striking. Head glossy black. Broad white forehead, lores and orbital ring. White collar-patch on sides of neck and upper back. Back black-grey. Rump grey. Tail black. Underparts: throat and upper breast orange-red, belly and undertail coverts reddish.

Plumage (female and non-breeding male): Head, back and tail grey-brown. Less contrasting; forehead and collar patch still visible but duller. Throat and breast buff-yellow transitioning to orange-red on breast and belly. Underparts diagnostic: distinctly red-orange on lower breast and belly.

Bill: Small, short, black; flattened laterally for catching insects.

Eyes: Dark; large for a small flycatcher; alert.

Legs and feet: Black; short and strong for perching.

Sexes: Males in breeding plumage are strikingly different with black head and white markings; females and non-breeding males are duller but red breast is always present.

Juveniles: Resemble females but duller and with spotted breast pattern initially.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A short, quiet warble; not often heard in wintering grounds.
  2. Contact calls: Soft, scratchy notes "tzzt" or "dritt".
  3. Alarm: Sharp, clicking calls.
हिंदी: सर्दी में शायद ही गाता है। नरम टिट्टी जैसी आवाज़।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Foraging: Hunts insects by sallying from a perch, often returning to the same branch (flycatching behaviour). Feeds on:

  • Small flying insects (flies, small moths, gnats)
  • Small insects on foliage and branches
  • Occasionally insects gleaned from vegetation

Social behaviour: Solitary or in loose associations with other insectivores. Non-territorial on wintering grounds. Alert and active; constantly moves from branch to branch.

Perching: Sits upright on exposed branches or twigs; frequently fans tail and droops wings slightly.

Movement: Restless; short flights to catch insects; returns to same perch or moves to nearby branch.

Microhabitat: Prefers open woodlands, gardens with scattered trees, orchards; visits areas with good insect availability.

हिंदी: अकेले या दो-तीन साथ रहते हैं। हमेशा चलते-फिरते रहते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Wintering season: September–May in eastern UP. Arrives as early as late August; departure begins in April, complete by May.

Peak presence: October–March, with January–February at peak numbers.

Breeding grounds: Central Asia, Himalayas, temperate forests of Kazakhstan and Russia; not breeding in UP.

No breeding in region: This is purely a winter visitor; all breeding occurs outside UP.

Moult: Complete moult before arrival at wintering grounds (pre-basic moult in breeding grounds).

Return migration: Reverse migration in April–May; males precede females by several weeks.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Schedule IV (as member of Muscicapidae).

Agricultural value: Feeds on small insects; insignificant direct agricultural effect due to small population and limited presence.

Cultural relevance: A delicate winter visitor; associated with cooler months and pleasant weather in the region.

Scientific value: Migratory patterns useful for understanding Palearctic-Indian migration routes.

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Red-breasted Flycatcher should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Winter visitor: GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya proper. Expected as a winter visitor (September–May) in eastern UP in open woodlands, orchards, and gardens with scattered trees. Numbers are generally small; often found in mixed-species feeding flocks during winter.

Where to look: Open deciduous woodlands, mango and citrus orchards, gardens with old shade trees. Often in mixed insectivore flocks with redstarts and other flycatchers.

Peak season: October–March. Often seen in cooler months; more active in early morning.

Detection: Look for characteristic sallying behaviour from exposed perches. Alert and active; watch for short flights to catch insects.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly (red breast visible, male's white forehead and black head if male), with location GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page of this atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in temperate forests from Europe and Central Asia through Russia to Japan. Winters in the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia.

In India: Winter visitor throughout the subcontinent; more common in Himalayan foothills and north Indian plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Uncommon winter visitor to northern and eastern regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Possible winter visitor in suitable habitats; records needed to confirm regular presence.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Bechstein in 1792. Muscicapa parva Bechstein, 1792. Type locality: Germany.

Subspecies. Three subspecies recognised:

  • F. p. parva (Bechstein, 1792) — Europe
  • F. p. albicilla — Central Asia and Russia (sometimes considered separate species)
  • F. p. sanfordi — Far East

The Indian wintering populations are primarily F. p. parva from European and Western Asian breeding grounds.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae (Old World flycatchers). Genus Ficedula comprises small Old World flycatchers of temperate and subtropical regions.

Phylogenetic notes. Ficedula parva is part of a complex of closely related small flycatchers; relationships with F. albicilla and other species have been subject to taxonomic revision.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Population is stable and widely distributed across its breeding and wintering ranges. Not threatened by exploitation. Protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 in India.
