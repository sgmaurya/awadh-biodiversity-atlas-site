कबासी टेंगरा / Gangetic Mystus

Mystus cavasius (Hamilton, 1822) — Bagridae

कबासी टेंगरा — छोटी देसी कैटफिश जो गंगा प्रणाली की नदियों, तालाबों और बाढ़ के मैदानों में पाई जाती है। हल्का और स्वादिष्ट माँस, स्थानीय पकवान। तीन जोड़ी दाढ़ी और चिकनी त्वचा से पहचानी जाती है। तालाब और नहर में रहती है, मिट्टी के कण खाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size12–20 cm
BodyElongate, cylindrical, slightly compressed; scaleless, slimy
HeadSomewhat flattened; mouth subterminal; 3 pairs of barbels
ColorationDark greyish-brown above; lighter silvery below

FinsDorsal fin short; adipose fin present; pectoral spines prominent

Field tip: A small brownish catfish with three pairs of barbels and serrated pectoral spines. Found in mud and vegetation on the pond bottom in eastern UP. The pectoral spine is painful if it pricks the hand. Easily confused with other small catfish but the three barbel pairs are diagnostic.

हिंदी: छोटी, भूरी कैटफिश; तीन जोड़ी दाढ़ी; छाती के काँटे तीव्र।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (field populations from eastern UP):

MeasurementRange

Weight30–80 g

Body structure: Benthic form: elongate, cylindrical, slightly compressed. Skin scaleless and covered with mucus. Eyes small; mouth subterminal.

Head: Somewhat flattened; snout obtuse. Three pairs of barbels: maxillary (longest), outer mandibular (medium), inner mandibular (shortest).

Coloration: Dark greyish-brown or olive-brown on the back; silvery-grey on the sides; white or pale yellow below. Fins dusky; adipose fin often darker.

Spines: Pectoral and dorsal spines with serrated edges, moderately elongated. The pectoral spine is venomous to the touch (causes localized pain and swelling in humans).

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A benthic, nocturnal catfish feeding on organic matter, detritus, small invertebrates, and fish eggs. Lives in mud and vegetation on the pond bottom; hides during the day and emerges at night to forage. Tolerates poor water quality and low oxygen; survives in stagnant ponds. Gregarious; often found in small groups among aquatic vegetation. Important for nutrient cycling in freshwater ecosystems.

हिंदी: रात में शिकार करती है, दिन में मिट्टी में छिपी रहती है। झुंड में रहती है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food. A valued food fish in local cuisine; sold fresh at weekly markets. Meat is tender and white; traditionally cooked whole in curries or broths. Nutritious; high in protein.

Protection status. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.

Aquaculture. Included in composite fish culture for its detritivorous role, improving water quality in culture ponds by consuming accumulated detritus and organic matter.

Today. Easily caught in seasonal ponds, tanks, and rice fields during monsoon. Still abundant in suitable habitat but declining due to habitat loss and water pollution.

हिंदी: अच्छी खाई जाने वाली मछली; नरम माँस। विधि अधिनियम 1972 के अंतर्गत संरक्षित है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Gangetic system across South Asia, from Pakistan through northern India to Bangladesh.

In India: Throughout the Ganga system and associated wetlands of the plains and foothills.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in rivers and ponds of the eastern plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in ponds, marshes, and monsoon floodplains; more abundant in monsoon months.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches. Originally placed in Pimelodus; transferred to Mystus by later authors. The genus name Mystus refers to whisker-like barbels.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Bagridae, the bagrid catfish family; order Siluriformes. The genus Mystus includes numerous Asian catfish species.

Phylogenetic notes. Mystus cavasius is closely related to other small Asian bagrid catfish. Recent molecular work confirms its position within Bagridae.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Widespread and abundant in suitable freshwater habitat; no major conservation threats documented, though habitat loss and water pollution are increasing concerns in some areas.
