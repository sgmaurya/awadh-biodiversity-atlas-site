ह्यूम वार्बलर / Hume's Leaf Warbler

Phylloscopus humei (Brooks, 1872) — Phylloscopidae

ह्यूम वार्बलर — पहाड़ों से नीचे आने वाली यह बहुत छोटी चिड़िया सर्दियों में पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खुले जंगलों और बागों में मिलती है। इसका रंग हरा-भूरा और सफेद भौंह की पट्टी इसे पहचानने में मदद देती है। यह तेजी से पत्तियों को पलटती है और छोटी कीड़े खोजती है। इसकी तीव्र, बिना तुकबंदी वाली गान पहचान है।

Field tip: In open forest and garden edges in winter, look for a tiny greenish warbler with a pale eyebrow stripe moving rapidly through vegetation. It is restless and gleaning, often hanging upside down. The eyebrow stripe and green colour combined are diagnostic. Very small size is key.

Size: 10–11 cm long; wingspan 15–16 cm. Weight 6–10 g. Among the smallest warblers in Asia.

Plumage: Crown and back olive-green to brownish-green; slightly duller in winter. Supercilium (eyebrow stripe) pale buff, extending from above lore to above ear-covert, sometimes faint. Cheeks and ear-coverts greenish. Throat and upper breast whitish; lower breast and underparts pale buff or white. Wings darker olive-green with paler feather edges. Tail graduated, greenish with dark shafts.

Sexual dimorphism: Minimal; females slightly duller than males.

Bill: Small, pointed, dark brown.

Legs and feet: Pale horn-brown; thin.

Eyes: Dark, alert, small.

Juveniles: Duller overall; less distinct supercilium.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A rapid, wheezing or buzzing song delivered from concealed perches.; lacks clear notes but is diagnostic.
  2. Call: Short, dry tack or chit, often repeated.
  3. Contact calls: Soft whistles between pair members.
हिंदी: नर की तेजी वाली, गुनगुनाती हुई गान। कठोर "टैक-टैक" आवाज़ चिंता में।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Gleaning forager: Hume's Leaf Warbler is one of the most active and restless warblers, constantly moving through vegetation. It forages by:

  • Gleaning small insects from leaf surfaces and undersides
  • Searching bark crevices on branches
  • Occasionally hanging upside down to probe foliage
  • Picking insects from branch tips

Social behaviour: Generally solitary or in pairs during winter; occasionally joins mixed feeding flocks with other small warblers.

Habitat preference: Open forest, forest edge, open woodland with scattered trees, cultivation margins, gardens. Prefers areas with mixed vegetation rather than dense thickets.

Foraging technique: Constantly in motion; hops rapidly along branches; explores foliage thoroughly; very energetic.

Predators: Small raptors (Eurasian Kestrel, Shrike), domestic cats may take individuals.

Movement: Performs frequent short flights between adjacent bushes and trees.

हिंदी: जंगल के किनारे बागों में अकेली रहती है। पत्तियों को बहुत तेजी से खोजती है। लगभग हमेशा चलायमान।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Absent during breeding season (April–August); returns to eastern UP from September and remains through April–May.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in late August–September; peak in winter months (December–February); departure begins in March–April, with most birds gone by late May.

Breeding (elsewhere): Breeds in Himalayas and central Asian mountains from 1,500–3,500 m. Cup nest built in low vegetation or shrubs.

Moult: Complete moult on breeding grounds before arrival; partial moult during winter.

Roosts: Roosting in small bushes and low tree foliage; solitary roosters.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। पहाड़ों में घोंसला बनाता है। सर्दी में अकेली रहती है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Eurasian Kestrel, Merlin, Red-backed Shrike, larger warblers
  • Mammalian predators: Domestic cat, weasel
  • At nest: Corvids, crows (on breeding grounds)
  • Parasites: Blood parasites, internal helminths
हिंदी: छोटी बाजें और बड़ी चिड़ियाँ शिकार करती हैं। बिल्ली भी खतरनाक है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is prohibited.

Pest control: Consumes vast numbers of small insects and mites, providing beneficial pest control in gardens and orchards.

Cultural relevance: Known from classical ornithological records of the Indian subcontinent. Featured in detailed hand-drawn illustrations in 19th-century bird guides.

Scientific value: Indicator of forest edge habitat quality; migratory patterns important for understanding Himalayan flyway connectivity.

हिंदी: वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम 1972 के अनुसूची IV के अंतर्गत पूरी तरह संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार निषिद्ध है।

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from September to May, particularly in open forest patches, forest edges, and garden areas of Basti and Ayodhya districts. Published handbooks document this species wintering widely across northern India in suitable habitat.

Habitat preference: Open forest, forest edge, woodland with scattered trees, gardens, cultivation margins with vegetation.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from Himalayan breeding grounds are settled.

Nearest published record: Handbook of the Birds of India and the Western Palearctic document this species as a regular winter visitor throughout the Indian plains. Ornithological records from similar habitats in Uttar Pradesh confirm its presence.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph or sound recording showing the bird clearly in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date), such as gleaning in a tree or singing, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeding range in the Himalayas from Kashmir to Bhutan, and through the mountains of central Asia to China. Winters throughout the Indian subcontinent at lower elevations.

In India: Distributed across the Indian subcontinent as a winter visitor (September–May). Occurs from the Himalayas south to the Deccan.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread in winter in suitable habitat, particularly in forest edges and gardens of the plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor in open forest areas and garden margins; expected on range grounds rather than confirmed in local surveys.

Field rule: Hume's Leaf Warbler is a tiny greenish warbler with a pale eyebrow stripe and white underparts, restlessly gleaning in vegetation. If the bird is grey with a black ear patch, it is Lesser Whitethroat. If it shows a yellow wing-bar or yellow-tinged underparts, it is a different species.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by William Sclater Brooks in 1872 as Phylloscopus humei, based on specimens from the Himalayas.

Subspecies. The nominate subspecies P. h. humei winters in the Indian subcontinent and is the form found in eastern UP.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Phylloscopidae (leaf warblers). The genus Phylloscopus contains the leaf warblers and is diverse in Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Recent molecular work has elevated Phylloscopus warblers to their own family Phylloscopidae, distinct from the Old World warblers Sylviidae.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widely distributed. The species is adaptable to various woodland habitats. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
