दूधराज / Indian Paradise Flycatcher

Terpsiphone paradisi (Linnaeus, 1758) — Monarchidae

दूधराज — गर्मी में दक्षिण से आने वाली सबसे सुंदर चिड़िया, जिसकी लंबी सफेद पूँछ हवा में लहरती है। बड़े पेड़ों पर ऊँची डालों पर बैठ कर पतली और मीठी आवाज़ में गीत गाती है। मई-जून में इसके घोंसलों को खोजना खेल-सा है — छोटा, कप-जैसा घोंसला पत्तों और मकड़ी के जाले से बना होता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeMale: 24–25 cm without tail; up to 30 cm with tail feathers. Female: 16–18 cm (much shorter tail)
Male plumageGlossy black head, neck, chest; white underparts; black back; long white streamers from tail
Female plumageChestnut crown and back; buff underparts; shorter tail without streamers
BillSmall, black, slightly hooked
LegsBluish-grey
BehaviourSolitary or in pairs; stays in canopy; active flycatcher; acrobatic

Field tip: Unmistakable in breeding plumage. Males with long white tail streamers are distinctive. Females lack long tail but show chestnut-buff colouration. Summer visitor; listen for melodious song from high canopy in forested areas April–September.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें:
नर चमकदार काला-सफेद, लंबी सफेद पूँछ लहरती है। मादा भूरा-खाकी रंग, छोटी पूँछ। ऊँचे पेड़ों पर बैठता है। गर्मी में ही दिखता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (excluding long tail streamers in males):

MeasurementRange
Total length (excl. tail)160–180 mm
Wing (flattened chord)78–88 mm
Tail (female)60–80 mm; (male) 100–140 mm

Bill (culmen)12–14 mm
Weight (male)18–21 g; (female) 14–16 g

Plumage (male): Head, neck, upper breast glossy black-blue (iridescent in good light). Lower breast and belly white or creamy-white. Back black; wings black. Tail has long white streamers (central feathers elongated). Eyes have blue orbital ring.

Plumage (female): Crown and nape chestnut-rufous. Back chestnut-brown. Wings dark. Underparts buff or peachy-buff. Throat white. Tail rufous-brown, shorter than male's.

Juveniles: Similar to females but duller; young males gradually develop black plumage and tail streamers over months.

Bill: Small, black, slightly hooked for gleaning insects.

Legs: Blue-grey; conspicuous.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: Clear, melodious, liquid whistles. Males sing from high perches. Song quality varies between individuals.
  2. Contact call: Soft tuit or wuit notes between pair members.
  3. Alarm call: Sharp chit-chit when predators approach.
हिंदी: नर चमकदार काला, पेट सफेद, लंबी सफेद पूँछ। मादा चेस्टनट भूरा। आवाज़ तरल और संगीत जैसी। पीली आँखों की रिंग खूबसूरत है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Feeding ecology: Solitary or in pairs. Forages in canopy and mid-storey by sallying from exposed perches. Catches small flying insects in mid-air; also gleans insects from foliage. Feeds on:

  • Small flies and other small insects
  • Small moths and butterflies
  • Tiny beetles
  • Small spiders
  • Rarely small berries (occasional)

Habitat use: Prefers tall forests and forest edges with large canopy trees. Often seen at considerable heights. Males defend breeding territories throughout summer months.

Breeding behaviour: Males display acrobatically with exaggerated movements of long tail; perform aerial displays to attract females.

Social behaviour: Generally solitary or in pairs; does not join mixed feeding flocks. Males highly territorial during breeding.

Activity: Diurnal; most active at dawn and late afternoon; less active during hot midday hours.

हिंदी: अकेले या जोड़े में रहता है। ऊँचे पेड़ों पर बैठ कर हवा में कीटों को पकड़ता है। नर अपनी लंबी पूँछ दिखाकर मादा को आकर्षित करता है। साल के एक हिस्से में ही दिखता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Summer visitor: Arrives April–May from wintering grounds (peninsular India and Central Asia). Departs August–September after breeding.

Breeding: Breeds April–July. Single brood per year.

Nesting: Builds small cup-shaped nest on slender branch, suspended below branch. Nest made of plant fibres, spider web, and lichen. Clutch 3–4 eggs; incubation 12–13 days; fledging 13–15 days.

Territorial behaviour: Males vocalize extensively to maintain territories and attract mates; singing peaks at dawn and dusk.

Moult: Gradual moult occurs after breeding; new tail feathers for males take time to fully elongate.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shikra and other small raptors
  • Mammalian predators: Snakes (nest predators), palm squirrels (occasional)
  • Nest predators: Snakes, crows (if nest located poorly)
हिंदी: शिकारी चिड़ियों से खतरा। घोंसले को साँपों और कौओं से बचाना पड़ता है। घोंसले का स्थान महत्वपूर्ण है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Indian Paradise Flycatcher is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Schedule I. All wild birds listed are protected; hunting and trapping are prohibited.

Pest control: Beneficial in gardens and agricultural areas adjacent to forests because it feeds on small insects that may damage crops.

Forest health indicator: Presence indicates well-maintained forest canopy with large trees suitable for breeding.

Cultural and aesthetic significance: One of India's most beautiful and celebrated birds. Featured in classical Hindi and Sanskrit poetry; important cultural and spiritual symbol. Popular with birdwatchers and photographers.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी विधि अधिनियम 1972 की पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है। भारत की सबसे खूबसूरत चिड़ियों में से एक है। कविताओं में इसका जिक्र है।

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Indian Paradise Flycatcher should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

GBIF holds published records from these districts during breeding season. The species is expected in sal and mixed deciduous forests and forest edges with mature canopy trees.

Habitat requirements: Large canopy trees in forested areas; requires adequate forest structure for breeding and feeding.

Seasonal presence: Expected April through September; absent rest of year.

Nearest published records: Ali & Ripley (1968) and subsequent forest surveys document regular presence in eastern Uttar Pradesh during summer breeding season.

First record needed: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly (males with long tail streamers are unmistakable) or audio recording of the distinctive song in a specific location (with GPS coordinates) submitted through the About page.

हिंदी: यह स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित है, न कि देखे गए बतौर प्रेक्षण। अप्रैल से सितंबर तक बड़े पेड़ों पर मिल सकता है। इसकी मीठी आवाज़ सुनो — पहचान जाओगे। पहला रिकॉर्ड के लिए फोटो या गीत की रिकॉर्डिंग चाहिए।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent and Central Asia. Breeding range extends to Central Asian regions; winters in peninsular India. Several subspecies recognised; T. p. paradisi occurs in India.

In India: Summer breeder in peninsular and central India, extending to the Gangetic plains. Winters in southern peninsular regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Summer visitor to forested regions, particularly eastern and central parts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular summer visitor in sal and mixed deciduous forests and forest edges with mature canopy trees. Breeding records documented.

Field rule: Indian Paradise Flycatcher male is unmistakable with long white tail streamers and glossy black-white plumage. Female and non-breeding male are chestnut-buff and occur in tall forest canopy in summer (April–September). No other bird matches this combination.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 as Muscicapa paradisi from India. Type locality is peninsular India.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognised; T. p. paradisi (Indian subcontinent), T. p. leucogaster (Himalayan foothills and Central Asia), T. p. floris (Sundaic), T. p. saturatior (Sundaic). Eastern UP hosts primarily T. p. paradisi and occasional T. p. leucogaster as passage migrant.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Monarchidae (monarchs and relatives). Genus Terpsiphone comprises the paradise-flycatchers of Africa and Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to African Black-headed Paradise Flycatcher; represents African-Asian clade of paradise-flycatchers.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Widely distributed and adaptable. Population stable despite some habitat loss. Protected throughout range in India.
