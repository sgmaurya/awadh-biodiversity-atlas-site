ब्लैक-हेडेड गल / Black-headed Gull

Chroicocephalus ridibundus (Pontoppidan, 1763) — Laridae

ब्लैक-हेडेड गल — यह एक मध्यम आकार की सफेद और भूरी चिड़िया है जो सर्दियों में बड़ी झीलों, तालाबों और नदियों के किनारों पर सितंबर से जून तक दिखती है। इसका काला सिर (सर्दियों में कम स्पष्ट) और सफेद पंख इसे तुरंत पहचान देते हैं। यह झुंड में रहती है, पानी पर तैरती है, और कचरे, मछली और जलीय जानवरों पर निर्भर करती है। इसकी तेज़ आवाज़ें सर्दियों की भीड़ में सुनाई देती हैं।

LegsRed or orange-red
BehaviourGregarious; loafs on water and land; omnivorous scavenger

Field tip: On large open-water bodies, look for medium white gulls with grey backs loafing in flocks on water or sandbanks. In winter, the dark ear-patch (but white crown) is diagnostic. In breeding plumage (rare in UP), the entire head is black. The red-orange bill and legs are distinctive. Often associated with urban areas and human food waste.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 37–39 cm long; wingspan 100–117 cm. Weight 240–330 g.

Winter plumage (as seen in eastern UP): Head white with brown or blackish ear-patch and loral region (not forming complete hood). Back and upper wings medium grey. Remiges (flight feathers) grey with white spots at tips (white mirrors). Underparts white. Tail white. Breast sometimes with peachy wash in fresh birds.

Breeding plumage (not seen in India): Entire head black; otherwise as above.

Bill: Red or yellow-orange, pointed; excellent for scavenging and catching prey. Tomium (cutting edge) pale.

Legs and feet: Red or orange-red in most birds; webbed feet.

Eyes: Dark brown; with pale orbital ring.

Sexual dimorphism: Males slightly larger; subtle plumage differences.

Juveniles: Mottled brown and white; black bill and legs; gradually acquire adult plumage over 2–3 years.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact call: Harsh kya-kya or kyow, given in flight and at rest.
  2. Alarm call: Loud, strident kyow-kyow, given when disturbed.
  3. Laughing call: Loud, raucous laughing cackle when competing for food.
हिंदी: तीव्र कर्कश आवाज़ — "क्या-क्या"। खाने के लिए लड़ते समय जोर से चिल्लाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Omnivorous scavenger: Black-headed Gulls feed on a diverse diet:

  • Fish and fish fry
  • Aquatic invertebrates (crustaceans, mollusks, insect larvae)
  • Human food waste (very opportunistic)
  • Grain and seeds
  • Occasionally small mammals and birds
  • Viscera and discards from markets

Feeding behaviour: Versatile feeders; can surface-feed, dive, walk and pick, or scavenge from land. Often follow fishing activities and urban waste dumps. Nocturnal feeding in response to food availability.

Social behaviour: Highly gregarious; flocks common. Roost together at night on large water bodies. Form feeding flocks at productive sites.

Flight: Strong, direct flight; capable of sustained flight.

Predators: Large eagles, sparrowhawks; generally safe due to gregarious behavior and large body size. Chicks vulnerable to crows and jackals.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहता है। कचरे, मछली और सब कुछ खाता है। पानी पर तैरता है और जमीन पर भी चलता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Breeds in central Asia and Mediterranean; arrives September–October, departs May–June.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive early September; numbers peak November–January; departure begins May, with most birds gone by June.

Breeding: Does not breed in India; breeding grounds in Mediterranean, Caspian, and central Asian wetlands.

Moult: Pre-breeding moult occurs during winter stay, acquiring breeding plumage for return to breeding grounds.

Roosts: Nocturnal roosts on large water bodies; congregate at dusk; day roosts in shallow water and banks.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, जून तक रहता है। अपने देश में अंडे देता है। बड़े तालाबों पर रात बिताता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Sparrowhawks
  • Mammalian predators: Jackals, wild dogs
  • At nest: Crows, jackals, snakes
  • Parasites: Avian helminths, ectoparasites
हिंदी: बड़ी शिकारी चिड़ियाँ और सियार इसका शिकार करते हैं। झुंड होने से बचाव मिलता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Urban and wetland association: Strong association with urban areas, landfills, and food-processing facilities. Benefits from human food waste; conversely, may compete with native scavengers.

Potential pest: In some areas, may occasionally damage grain crops or compete with native species.

Protected status: Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting and trade are prohibited.

Ecological indicator: Presence indicates adequate open-water habitats and invertebrate fauna. Absence may indicate pollution or water quality issues.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across Eurasia from Atlantic to Pacific; winters across southern Eurasia, Africa, and southern Asia.

In India: Distributed across the country as a winter visitor in wetlands and urban areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread winter visitor on large lakes and reservoirs throughout the state; also in urban areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor on large water bodies and urban areas; September to June with peak November–February.

Field rule: Black-headed Gull in winter has white head with dark ear-patch, red-orange bill, medium grey back, and red-orange legs. The medium size, ear-patch pattern, and red bill and legs are diagnostic. Compare carefully with Brown-headed Gull which has more extensive head coloring.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Morten Thaning Brünnich in 1763 (sometimes credited to Pontoppidan) as Larus ridibundus. Type locality is Denmark (Pontoppidan 1763) or uncertain.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised. The single population migrates across Eurasia.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Laridae (gulls, terns, noddies). Genus Chroicocephalus contains small to medium gulls.

Phylogenetic notes. Black-headed Gull is closely related to Brown-headed Gull (C. brunnicephalus) and other small Palearctic gulls.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Populations are stable and widely distributed. Highly adaptable to human-modified environments.
