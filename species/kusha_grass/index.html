कुश / Kusha Grass

Desmostachya bipinnata (L.) Stapf — Poaceae

कुश — वेदों की घास। पूजा में आसन, पवित्री की अंगूठी, श्राद्ध की पिंड-तृण — सब कुश से। ऊसर और नदी के किनारे यह कड़ी, नुकीली, गुच्छेदार घास उगती है जिसकी जड़ें ज़मीन को बाँधे रखती हैं। किनारे इतने तेज़ कि हाथ कट जाए, इसीलिए इसे ध्यान से तोड़ा जाता है। छप्पर, चटाई, रस्सी और झाड़ू में भी काम आती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
HabitStiff, erect tussock grass 0.5–1.5 m, spreading by stout creeping rhizomes into dense patches
LeavesLong, narrow, 30–60 cm, hard, rolled or flat, tapering to a sharp point, margins rough enough to cut skin
StemsRigid, solid, pale, with old sheaths at the base
Flower headA dense, erect, narrow panicle 20–60 cm with short crowded branches, pale straw to purple-tinged, July–October
SpikeletsFlattened, many-flowered, 5–12 mm, packed along the branches
WhereUsar patches, bunds, river banks: where few other grasses grow

Field tip: On white, crusted usar ground or a sandy river bank, a stiff tussock grass with needle-pointed leaves that cut if pulled through the hand, and in the monsoon a dense upright plume. Taller and far harsher than doob; shorter and stiffer than kans or munja.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: ऊसर या नदी किनारे की कड़ी, गुच्छेदार घास; पत्ती लंबी, नुकीली, किनारे चाकू जैसे; बरसात में सीधा, घना, हल्का बैंगनी फूल-डंठल। दूब से बहुत कड़ी, काँस-मूँज से छोटी।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (Indo-Gangetic plain):

MeasurementRange
Height50–150 cm
Leaf blade30–60 × 0.3–0.8 cm
Panicle20–60 cm
Spikelet5–12 mm, 10–40 florets
Rhizome depth30–100 cm

Rhizomes and roots: Thick, scaly, far-creeping rhizomes with fibrous roots that reach a metre down, giving the plant its hold on loose and saline soil and its reputation for being impossible to dig out.

Leaves: Linear, convolute when dry, glaucous, with a hard, pointed tip and finely toothed cutting margins; the ligule a fringe of hairs.

Inflorescence: A contracted panicle, the branches short and alternate so the head looks like a stiff bottle-brush; the many-flowered, laterally flattened spikelets are the mark of the genus and of its relatives in the Eragrostideae.

हिंदी: जड़ें मोटी, दूर तक फैलने वाली, एक मीटर गहरी — उखाड़ना लगभग नामुमकिन। पत्ती सूखने पर लिपट जाती है, सिरा नुकीला, किनारे दाँतेदार। फूल-डंठल बोतल साफ़ करने वाले ब्रश जैसा।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A specialist of hard places: saline and alkaline usar flats, sandy river terraces, bunds, and the dry banks of canals, where its deep rhizomes and tough leaves let it hold ground that supports little else. On usar it is often the first perennial to establish, and its litter and root channels slowly make the soil fit for other plants, which is why reclamation schemes have used it.

Cattle graze the young shoots after the first rains and leave the mature leaves alone; buffalo take more. The dense tussocks shelter quail, Red-wattled Lapwing chicks, lizards and Two-Spotted Cricket, and the plumes feed seed-eating birds and ants in autumn.

हिंदी: ऊसर, रेतीली और नमकीन ज़मीन की घास, जहाँ और कुछ नहीं उगता। गहरी जड़ें मिट्टी बाँधती हैं और धीरे-धीरे ज़मीन सुधारती हैं। बरसात की नई कोंपल पशु खाते हैं, बाद में नहीं। गुच्छों में बटेर, टिटहरी के बच्चे और झींगुर छिपते हैं।

हिंदी: बरसात में हरी, सर्दी में पीली और सख़्त, गरमी में सूखी गुच्छी; आग और चराई सह लेती है। मुख्य फैलाव जड़ों से।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Usar companions: grows with Munja Grass and Kans Grass on banks, and alone on the barest flats.
  • Shelter: Red-wattled Lapwing, quail and Two-Spotted Cricket in the tussocks.
  • Grazers: cattle and buffalo on the young growth; Mound-building Termite on the dry litter.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Few; the hard leaves are rarely attacked. Smut fungi occasionally blacken the spikelets.
  • Termites consume dead tussocks in the dry season.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use (documented uses; not medical advice):

  • Ritual. The darbha or kusha of the Vedas: seats for the officiant (kushasana), the pavitri ring worn on the finger during rites, the grass strewn at the shraddha and spread under the sacred fire. Its sharp point was said to drive off impurity; the Bhagavad Gita names it as the seat for meditation. This is its first and continuing use, and every village purohit knows where to gather it.
  • Thatch and mats. The stiff culms and leaves make a durable thatch and coarse mats and brooms.
  • Rope. Twisted into cordage where munja is scarce.
  • Soil reclamation. Planted or encouraged on usar and on eroding bunds; its rhizomes hold the soil and its litter adds organic matter.
  • Traditional medicine. The root, as darbha or kusha, is recorded in Kirtikar & Basu and Chopra as cooling, diuretic and for dysentery; a component of several classical formulations.

Today. Still gathered for ritual in every village; otherwise little used, and treated as a weed of fallows and bunds.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Kusha and related Desmostachya and Sporobolus grasses are studied as bio-reclamation species for sodic soils in Uttar Pradesh and Haryana, alongside gypsum treatment; published trials exist from the Central Soil Salinity Research Institute, Karnal. For a district with real usar patches that is a direct lead.

हिंदी: कुश वेदों की घास है — पूजा का आसन, पवित्री, श्राद्ध की तृण; हर गाँव का पुरोहित जानता है कि कहाँ मिलती है। छप्पर, चटाई, झाड़ू और रस्सी में भी। ऊसर सुधारने में इसकी जड़ें काम करती हैं; करनाल के संस्थान ने इस पर प्रयोग किए हैं।

To be expected on usar patches, railway and canal banks, sandy river terraces and old fallows across the district, in tussocks and spreading patches. The village priest will know the nearest stand; that is the quickest way to a first confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: North and East Africa through Arabia and the Middle East to the Indian subcontinent and Indo-China.

In India: Throughout the plains and foothills, especially on saline and sandy ground in the north and west.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common on usar land and river banks across the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected wherever usar, sand or bare bunds occur.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. Where does the village gather kusha for rituals? / गाँव में पूजा की कुश कहाँ से लाई जाती है? — Ask the purohit; map the stand.
  2. Does kusha grow where nothing else does? / क्या कुश वहाँ उगती है जहाँ और कुछ नहीं उगता? — On a usar patch, list every plant inside and outside a kusha tussock.
  3. Do cattle eat it, and when? — Watch grazing in July and in January.
  4. What lives inside a tussock? — Part the leaves of five tussocks in September and list what runs out.
  5. Has any usar field in the area been reclaimed, and did kusha play a part? — Ask farmers and the agriculture office.

Field rule: A stiff, sharp-leaved tussock with a dense upright straw-coloured brush of flattened spikelets, on usar or a sandy bank, is kusha.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Linnaeus, Mantissa Plantarum 1: 33 (1767), as Briza bipinnata. Transferred to Desmostachya by Otto Stapf in 1900 in Flora Capensis 7: 632. The genus name means "bundled spike"; bipinnata, "twice feathered", for the branched panicle.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised; the genus has this one species.

Family and order placement. Poaceae, subfamily Chloridoideae, tribe Eragrostideae; order Poales. Its nearest relatives are the lovegrasses (Eragrostis) and dropseeds (Sporobolus).

Phylogenetic notes. A C4 grass of the chloridoid line, like most grasses of hot, saline and dry habitats.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread and abundant.
