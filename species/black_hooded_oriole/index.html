ब्लैक-हुडेड ओरिओल / Black-hooded Oriole

Oriolus xanthornus (Linnaeus, 1758) — Oriolidae

ब्लैक-हुडेड ओरिओल (काली सिर वाली बुलबुल) — बस्ती में साल भर पाया जाने वाला एक सुंदर पक्षी, जिसका शरीर चमकदार पीला (golden-yellow) होता है और सिर पर काली हुड (hood) होती है। नर का रंग अधिक चमकदार होता है, जबकि मादा हरे-पीले रंग की होती है। यह पक्षी अपनी सुंदर संगीतमय सीटी के लिए प्रसिद्ध है, जो सुबह के समय बागों और पेड़ों में सुनाई देती है। यह पक्षी फलों और कीटों पर भोजन करता है, खासकर अंजीर और शहतूत के पेड़ों पर दिखाई देता है। इसका घोंसला बहुत ही सुंदर होता है, जो एक लटकी हुई टोकरी की तरह होता है।

Field tip: A strikingly colourful bird — male with brilliant yellow body and black hood is unmistakable. Females are greenish-yellow and may be confused with barbets or sunbirds, but the longer bill and different shape separate them.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length250–270 mm
Wing130–150 mm
Tail90–110 mm
Bill30–35 mm

Plumage (male): Head and neck glossy jet-black, forming a distinctive hood that extends from the crown to the upper breast. Body brilliant golden-yellow, brightest on the underparts. Wing feathers black with broad golden-yellow edges (coverts), creating a striking contrast. Tail golden-yellow with black central feathers. Undertail coverts pale yellow.

Plumage (female): Greenish-yellow overall, lacking the black hood entirely. Crown and upper parts greenish; underparts paler yellow. Wings and tail greenish with yellow edges. Less contrasting than males.

Bill: Long, pointed, and slightly decurved. Colour reddish-brown or pale horn in males; similar in females.

Eyes: Bright red or crimson in males; brown or dark brown in females. Bare orbital skin minimal.

Feet: Dull blue-grey.

Sexes: Highly dimorphic; males unmistakable with black hood; females could be confused with other yellow-green birds.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A loud, melodious "pio-piot" or "po-po-po" whistle, delivered from high perches at dawn and dusk.
  2. Contact call: Softer whistles and "churr" sounds between pair members.
  3. Alarm call: Harsh chattering when disturbed or defending nest.
हिंदी: सुंदर सीटी "पियो-पियो"। सुबह के समय ज़्यादा आवाज़।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Fruit and insect specialist: Black-hooded Orioles forage in the foliage of fruit-bearing trees, picking fruits (especially figs, mulberries, and other soft fruits) and gleaning insects from leaves and branches. They often hang acrobatically from twigs while feeding.

Vocal and territorial: Males are highly vocal during breeding season, perching on exposed branches and delivering loud whistles. They defend territories against other males.

Social structure: Usually found in pairs during breeding season; may gather in loose flocks during non-breeding season at food sources.

Nest building: Females construct elaborate suspended nests made of strips of leaves, palm fibres, and plant down, woven into a deep, pouch-like structure hung from tree branches. Nests are 12–20 cm deep.

Habitat: Prefer mature trees with good foliage cover and fruit production. Adapt well to orchards, gardens, and parks.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shikra, Eurasian Kestrel, occasionally monitor lizards raiding nests
  • Mammalian predators: Snakes (nest predators), monitor lizards, rarely cats
  • Nest predators: Crows, jungle mynas, woodpeckers (rival cavities)
हिंदी: बाज़ और सांपों का शिकार। घोंसलों पर कौवों का हमला।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Fruit crop impact: May cause minor damage to ripening fruits in orchards, but consumption is usually minor and the birds' role in controlling insects may offset losses.

Protected status: Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (Schedule IV). Killing or capturing is prohibited.

Ornithological significance: Popular with birdwatchers due to bright coloration and vocal activity. Frequently recorded in bird surveys.

Cultural significance: In classical Sanskrit literature, orioles are mentioned as birds of beauty and elegance.

Black-hooded Orioles are expected as year-round residents throughout Basti and Ayodhya districts, particularly in areas with mango groves, orchards, and wooded villages. Listen for their loud whistles in early morning hours. They are conspicuous when calling but skulk in foliage when foraging. Look for characteristic suspended nests in fruit-bearing trees. A first record should document location, date, time, and presence of nests (if found). Submit through the atlas About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia; found from Bangladesh to Thailand and Myanmar.

In India: Distributed throughout the plains and foothills up to 2,000 m; common in most provinces.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread resident in deciduous forests, orchards, and cultivated areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Year-round resident; common in mango groves, orchards, and wooded villages.

Field rule: A bright yellow bird with a black head and upper body is a male Black-hooded Oriole. Greenish-yellow birds with longer bills are females. Entirely black or different pattern males are other species.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The Black-hooded Oriole was first described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae (10th edition). The genus Oriolus is a large, cosmopolitan group of orioles distributed across the Old World.

Subspecies. Oriolus xanthornus xanthornus — the nominate subspecies found throughout India and Southeast Asia.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Oriolidae (orioles and figbirds). The genus Oriolus contains 28 recognized species.

Phylogenetic notes. Oriolus xanthornus is closely related to other Oriolus species in Asia. Molecular studies suggest the xanthornus group (including O. xanthornus and O. kundoo) forms a clade.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) due to wide range, stable populations, and ability to exploit human-modified habitats with fruit-bearing trees.
