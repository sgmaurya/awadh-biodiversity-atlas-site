हरी काई / Microcystis (genus-level profile)

Microcystis aeruginosa (Kützing) Kützing — Microcystaceae

हरी काई (जहरीली) — तालाब का पानी अचानक पीले-हरे से गहरे हरे रंग का हो जाता है, रंग बदलकर नीले-हरे जैसा, और सड़े हुए अंडे की गंध आने लगती है। यह Microcystis का खिलना है — जलीय जीवाणु का विस्फोट। यह सुंदर नहीं है, यह खतरनाक है। कुछ इसमें माइक्रोसिस्टिन नाम का ज़हर होता है जो गाय, भैंस और लोगों को बीमार कर सकता है। भारत और दुनिया के कई हिस्सों में यह समस्या बढ़ रही है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
AppearanceCloudy, opaque water; pea-green to bright green to blue-green or olive-brown colour
TextureSmooth, slimy film or suspension; not individual filaments visible to the eye
SmellEarthy, musty smell (resembles clean algae) OR foul, rotten smell (indicates toxin production)
DistributionEntire water body clouded; concentrates at surface where wind pushes it; can form a scum at edges
ScumA slimy, sticky layer or mat may form on the water surface; bright green or blue-green colour; does not float like water hyacinth
Particle sizeMicroscopic; individual colonies 10–150 µm (not visible to naked eye but visible as a suspension)
No filamentsUnlike Nostoc (which forms distinct jelly blobs) or Spirogyra (which forms threads), Microcystis is a cloudy suspension

Field tip: A pond that turns pea-green to blue-green from May to October, with a slimy film and earthy or foul smell, is likely a Microcystis bloom. If the water smells like rotten eggs or becomes milky opaque, it is a warning sign. Do not swim, drink from, or let cattle drink from water with visible bloom.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: तालाब का पानी अचानक गहरा हरा हो जाता है, धुंधला, लिजलिजा, गंध सड़ी-सी। मई-जून में शुरुआत, सितंबर-अक्टूबर तक रह सकता है। नदी के किनारों पर गहरी परत।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (blooming populations):

MeasurementRange
Colony diameter (aggregate)20–150 µm
Cell diameter2–5 µm
Cells per colonyTens to thousands
Bloom density10³–10⁶ cells per mL of water

Structure: Microcystis is a cyanobacterium (prokaryote), not a plant, though historically classified as a blue-green alga. Individual cells are spherical, organised into irregular (not filamentous) colonies held together by a polysaccharide sheath (often gelatinous). The lack of a cell wall in eukaryotes and the presence of cyanobacterial membranes (thylakoids) define the group.

Pigments: Chlorophyll a (blue-green). Many strains also contain the carotenoid echinenone and the phycobilin phycocyanin, which together give the cells blue-green or olive colouration.

Buoyancy: Gas vesicles (small, hollow protein structures) allow the cells to regulate buoyancy and float or sink.

Toxin production: A subset of Microcystis strains produce microcystin, a hepatotoxic (liver-damaging) peptide. Toxin production is facultative and varies with strain, temperature, nutrient availability and light. Not all blooms are toxic, but toxicity cannot be predicted visually.

हिंदी: न पौधा है, न शैवाल — जीवाणु है, पर हरा और प्रकाश से ऊर्जा पाता है। छोटी गोल कोशिकाएँ, समूह में। कुछ किस्मों में जहर बनता है। गैस की थैलियों से तैरता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Microcystis is a ubiquitous, cosmopolitan cyanobacterium found in freshwater systems worldwide. It thrives in nutrient-rich (eutrophic) water with abundant nitrogen and phosphorus. The species is relatively tolerant of low light, making it competitive in turbid, nutrient-polluted ponds where eukaryotic algae are less successful.

Blooming is a complex phenomenon triggered by a combination of factors:

  • Warm water: growth accelerates above 20 °C; peak blooms occur at 25–30 °C
  • Nutrient excess: agricultural runoff (nitrogen, phosphorus) from fields and sewage
  • Stagnant water: still ponds and slow-moving rivers; no flushing or disturbance
  • Low light competition: once established, Microcystis is shade-tolerant and outcompetes other algae
  • Allelopathy: some strains produce compounds that inhibit competitors

The toxin-producing strains (containing microcystin) have no known ecological advantage; toxin production may be a byproduct of stress or an evolutionary relic.

Microcystis blooms can occur in any eutrophic pond, but frequency and severity are increasing globally due to climate warming and nutrient pollution.

हिंदी: गर्म, प्रदूषित, ठहरे पानी में विस्फोट। गर्मी, खाद के कणों (नाइट्रोजन, फॉस्फोरस), अन्य शैवाल से मुकाबले की कमी — ये तीनों मिलें तो ब्लूम होता है। जहर का कोई फायदा नहीं, बस दुर्घटना।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Month / seasonEvent
April–MayWater warms; low Microcystis cells begin to increase
May–JuneFirst visible bloom in eutrophic ponds; bright green water develops
July–SeptemberPeak bloom period; toxin concentrations highest in August–September (when warmest)
October–NovemberBloom recedes as water cools; cells settle or are diluted
November–AprilLow abundance; cells dormant or dilute in sediment; brief resurgence in warm spells

Reproduction is entirely asexual, by cell division. Under optimal conditions, population doubling time is 24–48 hours, enabling rapid bloom formation.

हिंदी: अप्रैल में शुरुआत, मई-जून में पहली दिखाई, जुलाई-सितंबर चरम, अक्टूबर तक सो जाता है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Bloom companions: Other cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Oscillatoria, Nostoc) and eukaryotic algae (Chlorella, Scenedesmus) in the same water; competitive relationships
  • Nutrient sources: Agricultural runoff from Paddy fields, livestock and urban sewage
  • Grazers: Zooplankton (daphnia, copepods) feed on Microcystis, but cannot control toxin-producing blooms; ciliate and rotifer zooplankton decline in blooms due to toxins
  • Indicators: Where Microcystis blooms, Water Hyacinth and lotus decline; oxygen depletion follows

Toxin and Health Effects जहर और स्वास्थ्य

Microcystin toxicity statement: A subset of Microcystis strains produce microcystin, a cyclic peptide hepatotoxin. This toxin accumulates in liver and kidney, causing acute gastrointestinal illness (vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea) and, in severe cases, liver damage and death. Skin contact with toxic blooms can cause dermatitis and eye irritation.

Exposure routes: Drinking water from blooming water bodies, swimming in blooms (inhalation of aerosol toxin), and ingestion of fish or livestock exposed to the bloom.

Honesty about testing: No toxin testing has been conducted on Microcystis blooms in Basti or eastern UP. The published literature documents toxin production in blooms globally and in several Indian states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha); it is unknown whether strains present in Basti district are toxin-producing. A pond with a visible pea-green to blue-green bloom in May–October may or may not be toxic — testing is required to confirm toxicity.

Precautions: Do not drink, swim in, or allow livestock to drink from water with visible Microcystis bloom until tested. Do not eat fish from severely bloomed ponds (though toxin accumulation in fish is less certain than in direct water exposure).

हिंदी: कुछ किस्मों में माइक्रोसिस्टिन जहर बनता है। पी जाने से पेट का दर्द, उल्टी, डायरिया, लीवर को नुकसान, और मृत्यु। पूर्वी यूपी में परीक्षण नहीं हुआ, पर भारत के दूसरे राज्यों में मामले दर्ज हैं। गहरी हरी काई वाले तालाब से गाय-भैंसों को नहीं पिलाना, तालाब में नहीं नहाना।

Life Cycle / Phenology and Human Relevance जीवन चक्र और मानव प्रासंगिकता

Agricultural problem: Blooms reduce water quality and availability for irrigation and livestock. Clogged intake screens and pipes; reduced oxygen harms fish in culture ponds.

Public health: Drinking water sources contaminated by blooms pose a risk to humans and animals. Coastal and lakeside communities are at higher risk due to higher bloom frequency.

Prevention: The primary approach is reducing nutrient pollution:

  • Reduce agricultural runoff by improving field management and buffer strips
  • Treat sewage and livestock effluent before discharge
  • Restore vegetation in riparian zones (river banks and pond margins)
  • In climate-adapted irrigation systems, circulate and aerate stagnant water

Control of existing blooms:

  • Manual removal and disposal (labour-intensive)
  • Aeration of stagnant water
  • Copper sulfate or other algicides (risky; kills all algae, can mobilise toxins)
  • UV and ozone treatment of drinking water
  • No biological control agent is proven

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The prevalence of toxic vs. non-toxic Microcystis strains in Indian freshwater systems is poorly documented. Baseline toxin testing of ponds in eastern UP would establish risk. Understanding the drivers of bloom formation in specific agricultural regions could inform early-warning systems.

हिंदी: रोकना है तो खाद का प्रदूषण कम करो — खेत से बहकर आने वाली खाद, गोठ से निकलने वाला पानी, गाँव की नाली। नाले के किनारों पर घास-झाड़ियाँ लगाओ। ठहरे तालाब को हवा देना (नल से छिड़काव)। कॉपर सल्फेट से मारना जोखिम भरा है।

Microcystis blooms are to be expected in eutrophic village ponds and irrigation canals receiving agricultural runoff during the warm season (May–October), particularly in years of above-normal nutrient influx (heavy fertiliser use, livestock crowding). Ponds with fish kills in summer and a history of "green water" are candidates. A photograph and water sample (for toxin testing) would be valuable baseline data for eastern UP.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Worldwide in freshwater systems; ubiquitous in eutrophic regions.

In India: Common in ponds and lakes of all regions; toxic blooms reported from Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in eutrophic ponds throughout the state; toxic blooms reported from several districts in recent years.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in eutrophic ponds and irrigation canals; frequency and severity unknown; no published toxin records yet.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The genus Microcystis was first described by Kützing (1833). The species M. aeruginosa (Kützing) Kützing is the most common and widespread. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies and strains: Microcystis aeruginosa is a cosmopolitan, highly variable species. Many strains exist, differing in morphology, pigmentation and toxin production. Some authorities divide this into subspecies or ecotypes; molecular work continues to refine taxonomy. This profile treats the genus as a whole, since blooming populations often contain multiple species and strains that are difficult to identify in the field.

Family and order placement. Cyanobacteria (formerly "blue-green algae"), kingdom Bacteria. NOT a plant or eukaryotic alga. Cyanobacteria are prokaryotes that perform oxygenic photosynthesis; they are believed to have invented oxygen production over two billion years ago.

Phylogenetic notes. Cyanobacteria are ancient. Microcystis is thought to have radiated widely after the evolution of oxygen-producing photosynthesis. The toxin-producing strains are not a monophyletic group; toxin production genes may be horizontally transferred. The evolution and maintenance of toxin production is debated.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated; microorganisms are not formally assessed.
