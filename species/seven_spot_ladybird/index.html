सात बिंदी भृंग / Seven-spot Ladybird

Coccinella septempunctata Linnaeus, 1758 — Coccinellidae

सात बिंदी भृंग — चमकीली लाल पीठ पर काली सात बिंदियाँ, बहुत सुंदर और भाग्यशाली माना जाता है। इसके लार्वे और बड़े भृंग दोनों हज़ारों की संख्या में माहू खा जाते हैं और खेत बचाते हैं।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Adult length6–9 mm
Adult width5–8 mm
Larva length6–10 mm

Adult: Oval, convex beetle. Elytra (wing covers) bright red to orange-red, with exactly seven black spots (typically 3 + 3 + 1 shared). Pronotum (thorax) black, usually with pale or white markings forming an "M" or "W" shape. Underside black. Legs black. Antennae segmented, filiform.

Larva: Elongate, spindle-shaped, grey or dull black with orange-yellow spots or stripes. Body warty or granulate. Legs and antennae dark. Highly mobile; voracious feeder.

Pupa: Pale cream to yellow-orange, attached to leaf surface; pupation is brief (3–5 days).

हिंदी: लाल चमकीली भृंग, सात काली बिंदियाँ। लार्वा काला, पीली या नारंगी धारियाँ।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Seven-spot Ladybird is one of the most voracious aphid predators in temperate and warm regions. Both adults and larvae consume large numbers of aphids and other small soft-bodied insects (scale insects, mealybugs, young caterpillars, small flies). A single larva can consume 200–300 aphids during its development; an adult feeds continuously and can consume 50–100 aphids per day. The species is a multipurpose predator in agro-ecosystems and is widely regarded as a beneficial insect. It shows no host plant specificity; adults and larvae are found on any crop infested with suitable prey.

The beetle is highly migratory in temperate regions, showing seasonal movement and aggregation for overwintering. In tropical and subtropical regions such as eastern Uttar Pradesh, the species is sedentary and breeds year-round, with peak activity during the monsoon and post-monsoon months when aphid populations are high.

हिंदी: एक लार्वा अपनी ज़िंदगी में पचास से सौ माहू खा जाता है। खेत का रक्षक है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary prey:

  • Aphids: green peach aphid, cabbage aphid, corn leaf aphid, and 50+ other species
  • Scale insects
  • Mealybugs
  • Young caterpillars (eggs of harmful moths)

Found on:

  • Wheat, rice, maize, pulses
  • Cabbage, cauliflower, leafy vegetables
  • Cotton, sugarcane
  • Ornamental plants and gardens

The beetle is highly polyphagous; any plant with suitable prey will sustain populations.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid wasps: Telestes and other hymenoptera occasionally parasitise larvae
  • Fungal infections: rare; mainly in very wet conditions
  • Predatory flies: robber flies (Asilidae) capture adults
  • Spiders: web-builders and hunters; occasional predators
  • Birds: particularly insectivorous species consume both larvae and adults

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Natural pest control. The Seven-spot Ladybird is one of the most important biological control agents of aphids and other soft-bodied pests across the world. Farmers in many regions actively preserve and encourage ladybird populations by avoiding broad-spectrum insecticides. In organic and conservation agriculture systems, abundant ladybird populations are a sign of good crop health.

Documented benefits: A single adult ladybird can consume 100+ aphids per day; a larva will consume 200–300 during development. This makes the species extremely valuable in integrated pest management systems.

Cultural significance: The seven-spot ladybird is considered a symbol of good luck and protection in many cultures, including Hindu traditions where it is associated with the goddess Lakshmi.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widely distributed across temperate Europe, Asia, and introduced to North America for biological control. Native to the Palaearctic region; now cosmopolitan.

In India: Throughout India, with higher abundance in plains and foothill regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in agricultural areas across the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all cultivated fields with aphid populations, year-round; most abundant May–November.

Field rule: Bright red or orange-red beetle with exactly seven black spots (3 + 3 + 1), black pronotum with pale markings; both larva and adult consuming aphids actively on crop leaves: Seven-spot Ladybird (Coccinella septempunctata).

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition, as Coccinella septempunctata based on European specimens.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are formally recognised, though populations show geographic variation.

Family and order placement. Order Coleoptera, family Coccinellidae (ladybird beetles). The family comprises over 4,000 described species, most of which are predatory; Coccinella septempunctata is one of the largest and most economically important.

Phylogenetic notes. The genus Coccinella contains a group of closely related aphid-predatory ladybirds distributed across temperate regions.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a widespread, abundant, and beneficial species, no conservation assessment is warranted.
