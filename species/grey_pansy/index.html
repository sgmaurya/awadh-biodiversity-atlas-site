सलेटी पंखी / Grey Pansy

Junonia atlites Linnaeus, 1763 — Nymphalidae

सलेटी पंखी — यह एक मध्यम आकार की तितली है जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की खुली घास के मैदानों, सूखे खेतों और रास्तों के किनारे उड़ते हुए दिख जाती है। इसके पंख भूरे-सलेटी रंग के होते हैं, और पंखों पर काली, पीली और नारंगी धारियाँ होती हैं। यह तितली बहुत तेज़ी से उड़ती है और ज़मीन पर बैठकर अपने पंख खोलती है। इसके पंखों के निचले हिस्से पर दो या चार आँख जैसे निशान (eyespots) होते हैं, जो शिकारियों को डराने का काम करते हैं। यह तितली घास और पौधों पर अंडे देती है और इसके बच्चे (लार्वा) घास खाते हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeMedium butterfly; wingspan 42–50 mm
ColourPredominantly greyish-brown or grey; variable with seasonal and individual variation
ForewingGreyish-brown with darker markings; orange-yellow band near the margin
HindwingGreyish, with two prominent black eyespots with pale (yellow or white) centres and white or pale blue outer rings
BodyRobust; brown; antennae are clubbed
BehaviourFast, erratic flight; perches with wings open on the ground; likes basking in the sun

Field tip: A greyish-brown butterfly that darts quickly across grasslands and lands frequently on the ground with wings spread open. The eye-spots on the underside of the hindwings are the best field marks. It is often seen in loose aggregations on damp ground or mud.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: सलेटी-भूरी तितली, तेज़ उड़ने वाली, ज़मीन पर बैठती है। पंखों पर आँख जैसे निशान।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Wingspan42–50 mm
Forewing length24–28 mm

Wing structure: The upperside (dorsum) of the wings is predominantly greyish-brown with variable intensity. The forewing (front wing) has a curved shape and displays a pale orange-yellow band running along the distal margin (outer edge). The hindwing is greyish, with two prominent black ocelli (eyespots), each with a pale yellow, white, or pale blue central spot and a bright white or pale blue outer ring. The underside (ventral surface) of the hindwing is paler, with the eyespots more conspicuous.

Sexual dimorphism: Females are often slightly larger and paler than males. Males have scent patches (androconia) on the hindwings.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A butterfly of open, sunlit habitats with grass. It is primarily a grassland specialist, though it can adapt to disturbed, fallow, and semi-cultivated areas. Males perch on the ground and perform rapid "puddling" behaviour, drinking from damp soil or mud patches to extract minerals. Flight is rapid and erratic, with frequent dives and recoveries, making it difficult to follow. The eyespots likely serve as an anti-predator mechanism, directing predator attacks to non-vital regions of the wing.

हिंदी: खुली घास के मैदानों की तितली है। तेज़ उड़ती है, ज़मीन पर बैठती है, गीली मिट्टी से खनिज चूसती है।

Generations: Multiple overlapping generations per season (at least 4–5 per year in warm regions).

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Grass species, particularly Acanthus ilicifolius and related Acanthaceae
  • Various wild grasses in grassland habitats

Adult diet:

  • Nectar from wild flowers
  • Mineral water (puddling behaviour)

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Flycatchers and insectivorous birds hunt flying adults.
  • Spiders: Web-spinning spiders capture adults.
  • Parasitoids: Braconid and ichneumonid wasps parasitize larvae and pupae.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pollination. Grey Pansy butterflies visit flowering plants in grasslands and contribute to pollination, particularly of wildflowers. While not a major agricultural pollinator, they are part of the grassland ecosystem.

No direct agricultural significance. The larval host plants are primarily wild grasses, and the species does not damage cultivated crops.

Indicator species. The presence of Grey Pansy butterflies in an area indicates healthy grassland habitat with native vegetation.

हिंदी: फूलों पर जाती है, परागकण ले जाती है। फसलों को नुकसान नहीं पहुँचाती।

Expected in open grasslands and fallow fields throughout Basti district during warm months. Areas with native grass cover and minimal disturbance are most likely to harbour this species. A record would be a photograph of the butterfly showing the eyespots on the hindwing, with place and date, or a collected specimen.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia, from India through Myanmar, Thailand, and into Indonesia.

In India: Widespread in the Indo-Gangetic plains and adjacent regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded from multiple districts; particularly common in grassland areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in grasslands and fallow fields.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Linnaeus described the Grey Pansy as Papilio atlites in 1763 in Species Plantarum (12th edition, p. 769). The type locality was India. It was later placed in the genus Junonia. The genus name Junonia was inspired by the Roman goddess Juno. The specific name atlites likely refers to Atlas or has classical mythological origins.

Subspecies. Several subspecies are recognized across Asia, with J. a. atlites occurring in India.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Nymphalidae, subfamily Junonia (brush-footed butterflies).

Phylogenetic notes. Junonia atlites belongs to a clade of grassland-specialist pansy butterflies found across tropical Asia.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Abundant and resilient.
