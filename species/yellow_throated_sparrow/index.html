जंगली गौरैया / Yellow-throated Sparrow

Gymnoris xanthocollis (Franklin, 1831) — Passeridae

जंगली गौरैया — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खेतों, झाड़ियों और खुले इलाकों में पाई जाने वाली एक छोटी, भूरी चिड़िया, जो फसलों और घास के बीजों को खाती है। इसका नाम "जंगली गौरैया" इसलिए दिया गया क्योंकि यह शहर की घरेलू गौरैया की तुलना में ज़्यादा गँवारू (rough/wild) दिखती है और खुले इलाकों में रहना पसंद करती है। इसके गले पर पीले रंग का निशान और चेहरे पर काली धारी इसे आसानी से पहचान देती हैं। यह चिड़िया आमतौर पर झुंड में देखी जाती है।

Field tip: A small, pale brown sparrow with a distinctive yellow throat patch. Often in flocks in cultivated areas and scrub. The pale colouration and yellow throat distinguish it from House Sparrow.

Size: 14–15 cm long; small sparrow.

Plumage: Overall pale sandy-brown to buff-brown; paler and more uniform than House Sparrow.

Head pattern: Pale crown. Dark loral stripe (from bill through eye) diagnostic. Dark auricular region.

Throat and breast: Distinctive yellow patch on throat; diagnostic field character. Breast buff to buff-yellow.

Underparts: Pale buff-white on belly and undertail coverts.

Bill: Conical, stubby; blackish above, pale yellowish below. Typical sparrow bill adapted for seed-cracking.

Legs and feet: Pale yellowish-brown; relatively strong.

Eyes: Dark; small.

Sexes: Males slightly larger and more vivid on throat; females duller but similar pattern.

Juveniles: Duller than adults; yellow throat patch less distinct.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact call: Repeated chirp or chip, softer than House Sparrow.
  2. Flock calls: Chattering notes when flocking.
  3. Song: Simple sequence of chirping notes; less complex than other songbirds.
हिंदी: "चिप-चिप-चिप" आवाज़ करता है। झुंड में शोर मचाता है। गीत सरल होता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Granivore specialist: Yellow-throated Sparrows are primarily seed-eaters, foraging on ground and in low vegetation for seeds. Diet consists of:

  • Grass seeds
  • Crop seeds (millet, wheat, groundnut)
  • Small insect items (especially during breeding)
  • Buds and leaves (seasonal)

Feeding behaviour: Forage in flocks on ground, picking seeds from grass and low plants. Sometimes in fields during harvest, gleaning spilled grain.

Social structure: Highly gregarious; flocks common. Flocks maintain contact with frequent calls.

Nesting: Breed colonially or semi-colonially; multiple pairs in clusters.

Roosting: Communal roosts in bushes or grass clumps; gather at dusk.

Flight: Weak, fluttering flight; rarely fly long distances; prefer moving through low cover or on ground.

Nomadic tendency: May move locally following seed availability; less migratory than some sparrow species.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहता है। बीज खाता है। खेतों में अनाज चुनता है। कमज़ोर उड़ान, ज़मीन पर चलना पसंद करता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Year-round resident: Breeds in the region; no seasonal migration. Some local movements with food availability.

Breeding: March–July; peaks April–June in Uttar Pradesh. Monogamous pairs; sometimes polygamous.

Nest: Domed or half-domed nest of grass, straw, and feathers; placed in bushes, tree cavities, or man-made structures.

Clutch: 3–4 eggs (usually 4); incubation 12–13 days.

Fledging: Young leave nest at 12–15 days. 2–3 broods per breeding season.

Vegetation and food: Activity follows seed production; peak breeding coincides with grass and crop seed production.

Flocking: Form large communal roosts outside breeding season; roosting assemblies may include multiple species.

हिंदी: साल भर रहता है। मार्च से जुलाई में अंडे देता है। 4 अंडे देता है। 15 दिन में बच्चे उड़ जाते हैं। साल में 2-3 बार अंडे देता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shrikes, small eagles, Indian Scops Owl (at roost), hobby falcons
  • At nest: Crows, snakes, monitor lizards
  • Mammalian predators: Jackals, foxes (on ground), snakes
  • Parasites: Avian parasites, blood parasites
हिंदी: छोटी चील और श्रीके इसका शिकार करते हैं। उल्लू रात को खतरा हो सकते हैं। कौवे घोंसलों को परेशान करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Crop damage: Can cause significant losses to ripening grain crops (wheat, millets) when flocks feed in fields. Damage localized but substantial in some areas with large flocks.

Pest control: During breeding season, feed extensively on insects; contribute to natural insect control.

Habitat: Favours cultivated areas with nearby grassland and scrub; benefits from agricultural landscape with seed-producing plants.

Traditional hunting: Historically hunted for food in some regions; less common today.

हिंदी: यह चिड़िया वन्यजीव अधिनियम 1972 की अंतर्गत संरक्षित है। फसलों को नुकसान पहुँचा सकती है, पर कीटें भी खाती है, जो कृषि के लिए उपयोगी है।

Range: Expected in cultivated areas, grasslands, and scrub throughout Basti–Ayodhya region.

Habitat preference: Cultivation with nearby grassland and scrub; open areas with seed-producing vegetation.

Seasonality: Resident year-round; local movements following crop seasons and seed availability. Large flocks during dry season.

Published records: GBIF holds occurrence records from the region. Likely common but sometimes overlooked due to resemblance to House Sparrow.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph clearly showing the bird's distinctive yellow throat patch and pale colouration, with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent endemic; found only in India and Pakistan.

In India: Distributed across much of the peninsula and plains; more abundant in arid and semi-arid regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident throughout the state in suitable habitat; more common in eastern and central districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Resident in cultivated areas and grasslands; locally abundant where suitable habitat exists.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by William Franklin in 1831 in Proceedings of the Zoological Society of London, page 117, as Passer xanthocollis. Type locality is India (likely Deccan).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Passeridae (weavers, sparrows, and munias). The genus Gymnoris contains the yellow-throated sparrows, distributed from North Africa through Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Part of a larger radiation of Passeridae in the Old World. Closely related to other seed-eating sparrows.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations remain widespread and stable, benefiting from agricultural expansion. Habitat generalisation (cultivation use) supports populations despite some natural grassland loss.
