ग्रेट क्रेस्टेड ग्रीब / Great Crested Grebe

Podiceps cristatus (Linnaeus, 1758) — Podicipedidae

ग्रेट क्रेस्टेड ग्रीब — यह एक बड़ी, सफेद और काली जल चिड़िया है जो सर्दियों में बड़ी झीलों पर आती है। इसके सिर पर सर्दियों के समय छोटे बाल खड़े हो जाते हैं। यह पानी के अंदर गोता लगाकर मछली पकड़ती है और लगभग पानी पर हमेशा रहती है। इसकी पैर पानी के पीछे की ओर लगे हैं, इसलिए जमीन पर चल नहीं सकती।

Field tip: A large, striking white and black grebe on large water bodies. Slender white neck and dark cap are distinctive. Dives frequently to catch fish. Most striking in breeding plumage with elaborate crest, but winter birds still distinctive.

Size: 46–51 cm long. A large grebe, one of the largest grebes in the region. Weight 700–1050 g.

Plumage (breeding): Distinctive and elaborate. Crown black with prominent crest of long feathers. Face white. Cheeks and ear-region buff or chestnut with prominent ear tufts. Back grey-black. Wings dark grey with white patch. Underparts brilliant white. Neck white.

Plumage (winter): Simpler than breeding. No crest or ear tufts. Crown and nape blackish-grey. Face and underparts white. Neck white. Ears whiter than breeding birds.

Eyes: Bright red in breeding season; pale or yellow in winter.

Bill: Long, pointed, pale with dark tip. Adapted for catching slippery fish.

Neck: Long, slender, graceful.

Legs and feet: Placed far back on body. Powerful swimmers. Toes lobed, not webbed (diagnostic of grebes). Incapable of walking on land; shuffles on belly if needed.

Tail: Vestigial or absent.

Sexes: Essentially similar, though males slightly larger.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Courtship calls: Harsh, loud "kerrick-kerrick" and wailing calls during breeding displays.
  2. Contact calls: Quieter chirps and grunts.
  3. Alarm calls: Sharp calls if threatened.
हिंदी: सर्दियों में शांत रहता है। चिल्लाना नहीं सुना जाता। गर्मियों में जोर से पुकारता है (प्रजनन क्षेत्र में)।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Piscivore specialist: Feeds almost entirely on fish. Highly specialized diving predator.

Diving behaviour: Excellent diver. Dives from surface with characteristic kick-off. Can reach depths of 20 m or more. Dives lasting 20–30 seconds common.

Feeding: Hunts entirely by diving. Takes fish of various sizes. Swallows fish underwater.

Fish prey: Prefers small to medium fish. Can take larger fish if available.

Aquatic lifestyle: Essentially confined to water. Rarely leaves water. Sleeps on water. All activities performed on or in water.

Social behaviour: Often solitary in winter, though small flocks may gather on large water bodies. Pairs bond during breeding season.

Movement: Essentially flightless in winter in eastern UP, though capable of flight. Rarely observed in flight.

Swimming: Powerful swimmers. Streamlined body adapted for underwater pursuit.

हिंदी: पानी में रहता है। मछली खाता है। गहराई में गोता लगाता है। पानी से शायद ही निकलता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in October–November from northern breeding grounds. Departs April–May.

Breeding range: Europe from Iceland to central Asia. Breeds in lakes and marshes with vegetation.

Breeding grounds: Does not breed in Uttar Pradesh. Breeds in northern Europe and central Asia, April–July.

Migration: Long-distance migrant. Nocturnal migrant.

Wintering in UP: Solitary or in small flocks on large open water bodies. Remain in winter range November–April.

Courtship: Takes place on wintering grounds in early spring. Elaborate display rituals observed.

Moult: Complete moult before arrival at wintering grounds. Breeding plumage acquired during stay in winter range.

Timing in UP: Arrival October–November; peak December–February; departure April–May.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is illegal.

Fish predator: Feeds on fish but typically does not significantly impact fish populations in large water bodies.

Water body health: Presence indicates fish productivity and water body health. Indicates presence of adequate fish food supply.

Cultural significance: Known in Hindi as "pand hari" (water bird) or similar names. Mentioned in classical literature.

Ecological indicator: Indicator species of water body health and fish productivity.

Great Crested Grebe is a winter visitor to northern India, using large lakes and reservoirs. The species was recorded at Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary (Yashmita-Ulman & Singh 2025), across the district border from Basti. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species is expected on larger water bodies of eastern Uttar Pradesh with abundant open water and sufficient fish populations. The large size, distinctive appearance, and diving behaviour make them relatively easy to spot on suitable water bodies. Peak occurrence should be December–February. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Europe and central Asia, from Iceland to Japan. Breeds in temperate regions; winters in southern Europe, North Africa, central Asia, and south through India.

In India: Widespread winter visitor to large lakes, reservoirs, and suitable water bodies. Occurs across northern and central India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Regular winter visitor to large water bodies, lakes, reservoirs, and suitable river reaches throughout the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to larger water bodies and reservoirs. Expected October–May. Recorded at Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary (across the border in Gonda), suggesting presence in region.

Field rule: A large, elegant white-and-black grebe with long slender neck and dark cap on large open water bodies is Great Crested Grebe. The size, slender neck, and striking pattern are diagnostic compared to other grebes.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae. Type locality: Europe.

Subspecies. P. c. cristatus (Linnaeus, 1758) — European and western Asian populations. P. c. australis in some taxonomies refers to Australasian populations. Indian wintering birds from European populations.

Family and order placement. Order Podicipediformes; family Podicipedidae (grebes). A monogeneric family with seven species. Grebes are highly specialized water birds, not closely related to ducks or other waterbirds.

Phylogenetic notes. Podiceps cristatus is the type species for the genus. Closest relatives are other Podiceps species.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widespread. The species is adaptable to various water body types and has no major conservation threats.
