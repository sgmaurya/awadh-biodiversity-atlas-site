Stoll's Jewel Bug / Jewel Bug

Chrysocoris stolli (Wolff, 1801) — Scutelleridae

रत्न-कीड़ा — एक ऐसा कीड़ा जो चमकाई हुई धातु की चादर से काटा गया लगता है — धातुई हरा, नारंगी-लाल निशानों के साथ — जो हमारे पहले से दर्ज आक के पौधे की फलियों पर बैठा मिलता है। सही रोशनी में यह विश्वास करना मुश्किल है कि यह संकेत एक जीवित प्राणी का है।

Quick Identification शीघ्र पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size18–23 mm body length; noticeably large for a true bug
ColourBrilliant metallic grass-green overall with orange-red spots on the scutellum edges
ScutellumLarge, rounded shield covering nearly the entire back, folded wings, and abdomen
Reflective SurfaceSmooth, highly reflective metallic sheen caused by cuticle microstructure (structural coloration)

Field tip: Look for a large, glittering, metallic-green shield bug sitting on Calotropis pods or stems. The combination of brilliant iridescent green, large rounded scutellum, and orange-red spots makes it unmistakable. Unlike the smaller Red Cotton Bug, it has a shorter, wider, shield-like shape.

Morphology आकृति विज्ञान

MeasurementRange
Body length (adult)18–23 mm
Wingspan30–38 mm
Forewing length14–18 mm

Size: 18–23 mm body length. One of the larger true bugs (Hemiptera) found in UP.

Head: Small, green, with short blunt beak (proboscis) tucked under body. Eyes dark, not prominent.

Pronotum: The trapezoidal shield behind the head; green with slight darker patterning.

Scutellum: The enlarged, rounded plate that covers nearly the entire back — extending over folded wings and most of the abdomen. This is the defining character of Scutelleridae. Bright metallic green, smooth, convex. Orange-red spots mark the edges.

Abdomen margins: Narrow orange-red stripes visible at the edges beneath the scutellum.

Legs: Medium length, green with black spines.

Antennae: Black with orange-red banding; 4-5 segments.

Wings: Largely concealed under the scutellum; only the wingtips visible when folded. When the bug flies, the wings unfurl from beneath the scutellum.

Nymphs: Bright orange-red with metallic green patches — more colourful than adults; wingless. Change through 5 instars.

The Metallic Colour — How It Works धातुई रंग — कैसे होता है

The brilliant metallic green of Chrysocoris stolli is structural colour — not a pigment. The cuticle (outer shell) of the bug has microscopic layers of different refractive indices that interfere with light waves, reflecting specific wavelengths as green. This is the same mechanism that makes hummingbird feathers, morpho butterfly wings, and beetle elytra shimmer and change colour as the viewing angle changes.

Because it is structural rather than pigment-based, the colour does not fade after death the way pigment-based colour does. A dried specimen retains its metallic lustre.

The orange-red patches are pigment-based — they contrast with the green to create warning-coloration that deters some predators.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Host plant relationship with Calotropis: In eastern UP, C. stolli is most commonly found on Calotropis gigantea (आक, FLR-0002) and C. procera (मदार). It uses its piercing-sucking proboscis to feed on plant sap from stems, seed pods, and leaves. This makes it a minor pest of Calotropis, though the plant is tough and rarely severely harmed.

Why Calotropis? Calotropis produces powerful cardiac glycosides (calotropin) that deter most insects. Jewel bugs have evolved resistance or tolerance to these toxins — a specialisation that reduces competition for their food source. The sequestered toxins may contribute to the bug's own unpalatability.

Other hosts: On a wider diet, C. stolli also feeds on Terminalia (baheda / बहेड़ा), teak (Tectona grandis), and other tree species — but Calotropis is the dominant host in eastern UP village landscapes.

As prey: Despite warning coloration, Jewel Bugs are eaten by:

  • Drongos (Dicrurus spp.) — pick them off branches
  • Starlings and mynas — ground and canopy foragers
  • Lizards — garden lizard (Calotes versicolor, REP-0001)
  • Spiders with webs near Calotropis

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Incomplete metamorphosis (hemimetabolous): Egg → Nymph (5 instars) → Adult. No pupal stage — same as other Hemiptera.

  • Eggs: Laid in neat clusters on the underside of Calotropis leaves or on stems. Pale green to white, oval, ~2 mm.
  • Nymphs: Bright orange-red with green patches — more vivid than adults. 5 instars over 4–6 weeks.
  • Adults: Most abundant July–November (monsoon + post-monsoon). Small numbers persist into summer on Calotropis.
  • Peak visibility: October–November when Calotropis is fruiting (seed pods forming and opening) — adults and nymphs aggregate on fruiting pods.

Stoll's Jewel Bug is one of the most eye-catching insects in Basti district, especially when spotted on large, dusty-green leaves of Calotropis. Observers frequently notice them in village wastelands, canal margins, and roadside shrub patches. The adults sit prominently in full sunlight on the seed pods, where their metallic green color acts as a strong visual marker. Despite this high visibility, there are very few direct observations of local bird species successfully feeding on them, suggesting that their warning coloration is effective. Local residents and children often recognize the insect and refer to it by names indicating its gold/jewel appearance (sona keeda).

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South and Southeast Asia, including India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, and southern China.

In India: Widespread in the plains and low-elevation hilly regions of almost all states, particularly associated with deciduous forest and wasteland habitats.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common throughout the state, especially along roadsides, railway tracks, and village commons where wild Calotropis grows abundantly.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Observed frequently in rural wastelands, agricultural field margins, and canal banks on primary host plants.

Range notes: No significant range changes documented.

Field rule: A large (18–23 mm), brilliant metallic green bug with orange-red spots, on Calotropis or nearby vegetation — is almost certainly Chrysocoris stolli in eastern UP. The metallic green + large rounded scutellum is unique.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Chrysocoris stolli was first described by Johann Friedrich Wolff in 1801 as Cimex stollii in Icones Cimicum descriptionibus illustratae, 2: 48, t. 5, f. 45. Type locality: Ostindien (East Indies / India). The genus Chrysocoris was established by Carl Wilhelm Hahn in 1834, and the species was later transferred to it. The genus name is derived from the Greek chrysos (gold) and koris (bug), referring to its metallic sheen, while the species epithet stolli honors the Dutch entomologist Caspar Stoll.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Belongs to the order Hemiptera, suborder Heteroptera, family Scutelleridae (shield-backed bugs). The Scutelleridae is a family of true bugs known for having an enlarged scutellum that forms a continuous shield over the wings and abdomen.

Phylogenetic notes. Chrysocoris stolli is the type species of the genus Chrysocoris. Finer relationships within the subfamily Scutellerinae are subject to ongoing molecular review, but its placement in the tribe Scutellerini is stable.

IUCN status rationale. This species has not been formally assessed by the IUCN Red List. It is highly abundant across its range in agricultural and wasteland areas, and there are no known conservation threats.

Connection to Atlas Species अन्य प्रजातियों से संबंध

This species directly connects to two already documented atlas entries:

  • FLR-0002 (Calotropis gigantea — आक): The primary host plant. If you see a Jewel Bug, look for the Calotropis it is sitting on. If you photograph Calotropis, look underneath the leaves for Jewel Bug egg clusters.
  • REP-0001 (Calotes versicolor — garden lizard): A known predator of Jewel Bugs. Garden lizards have been observed taking Jewel Bugs from Calotropis stems.
  • INS-0001 (Dysdercus cingulatus — Red Cotton Bug): Co-habitant on Calotropis; often found on the same plant at the same time.
  • Black Drongo — predator; drongos pick large insects from vegetation and open perches (BRD-0005)
  • Signature Spider — orb-weaver predator near Calotropis patches; webs catch jewel bugs (SPD-0001)
