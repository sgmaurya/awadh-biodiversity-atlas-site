गूँच / Goonch

Bagarius bagarius (Hamilton, 1822) — Bagridae

गूँच — विशालकाय मीठे पानी की कैटफिश जो गंगा और इसकी बड़ी सहायक नदियों में पाई जाती है। 1 मीटर तक बड़ी हो सकती है। माँसाहारी मछली, घने कीचड़ और बड़े पत्थरों के बीच गहरे गड्ढों में रहती है। बहुत ताकत वाली, नदी का राजा।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size60–120 cm (can reach 160 cm)
BodyRobust, powerfully built; flattened head; massive
HeadVery large, flattened; wide mouth; small eyes
Barbels4 pairs: 2 long maxillary, 2 shorter mandibular
ColorationDark grey or black above; pale grey or whitish below
SpinesPectoral and dorsal spines large, serrated; venomous
TailLarge, powerful; caudal fin deeply forked

Field tip: The largest catfish of eastern UP waters. Massive head, very long maxillary barbels, and powerful build. Found in deep pools of rivers. Readily identified by size and robustness. Nocturnal and solitary.

हिंदी: बहुत बड़ी कैटफिश; शक्तिशाली; गहरे गड्ढे में रहती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (river populations):

MeasurementRange
Standard length60–120 cm
Maximum recorded160 cm
Weight8–40 kg
Maxillary barbel length10–20 cm
Dorsal spine length8–12 cm

Body structure: Robust and powerfully muscled. Benthic form, adapted for living in deep pools. Head flattened, depressed, very large relative to body.

Head: Extremely broad and depressed. Eyes very small; mouth large and terminal. Jaws powerfully developed, filled with fine teeth. Four pairs of barbels: maxillary pair very long, reaching to the caudal peduncle; mandibular pair shorter.

Coloration: Uniformly dark grey, dark brown, or almost black on the dorsal surface. Ventral surface pale grey or whitish. Fins dark.

Spines: Dorsal and pectoral spines are large, compressed, and serrated on the posterior edge. Pectoral spines are venomous; a prick causes severe localized pain and swelling in humans.

Skin: Smooth, slimy, without scales.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A solitary, nocturnal benthic predator. Lives in deep pools of rivers, often under submerged logs or in boulder crevices. A powerful piscivore that hunts large fish, smaller catfish, and other animals by ambush. Uses barbels to locate prey in turbid water. Spawns in monsoon, making migration runs upstream to fast-flowing sections. Makes localized movements following floodwaters. One of the apex predators of river systems.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Large fish (other catfish, carp, carps)
  • Smaller fish, mullet, and cyprinids
  • Aquatic mammals: crabs, turtles, mollusks
  • Organic carrion and detritus (scavenges)

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Humans: Primary threat; over-fished; used in food and for sport
  • Apex predator: No significant natural predators as adults; juveniles prey to larger fish

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food and fishery. A highly prized food fish; meat is firm, white, and flavourful. Caught with hook and line, traps, and nets. Sold fresh at premium prices in markets. Historically, the largest catfish catches came from major rivers.

Protection status. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. IUCN status: Vulnerable (VU) due to over-fishing, habitat loss, and dam construction.

Cultural significance. Considered a trophy fish; catching a large goonch is celebrated in fishing communities.

Today. Populations have declined dramatically. Habitat loss due to dam construction, pollution, and over-fishing have greatly reduced numbers. Now locally rare except in protected river sections.

हिंदी: बहुत कीमती मछली; माँस मजबूत और स्वादिष्ट। विधि अधिनियम 1972 के अंतर्गत संरक्षित है। खतरे में है; आबादी कम हो रही है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. To be expected in deep pools of the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers, particularly in less disturbed sections. Nocturnal and elusive; most likely to be encountered by night fishers. A photograph, specimen, or reliable eyewitness report with place and date is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Gangetic system and other large river systems of the Indian subcontinent.

In India: Found in major river systems with clean, flowing water. Declining in most areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Historically common in the Ganga and major tributaries; now rare except in protected sections.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in the Ghaghra river; status unclear due to habitat changes and fishing pressure.

Field rule: The largest catfish in eastern UP waters, with a massive flattened head, long maxillary barbels, and a powerfully built body, is a Goonch. No other catfish reaches this size.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Francis Hamilton in 1822 in An Account of the Fishes Found in the River Ganges and Its Branches. Originally placed in Silurus; transferred to Bagarius by later authors. The genus name Bagarius likely refers to its predatory nature (from baghar, tiger).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Bagridae, the bagrid catfish family; order Siluriformes. Bagarius is a monotypic genus of large Asian catfish.

Phylogenetic notes. Bagarius bagarius is a specialized piscivore adapted to large river systems; its relationships within Bagridae are well-supported by molecular data.

IUCN status rationale. Vulnerable (VU). Populations have declined by more than 30% in the past 20 years due to habitat loss, dam construction, pollution, and over-fishing. Protected under the Wildlife Protection Act in some states.
