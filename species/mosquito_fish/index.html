गम्बूसिया / Western Mosquitofish

Gambusia affinis (Baird & Girard, 1853) — Poeciliidae

गम्बूसिया — अमेरिका की छोटी मछली जो मच्छरों के लार्वे खाने के लिए दुनिया भर में लाई गई और अब गाँव के हर तालाब-गड्ढे में है। मादा गर्भवती होती है और जीवित बच्चों को जन्म देती है, इसलिए एक मादा से महीने में सैकड़ों। इतनी छोटी और जल्दी बढ़ने वाली कि देसी मछलियों के बच्चों को खा जाती है और जो जगह थी उसे अपनी कर लेती है। मच्छर के लार्वे खाती है, पर सब कुछ खाती है।

Field tip: Tiny, drab, grey-brown fish that schools in weeds in small ponds and ditches. The upturned mouth and small size distinguish it from other fish. Females are bulbous with the dark belly spot. Look in any still water with algae.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बहुत छोटी, मलैन-भूरी, पतली मछली, मुँह ऊपर की ओर। मादा पेट पर काले निशान से गर्भवती दिखती है। हर तालाब में, हर गड्ढे में।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (invasive populations):

MeasurementRange

Sexual dimorphism: Females are larger, deeper-bodied, and paler, with a gravid spot visible when pregnant. Males are smaller, darker, and slender, with a modified anal fin (gonopodium) used in reproduction.

Live-bearing reproduction: Unlike egg-laying fish, females retain fertilised eggs internally until fully developed; they release live juveniles ready to feed. This continuous reproduction makes the species extremely productive.

Mouth and feeding: The mouth is small, terminal, and angled slightly upward, adapted for taking surface organisms like mosquito larvae.

हिंदी: मादा बड़ी, हल्की, पेट पर कला निशान। नर छोटी, गहरी, पिछली पंख बदला हुआ। जीवित बच्चे पैदा करती है, अंडे नहीं। महीने में दर्जनों बच्चे, लगातार।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A species that thrives on chaos and disorder. It lives in schools in murky water with algae, feeding on mosquito larvae—the justification for its introduction—but also taking any small organism that fits in its mouth, including the eggs and fry of native fish. It is tolerant of low oxygen, heat and poor water quality. In warm water it reproduces every 3–4 weeks; a single female can produce hundreds of offspring in a breeding season. Because it gives live birth and matures at 4–6 weeks, the population can expand explosively. In ponds where native fish are spawning, gambusia eats the eggs and fry and prevents recruitment. It has been implicated in the extinction of endemic fish in isolated lakes worldwide.

As a mosquito control tool, its value is disputed: it eats some larvae, but it cannot survive in fully exposed open water and requires vegetation, the same habitat mosquito fish need to breed. In flooded rice paddies, open ponds and canals it is less effective than drainage or chemical control.

हिंदी: घोलमेल पानी की मछली। मच्छर के लार्वे खाती है पर सब कुछ खाती है, देसी मछलियों के अंडे-बच्चे भी। ख़राब ऑक्सीजन सहती है। महीने में प्रजनन; छह हफ़्ते में बालिग़। पूरी नस्ल नष्ट कर सकती है। मच्छर नियंत्रण के लिए लाई गई पर यह मापीय नहीं निकली।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • mosquito larvae of Culex, Anopheles and Aedes.
  • Insect larvae and small insects.
  • Eggs and fry of native fish.
  • Daphnia, copepods and other small crustaceans.
  • Algae when preferred food is scarce.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Indian Pond Heron, Little Egret on the young and small adults.
  • Fish: Dragonfly nymphs, large aquatic insects, Stinging Catfish, Great Snakehead.
  • Others: Large aquatic insects, dragonfly and damselfly nymphs.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Mosquito control. Introduced globally in the 20th century to control mosquito larvae and thus malaria and dengue. Its effectiveness is debated: it does eat mosquito larvae, but cannot compete with chemical control, environmental management and bed nets. Its ecological impact has been demonstrably negative, and it is now recognised as one of the world's 100 worst invasive species.

In India. Introduced in the 1920s for malaria control; now widespread in still waters throughout the country. Its impact on native fish is largely undocumented.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: none.

Today. Established in most still-water habitats; a permanent feature of village ponds.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The impact of gambusia on native fish recruitment in Indian ponds is not quantified; a comparison of fish diversity and fry abundance in gambusia-free vs. gambusia-infested ponds of similar age and size would provide evidence for management strategy.

हिंदी: मलेरिया नियंत्रण के लिए 1920 में भारत लाई गई। मच्छर के लार्वे खाती है पर रसायनिक नियंत्रण और बिस्तर-जाली से कम प्रभावी। देसी मछलियों को नष्ट करती है। दुनिया की सबसे 100 घातक आक्रामक प्रजातियों में।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the southern United States; introduced to over 90 countries on every continent except Antarctica.

In India: Throughout the country since the 1920s; particularly abundant in still-water habitats in the north.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread; established in ponds, ditches and canals throughout.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all still-water habitats; likely abundant.

Field rule: A drab, slender, tiny fish with an upturned mouth and a dark belly spot (in females) in still water is a gambusia; the gravid spot and live birth are diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Baird and Girard in 1853 as Heterandria affinis in A Report on the United States and Mexican Boundary Survey, from Texas. Placed in Gambusia (named after the Rio Gambusias, Mexico) later in the 19th century.

Subspecies. The species is variable geographically; the Indian population is derived from the more widespread southern stock.

Family and order placement. Poeciliidae, the livebearing family; order Cyprinodontiformes. The genus Gambusia contains several species, all small and live-bearing, originally from Mexico and the southern United States.

Phylogenetic notes. Gambusia is among the older lineages of poeciliids; the genus is monophyletic.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Extremely abundant and invasive; expanding in range.
