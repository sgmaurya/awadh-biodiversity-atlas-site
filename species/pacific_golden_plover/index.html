पैसिफ़िक गोल्डन प्लोवर / Pacific Golden Plover

Pluvialis fulva (Gmelin, 1789) — Charadriidae

पैसिफ़िक गोल्डन प्लोवर — यह एक छोटी चाँदी चिड़िया है जो सर्दियों में आर्कटिक क्षेत्र से भारत आती है। इसका नाम इसके सोने जैसे पीले रंग के धब्बों से आता है, जो सर्दियों में कम दिखाई देते हैं। ये चिड़ियाँ खुली जगहों पर दौड़ती हैं और तेजी से रुकती हैं। गर्मियों में ये आर्कटिक इलाकों में प्रजनन करती हैं।

Field tip: A small, active plover running on mudflats and grasslands. Larger than other small plovers. Winter birds show golden spotting on back and crown. Long dark legs distinguish from closely related American Golden Plover.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 24–26 cm long; wingspan 44–48 cm. Weight 40–65 g.

Plumage (winter, as seen in UP): Crown and upperparts brown with subtle golden spotting, particularly on crown and scapulars. Back warm brown. Rump white. Tail dark with white edges. Underparts white. Breast white with some golden-buff streaking. Flanks white. Undertail coverts white.

Plumage (breeding, not seen in UP): Completely transformed. Face and underparts black. Crown and back golden-spotted on black. Very striking.

Bill: Short, pointed, dark. Shorter and finer than larger plovers.

Legs: Long for body size, dark grey or dark. Longer than American Golden Plover.

Feet: Short toes.

Eyes: Dark, alert.

Tail: Short, square-ended.

Sexes: Very similar in winter plumage.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Main call: Clear two-note whistle "chu-wee" or "pee-wee", distinctive and recognizable.
  2. Alarm calls: Sharp calls when flushed.
  3. Contact calls: Quieter calls within flocks.
हिंदी: "चू-वी" या "पी-वी" की साफ सीटी। समूह में बोलती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Omnivore: Feeds on insects, invertebrates, and occasionally plant seeds.

Feeding behaviour: Characteristic "run-and-pause" feeding pattern. Runs actively on ground or mudflat, then stops to feed. Probes ground with bill for invertebrates.

Habitat preference: Open areas: mudflats, wet grasslands, cultivated fields, sandy areas. Avoids dense vegetation.

Gregarious: Often in flocks, sometimes with other plover species. Flocks can be quite large.

Active and alert: Constantly active. Alert to predators. Flocks take flight together at alarm.

Flight: Strong, swift flight. Makes distinctive flight calls.

Daily activity: Active throughout the day and into evening. Crepuscular feeding also observed.

हिंदी: खुली जगहों पर दौड़ता है। झुंडों में रहता है। कीड़े खोजता है। बहुत सतर्क रहता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Long-distance migrant: Breeds in Arctic Alaska and Siberia. One of the longest-distance migrants among waders.

Winter visitor: Arrives August–September from breeding grounds. Departs April–May.

Breeding range: Arctic coastal tundra of Alaska and eastern Siberia. Breeds May–July.

Migration: Extraordinary long-distance migrant. Undertakes flights of 10,000 km or more. Nocturnal migrant.

Wintering: Solitary or in small flocks in suitable habitat. Occupy same wintering territories year after year.

Moult: Complete moult in winter on wintering grounds. Breeding plumage acquired before departure.

Timing in UP: Early arrival August (vanguard); main arrival September; peak September–March; departure April–May.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Common Kestrel, Merlin, Egyptian Vulture, larger raptors
  • Mammalian predators: Jackal, fox, mongoose
  • Chick predators (breeding only): Arctic predators: skuas, owls

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is illegal.

Insect control: Feeds on insects, small invertebrates. May help control pest insects in cultivated areas and grasslands.

Agricultural habitat use: Uses cultivated fields and grasslands during winter. Generally not considered a pest.

Ecological indicator: Presence indicates suitable habitat for wintering waders. Presence of flocks may indicate habitat quality.

Cultural significance: Known locally as "dhotee" or similar names. Part of traditional winter fauna.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी विधि अधिनियम 1972 के अंतर्गत संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है। खेतों में कीड़ों को खाता है।

Pacific Golden Plover is a long-distance winter migrant to Asia and is expected throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh, particularly in open habitats including mudflats, grasslands, and cultivated fields. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species arrives from late August and can remain through May, with peak occurrence October–March. The distinctive "chu-wee" call and characteristic run-and-pause feeding behaviour make it identifiable in flocks. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Arctic Alaska and eastern Siberia. Winters throughout Asia from India to Australia and Pacific islands.

In India: Widespread winter visitor to open habitats across the country. Common throughout.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread winter visitor to suitable open habitats throughout the state. Common and regular.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor to open areas, mudflats, and grasslands. Expected August–May. Often overlooked due to similarity to other plovers.

Field rule: A small active plover with long dark legs running on mudflats with golden spotting on crown and back, giving "chu-wee" calls is Pacific Golden Plover. The long legs and golden spotting are diagnostic compared to American Golden Plover.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Gmelin in 1789. Type locality: Asia/Siberia.

Subspecies. P. f. fulva (Gmelin, 1789) — Asian populations (including those wintering in India). Monotypic or with minimal subspecific variation.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Charadriidae (plovers and lapwings). Genus Pluvialis contains three species of large plovers.

Phylogenetic notes. Pluvialis fulva is closely related to American Golden Plover (P. dominica), with which it was previously considered conspecific. Recent molecular work confirms them as distinct species.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widespread. No major conservation threats, though wetland degradation could affect wintering habitat.
