क्रो-बिल्ड ड्रोंगो / Crow-billed Drongo

Dicrurus annectens (Hodgson, 1836) — Dicruridae

क्रो-बिल्ड ड्रोंगो — एक मजबूत, भारी चोंच वाला वन ड्रोंगो। बेकर (1924) के अनुसार इसका पूरा शरीर चमकदार स्टील-नीली चमक वाला काला होता है और नथुनों पर चोंच चौड़ी होती है। यह अप्रैल से जून में हिमालय की तलहटी में 2,000 फीट तक प्रजनन करता है। यह उड़ते हुए कीड़ों और दीमकों का शिकार करता है और शिकारी पक्षियों पर हमला करके उन्हें भगा देता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1924):

MeasurementRange
Biometricstail 116 to 130 mm ; tarsus 19 to 20 mm (Baker 1924)

The Crow-billed Drongo is distinguished by its robust bill and glossy plumage. Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) described the species: "Whole plumage black, glossed with steel-blue except on the lores, ear-coverts, inner webs of wing-quills and inner webs of rectrices ; the tips of a few of the under wing-coverts and axillaries are nearly always tipped with tiny white dots."

The bill is broad and stout at the base, giving the bird its common name. Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) recorded soft parts: iris bright hazel to deep brown; bill and legs black. Young birds have bold white tips to the axillaries and under wing-coverts, with the lower plumage from breast to under tail-coverts more or less barred with white (Baker 1924, p. 354).

हिंदी:
बेकर (1924) के अनुसार इसकी पूंछ 116 से 130 मिमी और टार्सस 19 से 20 मिमी होता है। इसका पूरा शरीर चमकदार नीली झलक लिए काला होता है और चोंच काली होती है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Stuart Baker (1924, p. 355) noted that although not really migratory, it is "a curious wanderer in the non-breeding season backwards and forwards within a small area." It breeds only in the lower hills and adjacent plains, wandering into surrounding forest tracts after breeding (Baker 1924, p. 355).

Baker (1924, p. 355) observed: "They are not gregarious birds and are not found in flocks, but they are very sociable and several individuals are often to be seen feeding amicably together. They are as plucky and pugnacious as the other members of their family, and will tackle any hawk, eagle or four-footed adversary that enters their domain, assisting one another against their common enemy." Flight is like that of the common Black Drongo, but rather heavier and slower, though they display great activity in pursuit of insect prey (Baker 1924, p. 355).

हिंदी:
बेकर (1924) के अनुसार यह झुंड में नहीं रहती, किंतु कई पक्षी एक साथ शांति से भोजन तलाशते हैं। यह बहुत साहसी होती है और अपने क्षेत्र में आने वाले बाज़ या अन्य शिकारियों पर हमला कर देती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) recorded that it breeds "during April, May and June in the foot-hills of the Himalayas and up to some 2,000 feet throughout its range."

Baker (1924, p. 354) described the nest as a shallow saucer made of roots, weed-stems, bents, and grass blades bound with cobwebs and decorated with lichen and bark, placed on a horizontal fork of an outer swaying slender branch of a tree in thin forest or mixed bamboo. The clutch consists of handsome eggs varying from creamy-white to warm salmon, streaked with reddish or chestnut-brown (Baker 1924, p. 354).

हिंदी:
बेकर (1924) के अनुसार यह अप्रैल से जून में पतली टहनी के कांटे में घास और मकड़ी के जालों से प्यालेनुमा घोंसला बनाती है। अंडे मलाईदार सफेद या गुलाबी रंग के होते हैं।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Stuart Baker (1924, p. 355) noted: "Their flight is like that of the common Black Drongo, but rather heavier and slower, though they display great activity in the pursuit of termites and flying insect-prey. Their diet is entirely insectivorous."

हिंदी:
बेकर (1924) के अनुसार इसका आहार पूरी तरह कीटभक्षी है और यह विशेष रूप से उड़ने वाली दीमकों और अन्य कीड़ों का हवा में शिकार करती है।

In Uttar Pradesh, published records are known only from Lakhimpur Kheri district in the submontane Terai, where eleven records were obtained by thirteen observers between 2018 and 2025 during April to June and August.

Distribution वितरण

Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) recorded the distribution: "Nepal, Sikkim, Oudh, Assam, North and South of the Brahmaputra, Chin and Kachin Hills, Shan States, Karen Hills, Tenasserim, Siam and Malay Peninsula."

Baker explicitly named historical Oudh in its range, and stated that it breeds "during April, May and June in the foot-hills of the Himalayas and up to some 2,000 feet" (Baker 1924, p. 354). Modern Uttar Pradesh records are from the submontane forest tracts of Lakhimpur Kheri in April to June and in August.

हिंदी:
बेकर (1924) ने इसके वितरण में ऐतिहासिक 'अवध', नेपाल, सिक्किम और असम का उल्लेख किया था। आधुनिक उत्तर प्रदेश में इसके रिकॉर्ड लखीमपुर खीरी के तराई जंगलों से हैं।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Brian Houghton Hodgson in 1836 in India Review and Journal of Foreign Science and the Arts (Vol. 1, p. 326), under the basionym Buchanga annectens. The type locality is Nepal.

Synonyms and historical names. Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) and Blanford and Oates placed the species in Dicrurus. Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) rejected the proposed Siamese race siamensis (separated on supposed small bill size), concluding that continuous variation across the species range makes geographical subdivision untenable.

Subspecies. Stuart Baker (1924, p. 354) recognized no valid subspecies across its range.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes, family Dicruridae (drongos), genus Dicrurus.

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC) by the IUCN Red List (BirdLife International 2017).

हिंदी:
हॉजसन ने 1836 में नेपाल से इसका वर्णन किया था। यह डिक्रुरिडी (ड्रोंगो) कुल का सदस्य है और संकटमुक्त (LC) है।
