काला तीतर / Black Francolin

Francolinus francolinus (Linnaeus, 1766) — Phasianidae

काला तीतर — यह काली चिड़िया खेतों के घास और उजाड़ जगहों पर रहती है। नर बहुत काला होता है, मादा भूरी होती है। सुबह और शाम को इसकी ज़ोरदार आवाज़ सुनाई देती है, जैसे कोई तेजी से "कट-कट" बोल रहा हो। यह जमीन पर दौड़ता है, उड़ता कम है। खेतों में बीज और कीड़े खाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

BehaviourRuns rapidly on ground; reluctant to fly

Field tip: Look for this small, dark game bird in grasslands, crop margins, and scrubland in early morning and late evening when it calls. Males are entirely black; females are brown and heavily barred. The bird prefers to run rather than fly, and the distinctive call is often the first sign of presence.

Plumage — Males: Entirely black or very dark brown, with glossy sheen. Feather edges may show faint barring in some birds. Head and neck entirely black. Wings dark with subtle barring. Tail short, dark, held upright.

Plumage — Females: Brown and heavily barred; cryptic colouration. Head buffish with dark markings. Upper back and wings buff-brown with heavy dark barring. Underparts buff with dark barring. Tail brown with barring.

Head: Small, rounded. Eyes dark. Cheeks with red bare skin (more prominent in males). A small black crest may be erected.

Bill: Short, stout, pale horn-coloured.

Legs: Red-orange to bright red; longer in males than females. Males have prominent spurs used in fights.

Sexes: Strongly dimorphic; males entirely black, females brown and heavily barred.

Juveniles: Resemble females; duller and less distinctly marked initially.

Vocalisations

Highly vocal, particularly in early morning and late evening:

  1. Main call: A loud, distinctive kataak-kataak-kataak or kateek-kateek, repeated rhythmically; carries for long distance.
  2. Alarm call: A loud chak-chak-chak when flushed.
  3. Contact call: Softer chirps between pairs.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ground forager: Feeds primarily on the ground, consuming:

  • Seeds and grain (particularly lost grain in harvested fields)
  • Insects and larvae
  • Green shoots and leaves
  • Berries and small fruits

Feeding technique: Walks and runs through grass and crops, picking food from the ground. May scratch at soil or leaf litter to expose prey. Generally forages in early morning and late evening.

Territorial: Males are highly territorial during breeding season, defending areas with loud calls and physical fights. Territory size approximately 10–20 hectares.

Breeding displays: Males engage in aggressive encounters with rivals, fighting with spurs. Display involves raised crest and puffed plumage.

Social structure: Pairs breed; both sexes defend territory. Young remain with parents through first few months.

Habitat preference: Strictly a bird of grasslands and scrubland; avoids forests. Prefers areas with short grass and cultivated crops.

Sedentary: Largely resident year-round; minimal movement.

हिंदी: घास में रहता है, दौड़ता है। बहुत लड़ाका है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round in suitable grassland and cultivated habitat.

Breeding: March to August; peak April–June. Males highly vocal during breeding season.

Nesting: Ground nest — simple scrape in grass or crop field, lined with grass. Nest location chosen to blend with surrounding vegetation.

Eggs: 6–10 buff or olive eggs with dark markings; incubation approximately 18–20 days by female alone.

Chicks: Precocial; leave nest within hours. Parents guard chicks; chicks feed themselves immediately.

Fledging: Chicks become independent at approximately 8–10 weeks.

Roosting: On ground in tall grass; may roost communally in small groups post-breeding.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Raptors: Shikra, sparrowhawk, and other small eagles
  • Snakes: Monitor lizards and grass snakes
  • Crows and corvids: Depredate eggs and chicks
  • Jackals and wild dogs: May hunt adults
  • Habitat loss: Decline of grasslands
  • Hunting: Historically hunted for food
हिंदी: बाज़, साँप, कौवे का खतरा। घास के मैदान की कमी से संकट।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: Listed in Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, amended 2022. All wild birds are protected; hunting is prohibited.

Pest control: By consuming grasshoppers, crickets, and other invertebrates, the species provides some benefit in agricultural pest management, though this is not a primary role.

Habitat association: Dependent on grasslands, crop margins, and scrubland. The conversion of grasslands to cultivated fields and urban development has been detrimental to the species in many regions.

Seed dispersal: May assist in seed dispersal by consuming and defecating seeds while moving through landscapes.

Cultural significance: Historically hunted as a game bird in parts of South Asia; cultural traditions involving hunting have declined with legal protection.

हिंदी: विधि अधिनियम 1972 के तहत संरक्षित है। घास के मैदान जरूरी हैं।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Black Francolin is expected as a year-round resident in grasslands, crop margins, and scrubland of Basti and Ayodhya districts, particularly in areas with expanses of grassland interspersed with cultivation. The species should be listened for in early morning and late evening, when the loud kataak-kataak call is distinctive and carries well. Search in tall grass, rice stubble, and grass margins of fields. The entirely black male is unmistakable; brown, barred females are more cryptic. A first record would be a clear photograph or audio recording of the distinctive call, with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent, from the foothills of the Himalayas to south India. Also in parts of the Middle East.

In India: Widespread in suitable grassland and scrubland habitat throughout the mainland.

In Uttar Pradesh: Historically widespread in grasslands and cultivated areas; populations have declined due to grassland conversion.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in grasslands and crop margins throughout the region, though populations may have declined due to habitat loss.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1766 as Tetrao francolinus in Systema Naturae, 12th edition. Type locality: India.

Subspecies. Two subspecies recognised: F. f. francolinus (occurring in the Indian subcontinent, including eastern UP) and F. f. asiae (occurring in parts of Middle East). Eastern UP form is F. f. francolinus.

Family and order placement. Order Galliformes; family Phasianidae (pheasants, partridges, francolins). The genus Francolinus contains numerous partridge species found across Africa and Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies place Francolinus francolinus within a larger clade of Old World partridges and francolins.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) on the IUCN Red List. The species has a wide range and is adaptable to various grassland habitats, though it is dependent on the maintenance of grasslands and scrubland.
