ब्लू पैंज़ी / Blue Pansy

Junonia orithya (Linnaeus, 1758) — Nymphalidae

ब्लू पैंज़ी — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खुले मैदानों और घास की जगहों में आम रूप से पाई जाने वाली एक मध्यम-बड़ी तितली है। इसके पंखों का रंग भूरा होता है लेकिन इन पर चमकीली नीली पट्टियाँ और गहरी आँख जैसी लकीरें (eyespots) होती हैं। नर के पंखों पर नीली पट्टियाँ ज़्यादा चमकीली होती हैं। यह तितली तेज़ी से उड़ती है और अपने इलाके की सतर्कता से रक्षा करती है। इसकी इल्ली घास की पत्तियों पर खाती है।

Field tip: Look for a brown butterfly with striking bright blue iridescent patches or bands visible in certain angles of light, combined with eyespots. The combination of brown ground colour with bright blue patches and eyespots is distinctive. Males show more vivid blue than females.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements in northern Indian plains):

MeasurementRange
Body length23–27 mm
Wingspan62–78 mm
Forewing length29–36 mm

Body: Stocky, brown-fuscous, with clubbed antennae.

Wings:

  • Dorsal (upper) surface: Ground colour is warm brown or tan. The wings display distinctive bright iridescent blue patches or bands, particularly visible in cell and on the hindwing. The blue coloration is iridescent and changes with lighting angle. Two large eyespots are present, typically with pale yellow or whitish rings surrounding darker centers. The forewing usually has a pale or whitish patch on the apex.
  • Ventral (lower) surface: Paler brown, with similar eyespot structure but less vivid coloration.

Iridescence: The bright blue coloration is due to structural iridescence in the scales rather than pigmentation. The blue patches reflect light and may appear more or less vivid depending on viewing angle.

Sexual Dimorphism: Males show more vivid and extensive blue iridescence. Females are duller with less pronounced blue patches.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Swift grassland flyer with territorial behaviour. The Blue Pansy is an active butterfly of open grasslands and scrubland. Males are highly territorial and perch on low grass stems or rocks, rapidly chasing competitors and pursuing females. They are attracted to damp earth and mud. Females are more cryptic and spend more time on grass where they lay eggs. When threatened, the butterfly may open its wings rapidly to flash the eyespots, startling predators.

Grass specialist. The caterpillar is a grass feeder, specializing on Echinochloa and related grasses, making it a generalist in agricultural landscapes.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stage / SeasonEvent
EggLaid singly on grass stems; pale; hatch in 3–5 days
Larval (Caterpillar)4–5 instars; mature caterpillar 20–24 mm long; pale green or yellowish with darker bands; development 12–16 days
PupaSuspended chrysalis; green or brownish; emerges in 8–11 days
AdultEmerges year-round; peak abundance June to November; lifespan 2–3 weeks

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Echinochloa species (barnyard grass), Paspalum, and other common agricultural and wild grasses.

Adult nectar sources:

  • Low flowering herbs, wild flowers, weeds, and cultivated flowers.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Insectivorous birds; dragonflies are predators.
  • Spiders: Jumping spiders and grass spiders.
  • Parasitoid Wasps: Small wasps parasitize caterpillars and pupae.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pollinator. The Blue Pansy is a frequent visitor to flowering crops and grassland herbs, serving as a pollinator.

Grassland indicator. Its abundance indicates healthy grassland and habitat quality in agricultural landscapes.

To be expected in Basti district throughout the year in grasslands, scrublands, and margins of cultivated fields. Look for brown butterflies with distinctive bright blue iridescent patches visible when wings are in certain angles, combined with eyespots. Males are more frequently observed than females and show more vivid blue. Peak abundance during monsoon and post-monsoon months (June to November). The first local record would be a photograph with place and date; send it to the atlas contact on the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia, Southeast Asia, and parts of East Asia and Australia.

In India: Distributed throughout the peninsula and the Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant resident in all grassland and agricultural districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in all grass-dominated habitats throughout the year.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Linnaeus described the Blue Pansy as Papilio orithya in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition (p. 472). The type locality is Asia. The genus Junonia was later established for these pansy butterflies characterized by prominent eyespots.

Subspecies. The nominate subspecies J. o. orithya occurs throughout the Indian subcontinent.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Nymphalidae, subfamily Nymphalinae.

Phylogenetic notes. Junonia is a genus of Afro-Eurasian nymphalid butterflies, characterized by prominent eyespots and, in some species like J. orithya, iridescent patches.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Common and widespread, under no conservation threat.
