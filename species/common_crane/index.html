कूंज / Common Crane

Grus grus (Linnaeus, 1758) — Gruidae

कूंज — यह एक विशाल, लंबी गर्दन वाली सर्दियों की आने वाली चिड़िया है जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की नदियों और बड़े तालाबों में सितंबर से अप्रैल तक दिखती है। सारस से छोटी पर अभी भी बहुत बड़ी, इस चिड़िया की काली और सफेद गर्दन और काले पंख इसे तुरंत पहचान देते हैं। यह जोड़ी या छोटे झुंड में रहती है, और इसकी तूही आवाज़ सर्दियों की खाली खेतों में दूर तक सुनाई देती है।

Field tip: In large wetlands and river floodplains, look for a large grey crane with a distinctive black and white neck pattern. The white cheek and nape patch clearly distinguish it from the related Sarus Crane. Often in small flocks; flies in V-formation. The loud, distinctive call is often heard before the bird is seen.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 100–120 cm tall; wingspan 180–220 cm. Weight 3.6–6.1 kg. One of the largest flying birds.

Plumage: Upperparts ash-grey. Lower back and rump slightly darker grey. Flight feathers (primaries and secondaries) black, forming a striking black patch when wings are folded. Tail dark.

Head and neck: Crown of adult black (feathered, unlike Sarus Crane). Below the crown, from eye around to nape, is a distinctive white or pale buff patch (lores, cheeks, and nape). Main neck grey, lower neck and upper breast can show black feathering in some individuals.

Bare skin: A red patch on the crown (the "callosities"); not always easily visible in the field.

Legs and feet: Very long, dark grey-black; not suited for perching.

Bill: Long, straight, pale greenish-grey; pointed.

Eyes: Dark brown; small, alert.

Sexual dimorphism: Minimal; females slightly smaller.

Juveniles: Brown-grey overall; lacking distinctive white nape patch; crown feathered (no red).

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact and duetting call: Loud, resonant kroo-oo or gru-gru, given in flight and at rest; frequency around, very penetrating.
  2. Alarm call: Harsh, loud calls when disturbed.
  3. Display call: Unison calling during courtship display; males and females call together in synchronized duet.
हिंदी: तूही आवाज़, "क्रू-ऊ" या "ग्रू-ग्रू"। झुंड में एक साथ बोलता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Omnivorous wetland forager: Common Cranes feed on diverse plant and animal matter:

  • Plant material: seeds, grain (from stubble fields), roots, shoots of marsh plants
  • Aquatic invertebrates: aquatic insect larvae, crustaceans
  • Small vertebrates: frogs, small fish, small reptiles
  • Earthworms and mollusks

Feeding behaviour: Wader-style; walk slowly through wetlands probing and pecking. Also glean on cultivated fields, particularly after harvest. Feed both day and night in response to disturbance and feeding opportunities.

Social behaviour: Highly gregarious; winter visitors form flocks. Often associate with Glossy Ibis and other waders.

Flight: Strong, steady flight with slow wingbeats. Flocks fly in V-formation at high altitudes during migration.

Roosts: Nocturnal roosts on open water in large wetlands; day roosts in shallow water and grassland.

Predators: Generally too large for most predators as adults; chicks vulnerable to jackals, crows.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहता है। पानी में वेडिंग करता है, खेतों में भी जाता है। बहुत सावधान, आवाज़ पर उड़ जाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Breeds in Russia and eastern Europe (April–August); arrives in India September–October and departs by April.

Arrival and departure: First flocks arrive in mid-September; numbers peak in December–February; departure begins in March, with most birds gone by April.

Breeding: Does not breed in India. Breeding grounds are in extensive wetlands of Russia and eastern Europe.

Moult: Post-breeding moult occurs on breeding grounds before migration to India.

Roosts: Nocturnal roosts on large wetland areas (lakes, reservoirs, floodplain water); day roosts in shallow water and grassland areas.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। अपने देश में ही अंडे देता है। बड़े तालाबों पर रात बिताता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Large raptors of the plain
  • Mammalian predators: Jackals (occasional), wild dogs
  • At nest: Crows, ravens, jackals
  • Parasites: Avian helminths, ectoparasites
हिंदी: बड़ी शिकारी चिड़ियाँ और सियार इसका शिकार करते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Crop damage: May occasionally feed in rice stubble fields and grain storage areas; generally not considered a major pest species.

Protected status: Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting and trade are prohibited.

Ecological importance: Wetland flagship species; presence indicates maintenance of large wetland complexes.

Cultural significance: Named "kunj" in Hindi; a traditional symbol of winter migration. Mentioned in classical Sanskrit literature.

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Common Cranes should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from September to April. Large wetlands, particularly the floodplain lakes of the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems, are preferred habitats.

Habitat preference: Open wetlands with exposed water and mud; floodplain marshes; large lakes.

Peak occurrence: December–February, when visiting populations are highest.

GBIF records: GBIF holds published records from these districts in the core region.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date) submitted through the About page of this atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds across northern Eurasia from Scandinavia to northeastern Siberia; winters in Africa, India, and Middle East.

In India: Distributed across northern India as a winter visitor (September–April), with highest densities in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread winter visitor in large wetlands throughout the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor in large floodplain wetlands along the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems; present September to April with peak numbers December–February.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in the Systema Naturae (10th edition) as Ardea grus. Type locality is northern Europe (Scandinavia).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised. The single population migrates across Eurasia.

Family and order placement. Order Gruiformes; family Gruidae (cranes). The genus Grus contains true cranes of the northern hemisphere.

Phylogenetic notes. Common Crane is closely related to Whooper Crane (Grus leucogeranus) of northern Asia and to the North American Sandhill Crane (Antigone canadensis).

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Least Concern (LC). Populations are stable and widely distributed. The species is adaptable to human-modified wetlands and is not threatened by hunting in most of its range.
