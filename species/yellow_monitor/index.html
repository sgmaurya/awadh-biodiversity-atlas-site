सुनहरी गोह / Yellow Monitor

Varanus flavescens (Hardwicke & Gray, 1827) — Varanidae

सुनहरी गोह — भारत की सबसे छोटी गोह (monitor lizard), जिसका शरीर सुनहरे या पीले-भूरे रंग का होता है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के सूखे इलाकों, चट्टानी पहाड़ियों और नहर के किनारों में यह सरीसृप पाया जाता है। यह तेज़, चलंत शिकारी है जो छोटी गिरगिटों, छोटे सांपों, कीड़ों और छोटे चूहों का शिकार करता है। दिन में सक्रिय रहता है और बहुत तेज़ दौड़ता है। अपने नुकीले दाँत और पंजों से खतरनाक लग सकता है, पर लोग आमतौर पर इसे 'सांप खाने वाली' गोह के नाम से भी जानते हैं, क्योंकि यह सांपों का शिकार करता है।

Field tip: A small, slender, golden-brown lizard with sharp claws and a long tail. Extremely fast and alert. Unlike snakes, it has four legs. Found in dry areas, rocky hills, and river banks. Completely harmless despite looking fierce; it flees rather than fights.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for specimens from eastern UP):

MeasurementRange
Snout-vent length (SVL)15–25 cm
Tail length15–30 cm
Total length30–50 cm
Weight150–400 g

Head: Small, pointed snout with a median rostral scale. Eyes are large and protuberant with good visual acuity. Nostrils are round and located anteriorly on the snout. The jaw is lined with sharp, pointed teeth adapted for gripping prey.

Body: Slender compared to other monitor species. The dorsal surface is uniform golden-yellow to olive-brown. The ventral surface is lighter, often cream or pale yellow. Some individuals show faint longitudinal stripes or subtle banding on the body. The scales are uniform and keeled, giving a rough texture.

Limbs: Muscular and well-developed with four functional toes on each foot. Claws are sharp and pointed, adapted for climbing and gripping prey.

Tail: Long and muscular, nearly equal to or longer than the snout-vent length. The tail is prehensile to some degree and used for balance, swimming, and defense.

Sexual dimorphism: Males may be slightly larger and more robust than females; males possess larger preanal pores.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Diurnal generalist predator. The Yellow Monitor is highly active during daylight hours and is a swift and effective hunter.

  • Habitat use: Occupies dry, open areas with scattered rocks, river banks, ravines, and grasslands. Uses burrows, rock crevices, and dense vegetation as shelter. Can climb shrubs and low trees.
  • Foraging style: Active forager, moving rapidly across the ground and through vegetation searching for prey. Uses vision to locate prey; rapid strike and consumption follow.
  • Speed and agility: One of the fastest-moving lizards in eastern UP; can accelerate rapidly to escape predators or capture prey.
  • Social behaviour: Primarily solitary; males are territorial and may engage in combat with rivals during breeding season.
  • Defensive behaviour: Extremely shy; when approached, flees rapidly. Bites only if cornered or handled carelessly. May flatten body and hiss as a threat display.
  • Aquatic ability: Good swimmer; frequents water edges and can remain submerged for extended periods.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Carnivorous diet:

  • Primary prey: Small lizards (skinks, agamids), snakes (young keelbacks, other colubrids), eggs of ground-nesting birds
  • Secondary prey: Large insects (grasshoppers, beetles), small rodents, frogs
  • Hunting method: Visual stalking followed by rapid strike; swallows prey whole; solves eggs by manipulation and breaking

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Predatory birds: Martial Eagle, Bengal Eagle-Owl, and other raptors hunt adults and juveniles
  • Snakes: Indian Cobra and Common Krait may prey on smaller individuals or eggs
  • Larger reptiles: Bengal Monitor may prey on juveniles
  • Mammals: Jackals, hyenas, and wild boar may consume eggs and young monitors
  • Humans: Previously hunted; now protected

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest controller. The Yellow Monitor consumes significant numbers of agricultural pest species, including grasshoppers, locusts, and potentially crop-damaging rodents. Its presence in agricultural areas is generally beneficial. It is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 and is harmless to humans. The sharp teeth and aggressive appearance sometimes cause fear, but bites are rare and occur only if the animal is cornered or roughly handled. Local traditions sometimes view it positively as a "snake-eater" and pest controller.

Expected in dry, rocky areas and along riverbanks with suitable habitat in eastern Uttar Pradesh. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet confirmed from Basti–Ayodhya, though the species should occur in appropriate habitats in the region. Most active during the dry season (March–October). Search in rocky hills, dry grasslands, river ravines, and margins of agricultural areas during warm daylight hours. The first local record would be a clear photograph showing the distinctive golden coloration, small size, and four legs with place, GPS coordinates, and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Endemic to the Indian subcontinent; found across peninsular India and the Indo-Gangetic plain.

In India: Widespread in dry regions of peninsular India and the plains; absent from high mountains and dense forests.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident in dry and semi-arid regions; occurs throughout the plains, particularly in areas with rocky habitat.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Status uncertain; may be present in dry grasslands and rocky areas of the region; likely restricted to suitable drier habitats with rocky terrain.

Field rule: A small, slender, golden-brown lizard with sharp claws and four legs that runs very fast is a Yellow Monitor. It is India's smallest monitor and is found in dry areas and on riverbanks.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Thomas Hardwicke and John Edward Gray in 1827 as Varanus flavescens based on Indian specimens. Type locality is northern India. It remains in the same genus with no subsequent nomenclatural changes.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Squamata, suborder Lacertilia, family Varanidae (monitor lizards). The genus Varanus contains about 80 species of large predatory lizards distributed across Africa, Asia, and Australia.

Phylogenetic notes. Varanus flavescens is the smallest member of the genus Varanus. Molecular studies place it within the Asian clade of monitor lizards, distinct from African and Australian lineages.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) globally because of its wide distribution in suitable habitats across India and stable populations. It is not threatened by direct hunting, though habitat modification is a concern in some regions. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.
