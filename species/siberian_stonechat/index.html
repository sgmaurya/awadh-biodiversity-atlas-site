खर पिद्दा / Siberian Stonechat

Saxicola maurus (Pallas, 1773) — Muscicapidae

खर पिद्दा — यह एक सुंदर चिड़िया है जो साइबेरिया और मध्य एशिया से सर्दियों में भारत आती है। नर काली होती है, केवल कंधों और कूल्हों पर सफेद धब्बे होते हैं — जिससे यह "खर" (खुरदरी, ऊबड़-खाबड़) लगती है। मादा भूरी होती है। ये घास के मैदानों, खेतों के किनारों पर, और कम झाड़ियों पर रहती है। यह Pied Bushchat से अलग है।

Field tip: A small dark (male) or brown (female) bird with a striking white neck-patch and white rump, on grasslands or low perches — is a Siberian Stonechat. The white neck-patch (in males) and white rump are diagnostic. Often sits with upright posture on small shrubs or grass stems.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 12–13 cm long; wingspan 18–24 cm. Weight 11–16 g. A small songbird.

Plumage (male): Striking and diagnostic. Head black or charcoal-black. Throat white or off-white, forming a prominent white neck-patch (diagnostic). Shoulders and upper back white or cream (visible when perched). Lower back and wings black. Rump and uppertail coverts white (visible in flight). Underparts buff or orange-buff.

Plumage (female): Much duller. Head and body rufous-brown or khaki-brown. Throat paler. Neck-patch present but indistinct (brown on brown). Shoulder patch whitish but less contrasted. Rump still white (diagnostic). Underparts buff.

Legs and feet: Thin, dark.

Bill: Thin, pointed, black.

Eyes: Dark iris; alert.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: Scratchy warbling song.
  2. Call: Sharp tack or tick-tick, resembling two stones being tapped together.
  3. Alarm: Loud, harsh chur-ring call when threatened.
हिंदी: "टिक-टिक" की तेज़ आवाज़। दो पत्थरों के टकराने जैसी आवाज़।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Flycatcher and gleaner: Siberian Stonechats are primarily flycatchers, sallying from a perch to catch insects in mid-air. Also glean insects from ground and vegetation.

Feeding: Entirely insectivorous:

  • Flying insects caught mid-air (flycatcher style)
  • Ground insects gleaned from grassland and low vegetation
  • Occasionally berries in late winter

Feeding behaviour: Active hunter. Use "sally-and-return" technique from an exposed perch on a grass stem or low shrub. Spend much time scanning for prey from elevated perches.

Habitat preference: Prefer open grasslands and low scrubland. Avoid dense vegetation and forest. Often on sparsely-vegetated or "harsh" (khaar) habitats, hence the Hindi name.

Territorial: Establish individual wintering territories on grasslands. Males sing from elevated perches, especially at dawn and dusk.

Alert behaviour: Active and obvious; give sharp calls frequently. Bold for their size.

Flight: Direct, bouncing flight. White rump patch is distinctive in flight.

हिंदी: घास के पत्तों पर बैठते हैं। उड़कर कीड़े पकड़ते हैं। सफेद पीठ दिखाई देती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP from September, with main arrival October. Departs February–April, with most birds gone by April.

Breeding range: Central Asia and southern Siberia, from Kazakhstan to Siberia. Breeds April–August in grasslands and scrubland.

Migration: Long-distance migrant. Diurnal migrant.

Wintering: Solitary on individual grasslands and open areas. Return to same wintering territories year after year.

Moult: Complete moult occurs before arrival; breeding plumage acquired on wintering grounds.

Timing in UP: Early arrival September (vanguard); main arrival October; peak December–February; departure February–April.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आना शुरू करते हैं, अप्रैल तक चले जाते हैं। घास के मैदानों पर रहते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Listed under Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Ecological value: Important insectivore in grasslands; helps control flying insects and pests.

Wetland health indicator: Species present on grasslands and open areas near wetlands indicates good habitat diversity.

Cultural significance: Known locally as "khar pidda" (harsh stonechat). Name reflects its preference for harsh, open habitats.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Siberian Stonechat is expected to winter in the eastern UP region based on its known range and wintering distribution in northern India. The species breeds in Central Asia and southern Siberia (Kazakhstan, Mongolia, and adjacent areas) and winters from the Middle East through Central Asia to northern India. However, it is not recorded by the three published surveys (Mishra et al. 2019, Yashmita-Ulman & Singh 2022, 2025) from Bakhira, Parvati Arga, or Ayodhya district. The species prefers open grasslands and low scrubland rather than wetlands, so it may occur in suitable habitats (open fields, grasslands, scrubby areas) in eastern UP rather than on wetlands. Peak occurrence should be October–March if present. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Central Asia and southern Siberia; winters from Middle East through Central Asia to northern India.

In India: Rare and irregular winter visitor, primarily in northern and central regions. Scattered records throughout northern plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Rare winter visitor, primarily to northern and central districts. Most records are from open grasslands and scrubland areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as rare or irregular winter visitor to open grasslands and scrubland. No records from the three local surveys. September–April if present.

Field rule: A small bird with a white neck-patch (in males) and white rump patch, on grasslands during winter — is a Siberian Stonechat. The white neck-patch (especially diagnostic in males) and white rump are key field marks. Females duller but still show white rump.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Pallas in 1773 in Reise durch verschiedene Provinzen des Russischen Reichs (volume 2) as Motacilla maurus. Type locality: Russia.

Subspecies. Two subspecies are recognised:

  • S. m. maurus — Central Asia and Siberia (occurs in India)
  • S. m. neglectus — North Africa

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae (thrushes and chats). Genus Saxicola contains the stonechats (13 species).

Phylogenetic notes. Saxicola maurus is closely related to S. torquata (Common Stonechat), with which it shares many morphological traits.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. The global population is large and stable. The species is adaptable to various grassland types and is not globally threatened.
