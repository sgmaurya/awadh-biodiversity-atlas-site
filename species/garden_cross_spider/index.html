गार्डन क्रॉस मकड़ी / Garden Cross Spider

Argiope pulchella Thorell, 1881 — Araneidae

गार्डन क्रॉस मकड़ी — मानसून के बाद गाँव के बागों में एक छोटी, धारीदार मकड़ी जो अपना ऊर्ध्वाधर जाला बुनती है। शरीर काला-सफ़ेद और लाल धारियों से चिन्हित, पैर X की तरह सजे होते हैं। बागों में सबसे आम वेब-बनाने वाली मकड़ियों में से एक, फिर भी कम ध्यान देने वाली क्योंकि यह जोग मकड़ी से छोटी है। एक दिन में दर्जनों कीटों को पकड़ती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

Male size4–5 mm; brownish

Field tip: Smaller than Signature Spider; white markings more prominent; red/orange spots on abdomen distinguish it from other Argiope. Webs built lower to ground than Argiope anasuja.

Morphology आकारिकी

FeatureMeasurement
Female body length8–12 mm
Male body length4–5 mm
Female legspan20–28 mm
Web diameter25–35 cm

Female: Cephalothorax dark brown to black, covered in fine hairs. Abdomen broadly oval with bold transverse banding in black, white, and silvery; distinctive red or orange lateral spots. Legs banded in black and white, held in opposite pairs. Eyes typical of Araneidae.

Male: Much smaller, brownish, less boldly marked. Found at web periphery during mating season.

Web structure: Vertical orb with anchor lines to surrounding vegetation. Spiral threads sticky; radial threads non-sticky. Stabilimentum zigzag pattern, thinner and more delicate than in A. anasuja.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Aerial insectivore: Web captures flying insects including small flies, midges, small grasshoppers, small moths, tiny wasps, and parasitoid flies. More selective for smaller prey than larger Argiope species due to smaller web and capture threads.

Web ecology: Density in suitable habitat (hedgerows, field margins) can reach 3–8 webs per square metre during peak season, creating significant cumulative insect predation.

Predation: Hunted by pompilid wasps and small birds (Ashy Prinia).

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

MonthEvent
May–JunSpiderlings emerge, balloon, settle in low vegetation
Jul–AugRapid growth; adult size reached; webs establish
Aug–OctPeak abundance; maximum egg production
Oct–NovMating; 2–3 egg sacs laid (brown, 8–12 mm, 100–300 eggs)
Nov–AprOverwintering as eggs in sacs; adults die with cold

Single generation per year.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest control: Natural controller of small flying insects in gardens and agricultural margins. Particularly valuable in vegetable gardens where small pest flies are problematic.

Human safety: Venom harmless to humans; bites extremely rare and mild. Does not bite unless roughly handled.

Garden value: Easily overlooked compared to larger Argiope, but equally beneficial. Gardens with diverse web-building spiders have better insect regulation.

Where conditions are suitable, Argiope pulchella is expected in village gardens, hedgerows, and cultivated land margins June through November. Webs constructed in low shrubs, garden plants, and tall grass at heights 30 cm–1.5 m. Less conspicuous than Signature Spider but locally abundant in suitable microhabitats. Egg sacs overwinter on dried stems and can be found February onwards.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South and Southeast Asia — India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia.

In India: Distributed across the subcontinent in suitable warm-season habitat.

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded across the state wherever low vegetation provides web attachment.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in gardens and field margins during monsoon and post-monsoon; local abundance data needed.

Field rule: Small black-and-white striped orb-weaver with distinctive red or orange spots on abdomen, in low vegetation, web 25–35 cm, = Argiope pulchella.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Argiope pulchella was described by Thorell in 1881 in Studi sui Ragni Malesi e Papuani (p. 43). Type locality: Malaysia.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Araneae, family Araneidae (orb-weaving spiders). The genus Argiope Savigny, 1822 contains about 80 species globally, known for bold banded patterning and distinctive stabilimenta.

Phylogenetic notes. Argiope pulchella is closely related to A. anasuja and A. aemula — the three most abundant Argiope species in India. Phylogenetic resolution within Argiope remains incomplete pending molecular studies.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed. As a common, synanthropic species benefiting from human gardens, population status is stable.
