सूरन / Elephant Foot Yam

Amorphophallus paeoniifolius (Dennst.) Nicolson — Araceae

सूरन (जिमीकंद, जिमु) — बसंत से सर्दी तक उगने वाली एक बड़े पत्तों वाली बूटी, जिसकी जड़ों में भूमिगत कंद (सूरन) लगते हैं। पत्तियाँ बहुत बड़ी, छतरी जैसी, हरे-भरे। फूल गंध वाला, पीले-बैंगनी रंग का। कंद एक-डेढ़ किलो तक का होता है, सफ़ेद गूदा वाला। गर्मी-बरसात में भाजी के रूप में खाया जाता है — बहुत पौष्टिक और पचने में आसान। पूरे पूर्वी यूपी में घरों के बगीचों में लगाया जाता है। कभी-कभी पूरी तरह सूखे कंद को पिस कर आटा भी बनाया जाता है।

Field tip: An unmistakable aroid with a single enormous, deeply lobed, umbrella-shaped leaf on a large speckled petiole. The leaf expands to 1–2 meters tall. The foul-smelling spathe (flower wrap) appears before or with the leaf. The underground tuber is harvested for food.

Habit: Herbaceous, monocarpic (flowering after one season of growth), with a single large leaf.

Corm/Tuber: Underground, globose or flattened-globose, 12–25 cm diameter, weighing 0.5–1.5 kg, brown skin, white or pale yellow flesh; surrounded by aerial cormels (small tubers).

Petiole: Robust, 0.5–1.5 m tall, glabrous, mottled with brown, green, and white patches; cross-section nearly circular.

Leaf blade: Single, tripinnate, divided into numerous leaflets, leaflets 5–15 cm long, lanceolate-oblong, margin serrate-dentate, apex acuminate.

Inflorescence: A spadix (spike of tiny flowers) enclosed in a spathe (modified leaf). Spathe 10–15 cm, initially closed, opening at anthesis; color yellow-green, cream, or purple, often mottled.

Flowers: Unisexual, very small; male flowers in the upper portion of the spadix; female flowers below.

Fruits: Red berries, 0.8–1.2 cm, each containing 1–2 seeds.

हिंदी: एक ही बहुत बड़ी पत्ती, छतरी जैसी। डंठल बड़ा, धब्बेदार। जड़ में कंद, सफेद गूदा। फूल गंध वाला, अजीब।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Amorphophallus paeoniifolius is a tropical herbaceous perennial (usually grown as an annual crop) native to Southeast Asia and southern India. The plant thrives in warm, moist conditions with partial shade. The single large leaf photosynthesizes intensively during the growing season, accumulating carbohydrates that are stored in the underground tuber.

The plant is shade-tolerant and can be intercropped with or grown beneath larger trees. The foul odour of the spathe (resembling carrion) attracts carrion beetles and flies for pollination, though self-pollination also occurs. Seed production is variable; many cultivated populations are nearly sterile and rely on corm propagation.

The plant has a distinct dormancy period during the cool dry season (November to March), during which the leaf dies back and the tuber enters a resting state. This dormancy is shorter at lower elevations.

हिंदी: गर्मी-बरसात की फसल। छाया सहन कर सकता है। फूल की गंध अजीब (सड़े मांस जैसी)। सर्दी में सोया रहता है।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Intercropping: Often grown with Groundnut or other pulse crops to maximize land use
  • Shade provider: Can shade companion crops; requires partial shade in hot regions
  • Garden companions: Grown in kitchen gardens with herbs and other vegetables
  • No pest associations: Few documented pest or disease relationships in local conditions

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating caterpillars: Occasionally damage the leaf but rarely cause significant yield loss
  • Tuber rots: Waterlogging can cause tuber rot, especially in clay soils; ensure good drainage
  • Corm fly: In some regions, larvae can damage corms in storage
  • Generally hardy: Compared to other tuber crops, suran is relatively pest-resistant

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Food use: The underground tubers are cooked as a vegetable, either boiled and fried, or added to curries. The tuber is light, easily digestible, and suitable for children and elderly persons. Nutritional analysis shows moderate protein and carbohydrate content. The tubers can also be dried, sliced into chips, or powdered into flour for long-term storage.

Nutritional value: Fresh tubers are mostly water and starch, with a little protein and a range of minerals.

Medicinal use: In Ayurvedic medicine, the tuber is considered cooling (shita) and is used in traditional preparations for digestive complaints. Modern phytochemical studies are limited.

Cultivation advantages: The crop requires minimal external inputs beyond land, water, and corm planting stock. Tubers can be stored for extended periods by keeping them in cool, dry, well-ventilated conditions. The crop is well-suited to marginal lands and can be intercropped successfully.

Landrace diversity: Many landrace varieties are maintained by farmers in eastern UP, distinguished chiefly by tuber size, flesh color, and dormancy length.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Indo-Himalayan region and Southeast Asia; cultivated throughout tropical Asia.

In India: Widely cultivated in home gardens throughout the Indo-Gangetic plain and southern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in all vegetable-growing regions, particularly common in eastern UP.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common as a cultivated crop in homestead gardens throughout the district.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Originally described as Arum paeoniifolium Dennst. (1818), later transferred to Amorphophallus by John Nicolson. The genus name Amorphophallus is derived from Greek amorphos (shapeless) and phallos (penis), referring to the variable spadix structure. The specific epithet paeoniifolius means "with peony-like leaves," referring to the deeply lobed leaf.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Araceae (Arum family), order Arales. The family includes other cultivated aroids such as taro and elephant ears.

Phylogenetic notes. Amorphophallus paeoniifolius is closely related to A. paeonifolius var. atroviridis and other Asian Amorphophallus species.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as LC (Least Concern) due to wide cultivation and stable populations.
