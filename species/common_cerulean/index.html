कॉमन सेरुलियन तितली / Common Cerulean

Jamides celeno (Cramer, 1775) — Lycaenidae

कॉमन सेरुलियन तितली — एक छोटी नीली तितली जो बस्ती के बागों, खेतों और घास के मैदानों में पाई जाती है। इसके पंख नीले रंग के होते हैं और पीछे की तरफ़ सफ़ेद और भूरे धारियों के साथ होते हैं। यह तितली साल भर रहती है, विशेषकर बरसात में बहुत आम होती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall butterfly, wingspan 18-22 mm
Male colourBright cerulean or sky-blue upperside; iridescent sheen
Female colourPale blue or whitish-blue, often duller than males
UndersidePale with distinctive dark spots and line patterns
Hindwing undersideCharacteristic orange or red spot at angle; dark markings
FlightFluttering, stays close to vegetation

Field tip: A small blue butterfly, noticeably brighter and more cerulean-coloured than other local blue butterflies. Males are more brightly coloured than females. Look in gardens, grasslands, and near flowering plants.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: नीली तितली, नीचे सफ़ेद और भूरी धारियाँ। पूँछ के पास नारंगी धब्बा।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Wingspan18-22 mm
Forewing length9-11 mm
Body length7-9 mm

Wings:

  • Upperside (male): Bright cerulean or sky-blue, often with a silvery sheen; margins sometimes with dark borders.
  • Upperside (female): Paler blue or whitish-blue, less vibrant than male.
  • Underside: Pale grey or whitish with dark spots and line patterns; characteristic orange or red spot on hindwing angle; post-discal spots visible.

Body: Small, dark; antennae clubbed, pale-banded.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are brighter cerulean-blue; females are paler, making them visually distinct.

Tail-like extension: Small tail-like projection present on hindwing in some individuals.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stage / MonthEvent
January–DecemberYear-round breeding; multiple overlapping generations
EggLaid on or near host plants; incubation 4-6 days
LarvaGreen or brown caterpillar; feeds on leaves and buds; 2-3 weeks
PupaSmall, formed on or near host plant; 5-10 days
Adult2-4 weeks lifespan; most active during monsoon
हिंदी: साल भर पाई जाती है। बहु-पीढ़ीय।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval host plants:

  • Various herbaceous plants
  • Garden plants
  • Wild flowering plants
  • Legumes and other legume-family plants

Adult food sources:

  • Nectar from flowers
  • Pollen
  • Damp spots on ground

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Small birds: Flycatchers, warblers, and other insectivores
  • Spiders: Web-building and jumping spiders
  • Parasitoid wasps: Attack caterpillars and pupae

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minor agricultural role. The caterpillars feed on various herbaceous plants but cause negligible damage. Not considered an agricultural pest.

Garden indicator. Common in gardens and cultivated areas, indicating the presence of diverse flowering plants and a healthy garden ecosystem.

Today. Abundant in villages and gardens throughout the region where flowering plants and suitable habitat are present, particularly during monsoon and post-monsoon.

हिंदी: बागों में आम, नुकसान नहीं।

To be expected year-round in gardens, cultivated areas, grasslands, and field margins throughout the region, with peak abundance during the monsoon. Look in gardens, parks, and open areas with flowering plants. A photograph with date and location would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across tropical and subtropical Asia, from India and Sri Lanka through Southeast Asia to Japan and Australia.

In India: Distributed throughout Peninsular India, North East India, and other regions; common in most states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Found in gardens and cultivated areas throughout the state; common in villages and agricultural regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in gardens, grasslands, and field margins year-round, particularly abundant during monsoon.

Field rule: A small blue butterfly with males bright cerulean-blue and females paler blue, distinguished by the orange or red spot at the angle of the hindwing underside, is the Common Cerulean.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Cramer in 1775 in Uitlandsche Kapellen. Type locality: India.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognized across Asia. The subspecies found in India is Jamides celeno celeno (Cramer, 1775).

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Lycaenidae, subfamily Polyommatinae (blues). The genus Jamides includes several blue species.

Phylogenetic notes. Jamides species are highly successful in disturbed and cultivated habitats, showing adaptive radiation in tropical Asia.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed. Abundant and widespread; no conservation concerns.
