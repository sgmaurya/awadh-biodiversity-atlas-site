काली छाती बया / Black-breasted Weaver

Ploceus benghalensis (Linnaeus, 1758) — Ploceidae

काली छाती बया — पीले और काले रंग की चिड़िया जो पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के तालाबों और खेतों में झुंड में रहती है। नर पक्षी लटकती हुई जटिल घोंसलें बनाता है जो बहुत सुंदर होती हैं। सर्दियों में नर का रंग गहरा हो जाता है। साल भर यहाँ रहती है।

Field tip: A stocky, golden-yellow weaver with a black face and breast (in breeding males). Non-breeding birds are duller and more streaked. Always found in flocks around reed beds, wetlands, and cultivated areas. The presence of hanging woven nests is a sure indicator.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange

Size: 15–16 cm long; wingspan 25–28 cm.

Breeding male plumage: Head and nape yellow with black mask extending from lores to eye and across the throat. Breast black. Rest of underparts yellow. Back olive-yellow with darker streaking. Wings darker with yellow edges. Tail yellowish.

Non-breeding male plumage: Much duller; head yellowish with less distinct mask. Breast dusky-yellow rather than black. Back heavily streaked. Overall greenish-yellow.

Female plumage: Greenish-yellow overall. Head duller than breeding male. No black mask. Underparts yellowish with dusky streaking on breast and flanks. Back olive with darker streaking.

Bill: Pointed, robust, conical; specialized for weaving and eating seeds.

Legs and feet: Relatively long; adapted for climbing among reeds.

Juveniles: Similar to females; duller and more heavily streaked.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact calls: Various chirping and chattering notes; chit-chit-chit.
  2. Alarm call: Sharp scolding notes.
  3. Song: A rambling, twittering song given in breeding season. Frequency.
हिंदी: विविध "चीट" और चहचहाती आवाज़। अलर्म कॉल तीक्ष्ण। प्रजनन के समय गीत सुनने में आता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Highly social: Black-breasted Weaver is a highly gregarious colonial species. It feeds on:

  • Seeds of grasses and reeds
  • Small insects and caterpillars
  • Small beetles and bugs
  • Occasionally nectar and fruits

Foraging: Gleans seeds from grass heads and reeds. Also picks insects from vegetation. Sometimes forages on ground.

Social behaviour: Strongly gregarious; nests in colonies of up to 100+ individuals in suitable habitats. Males and females of non-breeding flocks roost communally.

Roosting: Communal roosts in reed beds and tall grass, often with other weaver species.

Predators: Snake eagles, kestrels, occasionally snakes. Chicks vulnerable to snakes and crows in nests.

Water dependency: Closely associated with wetlands; rarely found far from water.

हिंदी: बहुत झुंड में रहने वाली चिड़िया। पानी के पास रहना पसंद करता है। बीज खाता है। झुंड में सोता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

MonthEvent

November–DecemberNon-breeding plumage; flocking

Breeding: Resident breeder in eastern UP. Builds elaborate suspended nests woven from reed leaves and grass. Nests in colonies in reed beds and tall grass near water. Clutch typically 2–4 eggs. May have 2–3 broods per season in suitable conditions.

Moult: Two moults per year — pre-breeding (acquiring breeding plumage) and post-breeding (losing breeding plumage).

Site fidelity: Strong; returns to same breeding and roosting sites in successive years.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Snake Eagle, Common Kestrel, occasionally shrikes and crows
  • Reptile predators: Snakes, occasionally monitor lizards
  • Mammalian predators: Rarely; mongooses and jackals (chicks)
  • Nest predators: Snakes, crows
हिंदी: बड़ी शिकारी चिड़ियाँ और सांप इसका शिकार करते हैं। झुंड में होने से कुछ सुरक्षा मिलती है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: The Black-breasted Weaver is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is prohibited in India.

Agricultural benefit: Feeds on weed seeds and insect pests, particularly during breeding season when insect consumption increases.

Pest control: Consumes caterpillars and other insects, contributing to natural pest management.

Indicator species: Presence indicates healthy wetland and grassland ecosystems with good vegetation and insect populations.

Cultural significance: Known to villagers as "baya" and traditionally appreciated for the intricacy of its woven nests.

Conflict: Large flocks may occasionally feed in ripening rice fields but damage is generally minor.

हिंदी: यह विधि अधिनियम 1972 के तहत संरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.

Year-round range: Expected throughout eastern UP in reed beds and wetland areas. GBIF holds published records from these districts. Particularly suitable habitats include the Ghaghra and Rapti river reed beds, tank margins, and associated wetlands.

Habitat preference: Reed beds, tall grass, and reed-dominated wetland margins with nearby water.

Peak season: Year-round; more obvious during breeding season (May–July) when males show breeding plumage and build nests.

Detection: Easy to detect in suitable habitat; listen for colony calls or look for characteristic hanging nests. Early morning and evening activity periods are best.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the hanging nests or clear observation of the bird in eastern UP submitted through the About page, with location and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent and South-East Asia. Resident and partially migratory populations.

In India: Distributed throughout India in suitable wetland and grassland habitats.

In Uttar Pradesh: Throughout the state in suitable reed-bed and wetland habitats; particularly common in eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular resident and breeder in reed-dominated wetland areas along river systems.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Linnaeus in 1758. The type locality is Bengal, India.

Subspecies. Two subspecies recognized: P. b. benghalensis (eastern and central India) and P. b. westermanni (Nepal and Assam).

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Ploceidae (weaver birds). The genus Ploceus contains numerous weaver species across Africa and Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Related to other Ploceus species, particularly the Baya Weaver. Part of a clade of weavers with complex nesting behaviour.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widespread. The species is adaptable to various wetland habitats. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972.
