वर्डिटर फ़्लाईकैचर / Verditer Flycatcher

Eumyias thalassinus (Swainson, 1838) — Muscicapidae

वर्डिटर फ़्लाईकैचर — यह छोटी, नीली और सफेद चिड़िया सितंबर से अप्रैल तक पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में हिमालय से आती है। नर का नीला रंग बहुत चमकीला और सुंदर होता है। यह पेड़ों पर बैठकर हवा में कीड़े पकड़ता है। इसकी तीव्र सीटी और तेज़ गतिविधि इसे पहचानने में आसान बनाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall flycatcher; 15–16 cm long
Male plumageBrilliant blue-green above; white belly; blue-green ear-coverts
Female plumageMore dull blue-grey above; whitish below
BehaviourHunts insects from perches; sallying flycatcher style; restless
HabitatOpen woodlands, gardens; often at flowering trees

Field tip: A small, colourful flycatcher. Males are bright blue-green, distinctive. Females are duller but still show blue tones. Hunts insects by sallying from exposed perches. Restless and active; characteristic whistled calls.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length150–160 mm
Wing (flattened chord)88–95 mm
Tail55–65 mm
Bill11–13 mm
Tarsus18–20 mm

Size: 15–16 cm long; weighs 12–16 g. Small, compact flycatcher.

Plumage (male): Brilliant blue-green above; sides of face and ear-coverts blue-green. Back darker blue-green. Tail blue-green. Underparts largely white; flanks and undertail coverts blue-green. Wing edges and coverts blue.

Plumage (female): Duller overall. Blue-grey above; less brilliant. White below; flanks grey-blue. Duller than males but still showing blue tones.

Bill: Small, black, pointed; flattened laterally for catching insects.

Eyes: Dark; large; alert.

Legs and feet: Black; short and strong for perching.

Sexes: Males are distinctly more brilliant and saturated blue than females.

Juveniles: Resemble females but duller with greenish tones.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A series of loud, clear whistles; often a rapid "whit-whee-whee" or similar.
  2. Contact calls: Soft notes and chips.
  3. Alarm: Sharp, scolding notes.
हिंदी: तीव्र सीटें बजाता है। दिन भर सक्रिय रहता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Foraging: Hunts insects by sallying from a perch, catching them in flight. Also gleaning. Feeds on:

  • Small flying insects (flies, gnats, small moths)
  • Insects gleaned from foliage
  • Very occasionally insects from the ground

Social behaviour: Solitary or in loose associations. Territorial on wintering grounds but not strongly so.

Perching: Sits upright on exposed branches; frequently fans tail and droops wings.

Movement: Active and restless; frequent sallies; returns to same perch or moves between nearby branches.

Microhabitat: Prefers open areas with good visibility; frequently at flowering trees.

हिंदी: अकेले रहते हैं। हमेशा चलते-फिरते रहते हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: September–May in eastern UP. Arrives late August or September; departure begins in April, complete by May.

Peak presence: October–March, with January–February at peak numbers.

Breeding grounds: Himalayas at higher elevations (temperate and sub-temperate forests); not breeding in UP.

No breeding in region: Purely a winter visitor; all breeding outside UP.

Moult: Complete moult before arrival at wintering grounds (pre-basic moult).

Return migration: Reverse migration in April–May.

हिंदी: सितंबर-अक्टूबर में आता है। अप्रैल-मई में चला जाता है।

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Verditer Flycatcher should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Winter visitor: GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya proper. Expected as a winter visitor (September–May) in eastern UP in gardens, parks, and woodlands with good tree cover. Generally uncommon; often found in mixed-species flocks.

Where to look: Open woodlands with scattered trees; gardens and parks; cultivation margins with trees. Often at flowering trees during winter.

Peak season: October–March. Active in early morning.

Detection: Look for characteristic sallying behaviour. Restless and active; blue male is distinctive.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing blue plumage (especially male's brilliant blue), with location GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page of this atlas.

हिंदी: सर्दी में पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में आता है। पेड़ों पर दिखता है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in temperate Himalayas. Winters in the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia.

In India: Winter visitor throughout the subcontinent; more common in Himalayan foothills and northern plains.

In Uttar Pradesh: Uncommon winter visitor to northern and eastern regions.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Possible winter visitor in suitable habitats; records needed to confirm presence.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Swainson in 1838. Muscicapa thalassina Swainson, 1838. Type locality: Himalayas.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies, but populations are poorly differentiated. Indian wintering birds represent Himalayan breeders (E. t. thalassinus group).

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Muscicapidae. Genus Eumyias comprises blue flycatchers of tropical and subtropical Asia.

Phylogenetic notes. Eumyias is a well-defined Asian genus; relationships with other blue flycatchers being clarified through molecular work.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Population stable. Not threatened. Protected under Wildlife Protection Act 1972 in India.
