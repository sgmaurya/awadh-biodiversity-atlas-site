लकड़बग्घा / Striped Hyena

Hyaena hyaena (Linnaeus, 1758) — Hyaenidae

लकड़बग्घा — भारत का सबसे दुर्लभ बड़ा मांसाहारी, जो केवल नदी-घाटियों, रावाइनों और गन्ने की खेतों में पाया जाता है, और वह भी दुर्लभ है। बस्ती और अयोध्या जिलों में यह लगभग विलुप्त है। विशाल जबड़े, शक्तिशाली गर्दन, और धारीदार (स्ट्राइपेड) शरीर वाला एक हड्डी-चूसने वाला जानवर। रात को शिकार करता है, लेकिन ज़्यादातर मरे हुए जानवरों और कूड़े को खाता है। भारतीय वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम 1972 की अनुसूची III में सूचीबद्ध और सुरक्षित। घाघरा और राप्ती नदी प्रणालियों के रावाइनों में दुर्लभ अवशेष आबादी है।

Field tip: A heavily-built, powerful carnivore with pronounced vertical stripes, powerful jaws, and a short dark tail, seen only in ravines and thick vegetation at night. Extremely rare. The striped pattern and humped back distinguish it from any dog or jackal. Do not approach; it can be dangerous if cornered.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for Indian populations):

MeasurementRange
Head-body length90–120 cm
Tail length25–35 cm
Shoulder height60–90 cm
Weight35–60 kg

Build: Stocky and powerfully built, with the front legs slightly longer than the hind legs, creating a characteristic humped or sloped back. The hindquarters appear to slope downward.

Head: Massive and broad, with enormous jaw muscles visible as bulges on the sides of the head. The skull is heavily ossified and adapted for crushing bone.

Fur: Tawny-brown to yellowish-brown dorsally, with distinctive black or dark brown vertical stripes on the body and legs. The stripes are a key identification character.

Mane: A pronounced mane of longer hair runs along the neck and back, which can be erected as a threat display, making the animal appear larger.

Ears: Round and relatively small.

Tail: Short and bushy, dark-tipped.

Jaws: Exceptionally strong. The premolar teeth are adapted for bone-crushing (carnassial teeth). The bite force is the strongest of any terrestrial carnivore relative to body size.

Legs: Powerful and slightly bowed; the feet are adapted for sustained walking rather than running.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A primarily nocturnal, solitary scavenger, spending the day in caves, burrows, or dense scrub and emerging at dusk to forage. It walks long distances (up to 30 km per night) in search of carrion, using its powerful sense of smell.

Feeding: Opportunistic omnivore but primarily a bone-crusher and scavenger. It feeds on carcasses of large animals (killed by other predators or dead from disease), crushes bones to reach marrow, and feeds on small mammals, insects, and plant matter. A single hyena can consume up to 15 kg of food at one sitting.

Defecation: Fecal marking is prominent and used for territorial and sexual signaling. Feces are characteristically white due to the high bone-meal content.

Reproduction: Breeding is usually in winter; females give birth to 2–4 cubs in a burrow or cave.

Social structure: Largely solitary, though pairs may remain together temporarily. Not colonial, unlike the spotted hyena (Crocuta crocuta) of Africa.

Vocalizations: A variety of whoops, yips, and cackles, though less vocal than spotted hyenas. The characteristic whoop is a long-distance contact call.

Threats: Traditionally hunted for perceived danger to livestock and for body parts used in traditional medicine. Now protected, but persecution persists in some areas. Habitat loss and human expansion are primary threats.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Adults: No natural predators due to size and strength. Humans are the primary threat.

Cubs: Lions (historically, now absent), tigers (rare), leopards (rare); also snakes and large raptors.

Human persecution: Hunting and killing due to perceived threat to livestock and traditional beliefs; though now illegal: the species is listed in the schedules of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (Schedule III of the original Act; the schedules were reorganised by the 2022 amendment).

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Conservation status: Near Threatened. The global population is declining due to persecution and habitat loss. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (Schedule III of the original Act; the schedules were reorganised by the 2022 amendment), 1972.

Livestock predation: The hyena occasionally kills small livestock (goats, sheep) and poultry, though it is primarily a scavenger. Predation is much less common than livestock loss to other causes (disease, accident, theft).

Beneficial role: As a scavenger, the hyena plays an important ecosystem service by consuming carrion that might otherwise rot and spread disease. In areas where large predators are absent, the hyena is an important nutrient recycler.

Conflict history: Traditionally persecuted due to fear and perceived danger. Stories of hyena attacks on humans exist in folklore, though attacks are extremely rare. Historical use in traditional medicine (bones, teeth, organs) for unproven medicinal properties has driven hunting.

Cultural status: Viewed negatively in many communities, though the sacred status in some Hindu and Islamic traditions provides some protection.

Today: The species persists in very low numbers in the district, restricted to ravines and dense scrub where human disturbance is minimal. Population recovery depends on tolerance and protection.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Range-restricted and rare: The Striped Hyena is expected only in ravines, deep river valleys, and dense sugarcane tracts bordering the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers. It does not occur in open agricultural areas or near villages, except occasionally at night scavenging. The species is expected to be very rare in Basti and Ayodhya districts, with only occasional published reports from the Ghaghra–Rapti ravine system. A sighting would be notable.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: North Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia: Egypt, Sudan, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia, Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman, Iran, Pakistan, Afghanistan, and India.

In India: Scattered populations in Rajasthan, Gujarat, parts of Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, northern Uttar Pradesh, and the northeastern foothills.

In Uttar Pradesh: Extremely rare; restricted to the northern river valleys of the Ghaghra and Rapti, with occasional reports from neighbouring areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Extremely rare. Expected only in the Ghaghra–Rapti ravine system if at all. Any sighting would warrant documentation.

Field rule: A large, stocky, powerfully-built carnivore with distinctive vertical black stripes on a tawny body, short tail, and a humped back, found in ravines and scrub, is a Striped Hyena and should be approached only at a distance with extreme caution.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition. The epithet hyaena refers to the animal itself.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies are recognized across the range; the Indian populations are generally assigned to Hyaena hyaena felupescens or H. h. nichtedyi.

Family and order placement: Hyaenidae, the family of hyenas; order Carnivora, class Mammalia. The family comprises three genera: Crocuta (spotted hyena, Africa), Hyaena (striped and brown hyenas), and Proteles (aardwolf).

Phylogenetic notes: Hyaena hyaena is the most widely distributed hyena species. Its bone-crushing specialization is an evolutionary response to competition with other carnivores for soft tissue; it fills the niche of a scavenger and bone consumer.

IUCN status rationale: Near Threatened. The global population is declining due to persecution, habitat loss, and reduced prey availability. The species persists in fragmented populations across its range.
