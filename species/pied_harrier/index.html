पाइड हैरियर / Pied Harrier

Circus melanoleucos (Latham, 1790) — Accipitridae

पाइड हैरियर — मध्य एशिया से सर्दियों में आने वाली एक छोटी शिकारी चिड़िया। इसके नर का काला और सफेद चेहरा-पैटर्न और पंखों की काली धारियाँ इसे हेन हैरियर से अलग करती हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक यह घास के मैदानों और दलदलों के ऊपर अन्य हैरियरों की तरह धीरे-धीरे उड़ते हुए देखी जाती है। यह छोटे जानवर, पक्षी और कीड़े खाती है।

Field tip: Smaller and more compact than Hen Harrier. Males show distinctive pied head pattern and darker appearance. Females are heavily streaked and darker. The pied pattern in males and darker overall coloration distinguish it from Hen Harrier.

Size: 38–45 cm long; females significantly larger than males.

Male plumage: Distinctive. Head dark grey with white cheeks and chin — pied pattern. Back dark grey; wing coverts dark grey; flight feathers black. White patches on inner wing coverts visible in flight. Underparts white or buff. Rump white. Tail dark grey with barring.

Female plumage: Dark brown above; breast and underparts heavily streaked brown and buff. Rump white (key field mark). Tail brown with dark barring.

Face: Distinctive owl-like disc; white cheeks and chin in males; yellow eyes.

Bill: Hooked, dark with pale cere.

Legs and feet: Yellow or greenish-yellow; slender.

Sexes: Highly dimorphic; males darker and more patterned than Hen Harrier males.

Juveniles: Similar to females but duller.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Call: A harsh kik-kik-kik or chee-chee, given in various contexts.
  2. Alarm call: A louder, more insistent series when disturbed.
  3. Often silent: Usually silent while hunting.
हिंदी: तीक्ष्ण किक-किक जैसी आवाज़। शिकार करते समय आमतौर पर चुप रहता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Quartering hunter: Pied Harriers hunt by flying low over grassland in a quartering fashion, searching for prey on the ground. They feed on:

  • Small mammals (voles, mice, shrews, young hares)
  • Small birds (larks, pipits, warblers, finches)
  • Large insects (grasshoppers, dragonflies)
  • Small reptiles and amphibians

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs during winter. Males and females forage independently.

Feeding time: Active throughout the day, with peaks at dawn and dusk.

Roost behaviour: Nocturnal roosting on the ground in tall grass or rushes, or in low trees or shrubs.

Predators: Peregrine Falcon, and other harriers may take individuals.

Hunting strategy: Similar to Hen Harrier but faster and more agile in flight, allowing pursuit of flying birds.

हिंदी: घास के मैदान में तेजी से उड़ते हुए शिकार करता है। छोटे जानवर और पक्षियाँ खाता है। रात को घास में सोता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Breeds in Central Asia (April–August); absent during breeding season. Returns to eastern UP from October and remains through April.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in October; peak numbers in January–February; most depart by March–April.

Courtship: Takes place on breeding grounds. Males perform aerial displays.

Moult: Partial moult occurs during winter.

Site fidelity: Many individuals return to the same wintering territories year after year.

हिंदी: अक्टूबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। मध्य एशिया में प्रजनन करता है। सर्दियों में अपने इलाके में शिकार करता है।

  • Mammalian predators: Jackal (chicks in nest)
  • Parasites: Feather parasites, blood parasites

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: The Pied Harrier is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Pest control: Feeds on rodents and large insects, providing some benefit in agricultural areas.

Grassland indicator: Indicates the presence of open grassland habitats with abundant small mammal populations.

Historical records: Not specifically mentioned in Mughal hunting records; too small to be of sporting interest.

Cultural value: Recognized by birdwatchers as a distinctive winter visitor.

हिंदी: कानून से पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from October to April on grasslands and open areas. Documented in Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary surveys (Yashmita-Ulman & Singh, 2025).

Habitat preference: Open grasslands, marshes, and light scrub rich in rodents.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from Central Asian breeding grounds have moved south.

Nearest published record: Recorded in Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary by Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025).

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly in flight over grassland in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date) submitted through the About page of this atlas. The pied head pattern (in males) and darker overall appearance are diagnostic.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in Central Asia and southern Russia. Winters across Asia to Philippines and Australia. Monotypic species.

In India: Distributed as a winter visitor on grasslands and open wetlands, particularly in northern and eastern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to grasslands and wetland margins, particularly in the eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor to grasslands, documented in Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary surveys.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Latham in 1790 in Index Ornithologicus, as Falco melanoleucus. Later moved to the genus Circus.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Accipitriformes; family Accipitridae. The genus Circus contains the harriers.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to Hen Harrier (C. cyaneus); the two form a sister pair.

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widely distributed. Adaptable to grassland habitats. In India, protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
