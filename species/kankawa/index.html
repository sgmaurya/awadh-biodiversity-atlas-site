कनकौआ / Benghal Dayflower

Commelina benghalensis L. — Commelinaceae

कनकौआ (बसंतिका, खरपतवार) — बरसात के बाद खेत की पनीली जगहों और सिंचाई वाली क्यारियों में उगने वाली नीले फूलों वाली बेल। पत्तियाँ लंबी, नरम, एक जैसी। फूल सुबह खिलते हैं, कुछ घंटे में बंद हो जाते हैं (दिन-फूल)। तना लाल-हरे रंग का, हल्का-फुल्का, आसानी से मुड़ा करता है। गाँवों में खाज-खुजली और चर्म रोगों के लिए पारंपरिक दवाई में प्रयोग किया जाता है।

Field tip: A trailing herb with soft, succulent stems and distinctive bright blue flowers that open in the morning and close by afternoon. The leaves are simple and lance-shaped. Often found sprawling on wet field margins and irrigation bunds after the monsoon.

Morphology आकारिकी

MeasurementRange
Stem length30–100 cm
Leaf length5–15 cm
Leaf width2–6 cm

Stem: Succulent, herbaceous, trailing or weakly climbing, 30–100 cm; jointed at nodes; red or red-green tinged, especially toward base; rooting readily at nodes when in contact with moist soil.

Leaves: Alternate, lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 5–15 cm × 2–6 cm; apex acute; base subcordate or rounded; margin entire and smooth; texture smooth and soft; midrib prominent.

Flowers: Ephemeral (opening early morning, closing by afternoon); borne in small cymose clusters; sepals 3, one much larger, boat-shaped, enclosing the flower; petals 3, bright blue (rarely white), delicate; stamens 6 (3 fertile, 3 staminodal); ovary superior.

Fruits: Dehiscent capsules, 0.5–0.8 cm, thin-walled, containing 2–4 seeds per capsule.

Seeds: Small, black, smooth, spherical, 3–4 mm.

हिंदी: तना कोमल, लाल-हरा, हर गाँठ से जड़ निकालता है। पत्तियाँ सरल, नरम। फूल नीले, सुबह खिलते हैं, दोपहर को बंद।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Commelina benghalensis is a moisture-loving, frost-tender herbaceous plant that thrives in waterlogged and seasonally wet soils. The plant exhibits a trailing or semi-climbing habit, spreading laterally across wet ground or onto the stems of larger plants. Adventitious roots develop at nodes in contact with moist soil, allowing rapid horizontal spread and quick rooting of vegetative fragments.

The plant flowers prolifically during the monsoon and post-monsoon period when soil moisture is high. The ephemeral blue flowers are visited by small bees and other pollinators in the early morning hours. The plant tolerates partial shade and full sun equally well.

Commelina benghalensis is a moderate to high competitor for water and light in irrigated fields, particularly where excess water is present. It can suppress crop growth if left unchecked.

हिंदी: पानी की जगहों में खूब फैलता है। हर गाँठ से नई जड़ें निकलती हैं। मधुमक्खियों को आकर्षित करते हैं। सिंचित खेतों में फसल को दबाता है।

हिंदी: जून-जुलाई: बीज अंकुरण। जुलाई-नवंबर: फूल और फैलाव। दिसंबर से: सूखने लगता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि और मानव संबंध

Weed status: In irrigated fields and paddy bunds, Commelina benghalensis is a common and sometimes troublesome weed. The plant is easily hand-pulled when young, but its ability to root at nodes and spread rapidly means dense infestations can delay harvesting in wetland crops.

Traditional medicine: The leaf and stem of kankawa have been recorded in traditional medicine for treating skin conditions, particularly eczema, scabies, and other inflammatory skin diseases. The whole plant is sometimes boiled or made into a paste for application. Modern validation of these uses is limited.

Fodder: In some regions, the fresh herbage is occasionally cut and fed to livestock, though it is not a preferred forage.

Ecological indicator: The abundance of Commelina benghalensis indicates moist or waterlogged soil and is a useful indicator of soil drainage conditions in fields.

हिंदी: खरपतवार है, पर आसानी से निकाला जा सकता है। खाज-खुजली के लिए पारंपरिक दवाई। पानी की सूचक।

Commelina benghalensis is expected to occur commonly on wet field margins, irrigation bunds, and paddy bunds in Basti district during the monsoon and post-monsoon season (June to December). The bright blue flowers, visible in the early morning, should aid identification.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical South Asia; distributed throughout India, Bangladesh, and Southeast Asia.

In India: Widespread in moist habitats throughout the country, particularly in areas with irrigation and seasonal flooding.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in all irrigation zones and districts receiving good monsoon rainfall.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common as a weed of moist habitats; particularly abundant in irrigated fields.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Commelina benghalensis was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. The genus Commelina is named after three Commelini brothers (two prominent botanists and one mediocre one), with the plant's two prominent petals representing the two notable brothers. The specific name benghalensis refers to Bengal, India.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Commelinaceae (Spiderwort family), order Commelinales. The family is pantropical and includes many ornamental spiderworts and dayflowers.

Phylogenetic notes. Commelina benghalensis is closely related to other Old World Commelina species, particularly C. diffusa and C. nudiflora in overlapping range.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as LC (Least Concern) globally due to wide distribution and stable populations.
