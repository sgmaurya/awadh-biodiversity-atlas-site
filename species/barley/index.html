जौ / Barley

Hordeum vulgare L. — Poaceae

जौ — एक महत्वपूर्ण रबी अनाज की फसल जो बस्ती में सर्दियों में उगाई जाती है। यह गेहूँ से मिलता-जुलता लेकिन थोड़ा छोटा पौधा होता है, जिसके दाने अलग आकार के होते हैं। जौ के दाने को आटा, दाल या जानवरों के चारे के रूप में उपयोग किया जाता है। घरों में जौ का पानी ज्वर और पेचिश के इलाज में काम आता है। यह गेहूँ से कम सिंचाई और कम उपजाऊ ज़मीन पर भी उग सकता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
FormAnnual grass, 60–90 cm tall, erect bunches (tillers), fine stems
LeavesLinear, 5–10 mm wide, parallel-veined, with a rounded auricle at the base
InflorescenceDense spike (6–8 cm), with stiff, long, bristle-like awns; flowers arranged in triads
SpikeletsEach contains one fertile floret with a prominent awn (3–6 cm)
GrainOval, slightly pointed, 7–10 mm, smooth, varying in color from pale yellow to brown
RootsFibrous, shallow, water-seeking

Field tip: A winter grass taller than wheat with long, prominent awns on the seed head that stick out like straw. Spikelets are arranged differently than wheat. Grows October to April in cereal fields.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: सर्दी की फसल, गेहूँ जैसी पर पतली और थोड़ी कम। फूल की घड़ी में लंबी, कड़ी सियाल (awn) होती है जो पूरी तरह बाहर निकली रहती है। दाना गेहूँ से थोड़ा छोटा, चमकदार।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for barley in northern India):

MeasurementRange

Leaf width5–10 mm
Spike length6–8 cm

Stems: Erect, slender, hollow except at the nodes, jointed, with 4–6 leaves per plant.

Leaves: Alternate, linear, pointed, with fine parallel veins. Base of leaf (auricle) is rounded and clasps the stem.

Flowers: Arranged in a dense, unbranched spike. Each spikelet contains one fertile floret. The awn (bristle) is long and conspicuous, 3–6 cm.

Grain: The fruit is a caryopsis (grain), oval, slightly pointed, 7–10 mm long. The pericarp adheres to the seed coat.

हिंदी: पौधा पतला, पत्ती संकीर्ण। बाली में हर फूल पर लंबी कड़ी सियाल होती है। दाना चमकदार, अंडाकार।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A hardy cereal grass of temperate origin, now cultivated worldwide as a winter crop. Hordeum vulgare thrives in cool-season cultivation in eastern UP, germinating readily in October with the first autumn rains. It tolerates low temperatures and requires less water than wheat, making it suitable for rainfed fields. The plant grows rapidly through winter, flowers in January–February, and ripens by March–April. Nodal tillers appear if plants are given space, increasing the grain yield. The stiff, long awns are an identifying feature and serve a role in photosynthesis and water uptake at the grain surface.

हिंदी: रबी का अनाज जो ठंड सहता है और कम पानी में उग जाता है। अक्टूबर में बोया जाता है, मई तक तैयार हो जाता है। गेहूँ से कम देखभाल की ज़रूरत होती है।

हिंदी: अक्टूबर-नवंबर में बीज, दिसंबर में पत्तियाँ, जनवरी में फूल, मार्च-अप्रैल में दाना तैयार।

Barley in eastern UP is predominantly a winter crop; some rainy-season cultivation occurs in well-drained upland areas.

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Grain associates: Grown in rotation with Chickpea, Lentil, and Wheat.
  • Weed hosts: Winter weeds such as Chenopodium album and Melilotus grow among barley crops.
  • Fodder and straw: Stubble and straw left in fields provide forage for domestic cattle and water buffalo.
  • Seed predators: Birds (sparrows, munias) peck at ripening grain; rodents damage stored grain.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Foliar blights: Helminthosporium leaf spot and net blotch under humid winter conditions; early-sown crops are more susceptible.
  • Powdery mildew: White fungal coating on leaves, especially on susceptible varieties.
  • Loose smut: Fungal infection of the grain; black, sooty spores replace normal grain development.
  • Aphids and stem borers: Occasional damage, rarely severe.
  • Stored grain pests: Sitophilus beetles and Tribolium flour beetles infest stored grain.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Grain uses: Barley grain is ground into flour for roti and mixed-grain breads. It is boiled as a whole grain and eaten with pulses. In some parts of UP, barley is sprouted and dried to make jaunpuri sattu (barley flour), a traditional high-energy food for labourers.

Fodder: The entire plant is valuable fodder for cattle and buffalo, either grazed as winter green fodder or dried for storage.

Traditional medicine: A decoction of barley (called jau ka pani) is a traditional remedy for fever, dysentry, and urinary infections in UP villages. The cooled decoction is drunk to cool the body in summer.

Cultivation advantages: Barley requires less water than wheat and tolerates poor soils, making it suitable for rainfed cultivation. It is less attacked by pests than wheat in eastern UP.

हिंदी: दाना रोटी के आटे में मिलाया जाता है। जौ का पानी बुखार और पेचिश में देते हैं। चारा भी अच्छा होता है। गेहूँ से कम पानी लगता है।

Barley is to be expected in cultivated fields throughout Basti district during the winter season (November to March), particularly in rainfed upland areas where water availability is limited. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The crop grows alongside wheat and pulses and is harvested in March–April.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Domesticated in the Fertile Crescent over 10,000 years ago; now cultivated on every continent, particularly in temperate and cool subtropical regions.

In India: Cultivated mainly in Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Karnataka during the winter season.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widely cultivated as a winter cereal in all plain districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: A minor winter cereal crop, more common in rainfed and upland fields than in irrigated areas.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus described barley as Hordeum vulgare in 1753 in Species Plantarum, Vol. 2, p. 85. The genus name Hordeum is the classical Latin name for barley. The epithet vulgare means "common."

Subspecies. Barley is polymorphic. Modern cultivated barley comprises two main types: two-rowed (ssp. distichum) and six-rowed (ssp. vulgare). Six-rowed is more common in India.

Family and order placement. Poaceae, order Poales. Closely related to wheat (Triticum) and rye (Secale).

Phylogenetic notes. Hordeum vulgare was domesticated from wild H. spontaneum in the Fertile Crescent. Molecular evidence places Hordeum among the basal grass genera.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widely cultivated as a staple crop; no conservation concern.
