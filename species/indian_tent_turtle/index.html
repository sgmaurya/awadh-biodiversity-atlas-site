इंडियन टेंट कछुआ / Indian Tent Turtle

Pangshura tentoria (Gray, 1834) — Geoemydidae

इंडियन टेंट कछुआ — नदियों और तालाबों में रहने वाला एक मध्यम आकार का, पूरी तरह जलीय कछुआ, जिसके खोल (carapace) की बाहरी सतह पर एक विशिष्ट "पहाड़" जैसी उभरी हुई रेखा (ridge या keel) होती है, जिससे इसे "Tent" कहा जाता है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के नदियों और जलाशयों में यह पाया जाता है। यह पौधों, छोटी मछलियों और जलीय कीड़ों खाता है। बहुत ही शर्मीला कछुआ है और खतरा होने पर तुरंत पानी में छिप जाता है।

Field tip: A moderately-sized freshwater turtle with a distinctive tent-like ridge running down the middle of its shell. Shy and wary; dives immediately when approached. Found in rivers and lakes. Completely harmless.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements from Gangetic rivers):

MeasurementRange
Carapace length150–220 mm

Carapace: Oval and somewhat flattened. The most distinctive feature is the prominent central keel (ridge) running longitudinally down the middle of the carapace, giving it a tent-like profile when viewed from above. The surface is covered with concentric growth rings. The color is olive-brown to dark brown, often with lighter radiating streaks on individual scutes.

Plastron: Yellowish to pale cream, often with darker markings or patterns. The hinge between the plastron and carapace is flexible, providing some protection.

Head and neck: Head is moderate-sized with a somewhat pointed snout. Eyes are laterally positioned. The neck is reasonably long and retractable.

Limbs: Front limbs have five claws; hind limbs have four claws. The digits are webbed, providing swimming efficiency. The webbing is characteristic of aquatic turtles.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are typically smaller than females. Males have longer tails and more concave plastrons. Females are more robust and have flatter plastrons.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Strictly aquatic. The Indian Tent Turtle spends virtually its entire life in water, coming on land only to nest.

  • Habitat preference: Inhabits flowing and standing freshwater bodies including rivers, streams, lakes, and reservoirs. Prefers areas with aquatic vegetation and suitable basking sites (rocks, logs).
  • Aquatic activity: Strong swimmer. Propels itself using synchronized strokes of all four limbs. Can remain submerged for extended periods.
  • Basking behaviour: Basks on rocks, logs, and sandbanks, particularly during early morning and late afternoon. Basking is important for thermoregulation and shell health.
  • Feeding: Omnivorous; forages on the bottom and in vegetation for plant material and small animals. Most active feeder during monsoon months when food is abundant.
  • Shy behaviour: Extremely wary of humans and larger predators. Dives rapidly into deeper water when disturbed. May withdraw into shell for protection.
  • Seasonal activity: Somewhat reduced activity during winter months; dormancy possible in very cold conditions.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Omnivorous diet:

  • Plant material: Aquatic vegetation, algae, fallen fruits and seeds
  • Animal prey: Small fish, fish eggs, aquatic insects, snails, crustaceans
  • Feeding method: Forages on the bottom and in vegetation; crushes hard shells with powerful jaws

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Predatory birds: Large herons, eagles, cormorants hunt basking turtles and juveniles
  • Snakes: Water snakes may consume eggs or very small juveniles
  • Mammals: Jackals, wild boar, and otters prey on nesting females and eggs
  • Large fish: May consume hatchlings
  • Humans: Collected for the pet trade; now protected

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Ecosystem role. The Indian Tent Turtle plays an important role in freshwater ecosystems by consuming aquatic vegetation, regulating invertebrate populations, and serving as prey for larger predators. It is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 and is harmless to humans and agricultural interests. Turtle nesting sites should be protected from disturbance. The species is culturally significant in parts of India where it is associated with Hindu and Buddhist traditions.

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Indian Tent Turtles should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region and where they have been recorded by herpetologists.
यह प्रजाति स्थानीय अवलोकन नहीं है। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है।

Expected in the Ghaghra, Rapti, and other rivers and reservoirs of eastern Uttar Pradesh, particularly in areas with sandy banks and aquatic vegetation. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species is most likely to be seen during the monsoon season when water levels are high and basking sites abundant. Search along riverbanks and quiet water bodies for basking turtles, particularly on rocks and sandbars at dawn and dusk. The first local record would be a photograph showing the distinctive tent-like carapace ridge with place, GPS coordinates, and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Endemic to India; found widely across the subcontinent in suitable freshwater habitats.

In India: Distributed across peninsular India and the Gangetic plains in major river systems.

In Uttar Pradesh: Resident in the Ghaghra, Rapti, Gomti, and other river systems; common in suitable habitats throughout the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Occurs in the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and associated water bodies; likely fairly common in suitable habitat.

Field rule: A medium-sized freshwater turtle with a distinct tent-like ridge running down the middle of its shell is an Indian Tent Turtle. The central keel is the key identifier.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by John Edward Gray in 1834 in Zoological Miscellany as Testudo tentoria based on Indian specimens. Type locality is India. It was later moved to the genus Pangshura by Gray (1856).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Testudines, family Geoemydidae (Asian turtles). The genus Pangshura contains six species of Asian freshwater turtles.

Phylogenetic notes. Pangshura tentoria is one of the more basal species within the genus Pangshura, based on molecular evidence. It shares characteristics with other Indian freshwater turtles.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern (LC) because of its wide geographic distribution and stable populations in many areas. However, localized populations may be threatened by dam construction, water pollution, and collection for the pet trade. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.
