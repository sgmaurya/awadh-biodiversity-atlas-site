ब्लैक-स्पॉटेड पियरो तितली / Black-spotted Pierrot

Tarucus balkanica (Freyer, 1844) — Lycaenidae

ब्लैक-स्पॉटेड पियरो तितली — बेर के पेड़ों और कटीली झाड़ियों के आसपास उड़ने वाली एक छोटी और चंचल नीली तितली है। इसके पंखों का निचला हिस्सा सफेद होता है जिस पर बहुत सारे छोटे-छोटे गोल काले धब्बे छिड़के जैसे दिखते हैं। पिछले पंखों के किनारे पर चमकदार धातु जैसे बिंदु होते हैं। नर तितली ऊपर से बैंगनी-नीली होती है। इसके कैटरपिलर बेर की पत्तियों को खाते हैं और चींटियों द्वारा संरक्षित रहते हैं।

Field tip: Look around wild and cultivated Ziziphus (ber) bushes in dry, sun-baked wastelands and orchard borders. A small blue butterfly with separate round black spots (rather than fused stripes) covering its creamy white underside is the Black-spotted Pierrot.

हिंदी: बेर की झाड़ियों के पास उड़ने वाली इस छोटी तितली के निचले पंखों पर अलग-अलग गोल काले धब्बे होते हैं, जो इसे अन्य पियरो तितलियों से अलग पहचान दिलाते हैं।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics recorded from adult specimens (Kehimkar 2008; Evans 1932):

MeasurementRangeSource
Wingspan22–28 mmKehimkar (2008)

Adult description: The Black-spotted Pierrot displays pronounced sexual dimorphism and distinct seasonal variation between wet-season and dry-season broods (Bingham 1907). In males, the upperside is vibrant silky violet-blue, with narrow dark brown terminal margins (Evans 1932). The black spots of the underside show through faintly on the translucent wing disc, along with a distinct discocellular black spot on both forewing and hindwing (Bingham 1907). In females, the upperside is dark greyish-brown with extensive bluish-purple scaling over the basal and discal areas, accompanied by an irregular white postdiscal band (Wynter-Blyth 1957).

The underside of both sexes is pure chalky white or pale ivory (Kehimkar 2008). The key diagnostic feature of this species within the genus Tarucus is that the black markings in the basal, discal, and postdiscal areas remain as distinct, clearly separated, rounded spots and short dashes, without coalescing into continuous longitudinal or transverse bands (Bingham 1907). The hindwing underside features a complete marginal row of velvety black spots, each capped with glittering emerald-green or metallic turquoise-blue scales (Wynter-Blyth 1957).

A delicate filamentous black tail, tipped with white, projects from vein 2 of the hindwing (Bingham 1907). The antennae are black with fine white rings and an ochreous-tipped club; eyes are hairy (Kehimkar 2008).

Larval Stage इल्ली अवस्था

The mature caterpillar is onisciform, flattened, pale grass-green or yellowish-green, adorned with a reddish or dark green pulsating dorsal line and faint yellow lateral stripes (Kehimkar 2008). The larva possesses specialized nectar glands and tentacular organs on the abdominal segments that secrete sweet fluids to solicit protective attendance from ants (Kehimkar 2008). The pupa is pale green or light brown with dark mottling, strapped to a twig or beneath bark with silk.

हिंदी: इसका शरीर छोटा और पंखों का फैलाव 22 से 28 मिमी होता है। ऊपरी पंख चमकीले बैंगनी-नीले और नीचे के पंख सफेद होते हैं जिन पर गोल काले धब्बे होते हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

The Black-spotted Pierrot is an obligate associate of Ziziphus scrub in arid, semi-arid, and agricultural landscapes (Kehimkar 2008). Adults spend almost their entire lives in the immediate vicinity of their host bushes, rarely venturing more than a few dozen metres away across open fields (Wynter-Blyth 1957). Flight is low, jerky, and erratic, characterized by rapid fluttering hops around the thorny branches and terminal foliage of ber trees (Bingham 1907).

Males are highly territorial and perch on sunlit outer twigs or thorny sprigs, vigorously chasing off intruding males before returning to the same vantage point (Kehimkar 2008). When perched, the butterfly keeps its wings tightly folded vertically, periodically rubbing its hindwings together in a scissor-like motion; this moves the tails and metallic eye-spots, creating the illusion of a twitching head to misdirect bird and spider strikes away from the vital body (Wynter-Blyth 1957). Adults visit the tiny pungent flowers of Ziziphus and nearby composite weeds, and males frequently mud-puddle on moist soil near rural wells and ditches (Kehimkar 2008).

हिंदी: यह तितली अपना पूरा जीवन बेर के पेड़ों के आसपास ही बिताती है। यह कटीली टहनियों पर बैठकर धूप सेकती है और बैठते समय अपने पिछले पंखों को आपस में रगड़ती है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Seasonal phenology in northern Indian plains across the agricultural calendar (Kehimkar 2008; Evans 1932):

The female deposits tiny, flattened, turban-shaped pale green eggs singly on the young leaf buds, thorns, or tender leaves of Ziziphus. The egg hatches in a few days. The young caterpillar feeds on the epidermal layers of tender leaves, leaving distinctive brown scoring on the foliage. The caterpillar is assiduously attended by ants that protect it from parasitoid wasps and spiders in exchange for honeydew secretions (Kehimkar 2008). Larval development requires a few weeks, and the pupal duration lasts about a week to ten days before adult eclosion. Multiple broods occur through the year.

हिंदी: मादा बेर की पत्तियों पर एक-एक अंडा देती है जो कुछ दिनों में फूटता है। इसकी इल्लियों की चींटियाँ रक्षा करती हैं और बदले में मीठा रस पाती हैं।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Documented larval host plants from published Indian lepidopteran accounts (Kehimkar 2008; Bingham 1907):

  • Ziziphus mauritiana (Indian Jujube, family Rhamnaceae) — cultivated and wild jujube, the primary larval host across northern Indian plains (Kehimkar 2008).
  • Ziziphus nummularia (Jharber, family Rhamnaceae) — wild dwarf thorny bush widespread in grazing pastures and dry field bunds (Kehimkar 2008).
  • Ziziphus oenopolia (Makai / Jackal Jujube, family Rhamnaceae) — thorny scrambling shrub of woodland borders and village hedges.

Adult butterflies feed on nectar from the small greenish-yellow blossoms of Ziziphus mauritiana, Tridax procumbens (Tridax Daisy), and wild roadside herbs (Kehimkar 2008).

हिंदी: इसकी इल्लियाँ केवल बेर (देशी बेर, झरबेरी और मकोई) की पत्तियां खाती हैं। वयस्क तितलियाँ बेर के नन्हे फूलों और जंगली घासों के फूलों से रस चूसती हैं।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Common insectivorous predators of the Gangetic plain likely to take lycaenids on thorn scrub:

  • Spiders: Foliage-dwelling crab spiders (Thomisidae) and jumping spiders (Salticidae) wait inside Ziziphus flower clusters to ambush visiting adults.
  • Insects: Praying mantises and predatory assassin bugs capture caterpillars and resting adults on thorny stems.
  • Birds: Insectivorous birds, such as tailorbirds, prinias, and green bee-eaters, catch adults on the wing.
  • Parasitoids: Small eulophid and braconid micro-wasps attack the caterpillars whenever ant guards are absent.
हिंदी: बेर के काँटों में छिपी शिकारी मकड़ियाँ, गिरगिट और छोटी चिड़ियाँ इस तितली को खाती हैं। चींटियों की गैरमौजूदगी में परजीवी ततैये इसकी इल्लियों को नष्ट कर देते हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Orchard relationship: The Black-spotted Pierrot is closely linked to Ziziphus mauritiana (Indian Jujube), a commercially cultivated fruit crop in eastern Uttar Pradesh (Kehimkar 2008). While caterpillars feed on tender foliage and flower buds, they rarely cause economic loss in commercial orchards, as feeding is dispersed and mature trees easily compensate with vigorous new growth. Insecticidal sprays directed at fruit borers commonly suppress local pierrot populations in managed orchards.

Pollination contribution: Adults are frequent flower visitors and serve as minor diurnal pollinators of ber blossoms, facilitating cross-pollination in rural orchards and wild scrub (Kehimkar 2008).

Traditional: No traditional medicinal or direct economic uses are documented.

Today: Recognized as a benign co-inhabitant of rural jujube agro-ecosystems and an attractive element of village scrub.

Research direction (a lead, not a result): Investigating the ecological dynamics of mutualistic ant attendance across commercial ber cultivars versus wild Ziziphus nummularia.

हिंदी: यह तितली बेर के बागों में रहती है लेकिन फलों को नुकसान नहीं पहुँचाती। यह बेर के फूलों पर बैठकर परागण में मदद करती है, जिससे फलों की पैदावार पर अच्छा असर पड़ता है।

The Black-spotted Pierrot is expected throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh in commercial ber orchards, grazing pastures with wild jujube thickets, and along dry sunny field borders. Look around flowering Ziziphus bushes on warm sunny mornings from September through April. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. A photograph with date and location, submitted through the About page, would confirm the first local documentation for the district.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Spanning the southern Palaearctic and Oriental realms, from Southeastern Europe and Asia Minor across the Middle East, Central Asia, and Pakistan to India (Bingham 1907).

In India: Widely distributed across northwestern India, the dry northern plains, Rajasthan, Gujarat, central India, and southwards into peninsular India (Evans 1932; Kehimkar 2008).

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded from scrub forests, ravine tracts, and countryside throughout the Gangetic plain wherever Ziziphus occurs.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a resident breeding species in dry open wastelands, orchard tracts, and rural grazing lands supporting jujube bushes.

हिंदी: यह तितली दक्षिण-पूर्वी यूरोप से लेकर पूरे भारत में पाई जाती है। उत्तर प्रदेश के उन सभी जिलों में यह मौजूद है जहाँ बेर और झरबेरी की झाड़ियाँ हैं।

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Christian Friedrich Freyer in [1844] as Lycaena balkanica in Neuere Beiträge zur Schmetterlingskunde mit Abbildungen nach der Natur, Volume 5, page 63, plate 421, figures 1, 2. Type locality: "Türkei" (Balkans / European Turkey).

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies are recognized across its extensive Trans-Palaearctic and Oriental range. The population inhabiting northern India is classified as Tarucus balkanica nigra Bethune-Baker, 1918. Kehimkar (2008) spells the species name Tarucus balkanicus; GBIF's accepted spelling is Tarucus balkanica.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Lycaenidae, subfamily Polyommatinae, tribe Polyommatini. The genus Tarucus Moore, 1881, comprises the pierrots, specialized on rhamnaceous host plants.

Phylogenetic notes. Tarucus is closely allied to Castalius and Leptotes, characterized by specialized myrmecophilous larval adaptations and metallic tornal scaling on the hindwing (Bingham 1907).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. It is an abundant and ecologically resilient species across multiple continents with no significant conservation threats.

हिंदी: इसका वैज्ञानिक नाम Tarucus balkanica है और यह Lycaenidae परिवार का सदस्य है। प्राकृतिक रूप से यह पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है।
