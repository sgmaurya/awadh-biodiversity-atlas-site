ग्रे खंजन / Grey Wagtail

Motacilla cinerea Tunstall, 1771 — Motacillidae

ग्रे खंजन — यूरोप और मध्य एशिया से सर्दियों में आने वाली एक पतली और सुंदर चिड़िया। इसकी लंबी पूंछ, गहरी पीठ और सिर की काली धारियाँ इसे पहचान देती हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में सितंबर से मई तक नदियों और पानी की धारों के किनारों पर तेजी से दौड़ते हुए देखी जाती है। यह छोटे कीड़े खाती है और हमेशा पानी के पास रहती है।

Field tip: A small, slender wagtail with a very long tail and grey back. The yellow underparts and black-and-white head pattern (in males) are distinctive. Always associated with running water. The constant tail-wagging is characteristic of all wagtails.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 18–20 cm long, with the tail accounting for much of the length.

Breeding male plumage: Black cap and nape. White supercilium. Black lores. White cheek and chin. Grey back and wing coverts. Black and white wing pattern. Yellow throat, breast, and belly. Black tail with white outer edges.

Non-breeding plumage: Duller overall. Cap and nape grey (not black). White throat. Yellow restricted to undertail-coverts and vent. Tail still prominent and often shown raised.

Females: Duller than males; less yellow on underparts; grey cap (never black).

Bill: Fine, pointed, dark; specialized for catching small insects in flight.

Legs and feet: Dark grey; long, slender; adapted for walking on rocky surfaces.

Juveniles: Similar to females but duller.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Call: A sharp, metallic chit-chit or tzit-tzit, given as contact call.
  2. Flight call: A distinctive tee-tee-tzit given in flight.
  3. Song: A brief warble, rarely heard outside breeding season.
हिंदी: तीक्ष्ण चित-चित आवाज़। उड़ते समय ती-ती चिल्लाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Insect forager: Grey Wagtails forage by walking and running on rocks and ground near water, picking insects from surfaces and vegetation, and occasionally catching insects in flight. They feed on:

  • Small flies (mayflies, blackflies, gnats)
  • Small aquatic insects
  • Small terrestrial insects (grasshoppers, small beetles)
  • Small spiders

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs during winter; sometimes gather at night roosts with other wagtails.

Feeding time: Active throughout the day, foraging continuously.

Roost behaviour: Nocturnal roosting on branches overhanging water, or in reeds and bushes near water.

Predators: Hooded crows, house sparrows, and occasionally larger birds may take individuals. Chicks vulnerable in nest.

Water dependency: Highly dependent on water; rarely found far from rivers, streams, or other flowing water.

हिंदी: पानी के पास ही रहता है। पत्थरों पर छोटे कीड़े खाता है। अकेला या जोड़ों में रहता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Breeds in Central Asia, Himalayas, and southern Europe (April–August); returns to eastern UP from September and remains through May.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in September; peak numbers in January–February; most depart by April–May, with stragglers into June.

Courtship: Takes place on breeding grounds. Males perform display flights and sing.

Moult: Partial moult occurs during winter. Breeding plumage acquired before return to breeding grounds.

Site fidelity: Many individuals return to the same wintering territories year after year.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, मई तक रहता है। हिमालय में प्रजनन करता है। सर्दियों में नदी पर अपना इलाका रखता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: The Grey Wagtail is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Stream indicator: As a specialist forager on rocks in fast-flowing water, the Grey Wagtail indicates the presence of relatively clean, well-oxygenated rivers and streams.

Water quality indicator: Dependent on healthy stream ecosystems with abundant small aquatic insects.

Agricultural benefit: Feeds on small insects that might be agricultural pests, though the impact is minimal.

Historical records: Not mentioned in Mughal hunting records; too small to be of sporting interest.

Cultural value: Recognized by birdwatchers and naturalists as a charming and delicate winter visitor.

हिंदी: कानून से पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from September to May on rivers, streams, and water margins. Documented in Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary surveys (Yashmita-Ulman & Singh, 2025).

Habitat preference: Fast-flowing rivers and streams, particularly those with rocky banks and margins. Less common on still water than on flowing water.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from Himalayan and Central Asian breeding grounds have moved south.

Nearest published record: Recorded in Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary by Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025).

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly on a rock or at the water's edge in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date) submitted through the About page of this atlas. The long tail, grey back, and yellow underparts are diagnostic.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित है, न कि देखे गए बतौर प्रेक्षण। सितंबर से मई तक नदी के किनारे देखे जा सकते हैं। पहला रिकॉर्ड के लिए स्पष्ट फोटोग्राफ चाहिए।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds from Iceland and northwestern Europe to Japan. Winters across Europe, North Africa, and Asia. Polytypic with several subspecies.

In India: Distributed as a winter visitor on rivers and streams, particularly in northern and eastern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to the Ganga and its tributaries, particularly the Ghaghra, Rapti, and Gomti rivers.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular winter visitor to rivers and streams, documented in Parvati Arga Bird Sanctuary surveys.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Tunstall in 1771 in Ornithologia Britannica, as Motacilla cinerea. The genus Motacilla has remained stable.

Subspecies. Polytypic with several subspecies recognized; M. c. cinerea is the nominate subspecies breeding in Europe and western Asia.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Motacillidae (wagtails and pipits). The genus Motacilla contains the wagtails, a group of slender insectivores adapted to open and riparian habitats.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to other Motacilla species, particularly the Yellow Wagtail (M. flava) and White Wagtail (M. alba).

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widely distributed. Adaptable to various riparian habitats. In India, protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
