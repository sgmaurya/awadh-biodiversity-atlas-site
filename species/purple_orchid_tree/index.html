कनियार / Purple Orchid Tree

Bauhinia purpurea L. — Fabaceae

कनियार — एक सुंदर पेड़ जिसके फूल बिल्कुल ऑर्किड के फूलों जैसे लगते हैं। फूल गहरे बैंगनी, गुलाबी या सफेद रंग के होते हैं, और इनकी खुशबू तेज़ होती है। सर्दियों में, जब पत्तियाँ कम रहती हैं, तब फूल ज़्यादा खिलते हैं। पत्तियाँ विशेष आकार की होती हैं - बिल्कुल बँटी हुई, जिससे लगता है कि दो पत्तियाँ एक साथ जुड़ी हैं। पेड़ गाँवों और मंदिरों में सजावट के लिए लगाया जाता है।

Field tip: A semi-deciduous tree with distinctly bilobed leaves and showy orchid-like purple or pink flowers with darker veining. Flowers appear in autumn and early winter when leaves are sparse, making them very conspicuous. Pods are long and flat.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: पत्ती बँटी हुई, दो भाग में, दिल के आकार जैसी। फूल बड़े, बैंगनी या गुलाबी, ऑर्किड जैसे। सर्दी में खिलते हैं।

Trunk and bark: Erect, moderately stout, with pale gray-brown bark that becomes fissured with age. Wood is pale yellow, moderately hard.

Leaves: Unique, alternate, deeply bilobed, appearing as if two leaves are joined back-to-back; the apex is notched or rounded, with two distinct lobes. The leaf is thick, glossy, and entire. Venation is palmate.

Flowers: Large, showy, orchid-like, with five petals; ground color varies from deep purple to pink or white, with darker veining on the petals; the central petal is marked with darker lines. Flowers are fragrant and long-lasting (open over many days). Arranged singly or in small clusters on twig tips.

Fruit: A legume pod, linear, flat, 20–35 cm long, brown, containing 8–12 flat seeds.

हिंदी: पत्ती बँटी, दो भाग में। फूल बड़े, गहरे रंग के, सुगंधित। लंबी, चपटी फली।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A semi-deciduous tree native to the Indian subcontinent, inhabiting dry and semi-dry deciduous forests. Bauhinia purpurea is drought-tolerant and thrives in a range of soils. It flowers in autumn and early winter (September–November), precisely when many other trees are dropping leaves, making the orchid-like blooms highly conspicuous and valuable to bees and visiting insects. The species appears to have some nitrogen-fixing ability through root associations (though not to the extent of truly nodulated legumes). It has been cultivated as an ornamental tree in gardens and temple grounds throughout India for centuries, valued for its spectacular flowers. The tree produces abundant seed, which germinates easily, allowing it to spread into waste areas and forest margins near cultivation.

हिंदी: सूखे को सहता है। सर्दी में खूब फूल खिलते हैं। मधुमक्खियों के लिए अच्छा है। बीज आसानी से गजते हैं।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Pollinator visitors: Asian Honey Bee and solitary bees visit the flowers; butterflies and other insects also attracted.
  • Garden companions: Often planted near Jamun, Guava, and Neem in temple gardens.
  • Ornamental use: Highly valued in formal gardens and public spaces for autumn flowering.
  • Seed dispersal: Seeds are wind-scattered and also dispersed by water.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Leaf-eating caterpillars: Occasionally, defoliating caterpillars feed on the bilobed leaves; damage is usually light on healthy trees.
  • Leaf spots: Fungal leaf spots are occasional in humid monsoon conditions; rarely cause significant damage.
  • Pod rot: Moisture-loving fungi occasionally cause mold on developing pods in wet conditions.
  • Generally healthy: Purple orchid tree is hardy and pest-resistant in most environments.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Ornamental value: The primary value of purple orchid tree is its spectacular flowers. The showy, orchid-like blooms in autumn and winter make it one of the most prized ornamental trees in Indian gardens and public spaces. Temple committees and municipal authorities plant it extensively.

Shade and shelter: The dense canopy provides good shade and shelter for humans and animals.

Timber: The wood is moderately hard and suitable for fuel and tool handles, though it is rarely harvested for timber due to its ornamental value.

Traditional uses: The bark and leaves are used in traditional Ayurvedic medicine for their astringent properties, though no specific medicinal use is well-documented.

हिंदी: फूलों के लिए बहुत प्रसिद्ध। मंदिरों और बाग़ों में लगाया जाता है। छाया देता है। लकड़ी ईंधन के लिए सही है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Indian subcontinent, particularly the dry deciduous zones. Now cultivated in tropical and warm temperate regions worldwide.

In India: Native to dry regions of peninsular India and the Gangetic plain; extensively cultivated in gardens and public spaces throughout the country.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in homestead gardens and public spaces; occurs naturalized in some dry areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Present as a cultivated ornamental; records from core districts in temple and homestead gardens.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum, Vol. 1, p. 512. The genus name Bauhinia honors the Swiss botanist brothers Johann and Caspar Bauhin. The specific epithet purpurea refers to the purple color of the flowers.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Fabaceae, subfamily Cercidoideae, order Fabales. Related to other Bauhinia species and the senna family.

Phylogenetic notes. Bauhinia purpurea belongs to the "showy flowering" clade within Bauhinia, characterized by large ornamental flowers. Molecular evidence suggests it is a monophyletic species with little internal variation.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in cultivation and in natural deciduous forests; populations are stable.
