हरियल / Yellow-footed Green Pigeon

Treron phoenicopterus (Latham, 1790) — Columbidae

हरियल — आम के पेड़ों में हरी कबूतर जो घास जैसी आवाज़ करती है। इसकी पीली टाँगें, हरा रंग, और पंखों का लाल धब्बा इसे दूसरी कबूतरों से अलग करते हैं। यह फलों को खाती है, बीजों को दूर तक फैलाती है, और बहुत शर्माली होती है।

Field tip: A large green pigeon with bright yellow feet and a wine-red wing patch. Canopy dweller, often heard before seen. Listen for the distinctive "pis-pis-pisi-pit" call from tall trees.

Plumage: Head and neck greyish-green. Back, wing coverts and rump olive-green. Secondary coverts and bend of wing bright wine-red or maroon — highly distinctive. Primaries dark grey-green. Underparts yellowish-olive green, paler than back. Tail green with yellow tips. Remiges dark grey at tips.

Face: Orbital ring yellow; eye orange-red; bill yellow with red base.

Legs and feet: Bright yellow — diagnostic at any distance.

Sexes: Males have more yellow on head; females greener. Otherwise similar.

Juveniles: Duller green; wing patch less vivid; feet duller yellow.

Vocalisations

Yellow-footed Green Pigeon is a loud and distinctive bird, especially in the canopy where it spends most time.

  1. Contact and advertising call: A loud, repetitive "pis-pis-pisi-pit" or "gir-gir-gir", reminiscent of grass swishing; quite unlike other pigeons. Heard especially at dawn and in late afternoon.
  2. Alarm call: A sharp, nasal "wah-wah".
  3. Flight call: Explosive wing claps and whooshing sounds as flocks depart.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Canopy frugivore: Yellow-footed Green Pigeon is almost entirely dependent on tree fruits. It moves through the canopy with remarkable agility, feeding on ripe figs, berries, and drupes. Feeding flocks are often silent and inconspicuous despite their size.

Fruiting phenology: The distribution and abundance of the species within any region is determined almost entirely by fruiting trees. When fruit is abundant, large flocks congregate. When local fruits fail, birds move on (partial migration/nomadism).

Flock sizes: Usually in small groups (5–15), but large aggregations (50–100+) gather at heavily fruiting fig trees. These aggregations are temporary — they disperse as soon as fruiting is over.

Roost site: Roosting sites are in tall trees, often in dense foliage. Birds roost communally with other species of green pigeons.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • January–March: Fruiting of early figs; birds spread into new areas
  • March–June: Breeding season; pairs establish; calling most intense; small clutches (1 egg usually)
  • April–July: Monsoon fruiting begins; pigeon chicks fledge
  • July–September: Peak fruiting of mango, neem fruits, and monsoon figs
  • September–December: Post-monsoon fruiting; birds move between fruit sources

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Exclusive frugivore:

  • Figs (Ficus spp.) — Peepal, Banyan, wild figs — primary diet
  • Berries of various forest shrubs
  • Drupes of neem (Azadirachta indica)
  • Mango and guava fruits (occasionally)
  • Seasonal fruit availability determines local abundance

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Shikra (Accipiter badius) — primary predator; hunts pigeons in flight
  • Peregrine Falcon (Falco peregrinus) — diving attacks in open; less common predator
  • Jungle Crow (Corvus macrorhynchos) — raids nests; takes eggs and chicks
  • Rat snakes — climb to raid nests in trees

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Seed dispersal: Yellow-footed Green Pigeon is a critical seed-disperser for fruiting forest trees, especially figs. Seeds pass intact through the digestive tract and are deposited far from the parent tree. Studies on fig germination success show higher rates after passage through pigeon guts.

Orchard interactions: Occasional visitor to mango and guava orchards during fruiting; usually does minimal damage as birds consume entire fruits and move on. Unlike Spotted Doves, not a grain-feeder.

Habitat dependency: The presence and abundance of this pigeon is entirely linked to the health of fruiting trees in villages and along forest margins. Loss of old fig and neem trees directly reduces pigeon populations.

Protected status: Schedule IV of Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. Historically hunted for sport in some regions; now protected.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Yellow-footed Green Pigeon is expected in Basti and Ayodhya districts wherever old fig and neem trees are present. Look for them in mature Peepal and Banyan trees near villages and in forest remnants. Most visible during fruiting seasons. Listen for the distinctive "pis-pis" call from tall trees in early morning and late afternoon.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent — widespread from Afghanistan and Pakistan through India, Nepal, Bangladesh and Myanmar, typically at lower elevations.

In India: Common resident throughout India below 2,000 m; moves locally in response to fruiting phenology.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread resident in areas with adequate fruiting trees; less common in intensively cultivated areas without forest remnants.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in villages with mature fruit-bearing trees and along forest margins; populations fluctuate with fruit availability.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. Which trees do Yellow-footed Green Pigeons use most for feeding? / कौन से पेड़ों पर हरियल सबसे ज़्यादा खाती है? — Map fruiting trees in your village; check which ones have pigeons; count flocks.
  2. How much do pigeon flocks move during the year? / पूरे साल में ये कितना घूमते हैं? — Track groups between seasons; note if they return to same trees each year.
  3. Do Yellow-footed and other pigeon species roost together? / अलग-अलग कबूतरें एक साथ सोती हैं? — Observe roost sites at dusk; no equipment needed.
  4. How many seeds can one pigeon's droppings disperse? / एक कबूतर की बीट में कितने बीज हैं? — Collect droppings below roosting trees; examine and count seeds.
  5. Are pigeons eating the mangoes and guavas you want? / क्या कबूतरें आपके फल खा रही हैं? — Mark fruiting trees; count pigeon visits and damage; compare to loss from other causes.

Field rule: Large green pigeon with bright yellow feet, wine-red wing patch, and loud "pis-pis" call from the canopy = Yellow-footed Green Pigeon. The combination of yellow feet and maroon wing is diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Treron phoenicopterus was described by Latham in 1790 as Columba phoenicopterus, based on specimens from Bengal. The genus Treron was erected later.

Subspecies. Six subspecies recognised across the Asian range; phoenicopterus is the nominate form occurring in northern and eastern India.

Family and order placement. Order Columbiformes; family Columbidae. The genus Treron contains 13 species of green pigeons, mostly Asian.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies confirm Treron as a derived clade within Columbidae, sharing a recent common ancestor with African green pigeons.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern. The species remains widespread and abundant wherever fruiting trees persist, though local declines occur where old trees are cut.
