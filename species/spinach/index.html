पालक / Spinach

Spinacia oleracea L. — Amaranthaceae

पालक — अवध और पूर्वांचल के मैदानों में शीतकाल की प्रमुख पत्तेदार सब्जी, जिसके मुलायम, गहरे हरे पत्ते साग और दाल में खूब खाए जाते हैं। यह प्राचीन काल से मध्य एशिया से आकर भारतीय रसोई का अभिन्न अंग बन चुका है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurement not available in consulted sources. Spinach is an annual, glabrous, somewhat succulent herb cultivated primarily for its tender basal foliage. In the early vegetative phase, it forms a low, compact rosette of radical leaves. The leaves are alternate and fleshy, with prominent channeled petioles. Leaf blades are variable in outline, typically ovate-oblong to triangular-hastate, with obtuse or subacute apices and entire or sinuate-dentate margins; the base is often cuneate or distinctly auriculate with two spreading basal lobes.

During the transition to reproduction, the central crown elongates into an erect, striate, hollow, pale green flowering stem that is simple or sparsely branched. The cauline leaves on the upper stem are smaller, narrower, subsessile, and oblong-lanceolate, gradually grading into lanceolate bracts.

The species is predominantly dioecious, with male (staminate) and female (pistillate) flowers borne on separate individuals, though monoecious forms occur in some cultivars. Male flowers are clustered in terminal, leafless, interrupted spicate racemes; they possess a four- or five-parted membranous green perianth and four or five slender stamens with capillary filaments that exert large, pendulous, two-celled yellow anthers.

Female flowers are borne in dense, sessile, axillary clusters nestled in the leaf axils. The female perianth is tubular, two- to four-toothed, and completely encloses the single superior, unilocular ovary. During fruit maturation, the perianth segments become accrescent, thick, and indurated, adhering tightly to the utricle and developing into either smooth round capsules (in the smooth-seeded cultivar group) or armed with two to four sharp, rigid, divergent spines or horns (in the prickly-seeded group). The seed is solitary, lenticular or subglobose, with a smooth brown testa and starchy endosperm surrounded by a curved embryo.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार एवं पारिस्थितिकी

Spinacia oleracea is adapted to cool-season growing conditions, exhibiting rapid vegetative growth during the mild, bright days and chilly nights of the northern Indian winter. It is a long-day plant whose reproductive transition is governed strongly by photoperiod and rising spring temperatures. As photoperiods lengthen and ambient heat intensifies in February and March, the plants cease leaf production and bolt rapidly into flower.

Pollination in spinach is strictly anemophilous (wind-pollinated). Male plants produce enormous quantities of dry, buoyant, spherical pollen grains that are shed from dangling anthers and carried by thermal air currents across agricultural fields. Female flowers expose long, feathery, receptive stigmas from the axillary clusters to trap airborne pollen. Because female flowers produce no floral nectar or conspicuous visual attractants, insect visitors play negligible roles in its reproduction.

In the soil environment, the shallow fibrous root system explores the upper alluvial soil horizon, taking up soluble soil nutrients efficiently. The broad, spreading leaf rosette provides effective living mulch, shading the moist furrow surface and dampening diurnal soil temperature fluctuations.

हिंदी: यह हवा से परागित होने वाला पौधा है। नर पौधे भारी मात्रा में सूखा पराग उड़ाते हैं जिसे मादा फूल की लंबी वर्तिकाएं पकड़ती हैं।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र एवं ऋतुचक्र

Spinach seeds germinate within a few days under cool soil temperatures. The linear cotyledons emerge above ground, followed rapidly by the development of true leaves that arrange themselves into a flattened basal rosette. Under cool, short-day conditions, apical stem elongation remains suppressed while the crown continuously produces new succulent leaf blades.

Vegetative leaf production continues through December and January. With the advent of warmer spring days in February, hormonal triggers initiate rapid bolting. The central flowering stem shoots upward, rapidly exhausting the plant's moisture and reserves. Male plants flower first, releasing yellow clouds of wind-borne pollen for several days before senescing and dying.

Female plants maintain green stems longer as their axillary ovaries swell and harden into protective, spiny or rounded fruiting husks. The fruits ripen by late March or early April, turning dry and pale brown, at which stage seed growers harvest whole female plants for threshing and seed collection.

हिंदी: बीज कुछ ही दिनों में उगते हैं। सर्दियों में लगातार पत्ते निकलते हैं और फरवरी-मार्च में पौधे में डंठल निकलकर बीज बनते हैं।

Associated Species / Interactions सहजीवी प्रजातियाँ एवं पारिस्थितिक संबंध

While spinach flowers do not support nectar-seeking insects due to wind pollination, the dense, humid foliage of winter spinach beds provides daytime microclimate shelter for numerous ground beetles and spiders.

The foliage is occasionally utilized by generalist leaf-feeding caterpillars and sap-sucking bugs.

हिंदी: नम क्यारियों में शिकारी भृंग और मकड़ियां शरण लेती हैं।

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

A major insect challenge in Gangetic plain spinach production is aphids, which colonize leaf undersides, producing sticky honeydew that fosters sooty mold. Foliage is also attacked by cutworms that sever young seedlings at the soil line at night.

Among fungal pathogens, downy mildew is a destructive disease during foggy, humid winter spells, appearing as pale yellowish spots on upper leaf surfaces matched by purplish-grey fungal felt underneath. Leaf spot produces small circular spots with reddish-brown margins. Damping-off of emerging seedlings occurs if seedbeds are over-watered. Disease prevention relies on raised-bed drainage, crop rotation, and clean cultivation.

हिंदी: डाउनी मिल्ड्यू (फफूंद) और माहू इसके मुख्य रोग-कीट हैं। जलभराव रोकने और फसल चक्र अपनाने से फसल स्वस्थ रहती है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि एवं मानव प्रासंगिकता

Spinach is one of the most widely consumed green leafy vegetables (saag) in northern India. In rural and urban households alike, it is cooked into dal-palak, combined with paneer or potatoes, or boiled and pureed for winter dishes. The tender young leaves are also incorporated into besan fritters (palak pakoras). Kirtikar & Basu (1918, 2: 1067) record that the green plant was believed useful in urinary calculi, and the seeds laxative and cooling. Chopra et al. (1956, p. 232) list iodine and lecithin among its constituents. This is a record of published traditional use, not medical advice.

हिंदी: पालक दाल, पनीर और भुर्जी में खाया जाने वाला लोकप्रिय साग है।

Distribution वितरण

Spinacia oleracea is cultivated worldwide across temperate and subtropical agricultural zones. The species originated in ancient Persia (modern-day Iran and adjacent southwest Asia), where it was domesticated from wild ancestral forms such as Spinacia turkestanica. It spread across Asia and into India in antiquity and was later introduced to Europe through Moorish Spain.

Similar Species मिलती-जुलती प्रजातियाँ

Spinach (Spinacia oleracea) is frequently confused in colloquial terminology with other cultivated leafy greens known as "palak" or "saag" in northern India. Palak beet (palak beet, Beta vulgaris, FLR-0268), widely sold as desi palak, has thicker, broadly ovate, glossy leaves with thick, succulent white or reddish ribs; its flowers are perfect and clustered in long glomerules, lacking the dioecious spiny husks of true spinach. Malabar spinach (malabar spinach, Basella alba, FLR-0279), known as poi, is a twining perennial vine of the rainy season with thick, succulent, mucilaginous leaves and dark purple fleshy berries, completely unlike the erect winter annual herb Spinacia. Bathua (bathua, Chenopodium album, FLR-0073) is a wild or tolerated winter weed with smaller, rhombic-ovate leaves covered in mealy white farina and non-spiny flower clusters.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण एवं नामकरण

Spinacia oleracea was formally established by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. The genus Spinacia comprises three or four species native to central and southwestern Asia, historically placed in Chenopodiaceae and now classified within the broader family Amaranthaceae (subfamily Chenopodioideae). Cultivated spinach is broadly divided into two major horticultural forms: the prickly-seeded form (sometimes referenced as var. spinosa) and the smooth-seeded form (var. inermis). In J. D. Hooker's Flora of British India, the plant is documented under Chenopodiaceae as an extensively cultivated cold-season vegetable throughout India.

Original description. Spinacia oleracea was named and described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum (vol. 2, p. 1027).

Subspecies. Cultivated spinach is grouped into prickly-seeded forms (var. spinosa) and smooth-seeded forms (var. inermis).

Family and order placement. The genus Spinacia is placed within the subfamily Chenopodioideae of the family Amaranthaceae (formerly Chenopodiaceae), order Caryophyllales.

IUCN status rationale. Spinacia oleracea is a worldwide cultivated domesticate and is categorized as Not Evaluated (NE) on the IUCN Red List.
