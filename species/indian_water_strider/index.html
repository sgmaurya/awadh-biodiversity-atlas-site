जल-सर्पी / Indian Water Strider

Gerris spinolae Lethierry & Severin, 1896 — Gerridae

जल-सर्पी (इंडियन वाटर स्ट्राइडर) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के तालाबों, पोखरों और सिंचाई नालियों की सतह पर तैरने वाला एक अत्यंत सामान्य और रोचक निवासी जल-कीड़ा (water bug) है। यह पानी की सतह के तनाव (surface tension) का उपयोग करके पानी के ऊपर बिना डूबे बहुत तेज़ी से दौड़ या फिसल सकता है। इसके पैर बारीक वाटरप्रूफ बालों से ढके होते हैं जो पानी को दूर धकेलते हैं। इसके बीच और पीछे के पैर बहुत लंबे होते हैं जो इसे तैरने और दिशा बदलने में मदद करते हैं, जबकि आगे के छोटे पैरों का उपयोग यह शिकार पकड़ने के लिए करता है। यह पानी की सतह पर गिरने वाले छोटे कीड़ों की लहरों (vibrations) को महसूस करके उन पर हमला करता है। तेज़ धूप में तालाब की तली पर इसके पैरों की परछाई गोल काली बूंदों के रूप में दिखाई देती है, जिनके चारों ओर प्रकाश का एक चमकदार घेरा (light halos) होता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSlender, elongated body; body length 11–14 mm; wingspan 9–11.5 mm
LegsVery long middle and hind legs; short forelegs positioned close to the head
ColourationDark greyish-black or dark brown above; pale silvery-grey below with water-repellent hair
PronotumElongated, dark brown with a faint median light line in the anterior part
MovementSkates rapidly across water; makes sudden, jerky lunges to capture prey
AntennaeLong, 4-segmented, and slender antennae extending forward

Field tip: Look at the calm surface of a village pond or ditch on a sunny day. Watch for slender insects skating rapidly on the water. Look at the bottom of the pond: you will see six dark, circular shadows cast by the tips of their legs, surrounded by bright halos of light where the water surface bent but did not break.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for adult specimens in local ponds):

MeasurementRange
Body length11.0–14.0 mm
Wing length9.0–11.5 mm

Body details: The body is slender and spindle-shaped. The outer cuticle is covered by thousands of tiny hydrofuge microhairs (microsetae) that trap air and repel water. The head is small with large, globose compound eyes. The forelegs are short, thick, and modified for grasping prey (raptorial). The middle legs are extremely long and thin, acting as oars to propel the insect. The hind legs are also very long and function as rudders for steering. The abdomen is narrow. Most local individuals are winged (macropterous) and are capable of flying between water bodies, though wingless (apterous) forms also occur.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Water surface specialist.

  • Surface tension locomotion: The water strider relies on surface tension, using the hydrophobic hairs on its tarsi to distribute its weight. The leg tips press against the water surface, creating dimples without piercing the surface film.
  • Vibrational detection: Mechanoreceptors on their legs detect tiny ripples (vibrations) made by struggling insects that fall onto the water. They locate and rush toward the prey in milliseconds.
  • Territorial groupings: They often gather in loose groups in shaded water patches, maintaining individual distances by pushing away intruders with quick leg kicks.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Epineustonic predator.

  • Surface cleaner: They consume terrestrial insects (like ants, flies, and beetles) that accidentally fall into the water, serving as a clean-up link on the water surface.
  • Mosquito population control: Aggressively prey on mosquito larvae and pupae when they surface to breathe.
  • Prey item: Serve as a food resource for predatory aquatic insects (like backswimmers), skittering frogs, and small fish.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Breeding: Occurs primarily during spring and monsoon seasons when water bodies are stable and full.
  • Egg laying: The female deposits elongated, translucent eggs on submerged leaves or floating plant stems, gluing them in place. Eggs hatch in 7–10 days.
  • Development: Hemimetabolous development. The nymphs pass through 5 instars over 3–4 weeks. The nymphs look like small, wingless adults and feed on the surface immediately after hatching.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Prey:

  • Mosquito larvae and pupae (Culex and Anopheles).
  • Small midges, houseflies, ants, and leafhoppers that fall onto the surface film.
  • Cannibalise smaller water strider nymphs when food is scarce.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Amphibians: Skittering Frogs (such as [AMP-0003 Skittering Frog]) and young bullfrogs catch them from below.
  • Fish: Surface-feeding pool barbs (such as [FSH-0015 Pool Barb]) and juvenile snakeheads.
  • Insects: Giant water bugs and predatory water scorpions hunt them from beneath the surface.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Natural vector control ally.

  • Mosquito management: By consuming surfacing mosquito pupae and larvae, they help reduce mosquito populations around rural homes and cattle sheds.
  • Scientific study: Used locally in school demonstrations to illustrate surface tension and fluid dynamics in physics and biology classes.

Very common and easily observed in stagnant pond systems (talab and pokhra) in rural areas of Basti, including Vikramjot, Vikrampur, and Harraiya blocks. They are particularly active during sunny afternoons, skating under the shade of water hyacinth (jalkumbhi) mats.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across the Indian Subcontinent, covering India, Pakistan, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and parts of Myanmar.

In India: Found in all freshwater ecosystems of the plains and lower hills.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant resident across all wetlands and water channels.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Highly common in all rural and agricultural blocks.

Field rule: Slender elongated body (~11–14 mm), dark brownish-grey dorsal surface, very long middle and hind legs, skating rapidly on stagnant waters, identify the Indian Water Strider.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Lucien Lethierry and Guillaume Severin described the Indian Water Strider as Gerris spinolae in 1896 in Catalogue Général des Hémiptères. Tome III. Hétéroptères (p. 60). The species was named to honor the Italian entomologist Massimiliano Spinola. The genus name Gerris is Latin for "a type of small fish" or "bug".

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Hemiptera, suborder Heteroptera, infraorder Gerromorpha, family Gerridae (water striders).

Phylogenetic notes. Gerris spinolae belongs to the subfamily Gerrinae, representing a highly specialized lineage of water bugs that adapted to a semi-aquatic lifestyle on the air-water interface through hydrophobic hair structures.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) by the IUCN Red List. It is extremely common and widespread across South Asia, with no immediate conservation threats.
