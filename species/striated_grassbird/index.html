स्ट्रिएटेड ग्रासबर्ड / Striated Grassbird

Megalurus palustris Horsfield, 1821 — Acrocephalidae

स्ट्रिएटेड ग्रासबर्ड — यह छोटी गहरी भूरी चिड़िया ऊँची घास और सरकंडे के जंगल में रहती है। इसका गहरा रंग और पतली चोंच इसे घास में छिपी रहने के लिए बिल्कुल अनुकूल बनाती हैं। सर्दी और गर्मी दोनों में पूरे साल पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की नहरों और तालाबों के पास देखी जाती है। यह धीमी, दोहरी सीटी जैसी आवाज़ देती है।

Field tip: A dark, heavily streaked grassland warbler that stays deep in vegetation. Look along reed beds and tall grass near water; often detected by its distinctive warbling song. Difficult to observe as it remains hidden; patience and dawn visits recommended.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length180–190 mm

Size: 18–19 cm long; weighs 16–20 g. A small warbler with proportionally long tail and bill.

Plumage: Dark olive-brown above with heavy dark streaking on crown and back. Whitish or buff supercilium (eyebrow stripe). Underparts buff or pale brown with buff-yellow vent. Flanks heavily streaked. Tail dark brown with buff edges.

Bill: Long, slender, pointed; yellowish at base, dark at tip. Adapted for picking insects from vegetation.

Legs and feet: Brown; strong, long-toed, adapted for gripping reed stems.

Sexes: Similar; no obvious sexual dimorphism.

Juveniles: Similar to adults but slightly duller.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: A distinctive, rambling warble of short notes, often uttered from deep in tall grass; warbler-like quality.
  2. Contact calls: Soft, churring notes; also sharp ticks.
  3. Alarm call: A loud, scolding chatter when disturbed.
हिंदी: गहरी आवाज़ में कलरव करता है। घास में बैठकर गाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Foraging behaviour: Skulks through dense vegetation, gleaning insects from grass stems, reeds, and foliage. Picks insects from plants and occasionally from the ground. Feeds on:

  • Grasshoppers and crickets
  • Small moths and caterpillars
  • Spiders and small insects
  • Occasionally small seeds

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs. Males are territorial and sing from perches within tall grass. Keeps to dense cover; rarely ventures into open.

Nesting: Monogamous. Builds a woven cup nest suspended among reed stems or grass bunches.

Movement: Moves through grass with quick, skulking movements. Flight is weak and reluctant; flies low between patches of grass, rarely rising above vegetation.

Vocalisations: Males sing persistently, especially during breeding season and at dawn. Song is diagnostic but habitat preference makes it easier to locate than to observe.

हिंदी: घास में छिपा रहता है। जोड़ों में रहता है। गाना बहुत ज़्यादा गाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Breeding season: May–September in eastern UP; peak June–July.

Nest construction: Female builds a deep, woven cup nest of grass and reed stems suspended between reed stalks or grass bunches, 1–2 m above water or ground.

Clutch: 3–4 eggs; pale greenish with reddish-brown markings.

Incubation: About 12–14 days; female incubates.

Fledging: Chicks fledge in 12–14 days; both parents feed young.

Moult: Complete moult post-breeding; August–October.

Resident population: Year-round resident; some local movement in response to water levels.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Shrike species, small raptors, crows
  • Mammalian predators: Weasels, snakes, domestic cat
  • Nest predators: Snakes, corvids
  • Parasites: Avian parasites, mites
हिंदी: चिल्ली और बड़ी चिड़ियाँ इसका शिकार करती हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Schedule IV (as member of Acrocephalidae).

Agricultural value: Feeds on grasshoppers and other crop pests; beneficial in paddy fields.

Habitat decline: Drainage of wetlands and conversion of grasslands to agricultural land has reduced suitable habitat. Loss of natural reed beds and tall grasslands is a conservation concern.

Cultural relevance: Known to villagers in wetland areas; its song indicates presence of reed beds and wetland habitats.

Research value: Used as an indicator species for grassland and wetland health in the Gangetic plain.

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Striated Grassbird should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Habitat and occurrence: Expected year-round in eastern UP in reed beds, tall grasslands, and marshes, particularly along the Ghaghra, Rapti and Ghaghara river systems. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya. Most easily detected by voice during breeding season (May–August).

Where to look: Margins of larger tanks, ponds, and wetlands with extensive reed beds or tall grass. Particularly in areas with Phragmites australis reed beds. Less common in areas with short grass or heavily grazed vegetation.

Seasonality: Year-round resident; populations may shift locally in response to flooding and water levels.

Detection method: Early morning visits to reed beds during breeding season yield best results. Song is diagnostic; familiarity with local environment increases detection probability.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph or video showing the bird clearly in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date) submitted through the About page of this atlas.

हिंदी: घास के जंगल में साल भर रहता है। गर्मी में इसका गाना सुनना सबसे आसान है।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: India and Southeast Asia. Resident in the Indian subcontinent from the foothills to peninsular India, with distribution concentrated in wetland and grassland regions.

In India: Widespread in wet grasslands and reed beds; more common in peninsular India and eastern regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded in reed beds and wetlands of the Gangetic plain; documented from Lucknow, Sultanpur and other wetland areas.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Likely resident in suitable wetland habitats along the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems; range needs confirmation through targeted surveys.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Thomas Horsfield in 1821 from Java. Megalurus palustris Horsfield, 1821, in Transactions of the Linnean Society 13: 160.

Subspecies. Two subspecies recognised:

  • M. p. palustris Horsfield, 1821 — resident in India, Nepal, Bangladesh
  • M. p. cinereus Swinhoe, 1863 — southeast Asia

The Indian populations, including those in eastern UP, are M. p. palustris.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Acrocephalidae (Old World warblers). Genus Megalurus comprises grassland warblers of Asia and Australia.

Phylogenetic notes. Megalurus is part of the Acrocephalidae family, which underwent considerable taxonomic revision in the 2000s. Megalurus palustris is sister to other Megalurus species and is adapted to grassland and reed bed habitats.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Population is stable and widespread across suitable habitats in Asia. In India, it is protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 and is not threatened by exploitation. Loss of wetlands is the main threat to local populations.
