डुबडुबी / Little Grebe

Tachybaptus ruficollis (Pallas, 1764) — Podicipedidae

डुबडुबी — तालाब की सतह पर तैरता हुआ एक छोटी, भूरे रंग की चिड़िया जो निरंतर पानी में डूबती और निकलती है। इसकी पीठ और पेट पर अलग-अलग रंग होते हैं — ऊपर काला-भूरा और नीचे सफेद। इसके पैर शरीर के बहुत पिछले हिस्से में हैं, जिससे पानी में तो यह बेहद तेज है पर ज़मीन पर लगभग असहाय लगता है।

Field tip: A small, dumpy brown bird on water that frequently dives suddenly and resurfaces some distance away — is a Little Grebe. The silhouette is very compact and rounded. In breeding season, the rufous neck is visible. On land, it looks completely out of place and waddles awkwardly.

Size: 23–29 cm; one of the smallest grebe species in the world. Wingspan 40–50 cm. Weight 130–220 g.

Plumage (non-breeding): Blackish-brown above; white below. Neck buff-brown. Cheek and throat white.

Plumage (breeding): Develops rich chestnut-rufous on the neck and upper breast; head darkens. Appears more richly coloured.

Bill: Short, pointed, slightly upturned; colour varies yellow to dark.

Eyes: Red iris (especially in breeding season).

Feet: Lobed (not webbed); placed very far back on body; greenish-grey. The backward placement makes Little Grebes supremely adapted for diving and swimming but completely helpless on land.

Tail: Vestigial — barely visible as a stub.

Sexes: Identical in plumage. Juveniles duller, streakier.

Vocalisations. Three main call types:

  1. Contact and alarm call: A loud, distinctive wailing trill — whee-whee-whee-whee — given when alarmed, audible over considerable distance; frequency 2.0–4.5 kHz.
  2. Courtship song: Loud, rapid, accelerating chattering and trilling sounds produced by pairs during breeding, often as a duet.
  3. Territorial call: Sharp clicks and whirring sounds from water, particularly during dawn and dusk.
हिंदी: तेज़, ऊँचा, रुदन जैसा कूटना — यही इसकी सबसे खास आवाज़ है। सुबह और शाम को बार-बार सुनाई देती है।

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Aquatic predator: The Little Grebe is a diving specialist, feeding almost entirely on small aquatic animals:

  • Small fish (Puntius, Aplocheilus, larvae)
  • Aquatic insects and nymphs (dragonflies, mayflies, diving beetles)
  • Tadpoles and small amphibians
  • Aquatic crustaceans and small mollusks

Diving specialist: The backwards leg placement and lobed foot structure make the Little Grebe an extremely efficient diver. A typical dive lasts 5–10 seconds; the bird can remain submerged up to 20 seconds if pursuing prey.

Pond health indicator: The presence of Little Grebes indicates a clear, productive freshwater body with abundant invertebrate life. Their absence from suitable habitat may signal pesticide pollution or oxygen depletion.

Prey of: Fish eagles, marsh harriers, snakes, and jackals may take chicks at the nest.

हिंदी: छोटी मछलियाँ, कीड़ों के बच्चे, और टैडपोल खाता है। 5-10 सेकंड तक पानी में डूबा रह सकता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round in eastern UP; no migration, though local movements occur with water levels.

Breeding: March–November (peaks May–August). Solitary or in loose pairs.

Nesting: Platform nest of aquatic vegetation built among reeds and rushes in shallow water, often floating. Both parents build.

Eggs: 3–6 pale buff eggs, often stained. Both parents incubate ~22 days.

Chicks: Precocial; leave the nest within hours. Fed small invertebrates and small fish by parents. Ride on parents' backs initially.

Fledging: Young fly at ~6 weeks; independent shortly after.

Roosting: Solitary or in small loose groups; sleeps on water.

हिंदी: पूरे साल रहता है। मार्च से नवंबर तक प्रजनन करता है। सरकंडों के बीच पानी में घोंसला बनाता है।

Conservation Status संरक्षण स्थिति

IUCN Status: Least Concern (LC)

Wildlife Protection Act, 1972: Listed under Schedule IV (Protected) of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, as amended in 2022. Killing or harming a Little Grebe is illegal in India.

Threats: Drainage of wetlands; pollution of ponds with pesticides; water extraction for irrigation; entanglement in fishing nets (though rare due to small size); predation at nests by terrestrial mammals.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी भारतीय कानून में संरक्षित है। इसे मारना गैरकानूनी है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि और मानव संबंध

Aquatic insect control: Little Grebes feed heavily on dragonfly nymphs and other aquatic insects in paddy fields and ponds, providing natural pest control during the monsoon season when mosquito and other insect populations peak.

Pond health indicator: Traditional fishermen in eastern UP recognize the presence of Little Grebes as a sign of a clean, productive pond. Their disappearance often precedes fish diseases or algal blooms.

Neutral cultural status: Unlike herons and storks, the Little Grebe has no special cultural significance. It is not hunted or revered locally.

हिंदी: यह कीड़ों का शिकारी है, जो खेती के लिए अच्छा है। तालाब स्वच्छ है तो यह आता है।

Common and abundant year-round on every suitable water body across Basti district — ponds, lakes, channels, and slow sections of rivers. The distinctive trilling call is often the first indication of presence. Nesting birds are reported in reed beds throughout the monsoon season.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across Eurasia and Africa, from the British Isles to Japan and down to South Africa.

In India: Found throughout India in freshwater wetlands from sea level to about 2,000 m elevation.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common resident throughout all districts, concentrated in the plains and Gangetic region.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Abundant year-round resident on all suitable water bodies — ponds, lakes, rivers, and channels.

Field rule: The smallest grebe; a tiny compact brown-and-white diving bird with a loud wailing trill and backward leg placement — is a Little Grebe. The constant diving behaviour and inability to walk on land are distinctive.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Tachybaptus ruficollis was described by Peter Simon Pallas in 1764 in his Avium Enumeratio as Colymbus ruficollis. Type locality: Siberia. The genus Tachybaptus was established by Mathews (1913) for the little grebes of the Old World.

Subspecies. Five subspecies are recognised:

SubspeciesAuthorityRangeNotes
T. r. ruficollis(Pallas, 1764)Central Asia and eastern EuropeEastern UP subspecies — nominate form; darker
T. r. podiceps(Macgillivray, 1842)British Isles, western EuropeSlightly paler; has short crest
T. r. iraquensis(Ticehurst, 1925)Middle East and north AfricaPaler and greyer
T. r. capensis(Salvadori, 1884)Southern AfricaDarker below; rufous-chestnut breast
T. r. tricolor(Hartert, 1923)Indonesia (Sumatra and Java)Variant of nominate form

Family and order placement. Order Podicipediformes, family Podicipedidae (grebes), containing 6 genera and approximately 20 species worldwide. The genus Tachybaptus contains 3 species of small grebes — only this species occurs in the Old World.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies (Mayr, 2010) show that Tachybaptus ruficollis is sister to the New World T. dominicus (Least Grebe). The genus is monophyletic and the most basal (earliest-branching) genus within Podicipedidae.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). Extremely large range, very large population, and generally stable numbers across most of Eurasia and Africa. Wetland loss is a slow pressure but populations remain robust in the Indian subcontinent.
