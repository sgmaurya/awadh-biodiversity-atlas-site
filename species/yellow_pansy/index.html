येलो पैंज़ी तितली / Yellow Pansy

Junonia hierta (Fabricius, 1798) — Nymphalidae

येलो पैंज़ी तितली — एक सुंदर तितली जिसके पंख नारंगी-पीले और काले रंग के होते हैं, जिसमें बड़े-बड़े गोल "आई-स्पॉट" या "ओसेली" होती हैं। ये नीली और सफ़ेद केंद्र वाले धब्बे शिकारियों को धोखा देते हैं। यह तितली खुले मैदानों और घास के मैदानों में पाई जाती है। इसका कैटरपिलर विभिन्न घास की प्रजातियों पर भोजन करता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeMedium butterfly, wingspan 40-50 mm
ColourOrange-yellow to yellow with black markings
PatternLarge, distinctive eyespots (ocelli) with blue and white centers on both wings
EyespotsVery prominent; metallic blue or iridescent centers; give name "pansy" to several Junonia species
UndersidePaler; eyespots still visible; brown or tan base colour
FlightLow, fluttering flight; stays close to ground

Field tip: A striking butterfly with large, prominent eyespots with iridescent centres on orange-yellow wings. Very distinctive and easy to identify. Look in grasslands and open fields.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: नारंगी-पीली तितली, बड़े-बड़े नीले गोल धब्बे। खुले मैदान में।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Wingspan40-50 mm
Forewing length19-24 mm
Body length12-15 mm

Wings:

  • Upperside: Orange-yellow to deep yellow ground colour; black markings and borders; the most distinctive feature is a row of very large eyespots (ocelli) with iridescent metallic blue or purple centers surrounded by pale (often white) rings and dark borders.
  • Underside: Paler, tan to brown ground colour; eyespots still prominent; less vivid than upperside.

Body: Dark brown to black; medium-sized.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are generally more brightly coloured with deeper orange-yellow; females are paler and may have more extensive dark markings.

Eyespots: The large, iridescent-centred eyespots are among the most distinctive of any Indian butterfly; they are defensive structures that mimic large eyes and startle or confuse predators.

Adult food sources:

  • Nectar from flowers
  • Damp spots on ground
  • Rotting fruit
  • Animal dung

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds: Small insectivorous birds; eyespots may deflect attacks
  • Spiders: Web-building and hunting spiders in grasslands
  • Parasitoid wasps: Attack caterpillars and pupae
  • Insects: Dragonflies and other predatory insects may capture adults

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Grassland indicator. Common in grasslands, wastelands, and open disturbed areas, indicating diverse grass growth. The species is an indicator of grassland habitat presence.

Minor agricultural role. Caterpillars feed on various grass species but cause negligible damage to cultivated crops. Not an agricultural pest.

Cultural presence. A well-known and easily recognizable butterfly across India; familiar to many villagers due to its bright colours and grassland habitats.

Today. Abundant in grasslands, open fields, and disturbed areas during monsoon and post-monsoon seasons. Common in villages with open grazing areas.

To be expected year-round in grasslands, open fields, wastelands, and cultivated margins throughout the region. Peak abundance during monsoon and post-monsoon seasons (July–November). Very easy to spot in open areas. A photograph with date and location would confirm a local record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Africa, Middle East, and Asia, including India. Very widespread.

In India: Abundant throughout most regions; one of the commonest butterflies in grasslands and open areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common throughout the state in suitable habitats.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in grasslands, open fields, and disturbed areas year-round, particularly abundant during monsoon and post-monsoon (July–November).

Field rule: A bright orange-yellow butterfly with very large, iridescent-centred eyespots on orange-yellow wings, found in open grasslands, is the Yellow Pansy.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Fabricius in 1798 in Supplementum Entomologiae Systematicae. Type locality: India.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies recognized. The subspecies found in India is the nominate form Junonia hierta hierta (Fabricius, 1798).

Synonymy. Historical synonyms include Precis hierta and older nomenclatural variants.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Nymphalidae, subfamily Nymphalinae. The genus Junonia (pansy butterflies) includes several species with similar eyespot patterns and grassland habitats.

Phylogenetic notes. Junonia species share the distinctive eyespot patterns and grassland habitats, representing a successful radiation in open, disturbed habitats.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed. Abundant and widespread throughout range; no conservation concerns.
