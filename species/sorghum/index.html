ज्वार / Sorghum

Sorghum bicolor (L.) Moench — Poaceae

ज्वार — एक महत्वपूर्ण अनाज फसल जो सूखे सहन करने की क्षमता के लिए विश्व प्रसिद्ध है। यह एक सीधे बढ़ने वाला, शाखित, लंबा घास (1–3 मीटर) है। पत्तियाँ चौड़ी, तलवार जैसी, दोनों ओर खुरदुरी होती हैं। तने के शीर्ष पर एक गहरा, कॉम्पैक्ट, दानों का गुच्छा होता है। दाने गोल, सफ़ेद, सुनहरे, लाल या काले रंग के हो सकते हैं। ज्वार भारतीय रसोई में एक मुख्य अनाज है। इसकी दलिया, रोटी, खीर और पूरी बनाई जाती है। पत्तियों को मवेशियों के लिए हरे चारे के रूप में भी काटा जाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
FormErect, branched annual grass, 1–3 m tall, single thick stem with starch-filled pith
LeavesAlternate, linear, 15–60 cm long, 2–5 cm wide, gray-green, waxy coating, serrate margins
InflorescenceTerminal panicle, compact, ovoid or cylindrical, 10–30 cm long, 3–8 cm wide
SpikeletsSmall, paired on the rachis; outer glumes are dark, hardened; pale inner florets
GrainsRound, 0.5–0.8 cm diameter, variable color (white, yellow, red, black); hard starch endosperm

Field tip: A robust, tall grass with a thick stem, waxy gray-green leaves, and a compact dark cylindrical head of grains; highly drought-tolerant.

Root system: Fibrous roots with adventitious roots from lower stem nodes; deep rooting in dry soil.

Stem: Solid, starch-filled, erect, becoming woody at maturity; prop roots emerge from lower nodes.

Associated Species / Interactions सहजीवी संबंध

  • Insect host: Serves as host for various grain borers and stem flies
  • Bird food: Ripe panicles are eaten by munias, sparrows, and house crows
  • Fodder: Leaves and immature panicles are primary fodder crop
  • Rotation value: Part of cereal rotations with pulses
  • Shade provider: Tall crop stands shelter smaller plants

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Sowing: Sown in June–July (Kharif) or September–October (Rabi) on well-prepared fields
  • Germination: Emerges within 5–7 days with adequate moisture
  • Vegetative growth: Grows rapidly through July–August or October–November; develops multiple leaves and tillers
  • Flowering: Panicle emergence and flowering in August–September or November–December
  • Grain filling: Grains mature through September–October or December–January
  • Harvest: Mature panicles are harvested in September–October or January–February; threshed to yield grain

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Shoot fly: Atherigona soccata larva feeds in leaf whorl, killing the growing point
  • Stem borer: Chilo partellus tunnel inside stems, causing lodging
  • Anthracnose: Colletotrichum causes leaf spots and panicle infection
  • Grain mold: Fusarium and Aspergillus species infect developing grains in wet seasons

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Drought-tolerant grain and fodder crop.

  • Grain preparation: Grains are ground into fine flour for making roti, dosa, and traditional flatbreads
  • Nutritional value: Rich in protein, fiber, and micronutrients; gluten-free cereal for those with celiac disease
  • Savory dishes: Cooked whole grains are eaten as khichdi (with legumes) or in rice-like preparations
  • Desserts: Ground flour is used for sweet preparations like kheer, laddu, and halwa
  • Green fodder: Entire plant cut at pre-flowering stage provides high-quality green fodder for livestock
  • Cultivars: Sown varieties include CSH-9, Phule Anuradha, and local landraces

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use

  • Festival food: Traditional flatbreads from jowar flour are staple in rural North India
  • Strength food: Considered nutritious for growing children and nursing mothers
  • Threshing: Panicles are threshed by beating with wooden poles or trampling by animals

Sorghum bicolor is expected as a monsoon and winter cereal crop in Basti district. Look for it in fields from June through October or from September through February, recognizable by tall grass plants with compact grain heads. The first local record would be a photograph with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Domesticated in Africa; now cultivated worldwide as a drought-tolerant cereal.

In India: Widely cultivated in all states; major producing states include Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in all plain districts during monsoon and winter seasons.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Cultivated resident; important monsoon and winter cereal crop.

Field rule: A tall grass with thick starch-filled stem, waxy gray-green leaves, and a compact dark cylindrical panicle of round grains distinguishes sorghum.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus first described the species as Holcus bicolor in 1753. It was later transferred to Sorghum bicolor by Wilhelm Moench in 1794. The genus name Sorghum is derived from the Italian sorgo. The specific epithet bicolor refers to the two-colored grain types.

Subspecies. Monotypic; cultivated varieties are classified into grain types (white, red, black) and growth habits (dwarf, tall).

Family and order placement. Order Poales, family Poaceae (grass family), tribe Andropogoneae.

Phylogenetic notes. Sorghum bicolor is derived from Sorghum bicolor subsp. verticilliflorum, a wild species native to Africa.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a globally cultivated crop, it faces no conservation threats.
