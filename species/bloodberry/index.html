ब्लडबेरी / Bloodberry

Rivina humilis L. — Phytolaccaceae

ब्लडबेरी — अमेरिका का एक छोटा, सुंदर पौधा, जो भारत में बगीचों से निकलकर खेतों और बंजर भूमियों पर जंगली हो गया है। यह बस्ती में बहुत कम पाया जाता है, लेकिन नम जलवायु वाले इलाकों में बढ़ रहा है। इसके पतले पत्ते नरम होते हैं, और छोटे-छोटे सफेद या गुलाबी फूल लंबी डंडियों पर लगते हैं। फल चमकीले लाल गोलियाँ हैं, जिन्हें पक्षी खाते हैं और दूर-दूर तक फैलाते हैं। नाम इन्हीं रक्त-लाल फलों से आया है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription

LeavesAlternate, ovate to lanceolate, 3–8 cm long, thin, soft, margins wavy
FlowersSmall, white or pale pink, 3–4 mm diameter, in slender axillary or terminal racemes
FruitBright red, globose berries, 5–7 mm diameter, shining, juicy, one-seeded
StemHerbaceous, green, hairy, becoming woody at base

Field tip: A small shrub with delicate leaves and striking bright red berries. The berries are the most distinctive feature; no other small weed in eastern UP has such glossy, bright red fruit.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: नरम, पतली पत्तियाँ, छोटे सफेद-गुलाबी फूल, चमकीले लाल फल। पक्षियों द्वारा खाया जाता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Stem and bark: Herbaceous, green, hairy, branching profusely from base.

Leaves: Alternate, ovate to lanceolate, 3–8 cm long, thin and soft, hairy, margins wavy or entire.

Flowers: Small, white or pale pink, with 4–5 sepals and no petals (characteristic of Phytolaccaceae), in slender axillary or terminal racemes.

Fruit: A one-seeded berry, bright red, glossy, juicy, 5–7 mm diameter. The seed is black, spherical.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A tropical American plant introduced to India as an ornamental ground cover, now naturalized in warm regions. It thrives in moist, disturbed ground and light wastelands. The bright red berries are attractive to birds, which eat them and disperse the seeds widely. The plant is not strongly competitive and is easily pulled when young.

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Bird dispersal: Various bird species eat the ripe berries and disperse seeds.
  • Pollinators: Small bees and flies visit flowers.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Mostly pest-free.
  • Leaf spot fungi occasionally appear in very humid conditions.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Ornamental heritage: Widely grown in Indian gardens as an ornamental ground cover for its attractive red berries. It has escaped from gardens into wastelands.

Today: A minor weed in eastern UP, appearing sporadically in gardens, wastelands, and field margins. It is not aggressive and does not seriously compete with crops. Its presence is valued by some for the berries, which are attractive but not edible.

Control: Easily pulled when young. No chemical control needed.

हिंदी: बस्ती में बागों से निकलकर जंगली हो रहा है। सुंदर लाल फल के लिए कुछ लोग इसे रखते हैं।

Expected as a garden escapee or volunteer in wastelands near human habitation in Basti district. More common in tropical gardens than in open fields. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya core.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical and subtropical Americas; now naturalized in tropical and warm-temperate regions worldwide.

In India: Warm regions, particularly gardens and wastelands in southern and coastal India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Rare; mainly in warmer districts and gardens.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Garden escapes; naturalized resident in scattered locations.

Field rule: Bloodberry is a small shrub with delicate thin leaves and striking bright red berries. The small white flowers and one-seeded fruits distinguish it from other red-fruited plants.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum, Vol. 2, p. 472. Type locality is tropical America.

Subspecies. Monotypic.

Family and order placement. Order Caryophyllales, family Phytolaccaceae (the pokeweed family), which includes pokeberries and other genera with berries but no petals.

Phylogenetic notes. Rivina is the only genus in Phytolaccaceae with a one-seeded berry; other genera have many-seeded berries.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Invasive in some regions but minor in eastern UP.
