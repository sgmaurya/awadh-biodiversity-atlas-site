सिंघाड़ा / Water Chestnut

Trapa bispinosa Roxb. — Lythraceae

सिंघाड़ा — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के ग्रामीण तालाबों की सतह पर हरी टाइल्स जैसी सघन मोज़ेक बनाने वाला एक अत्यंत महत्वपूर्ण जलीय पौधा। इसके त्रिकोणीय तैरते पत्तों की डंठल में हवा भरी फूली हुई संरचना होती है जो इसे पानी पर तैरने में मदद करती है। शरद ऋतु में इसमें दो कांटों (सींगों) वाला कठोर तिकोना फल आता है, जिसके अंदर पौष्टिक और स्टार्चयुक्त गिरी होती है। यह न केवल उपवास (व्रत) के भोजन का एक प्रमुख हिस्सा है, बल्कि तालाब पट्टे पर लेने वाले मल्लाह समुदाय की आजीविका का भी मुख्य आधार है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Habitat ClassFloating-leaved rooted aquatic plant; anchored in pond mud by long stems
LeavesDiamond-shaped or triangular (2.5–5 cm), sharply saw-toothed margins; arranged in flat, floating rosettes
PetioleLeaf stalk (5–15 cm long) with a distinct spongy, air-swollen middle part that acts as a float

FruitA hard, nut-like triangular drupe with two sharp, woody spines (horns)

Field tip: Look for dense, green geometric mats covering the surface of village ponds. The combination of triangular, saw-toothed leaves and spongy, air-swollen petioles (stalks) is a diagnostic feature. In autumn, look for the sharp, two-spined, dark brown or green nuts hidden beneath the floating leaves.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements in the Gangetic plain):

FeatureMeasurement
Plant height (stem length)1.5–3.0 m
Leaf length (floating lamina)2.5–5.0 cm

Flower diameter8–12 mm
Fruit dimensions2.0–4.0 cm

Stem: Long, flexible, submerged stems anchored to the muddy pond bottom. They can grow up to 3 metres long depending on water depth.

Leaves: The plant exhibits heterophylly (two distinct leaf types):

  • Submerged leaves: Opposite, pinnately divided into fine, green, root-like segments that float in the water column and function like roots.
  • Floating leaves: Form a rosette on the water surface. They are rhombic (diamond-shaped) to triangular, dark green and glossy above, often reddish beneath, with sharply serrate margins on the upper half. The petioles (leaf stalks) are spongy and have a swollen, air-filled middle section that provides buoyancy.

Flowers: Small, white, solitary, 4-petalled flowers arising from the leaf axils. They open above the water surface for pollination.

Fruit: A woody, top-shaped drupe, 2–4 cm wide, containing a single large, white, starchy seed. The fruit is armed with two prominent, sharp, downward-pointing spines developed from the persistent sepals, protecting it from seed-eating aquatic animals.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Nutrient filter. Trapa bispinosa is a highly efficient bio-accumulator. It absorbs excess agricultural runoff, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus, from village waterbodies, preventing rapid algal blooms and purifying pond water.

Habitat creator. The extensive floating mats provide shade and cover for young fish, frogs, and aquatic insect larvae. Micro-invertebrates colonize the submerged root-like leaves, providing food for insectivorous fish.

Food source. The starch-rich seeds are consumed by aquatic mammals like wild boars and various waterfowl. Water snails feed on the decaying leaf margins.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Germination: Annual plant. Seeds overwinter in pond mud and germinate in late spring (April–May) as temperatures rise.
  • Vegetative growth: Stems grow rapidly toward the surface, forming floating rosettes by June.
  • Flowering: Solitary white flowers appear from July to September, opening in the afternoon and evening.
  • Fruiting: Fruits mature from September to November. As they ripen, they become heavy and sink to the bottom of the pond.
  • Die-back: During winter (December–January), the vegetative parts of the plant rot and die back, leaving the hard seeds dormant in the mud.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Associated organisms:

  • Larvae of the water lily beetle and water chestnut weevil feed on the floating leaves.
  • Micro-algae and periphyton attach to the submerged leaf segments.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Pond Snails (Pila globosa) — consume young leaves and stems.
  • Wild Boar (Sus scrofa) — dig up the hard nuts from dried pond beds during summer.
  • Fungal Pathogens — leaf spot fungi can cause large-scale rotting of the floating mats.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Cultivated crop. Water chestnut is a major aquaculture crop in Basti district. Ponds are leased out to traditional fishing communities (Mallah, Kewat), who plant seed nuts and manage the crop.

Food and nutrition. The white, starchy kernel is highly nutritious and gluten-free.

  • Raw consumption: Eaten fresh when harvested in autumn; crisp and sweet.
  • Boiled consumption: Boiled and sold as a popular street snack.
  • Flour (Singhare ka atta): Dried kernels are ground into a fine flour. Since it is not a true grain, it is extensively used to make puris and halwa during Hindu fasting periods (vrat like Navratri).

Cultivated in seasonal and perennial village ponds throughout Basti district. The green mosaic pattern of rosettes covers entire ponds from July to November. During harvest season (September–November), farmers and fishermen in small wooden boats (dongi) or floating on large inflated truck tire tubes can be seen gathering the thorny nuts from beneath the leaves.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical and warm-temperate regions of Asia and Africa, including Pakistan, India, Nepal, Bangladesh, China, Southeast Asia, and tropical Africa.

In India: Widely distributed and cultivated in freshwater ponds and lakes throughout the northern plains, central plateau, and parts of southern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant and cultivated extensively in almost all districts, particularly in eastern UP due to the high density of natural wetlands and ponds.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common across rural areas, grown in almost every village pond suitable for aquaculture.

Range notes: No significant range changes documented.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. William Roxburgh described the species as Trapa bispinosa in 1815 in his catalog Hortus Bengalensis (p. 11), with a detailed description published in Flora Indica (Vol. 1, p. 443) in 1820. The type locality is Bengal, India. The genus name Trapa is derived from the Latin calcitrapa (caltrop), referring to the thorny fruit. The species epithet bispinosa refers to the two spines of the fruit.

Subspecies. Often treated as a variety of the European species under Trapa natans var. bispinosa in global databases, but recognized as a distinct species Trapa bispinosa in Indian regional floras.

  • UP form: Nominate Trapa bispinosa Roxb.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Myrtales, family Lythraceae. It was historically placed in its own family, Trapaceae, but recent molecular phylogenetics (e.g., Graham et al., 2005) nested the genus Trapa within Lythraceae.

Phylogenetic notes. The genus Trapa represents a distinct aquatic lineage characterized by highly modified heterophylly and starchy, woody fruits. It is closely related to Lythrum.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed by the IUCN Red List (Not Evaluated). However, the species is highly abundant, extensively cultivated, and secure across its native range in South Asia.
