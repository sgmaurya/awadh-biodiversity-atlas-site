क्राउन्ड रिवर कछुआ / Crowned River Turtle

Hardella thurjii (Gray, 1831) — Geoemydidae

क्राउन्ड रिवर कछुआ — भारत का सबसे बड़ा ताज़े पानी का कछुआ, जो बड़ी नदियों और जलाशयों में रहता है। इसका नाम इसके सिर पर एक विशिष्ट मुकुट जैसी संरचना (cranial crown) के कारण "Crowned" है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की घाघरा नदी और अन्य बड़े जलस्रोतों में यह दुर्लभ है। बहुत बड़ा और शक्तिशाली कछुआ है, जो मछलियों, जलीय पौधों और जानवरों को खाता है। यह कछुआ अब संकट में है क्योंकि बहुत से लोग इसका शिकार करते हैं।

Field tip: India's largest freshwater turtle with a distinctive crowned head and large robust shell. Found in major rivers. Any sighting in eastern UP should be carefully documented and reported to conservation authorities, as this species is becoming rare.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements from Gangetic rivers):

MeasurementRange
Carapace length250–350 mm (can exceed 400 mm)
Carapace width200–280 mm
Plastron length230–320 mm
Weight5–15 kg (exceptionally to 25 kg)

Carapace: Large and oval, distinctly flattened. The color is brown to olive-brown, sometimes with darker scutes. The carapace may show subtle ridges but is generally smooth with distinct growth rings. The shell is robust and relatively smooth, without pronounced keels.

Plastron: Yellowish, cream, or pale, often with darker markings or lines along the seams. The plastron is smaller and narrower than the carapace. There is a pronounced plastron-carapace hinge.

Head and neck: The most distinctive feature is the very large head with a prominent, raised, crown-like bony structure on the top (hence "Crowned"). The snout is moderately pointed. The head is often marked with yellow stripes or spots on a dark background, particularly on the cheeks and neck. Eyes are laterally positioned.

Limbs: Robust and powerful. Front limbs have five claws; hind limbs have four claws. Webbing is extensive, extending to the claws. Feet are very large for such a massive turtle.

Sexual dimorphism: Males are generally smaller than females and have longer tails, more concave plastrons, and larger preanal pores.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Aquatic specialist of large rivers. Hardella thurjii is adapted for life in large flowing rivers and deep water bodies.

  • Habitat preference: Inhabits large rivers, major streams, lakes, and reservoirs, particularly in areas with sandy banks and islands. Prefers deeper water and larger river systems than smaller turtle species.
  • Aquatic activity: Powerful and agile swimmer despite large size. Uses synchronized limb movements and can dive to considerable depths. Strong migratory behaviour, moving between suitable feeding and nesting areas.
  • Basking: Regularly basks on sandy banks, islands, rocks, and logs. Uses basking for thermoregulation and shell maintenance. Often found in large groups basking in early morning.
  • Feeding: Omnivorous forager; feeds on aquatic vegetation, fallen fruits and seeds, small fish, fish eggs, aquatic invertebrates, and carrion. Powerful jaw capable of crushing hard shells.
  • Defensive behaviour: Generally shy and wary of humans. Dives rapidly into deep water when disturbed. Bites only if severely provoked or handled roughly.
  • Migration: Undertakes seasonal migrations between feeding and nesting areas, sometimes travelling considerable distances.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Omnivorous diet:

  • Plant material: Aquatic vegetation, algae, fallen fruits (particularly Ficus seeds), seeds
  • Animal prey: Small to medium fish, fish eggs, crustaceans, aquatic insects, mollusks, carrion
  • Feeding method: Forages on bottom and in vegetation; crushes hard shells and seeds with powerful jaws

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Predatory birds: Eagles hunt juveniles and occasionally adults
  • Snakes: Large snakes may consume eggs
  • Mammals: Jackals, hyenas, and wild boar prey on nesting females and eggs
  • Large reptiles: Gharials and large monitors may consume eggs and hatchlings
  • Humans: Hunted for meat; major threat to survival

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Ecological importance. The Crowned River Turtle plays an important role in large river ecosystems as an omnivorous forager and as a prey species for large predators. It is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule I — highest protection) but faces severe threats from poaching for meat, eggs, and shell. The species is now Vulnerable due to population declines across its range. Conservation of this species depends on protection of river habitats and enforcement of hunting bans.

Expected in the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems, particularly in deeper sections with sandy banks suitable for nesting. GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain. The species is now extremely rare in eastern UP due to over-hunting. Search along large river systems, particularly in areas with sandy banks and river islands. Any sighting in eastern UP is likely of major conservation importance. The first documented record should be photographed and reported immediately to state wildlife authorities.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Endemic to the Indian subcontinent; found across peninsular India and the Gangetic plains.

In India: Historically widespread in all major river systems; now fragmented into isolated populations.

In Uttar Pradesh: Historically common in the Ghaghra, Rapti, Gomti, and Yamuna river systems; now extremely rare with few confirmed sightings.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Status highly uncertain; very rare, and its present status here is unknown. Any confirmed sighting would be of major conservation importance.

Field rule: India's largest freshwater turtle with a distinctive crowned head is the Crowned River Turtle. Any large turtle in the Ghaghra river with a prominent head crown is likely this species.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by John Edward Gray in 1831 as Testudo thurjii based on Indian specimens from the Ganges river. Type locality is the Ganges River, India. The specific name honours the Thar of Sind (Sindhian ruler). It was later placed in the monotypic genus Hardella Blyth (1856).

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Testudines, family Geoemydidae (Asian turtles). The genus Hardella contains only this single species, representing a distinct lineage.

Phylogenetic notes. Hardella thurjii represents a derived, large-bodied form within Geoemydidae, adapted for large river systems and long-distance migration.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Vulnerable (VU) by the IUCN Turtle Specialist Group due to significant population declines, restricted range, and ongoing threats from poaching. Protected under the Act (highest protection) of the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972.
