गर्दन ऐंठ / Eurasian Wryneck

Jynx torquilla Linnaeus, 1758 — Picidae

गर्दन ऐंठ — उत्तर से आने वाली एक अजीब किस्म की चिड़िया जो सर्दियों में पूर्वी यूपी के खुले मैदानों में दिखती है। इसकी गर्दन को 180 डिग्री तक घुमाने की अद्भुत क्षमता इसका सबसे बड़ा विशेषता है — शिकारी से बचने के लिए इसकी गर्दन पीछे की ओर मुड़ जाती है। रंग भूरा और धूसर — पत्ते और पत्थर जैसा छद्मवेशी। चोंच काँटे जैसी, लंबी जीभ — चीटियों के शिकार के लिए। सीधी गड़ी बिल में घोंसला नहीं बनाता, खोखले तने के किसी सूराख़ में घोंसला बनाता है। इसका नाम "wryneck" (ऐंठी गर्दन) सार्थक है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall, 12–13 cm; 27–43 g; sparrow-sized but with longer tail
PlumageCryptic brown and grey, heavily barred and mottled; pale underparts with brown streaks
Head patternBrown crown with thin dark line down centre; pale supercilium; brown ear-coverts
BillSmall, straight, slightly compressed, adapted for probing
TailRelatively long, brown with barring; used for balance on ground
GaitWalks upright on ground, often freezing when alarmed
BehaviourSolitary or in pairs; secretive; often found on ground among grass

Field tip: A cryptic, small, ground-loving bird with heavily mottled brown and grey plumage that blends in perfectly with rocky or sparsely vegetated ground. Look for it on the ground, probing for ants. Its ability to twist its head dramatically when alarmed is diagnostic. Unlike woodpeckers, it does not climb trees.

Body: Compact, squat posture on ground; short legs suited for walking. Head slightly disproportionate; neck long and highly mobile (unique among woodpeckers).

Plumage: The entire plumage is a complex cryptic pattern of browns, greys, and buffs, heavily barred and mottled throughout. Crown brown with a thin dark median line. Back brown, finely vermiculated (worm-like patterns) with darker barring. Underparts pale buff to whitish, streaked with brown. Tail brown, barred with pale bands.

Bare parts: Bill small (12–15 mm), straight, slightly compressed. Eyes dark. Legs yellowish-grey or pale blue-grey.

Tongue: Very long, extensible to 4–5 cm, barbed at tip for capturing ants; stored in an enlarged salivary gland in the skull.

Vocalisations

The Eurasian Wryneck is usually silent on the wintering grounds in India but may give occasional quiet calls.

  1. Call in breeding range: A loud, harsh, repeated kee-kee-kee-kee or ki-i-i-i, given from a perch; carries well.
  2. Distress call: A faint, grating churr when alarmed.
  3. Ground behaviour: Generally silent while feeding and walking.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Specialist ant-feeder: The Eurasian Wryneck is one of the most specialized ant-eating birds in Eurasia. It forages almost entirely on the ground, flipping over leaves and probing in soil to expose ants and their larvae. A single bird may consume hundreds of ants per day.

Ant species consumed: Targets various ground-nesting ant species (Lasius, Formica, Pheidole). The bird appears to detect ants by their presence and trail movements rather than by visual search.

Ground insect control: Through high ant consumption, wrynecks provide minor but real pest suppression in agricultural regions, as some ant species farm sap-feeding insects (scale insects, mealybugs).

Cryptic nester: Unlike other woodpeckers, the wryneck does not excavate its own nest holes. It uses pre-existing cavities (abandoned holes of other woodpeckers, tree crevices, or nest boxes). This reduces competition with hole-nesting predators and reduces energetic cost of breeding.

Migration: Nocturnal migrant; winters across a broad front from North Africa through the Middle East to South Asia. Published sources note that in Uttar Pradesh the species is strictly a winter visitor with no breeding records.

Lifespan: Up to 10–15 years in the wild (banded individuals).

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Ant species:

  • Ground-nesting ant genera: Lasius, Formica, Pheidole, Tapinoma
  • Ant larvae and pupae preferred over workers

Other arthropods (minor):

  • Termites (if encountered)
  • Small beetles
  • Spiders (occasionally)

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Shikra (Accipiter badius) — primary aerial predator in open habitats
  • Feral cats — ambush on ground
  • Indian Rat Snake (Ptyas mucosa) — preys on eggs and young in breeding grounds
  • Crows and drongos — mobbing and potential nest predation in breeding grounds

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: The Eurasian Wryneck is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, Schedule IV. Hunting is prohibited. The species is highly sensitive to persecution and habitat disturbance during breeding.

Pest suppression: While ant-eating provides minor insect suppression, the wryneck is not deliberately conserved for this purpose. Its ecological role is incidental to its conservation value as a migratory bird.

Cultural significance: The genus name Jynx derives from Greek mythology (a female enchantress), referring to the bird's remarkable ability to twist its head and neck. The common name "wryneck" directly translates this extraordinary behaviour. In Chinese and Japanese traditions, this bird is considered auspicious, representing longevity and good fortune.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

The Eurasian Wryneck is to be expected on range grounds as a winter visitor (October to March) in open scrubland, sparsely vegetated field margins, and thorn forest edges throughout Basti district. According to published range and migration data in Ali & Ripley (1987) and Grimmett et al. (2011), the species should be present during the winter months. However, it is not documented in any of the three recent local surveys (Bakhira, Parvati Arga, Samda). The first local record would require a photograph showing the cryptic mottled plumage, taken on the ground or in open habitat with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds throughout temperate Europe and Asia from the British Isles east to Japan, and south to North Africa and the Middle East. Winters across sub-Saharan Africa and southern Asia.

In India: Winter visitor across northern and central India; recorded from the Himalayan foothills south to the Deccan.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor across all districts; published handbooks document it from October to March.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected based on published range and migration data, though not yet documented in the three recent avifaunal surveys.

Field rule: A small (12–13 cm), cryptic, heavily mottled brown and grey bird found on the ground in open scrubland, with the remarkable ability to twist its head dramatically when alarmed, feeding on ground ants — is the Eurasian Wryneck. Among Indian birds, no other species shows this distinctive head-twisting behavior.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Jynx torquilla was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th Edition, p. 112. Type locality: Sweden. The genus name Jynx is derived from Greek mythology (a female enchantress). The epithet torquilla is Latin meaning "twister," referring to the bird's remarkable neck-twisting ability.

Subspecies. Two subspecies are currently recognised:

SubspeciesAuthorityRangeNotes
J. t. torquilla(Linnaeus, 1758)Europe, North Africa, Asia Minor to Central AsiaEastern UP form — nominate form; breeds in Himalayas above 1,500 m
J. t. tschusii(Stresemann, 1930)Far East, East SiberiaNot occurring in India

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Piciformes, family Picidae (woodpeckers and wrynecks). Unlike true woodpeckers (Picidae proper), the wryneck does not excavate its own nest holes or drum on bark.

Phylogenetic notes. The genus Jynx is considered the most basal (primitive) genus within Picidae, differing from true woodpeckers in nest-site selection, foraging behaviour, and climbing ability.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) by the IUCN Red List. It has a large global range and stable population, though local declines in some regions are documented due to habitat loss and pesticide use reducing ant availability.
