पिलकिया / पीली खंजन / Western Yellow Wagtail

Motacilla flava Linnaeus, 1758 — Motacillidae

पीली खंजन — यूरोप और मध्य एशिया से सर्दियों में आने वाली एक छोटी और पीली चिड़िया। इसका सबसे विशिष्ट लक्षण है नर पक्षी का पीला सिर और काली पूंठ का पैटर्न, जो नस्लों और आयु के अनुसार बदलता है। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में सितंबर से मई तक यह खुली घास के मैदानों, कृषि क्षेत्रों और तालाबों के किनारों पर झुंड में दिखाई देती है। यह छोटे कीड़े खाती है और जमीन पर टहलते हुए भोजन खोजती है।

Field tip: A small, slender wagtail with distinctive yellow coloration on head and underparts. Males show more yellow than females; non-breeding birds are duller. The yellow throat is visible in both males and females. Always on the ground or in short vegetation, never in trees. Tail-wagging is constant.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 16–17 cm long; slightly larger than Grey Wagtail but with relatively shorter tail.

Male breeding plumage: Highly variable by subspecies and individual. Typically: head yellow, with black or olive markings on crown or nape depending on race. Back black or grey. Underparts bright yellow. Tail dark with white outer edges.

Female and non-breeding plumage: Duller overall. Head yellowish-green to olive. White or pale supercilium. Yellow throat and underparts duller than males. Back greenish-brown. Yellow limited to undertail-coverts and vent.

Bill: Fine, pointed, dark; specialized for catching small insects.

Legs and feet: Dark; slender; adapted for walking on open ground.

Tail: Characteristically dark, wagged constantly.

Sexes: Males much more colourful than females.

Juveniles: Similar to females but duller.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Call: A sharp, metallic tchup or chip, given as contact call.
  2. Flight call: A distinctive clip or tzip given in flight.
  3. Song: A brief warble, rarely heard outside breeding season.
हिंदी: तीक्ष्ण चिप जैसी आवाज़। झुंड में उड़ते समय क्लिप चिल्लाहट।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

Ground forager: Yellow Wagtails forage by walking and running on open ground, mudflats, and wet margins, picking insects from surfaces. They feed on:

  • Small flies and gnats
  • Small terrestrial insects
  • Aquatic insects on mudflats
  • Small spiders
  • Occasionally small seeds

Social behaviour: Gregarious, particularly in winter; form flocks of 10–50 or more birds on suitable grasslands and mudflats. Males and females often segregate during the day.

Feeding time: Active throughout the day, foraging continuously.

Roost behaviour: Nocturnal roosting in flocks on the ground in tall grass or reeds, or in low trees and shrubs.

Predators: Hooded crows, other small raptors, and occasionally larger birds may take individuals.

Habitat preference: Highly dependent on open grasslands and wet margins; rarely found in forests or dense vegetation.

हिंदी: झुंड में रहने वाली चिड़िया। घास के मैदान और दलदल पर छोटे कीड़े खाता है। रात को झुंड में सोता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Breeds in Europe and Central Asia (April–August); returns to eastern UP from September and remains through May.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in September; peak numbers in January–March; most depart by April–May.

Courtship: Takes place on breeding grounds. Males perform display flights and sing.

Moult: Partial moult occurs during winter. Breeding plumage acquired before return to breeding grounds.

Flocking behavior: Forms large flocks during migration and wintering.

हिंदी: सितंबर में आता है, मई तक रहता है। यूरोप में प्रजनन करता है। सर्दियों में बड़े झुंड में रहता है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: The Western Yellow Wagtail is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule IV, amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Grassland indicator: As a specialist of open grasslands, the Yellow Wagtail indicates the presence of expansive, undisturbed grassland habitats.

Pest control: Feeds on small insects, many of which might be agricultural pests, though the impact is minimal.

Agricultural habitat: Often found in harvested rice fields and stubble areas where insects are abundant.

Historical records: Not mentioned in Mughal hunting records; too small to be of sporting interest.

Cultural value: Recognized by birdwatchers and villagers as a common and charming winter visitor; local names reflect its yellow coloration.

हिंदी: कानून से पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

No published records from the local surveys (Bakhira, Parvati Arga, or Ayodhya wetlands surveys), though the species is expected on range grounds as a winter visitor to suitable habitats.

Habitat preference: Open grasslands, rice fields, and mudflats where ground foraging is possible.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from European and Central Asian breeding grounds have moved south.

Nearest published record: The species is mentioned in Handbook of the Birds of India and Pakistan as a winter visitor to the Gangetic plains, including Uttar Pradesh.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds from Iceland and northwestern Europe to Japan. Winters across Africa, Mediterranean, and Asia. Polytypic with several subspecies, including M. f. flava (Western Yellow Wagtail).

In India: Distributed as a winter visitor on grasslands and cultivated areas, particularly in northern and eastern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to grasslands, rice fields, and wetland margins, particularly in the eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a winter visitor to grasslands and cultivated areas, though not yet documented in the published local surveys.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in the Systema Naturae (10th edition), as Motacilla flava. The genus Motacilla has remained stable.

Subspecies. Polytypic with several subspecies recognized. The subspecies wintering in India include M. f. flava (Western), M. f. feldegg (Black-headed), and occasionally other subspecies. They form a complex of closely related populations with significant plumage variation.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Motacillidae (wagtails and pipits). The genus Motacilla contains the wagtails.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to Grey Wagtail (M. cinerea), White Wagtail (M. alba), and other Motacilla species. Molecular studies confirm its position within the genus.

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. Populations are stable and widely distributed across Europe and Asia. Highly adaptable to grassland and agricultural habitats. In India, protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (Schedule IV).
