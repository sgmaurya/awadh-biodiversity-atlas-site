चिचिंडा / Snake Gourd

Trichosanthes cucumerina L. — Cucurbitaceae

चिचिंडा — एक महत्वपूर्ण ग्रीष्मकालीन सब्जी फसल है जो पूरे भारत में उगाई जाती है। यह एक लंबी बेल है जो 2–4 मीटर तक फैलती है, बड़ी पत्तियाँ और नाज़ुक, लंबी, सांप जैसी (60–90 सेमी), हरी, पतली फलियाँ होती हैं जो एक सिरे पर तीव्र बिंदु के साथ होती हैं। सफ़ेद, सुगंधित फूल रात में खिलते हैं। फल हरी सब्जी के रूप में कोमल अवस्था में काटे जाते हैं। स्थानीय लोग इसे मसालेदार सब्जी बनाते हैं। इसका स्वाद हल्का और कोमल होता है। फल पकने पर नारंगी-लाल हो जाते हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
FormClimbing annual vine, 2–4 m long, with prominent tendrils and large lobed leaves
LeavesAlternate, deeply lobed (palmate), 8–15 cm diameter, coarse texture, prominent veins
FlowersUnisexual, white with delicate fringed margins, 1–2 cm across; male flowers in clusters, female flowers solitary; fragrant, opening at night
FruitExtremely long, thin, curved, snake-like, 60–90 cm long, 2–3 cm diameter; ridged; bright green when young
Mature fruitOrange-red when ripe; bitter; contains red arils; not used as food

Field tip: A climbing vine with large lobed leaves, night-blooming fragrant white flowers, and extraordinarily long, thin, snake-like green fruits that are harvested as immature vegetables.

Tendril: Thin, unbranched, curling; typical of Cucurbitaceae.

Root system: Taproot with extensive lateral roots in shallow soil layer.

Associated Species / Interactions सहजीवी संबंध

  • Night pollinator: Flowers are pollinated by nocturnal insects and moths attracted by fragrance
  • Insect host: Hosts various beetles and caterpillars
  • Bird seed dispersal: Ripe fruits are eaten by birds; seeds germinate readily
  • Trellis crop: Commonly trained on poles and trellises for vertical farming

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Sowing: Sown in May–July in warm season
  • Germination: Emerges within 5–7 days with warmth
  • Vegetative growth: Rapid vine growth in June–July; leaves expand; tendrils develop
  • Flowering: Night-blooming flowers appear in June–August
  • Fruit development: Young fruits develop rapidly; mature in 15–20 days
  • Harvest: Immature green fruits are harvested in July–October for vegetable use

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Powdery mildew: Podosphaera xanthii causes white powder on leaves
  • Downy mildew: Pseudoperonospora cubensis causes yellowing and leaf drop in wet seasons
  • Spotted beetles: Aulacophora species cause holes in leaves and fruits
  • Red spider mite: Infestation causes yellowing and early senescence

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Summer vegetable crop.

  • Vegetable preparation: Young tender fruits are cut into pieces and cooked as a curry with onions, tomatoes, and spices; mild flavor and tender texture
  • Nutritional value: Low in calories; rich in fiber, vitamins A and C, and potassium
  • Taste: Mild, delicate vegetable; preferred by children
  • Cultivars: Hira, Suruchi, and local landraces
  • Market demand: Popular in summer markets across North India

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use

  • Summer vegetable: Traditional summer dish in North Indian cuisine
  • Digestive aid: Considered cooling and digestive in summer diet
  • Garden cultivation: Commonly grown in home gardens for fresh vegetable supply

Trichosanthes cucumerina is expected as a summer vegetable crop in Basti district. Look for it in gardens and fields from May through October, recognizable by long snake-like green fruits on climbing vines. The first local record would be a photograph with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to South Asia; now cultivated in India and Southeast Asia.

In India: Widely cultivated throughout the Indo-Gangetic plain and South India as a summer vegetable.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in all districts as a summer and monsoon vegetable crop.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Cultivated resident; very common in home gardens and fields.

Field rule: An extremely long, thin, snake-like, smooth, dark green fruit (60–90 cm) on a climbing vine with large lobed leaves and night-blooming white flowers distinguishes snake gourd.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus described Snake Gourd as Trichosanthes cucumerina in 1753 in Species Plantarum. The genus name Trichosanthes is from Greek trichos (hair) and anthos (flower), referring to the fringed flower margins. The specific epithet cucumerina means "cucumber-like."

Subspecies. Monotypic; no recognized subspecies.

Family and order placement. Order Cucurbitales, family Cucurbitaceae (gourd family).

Phylogenetic notes. Trichosanthes cucumerina is native to South and Southeast Asia; cultivated extensively throughout Indian agriculture.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a widely cultivated crop, it faces no conservation threats.
