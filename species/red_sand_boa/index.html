दोमुँहा / Red Sand Boa

Eryx johnii (Russell, 1801) — Boidae

दोमुँहा (लाल बालू का साँप / Dumeril's Boa) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश की रेतीली मिट्टियों का एक छोटा-बड़ा, गहरा लाल-भूरा या काला साँप, जिसकी सबसे अजीब खासियत है इसकी छोटी, कुंद (blunt) और चपटी पूंछ, जो दिखने में बिल्कुल इसके सिर जैसी लगती है — इसीलिए इसे 'दो-मुँहा' (two-headed) कहते हैं। कहानी है कि यह साँप छह महीने सिर की तरफ़ से चलता है और छह महीने पूंछ की तरफ़ से, जो सरासर अंधविश्वास है। यह साँप बिल्कुल जहर-रहित है, एक तंग गले वाला पकड़ने वाला (constrictor) है जो छोटे चूहों, छिपकलियों और कीड़ों को अपनी कुंडली में दबाकर शिकार करता है। रेतीली नदी के किनारों, सूखी ज़मीन के खेतों और बंजर जमीनों में छिपा रहता है। भारतीय वन्यजीव संरक्षण अधिनियम (WPA) की अनुसूची II के तहत कानूनी सुरक्षा पाता है। अंतरराष्ट्रीय अवैध व्यापार और तांत्रिक कारणों से तस्करी का शिकार है।

Non-Venomous Status and Conservation Warning गैर-विषैला होने की पुष्टि और चेतावनी

[!WARNING]
दोमुँहा (लाल बालू boa) पूरी तरह से बिना जहर का होता है। इसके काटने से कोई जहर नहीं फैलता है। हालांकि, इसके लाल-भूरे या काले रंग के कारण ग्रामीण अक्सर इसे रसेल वाइपर (Russell's Viper, REP-0010) समझ लेते हैं और डर के मारे इसे मार डालते हैं।
इसके अलावा, दोमुँहा की गुप्त रूप से तांत्रिक और धार्मिक अनुष्ठानों (जहाँ अंधविश्वास के कारण इसे "शक्ति प्राप्त" माना जाता है) और अंतरराष्ट्रीय अवैध पालतू जानवरों के व्यापार (exotic pet trade) के लिए भारी तस्करी की जाती है। वन्यजीव (संरक्षण) अधिनियम के तहत इसे पकड़ना, रखना या बेचना एक दंडनीय अपराध है। इस निरीह और शांत जीव को मारना पूरी तरह गैर-कानूनी है।
लोकल कहानियाँ: अंधविश्वास है कि यह "दोहरे सिर वाला" (two-headed) साँप है, या छह महीने सिर की तरफ़ से और छह महीने पूंछ की तरफ़ से चलता है। ये सब झूठ हैं — यह साँप एक सिर वाला है, साधारण जानवर है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size & BuildSmall to medium (75–100 cm), stout, compact body with little taper from body to tail
TailVery short, flat, blunt, rounded at the tip, closely resembling the head
Color & PatternReddish-brown, orange-brown, or deep blackish-brown dorsally; uniform or with faint darker bands; belly lighter
ScalesSmooth to slightly keeled; those on the tail are notably keeled and rough
HeadSmall, flat, wedge-shaped, not distinct from neck; eyes small, positioned dorsolaterally
BehaviourCryptic; attempts to bury itself in loose soil when disturbed; raises tail defensively

Field tip: Look in sandy soils, ploughed fields, and dry river margins. A small-to-medium, uniformly reddish or blackish stout snake that tries to bury itself backward when uncovered is a red sand boa. The tail is very short and blunt, mimicking the head. It is non-venomous and protected by law.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: छोटा-बड़ा, लाल-भूरा या काला, भारी शरीर, बिल्कुल रेत में दबा-दबा सा दिखता है। सिर गोल, पूंछ भी गोल और सिर जैसी छोटी। पूरी तरह बिना जहर का।

Head and Snout: The head is small, flattened, and wedge-shaped, adapted for burrowing through sand. The snout is blunt and rounded. Eyes are small, with vertical pupils, positioned high on the head.

Body Scales: Smooth on the anterior body, becoming keeled and rougher toward the tail. The keeled tail is used for anchoring while burrowing or constricting prey.

Coloration: Dorsal surface is typically reddish-brown, russet, orange-brown, or deep blackish-brown. Some individuals show faint darker bands or blotches. The ventral surface is pale cream or white, sometimes with darker markings.

Sexual dimorphism: Females are slightly larger than males.

हिंदी: शरीर मजबूत, लाल-भूरा। सिर गोल-कुंद, रेत में दबाने के लिए बना। पूंछ गोल, कुंद, सिर जितनी छोटी।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Fossorial lifestyle. The red sand boa is highly specialized for life in loose sandy or dry soils. It spends most of the day buried just below the surface, with only its eyes exposed, waiting in ambush. When a small rodent, lizard, or other vertebrate approaches, it strikes rapidly with powerful jaw muscles and kills by constriction.

Tail mimicry defense. When threatened, the snake coils its body in a tight ball, hiding its true head beneath its coils and raising its blunt tail. It moves the tail slowly, mimicking a striking head to confuse predators.

Nocturnal forager. Emerges to hunt in the evening and early morning hours, when small mammals and reptiles are active.

Reproduction. Ovoviviparous (produces live young). Females give birth to 4–15 young snakes after a gestation period of several months.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Small rodents (House Mouse, Indian Gerbil)
  • Lizards, especially ground-dwelling species
  • Small snakes, including juvenile kraits and rat snakes
  • Occasionally insects and small arthropods

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

Adults: Relatively few due to cryptic, burrowing lifestyle.

Juveniles and small individuals:

  • Monitor lizards
  • Large snakes (Indian Python, king cobra)
  • Raptors (Black Kite, Common Kestrel)
  • Jackals and mongoose

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Rodent control. The red sand boa is a beneficial species in agricultural areas, controlling rodent pests like the Indian Gerbil and house mice. Its presence in fields reduces crop damage.

Superstition and harm. The red sand boa faces heavy persecution due to:

  • Misidentification: Often mistaken for the venomous Russell's Viper, leading to unnecessary killing.
  • Folk beliefs: The "two-headed snake" mythology causes fear and harm.
  • Illegal trade: Traded heavily in the illegal exotic pet trade and for use in superstitious rituals, despite legal protection.

Legal protection. Listed in Schedule II of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972. Catching, keeping, or selling this species is a criminal offense.

Conservation status. Globally Least Concern, but locally declining due to habitat loss (conversion of sandy soils to irrigated agriculture) and illegal collection.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Red sand boas are expected in sandy tracts along the Ghaghra river floodplain in Basti district, particularly in areas with loose, well-drained sandy soils. They are most likely to be found during soil ploughing in March–April and again in August–September, when they are more active near the soil surface. Sandy river margins and cultivated sandy fields should be monitored. The first local record should document location (field name, block, sandy tract name), date, time of day, substrate type, and photograph. Report all sightings to the atlas — this is a protected, harmless, and beneficial species that needs documentation and protection.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: South Asia — India, Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Myanmar.

In India: Distributed widely in arid and semi-arid sandy plains of northern, central, and western India. Particularly common in Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, and Haryana.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in sandy tracts and river floodplains, especially along the Ganges, Yamuna, and Ghaghra river systems.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in sandy soils along the Ghaghra river; distribution poorly documented.

Field rule: A small-to-medium, uniformly reddish or blackish stout snake (75–100 cm) with a blunt, head-like tail in loose sandy soils is a red sand boa. It is harmless, protected by law, and beneficial for rodent control. Do not harm it.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Patrick Russell in 1801 in An Account of Indian Serpents collected on the Coromandel Coast. The epithet johnii honors John, Russell's associate.

Subspecies. Eryx johnii johnii — the nominate subspecies found in India. Monotypic; no subspecies are currently recognized.

Family and order placement. Boidae, the boas and pythons; order Squamata, suborder Serpentes.

Phylogenetic notes. Eryx is a genus of sand and ground boas found across the Old World. The red sand boa is specialized for fossorial life in arid habitats and represents an adaptation to sandy soils distinct from the common sand boa.

IUCN status rationale. Listed as Least Concern due to its wide range and stable population across suitable habitats. However, local declines occur due to habitat conversion and illegal collection for the exotic pet trade.
