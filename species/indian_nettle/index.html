कुप्पी / Indian Nettle

Acalypha indica L. — Euphorbiaceae

कुप्पी (भारतीय बिच्छू घास) — एक छोटा, बुरी तरह शाखित खरपतवार जो बगीचों, खेतों और खाली ज़मीन पर उगता है। पत्तियों पर बारीक बाल होते हैं जो छूने में खुजली पैदा कर सकते हैं, इसीलिए हिंदी में इसे कुप्पी कहते हैं। पारंपरिक आयुर्वेदिक चिकित्सा में इसका उपयोग त्वचा संबंधी समस्याओं, श्वास रोग और पाचन विकारों के लिए किया जाता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Plant habitAnnual herb, 20–60 cm, bushy, branching from base
LeavesOvate to oblong, 4–10 cm, coarsely toothed, hairy on both surfaces
FlowersMales in short racemes; females in longer catkin-like spikes, small and inconspicuous
StemsHerbaceous, green, hairy, angular
FruitsSmall capsules with distinctive lobed appearance

Field tip: In a disturbed area or vegetable garden, look for a bushy herb with broadly toothed, hairy leaves. Brush the leaf and it may cause a mild irritation. The flowers are small and easily overlooked.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: छोटा, हरा-भरा पौधा, पत्तियों पर पूरी सतह बालों से ढकी हुई, खेत और बाग़ों में आम।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Plant height20–60 cm
Leaf length4–10 cm
Leaf width2–6 cm
Flower spike length1–2.5 cm

Stem and branches: Succulent, hairy, green or purplish, much branched.

Leaves: Simple, alternate, ovate-oblong, margins coarsely serrate, base broadly cuneate, both surfaces clothed with fine hairs.

Flowers: Monoecious; male flowers in small axillary racemes; female flowers in longer, stalked axillary spikes.

Fruits: Small capsules, 4–5 mm across, with three lobes; seeds smooth and shining.

हिंदी: पूरा पौधा बालों से ढका, बहुत शाखें, पत्तियों की कटी-फटी किनारियाँ।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A common annual herb of disturbed ground and cultivated areas, thriving in both moist and drier situations. It germinates throughout the warm season and establishes quickly in neglected gardens and fallow fields. The fine hairs on the leaves and stem are irritant to sensitive skin, hence the local name "nettle". The plant is prolific in seed production, making it a persistent weed in agricultural systems.

हिंदी: गर्म मौसम में उगता है, बहुत तेज़ी से बढ़ता है, बहुत बीज देता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Seeds are persistent in the soil seed bank, germinating whenever conditions are favorable.

हिंदी: गर्मी से शुरू होकर सर्दी तक चलता है, सबसे ज़्यादा बड़ता है मई-अगस्त में।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Field companions: Spiny Amaranth, Black Nightshade, cholpa in disturbed cultivated ground
  • Traditional associations: used in Ayurvedic formulations alongside Brahmi and False Daisy

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

Occasionally attacked by red spider mites and leaf-eating caterpillars during the monsoon, but rarely to the point of serious damage. The hairy leaves provide some natural protection from insect feeding.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Agricultural impact. A common weed in vegetable gardens, nurseries, and fallow land. It competes with crops for water and nutrients. Control is by hand-pulling or hoeing. Does not tolerate regular cropping or shading.

Ethnobotanical use (documented traditional knowledge; not medical advice). In Ayurvedic practice, the whole plant is dried and used in decoctions for respiratory complaints, skin diseases, and digestive disorders. It is listed in classical Ayurvedic texts as a plant with cooling and expectorant properties. The plant is also used in traditional folk medicine across South and Southeast Asia.

Today. Occasionally used in Ayurvedic preparations; some commercial interest for phytochemical research.

हिंदी: किसानों को खरपतवार के रूप में परेशानी देता है। आयुर्वेद में श्वास, त्वचा और पाचन के लिए इस्तेमाल होता है।

GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain. To be expected in disturbed ground, vegetable gardens, and field margins throughout the warm months (May to November). Most abundant after monsoon rainfall.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to tropical Asia; widespread across the Indian subcontinent and into Southeast Asia.

In India: Found from the Indo-Gangetic plain to peninsular India and the Deccan.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread throughout both upland and alluvial plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Common in disturbed ground, gardens, and field margins.

Field rule: A bushy herb with broadly toothed, densely hairy leaves that may cause mild itching, and very small flowers in spikes is Acalypha indica.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum, ed. 2. The name Acalypha is from the Greek akakalesiphe, an ancient name for a plant.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Euphorbiaceae (the spurge family), order Malpighiales. A genus of about 450 species found across tropical regions.

Phylogenetic notes. Acalypha is well-established within Euphorbiaceae; Asian species form a distinct clade.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread as a weedy species; no conservation concern.
