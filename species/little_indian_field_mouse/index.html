खेत की चुहिया / Little Indian Field Mouse

Mus booduga Gray, 1837 — Muridae

खेत की चुहिया — हल के पीछे निकली छोटी-सी चुहिया जिसके कान और पूँछ बिल्कुल बड़ी चुहिया जैसी नहीं। बाल कड़े, काँटेदार। गन्ने और धान के खेत में बिल बनाती है और अनाज, बीज, कीड़े खाती है। बिल्ली और साँप इसका शिकार करते हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में हर खेत और बाग़ में यह है, पर किसान इसे पहचानते नहीं हैं — सब चुहिया को एक समझते हैं।

Field tip: The smallest mouse in agricultural fields. If a mouse is found active by day in a ploughed field, has a distinctly spiny coat (rough to touch), and a naked tail equal to body length, it is Mus booduga. Much smaller than house rats; the stiff spiny hairs are diagnostic.

हिंदी: सबसे छोटी चुहिया, खेत में। बाल कड़े। दिन में दिखती है। पूँछ नंगी।

Morphology आकारिकी

Fur: Coarse and spiny; guard hairs stiff and sharp to the touch, not soft like house mouse. Upper body grey-brown with a speckled appearance; underparts and feet white or grey.

Head: Small, with moderately large rounded ears. Eyes small and dark. Whiskers (vibrissae) well-developed.

Tail: Long, equal to or slightly longer than head-body length; naked, scaled, non-prehensile.

Feet: Small, five digits on hind foot; claws short and sharp.

Sexual dimorphism: Minimal; sexes similar in appearance.

हिंदी: बहुत छोटी। बाल कड़े, खुरदुरे। पूँछ लंबी, शरीर जितनी।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Activity pattern: Diurnal and crepuscular; active throughout the day and early evening, unusual for rodents. Often observed foraging in daylight in ploughed fields.

Burrow dwelling: Constructs simple burrow systems in field soil, with 1–3 entrance holes. Burrows are shallow (15–30 cm deep) and located near field margins or between crop rows. Burrows are used for shelter and nesting.

Feeding ecology: Primarily granivorous (seed-eater), consuming seeds of rice, wheat, millets, and wild grasses. Also takes emerging seedlings, insects, and green plant material. Forages on soil surface and within field litter.

Population dynamics: Populations fluctuate seasonally; peak abundance in post-harvest months (November–February) when spilled grain is abundant. Numbers decline during monsoon when fields are flooded and wet.

Social structure: Solitary or in small family groups; not gregarious. Male and female may share burrow system during breeding.

Predators: Natural predators include raptors (kestrels, eagles), snakes (cobras, kraits), and carnivores (fox, stray dogs, domestic cats).

हिंदी: दिन में सक्रिय, अकेले रहती है। छिछली बिल खोदती है। बीज खाती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Seeds: rice, wheat, millet, wild grass seeds
  • Seedlings: emerging crop seedlings
  • Insects: grasshoppers, beetle larvae, termites
  • Green plant material: young leaves and shoots
  • Fungi: occasionally mushrooms and fungal spores

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Kestrels (Falco tinnunculus), pallid harriers, buzzards
  • Snakes: Cobra, krait, rat snake
  • Carnivores: Fox, wild boar, domestic cat, dog
  • Parasites: Fleas, lice, internal helminths

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Seed predation: Mus booduga can consume seed at grain storage and in fields, but damage is typically minor compared to larger rodents. Post-harvest grain loss attributed to this species is generally less than 2–3% of yield in field surveys.

Field ecology role: Burrow construction aerates compacted soil; seed predation contributes to dispersal and natural selection of wild and cultivated plants. The species is part of field agroecology rather than a major pest.

Protected status: Under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule III), all wild mammals including rodents are protected from hunting. The species cannot be killed for control purposes; only preventive measures (field sanitation, grain storage) are legal.

Indicator species: High abundance of field mice indicates suitable habitat and suggests that larger mammalian predators (raptors, snakes) are present to control rodent populations naturally.

हिंदी: खेत की सफ़ाई से इसकी संख्या कम होती है। कानून से सुरक्षित है — मारा नहीं जा सकता। उल्लू और सांपों का भोजन है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Little Indian Field Mouse is to be expected in cultivated fields, fallow land, and grassland throughout Basti and Ayodhya districts. The species should be visible during daylight in ploughed fields, particularly in post-harvest months (November–February) when foraging is concentrated on spilled grain. A confirmed local record would require a trapped specimen, a clear photograph showing the characteristic spiny coat and small size, or a burrow system with identification of droppings and nesting material, with place and date documented.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Endemic to the Indian subcontinent; distributed from the Himalayan foothills through peninsular India.

In India: Found across most states; particularly abundant in agricultural regions of Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and parts of the Deccan.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread in agricultural regions, especially in the Indo-Gangetic plain.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected throughout cultivated and fallow land; common in rice, wheat and sugarcane fields.

Field rule: The smallest mouse in eastern UP fields, with distinctly spiny coarse fur, small size (fist-sized when held), and active in daylight, is Mus booduga. Any larger mouse is a different species.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Mus booduga was described by John Edward Gray in 1837 in the Proceedings of the Zoological Society of London. The type specimen is from India; the species epithet booduga is derived from a local South Indian vernacular name.

Subspecies. No subspecies are formally recognised; populations across the Indian subcontinent are treated as monotypic.

Family and order placement. Muridae (mouse and rat family), order Rodentia. Muridae is the largest family of living mammals.

Phylogenetic notes. Mus booduga is the sister species to Mus caroli and belongs to the genus Mus proper, distinct from Rattus (rats) by its smaller size and simpler molars.

IUCN status rationale. Classified as Least Concern (LC). The species is widely distributed and abundant; populations are stable.
