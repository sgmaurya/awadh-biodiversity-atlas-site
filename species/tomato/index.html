टमाटर / Tomato

Solanum lycopersicum L. — Solanaceae

टमाटर (तमातर / टोमैटो) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के खेतों और घरेलू बाड़ियों में शरद ऋतु (Rabi season) में उगाई जाने वाली एक अत्यंत लोकप्रिय, शाखाओं वाली और रसीली वार्षिक शाकीय बेल (spreading annual herb) है। यह मूल रूप से दक्षिण अमेरिका का निवासी है, जिसे 16वीं शताब्दी में पुर्तगालियों द्वारा भारत में पेश किया गया था। इसकी सबसे बड़ी पहचान इसके कटे-फटे रोएँदार पत्ते, छोटे पीले रंग के तारे जैसे फूल (yellow star-shaped flowers), और पकने पर गहरे चमकदार लाल होने वाले रसीले खट्टे फल हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में टमाटर का उपयोग प्रत्येक सब्जी (tarkari) को खट्टापन और गाढ़ा स्वाद देने, सलाद के रूप में खाने, और धनिया-हरी मिर्च के साथ पीसकर तीखी चटनी (tomato chutney) बनाने के लिए रोजाना किया जाता है। आलू, बैंगन और मिर्च की तरह यह भी सोलेनेसी (Solanaceae) परिवार का सदस्य है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Plant habitSpreading, scrambling annual herb up to 60–180 cm high, requiring stick supports (staking) to stand upright
LeavesAlternately arranged, deeply pinnately divided (15–30 cm long), covered with fine sticky glandular hairs
FlowersSmall (10–15 mm), bright yellow, star-like, hanging in small clusters of 3 to 12 along the stem
FruitA fleshy, juicy, globose or oval berry, turning from green to bright glossy red, yellow, or pink when ripe
OdorThe leaves and stems exude a strong, characteristic resinous smell when brushed or bruised

Field tip: Look in vegetable patches during December. Look for green, hairy vines tied to bamboo sticks. Smell the foliage; it has a strong green resinous scent. The bright yellow star-like flowers hang down next to clusters of round green and red tomatoes.

Stem and Branches: Stems are round, green, fleshy when young, covered in dense simple and glandular hairs that secrete an aromatic oil.

Foliage: Alternately arranged, compound leaves, with irregular lobed leaflets.

Flowers: Bisexual, 5-merous, arranged in lateral cymes. The green sepals are narrow and remain attached to the mature fruit base.

Fruit and Seeds: A 2-to-many-celled juicy berry containing flat, oval, hairy, light brown seeds suspended in a slippery gel.

Cultivation Calendar कृषि कैलेंडर

  • Sowing: Nursery beds are sown in October; seedlings are protected from heavy sun and monsoonal rains.
  • Transplantation: Seedlings are transplanted in November when they reach a height of 10–15 cm, spaced 45–60 cm apart.
  • Support & Care: Staking with bamboo poles is done in December to keep the heavy fruit clusters off the damp soil.
  • Harvesting: Begins in January, peaks in February–March, and finishes in April when the hot winds (Loo) start.

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Family Connection: Belongs to the nightshade family (Solanaceae) alongside Brinjal (FLR-0093) and Chilli (FLR-0095), sharing similar pests and soil nutrient requirements.
  • Pollinators: Self-fertile, but bumblebees and wind vibration greatly improve fruit set through buzz pollination.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Tomato Fruit Borer: Helicoverpa armigera caterpillars bore clean round holes into the ripening fruits, feeding on the inner pulp.
  • Alternaria Blight (Early Blight): Fungal disease causing target-like brown spots on the lower leaves, leading to defoliation.
  • Tomato Leaf Curl Virus: Transmitted by whiteflies, causing leaves to curl upward and become severely deformed.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use पारंपरिक और नृवंश文स्पति उपयोग

  1. Tamatar ki Meethi Chutney: Tomatoes are boiled, mashed, and cooked with jaggery (gur), ginger, and fennel seeds to make a sweet red chutney served at rural weddings and festivals.
  2. Digestive Appetizer: Raw green tomatoes are sliced, salted, and served with black pepper as a traditional appetizer to stimulate gastric juices before heavy meals.
  3. Skin Burn Relief: Fresh tomato pulp is applied topically over minor sunburns or kitchen scalds to cool the skin and reduce redness.
  4. Acidity Regulator: Consumed raw in winter salads to provide vitamin C and alkalize the digestive tract in traditional diet systems.

The primary winter cash crop for small farmers in the Basti district, regularly observed in the Harraiya, Vikramjot, and Basti blocks. Local markets are filled with fresh red tomatoes from January to March. Farmers state that staking tomatoes (danda chadhana) is essential; unstaked plants lose half their fruit crop to soil dampness and fungal rot during winter morning fogs.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the Andes region of South America; introduced globally and now cultivated universally in all temperate and tropical countries.

In India: One of the most widely grown vegetable crops across all states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated heavily in all agricultural plains.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Abundant in winter farms and homesteads.

Field rule: Spreading annual hairy herb with deeply divided leaves, small star-like yellow flowers, and juicy red or orange berries, is the Tomato.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus described the Tomato as Solanum lycopersicum in 1753 in his Species Plantarum (Vol. 1, p. 185). The specific name lycopersicum is derived from the Greek lykos (wolf) and persikon (peach), referencing early European beliefs that the fruit was toxic (hence "wolf-peach").

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised in the main index.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Solanales, family Solanaceae (potato or nightshade family), subfamily Solanoideae, tribe Solaneae.

Phylogenetic notes. Placed in the Lycopersicon section of the genus Solanum, sharing close molecular ties with wild wild-type ancestors from Peru, such as Solanum pimpinellifolium (currant tomato).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) by the IUCN Red List. It has a massive, secure global agricultural population.
