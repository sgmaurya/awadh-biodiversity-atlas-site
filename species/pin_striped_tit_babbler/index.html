पिन-स्ट्राइप्ड टिट-बैबलर / Pin-striped Tit-babbler

Macronus gularis (Horsfield, 1822) — Timaliidae

पिन-स्ट्राइप्ड टिट-बैबलर — तराई के घने जंगलों की निचली झाड़ियों और बांस के झुरमुटों में फुदकने वाला एक छोटा, चंचल और मिलनसार पक्षी है। इसका सिर और पीठ का ऊपरी हिस्सा हल्का कत्थई-भूरा होता है, जबकि सीना और पेट चमकीले हल्के पीले रंग का होता है जिस पर सुई जैसी बारीक काली धारियां (पिन-स्ट्राइप्स) बनी होती हैं। यह झाड़ियों के घने पत्तों के बीच छिपकर लगातार 'चंक-चंक-चंक' जैसी तेज आवाज निकालता है और जमीन के पास छुपे कीड़ों को खोजता रहता है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
HabitSmall, inquisitive, highly active understory babbler of moist forests
CrownRufous-chestnut or ferruginous crown blending into olive-brown mantle
UnderpartsPale sulfur-yellow on throat and breast, marked with fine black shaft streaks
EyebrowDistinct pale yellowish or whitish supercilium contrasting with dark lores

Field tip: Listen inside dense cane brakes and bamboo undergrowth for a loud, metronomic popping call. The bright yellowish breast with fine black pin-stripes and rufous cap distinguish it from other small babblers.

हिंदी:
यह एक छोटा पक्षी है जिसका सिर कत्थई और सीना हल्का पीला होता है।
इसके पीले सीने पर पतली काली धारियां होती हैं जो सुई जैसी दिखती हैं।
यह बांस और घनी झाड़ियों में छिपकर लगातार टुक-टुक जैसी आवाज निकालता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Measurements as given by Baker (1922 under "Mixornis rubricapilla"):

MeasurementRange
Wingwing 53 to 58 mm. (Baker 1922 as Mixornis rubricapilla)

The Pin-striped Tit-babbler is a diminutive, tit-like timaliid with a rounded body and compact proportions. The forehead, crown, and nape are pale ferruginous to chestnut-rufous, contrasting softly with the warm olive-green mantle, scapulars, and rump.

The lores and narrow eye-stripe are dusky, set off by a soft yellowish-buff supercilium. The cheeks, chin, throat, and upper breast are pale primrose-yellow, finely patterned with conspicuous longitudinal black hair-streaks along the feather shafts.

The belly is immaculate pale creamy-yellow, shading into olive-grey on the lower flanks and vent. The wings and tail are rufous-brown. The bill is dark slate-brown above with a paler horn-flesh lower mandible. The legs and feet are slender and yellowish-olive.

Vocalisations. The territorial call is an unmistakable, loud, rhythmic series of hollow popping notes rendered as chonk-chonk-chonk-chonk, delivered from deep foliage. When alarmed, it utters harsh churring scolds.

हिंदी:
इसके पंखों का फैलाव 53 से 58 मिमी तक होता है।
इसके शरीर का निचला हिस्सा पीला और ऊपरी हिस्सा जैतूनी भूरा होता है।
यह खतरे के समय तेज घुरघुराहट जैसी आवाज निकालकर अपने साथियों को सचेत करता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Macronus gularis is an obligate understory specialist, dwelling almost exclusively within the lowest vegetation layer of bamboo clumps, tangled vines, and cane brakes. It rarely ascends into the mid-canopy and avoids open spaces.

Individuals forage in lively family parties or pairs, creeping through dense foliage with agile hops and acrobatic clinging. They frequently serve as nuclear or attendant members in mixed-species foraging flocks, associating with fulvettas, flycatchers, and fantails. Their loud contact notes help maintain flock cohesion across dense undergrowth where visual contact is impossible.

हिंदी:
यह पक्षी हमेशा जमीन से थोड़ी ऊंचाई पर घनी झाड़ियों और लताओं में ही रहता है।
यह छोटे-छोटे झुंडों में रहकर अन्य कीटभक्षी पक्षियों के साथ मिलकर शिकार खोजता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

In Bengal, Assam, and northern foothills, breeding takes place during April, May, and June, and sometimes into July, both in the plains and at moderate elevations (Baker 1922, p. 274 as "Mixornis rubricapilla").

The nest is a loosely woven, ball-shaped or domed structure with a circular side entrance, constructed from broad bamboo leaves, dry grass blades, and fine fern roots, hidden inside a low thorny bush or bamboo clump. A small clutch of eggs is laid. Both parents share incubation duties for a few weeks and cooperate in brooding and feeding the chicks until they fledge.

हिंदी:
यह अप्रैल से जून और कभी-कभी जुलाई तक प्रजनन करता है (बेकर 1922)।
यह बांस के पत्तों और सूखी घास से गेंद जैसा गोल घोंसला बनाता है जिसका मुंह एक तरफ होता है।
कुछ सप्ताह अंडे सेने के बाद बच्चे बाहर आते हैं और माता-पिता मिलकर उन्हें कीड़े खिलाते हैं।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

The Pin-striped Tit-babbler is an active insectivore that gleans diverse small invertebrates from forest foliage:

  • Small caterpillars, leafhoppers, and aphids gleaned from the undersides of leaves
  • Spiders and small harvestmen captured from webs among bamboo culms
  • Small beetles, ants, and termites encountered on fallen deadwood and rotting branches

Distribution वितरण

Global and historical range: Widespread across South and Southeast Asia, from the eastern Himalayas and northeastern India through Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Indochina, the Malay Peninsula, and the Indonesian archipelago.

Regional status: Resident in dense undergrowth of the northern Terai belt of Uttar Pradesh, particularly in Katarniaghat Wildlife Sanctuary and Dudhwa National Park.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Thomas Horsfield in 1822 in Transactions of the Linnean Society of London (vol. 13, p. 150), under the basionym "Timalia gularis".

Synonyms and historical names. Stuart Baker (1922, p. 273) treated the taxon under "Mixornis rubricapilla" (and Mixornis gularis). The GBIF taxonomic backbone classifies the species under Macronus gularis, whereas the IUCN Red List and other checklists maintain the genus Mixornis as Mixornis gularis.

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies are recognized across Asia; northern Indian and Terai birds represent the mainland race "Macronus gularis rubricapilla" (Tickell, 1833).

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Timaliidae (babblers); genus Macronus.

IUCN status rationale. Evaluated as Least Concern (LC) on the IUCN Red List (under Mixornis gularis) because of its extremely wide geographical range and absence of severe population declines.
