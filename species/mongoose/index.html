Small Indian Mongoose / Indian Grey Mongoose

Herpestes edwardsii (É. Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire, 1818) — Herpestidae

नेवला — पूर्वी UP का सबसे परिचित छोटा मांसाहारी जीव है। दिन में सक्रिय रहता है और अक्सर खेत की मेड़ पर, बाग में, या गाँव के किनारे दिख जाता है। रिक्की-टिक्की-टावी एक नेवला था — किपलिंग की वह कहानी किसी भी पूर्वी UP के बगीचे में हो सकती थी। नेवला शायद इस क्षेत्र का सबसे महत्वपूर्ण छोटा मांसाहारी है — साँप का दुश्मन, चूहे का शिकारी, और लोककथाओं का नायक।

Field tip: Grizzled salt-and-pepper small carnivore + low-slung body + long bushy tail with dark tip + daytime activity + village/farm edge habitat = nela. No other common eastern UP carnivore matches this combination.

Morphology आकारिकी

FeatureMeasurement
Head-body length35–45 cm
Tail length25–35 cm
Weight0.9–1.5 kg (males slightly larger)

Fur: Dense, coarse, grizzled — each guard hair banded black and white/brown, creating the characteristic "salt-and-pepper" overall appearance. Underparts slightly paler.

Body: Elongated and low-slung; adapted for moving through dense grass, burrows, and along bunds. Five toes on each foot with non-retractile claws.

Head: Pointed, narrow snout. Small rounded ears. Eyes dark, small. Anal scent glands present — used for scent marking territory.

Tail: Bushy, approximately 60–70% of body length, tapering to a dark tip.

Gait: Low-slung, sinuous running — can sprint at 15–20 km/h in short bursts. Extremely agile with rapid directional changes.

The Snake-Fighting Ability साँप से लड़ने की क्षमता

The mongoose is one of very few animals that can consistently overcome venomous snakes. Several adaptations work together:

Speed: A mongoose's strike is faster than a cobra's — 70 ms vs. ~200 ms. The mongoose repeatedly darts at the snake, moving in and out of range.

Partial venom resistance: Mongoose acetylcholine receptors are mutated in a way that partially blocks neurotoxins — the cobra and krait venom components that paralyze the nervous system bind less effectively. This is not full immunity; a lethal dose of venom still kills a mongoose.

Thick, loose fur: The cobra's fangs must penetrate the fur, then skin, then fatty tissue — longer path to the bloodstream. The loose skin can be grabbed without injecting the bite deeply.

Strategy: The mongoose doesn't just charge. It harasses the snake, induces it to strike repeatedly (tiring it), and waits for a moment of vulnerability to seize the snake behind the head — where it delivers a killing bite.

Ecological Role पारिस्थितिक भूमिका

Primary diet (not snakes): Rats and mice are the most common prey — Rattus species, field mice, gerbils. A mongoose living near a paddy field primarily hunts rodents. One mongoose can consume 1–3 rodents per day.

Other prey: Beetles, grasshoppers, earthworms, scorpions; frogs (including bull frogs and toads); bird eggs and chicks; snakes — Indian Rat Snake, checkered keelback, various colubrids; occasionally venomous species (cobra, krait).

As prey: Larger raptors (Changeable Hawk-Eagle, Shikra take juveniles); Indian Python (rare in eastern UP but documented); feral dogs in village settings.

Pest control service: The combination of rodent and snake control makes the mongoose one of the most economically valuable wild mammals for agriculture. Its presence in a field bund system is effectively free rodent management.

Ecological cascade: When mongoose populations decline (through persecution, roadkill, or habitat loss), rodent populations increase — leading to increased agricultural loss and potentially more snake encounters.

Territory: 1–3 hectare territory, scent-marked with anal gland secretions; defended by solitary individuals or small family groups. Lifespan: 5–8 years in the wild.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि एवं मानव उपयोग

Rodent control: Alongside the rat snake, the mongoose is the most important wild rodent controller in eastern UP. In orchards, homestead gardens, and paddy field margins, mongoose territories provide continuous rodent pressure.

Snake control: Mongoose presence in village margins reduces the probability of cobra encounters near human habitations. Farmers often welcome mongooses deliberately.

Illegally trapped: Despite WPA 1972 protection, mongooses are sometimes captured for snake-fighting performances (severely illegal, persists in some areas) and for fur (hair brushes historically made from mongoose fur — still illegally traded).

Cultural significance: Rikki-Tikki-Tavi (The Jungle Book, Kipling, 1894) — a mongoose living with an Indian family; translated into Hindi and read by generations of Indian children. नेवला is a symbol of speed, cunning, and courage in folk tales. "नेवला साँप का दुश्मन" — "mongoose is the snake's enemy" — a stock phrase in eastern UP.

In Basti district, the mongoose is commonly seen along field bunds, in mango orchards, and at village edges in the morning and late afternoon. The characteristic low-slung trot along a field boundary or a sudden dash into the grass is a frequent sight for anyone walking rural paths before 9 AM. The species is widely known locally as nevla and is generally tolerated or welcomed by farmers for its rodent-controlling services.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Throughout the Indian subcontinent — India, Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and Bangladesh. Also introduced into several Caribbean islands (where it is now a major invasive pest) and parts of the Middle East and Fiji.

In India: Found across all warm regions of the country, from the plains and lower Himalayan foothills to peninsular India. Absent only from high-altitude and true dense forest zones.

In Uttar Pradesh: Widespread across all districts; found in agricultural zones, village margins, open scrub, and light woodland.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Very common in agricultural landscapes — field bunds, orchard margins, homestead gardens, and along the Saryu river embankments. Year-round resident.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Herpestes edwardsii was described by Étienne Geoffroy Saint-Hilaire in 1818 in Description de l'Égypte, Mammifères, planche 11. The species epithet edwardsii honours Milne-Edwards, the French naturalist. The species name is sometimes written edwardsi (with one 'i') — edwardsii (with two 'i's) is the correct spelling per ICZN rules.

Taxonomic note. Recent molecular phylogenetics has led some authorities (including the American Society of Mammalogists) to place this species in the resurrected genus Urva Gray, 1865, as Urva edwardsii. GBIF currently accepts Herpestes edwardsii under the Backbone Taxonomy. The atlas uses Herpestes for consistency with most Indian field guides and the Zoological Survey of India.

Subspecies. Several subspecies are recognised across the large range:

SubspeciesRange
H. e. edwardsiiMain Indian subcontinent
H. e. ferrugineusPakistan and northwestern India
H. e. nyulaSri Lanka

Family and order placement. Belongs to the order Carnivora, family Herpestidae (mongooses), genus Herpestes. The family Herpestidae contains approximately 35 species of mongooses. The genus Herpestes includes several species in India; H. edwardsii is the most widespread and the only species regularly associated with agricultural and village habitats in eastern UP.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) by the IUCN Red List. Widespread, adaptable, and abundant. Primary threats include road kills and illegal capture for performances and the brush-hair trade.
