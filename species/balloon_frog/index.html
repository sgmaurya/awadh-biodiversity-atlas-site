गुब्बारा मेंढक / Marbled Balloon Frog

Uperodon taprobanicus (Parker, 1934) — Microhylidae

गुब्बारा मेंढक — गोल-मटोल, छोटे मुँह और छोटी टाँगों वाला मेंढक जो डरने पर फूलकर गेंद बन जाता है, और जिसकी पीठ पर भूरे पर नारंगी-लाल धब्बों का ऐसा रंग है जैसे किसी ने रंग दिया हो। साल भर पेड़ के खोखले, दीमक की बाँबी और दीवार की दरार में छिपा रहता है, सिर्फ़ चींटी और दीमक खाता है, और पहली मूसलाधार बरसात की रातों में ही बाहर आकर गड्ढों में भैंस जैसी गहरी आवाज़ में बोलता है। कुछ रातें, और फिर अगले साल तक ग़ायब।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
Size50–75 mm; a stout, round frog
ShapeBody globular, head small and pointed, mouth tiny, limbs short; cannot leap far
ColourDark brown to grey-brown with large irregular orange, red or yellow blotches, especially along the sides and on the limbs; belly pale and spotted
SkinSmooth above, slightly granular
FeetFingers and toes with small discs at the tips; two spades on the heel for digging
DefenceInflates with air to a ball, lowers its head and presents its back; the skin exudes a sticky, bitter fluid
CallA deep, loud, resonant "whoooonk", repeated, from a pool after heavy rain; like a bullfrog's but shorter

Field tip: Only on the nights of the first downpours. Follow the deep bellowing to a rain-filled ditch and look for a round, painted frog floating with its vocal sac inflated. By day, lift the bark of a dead tree or look in a tree hole near the ground. If it puffs up into a ball in your hand, you have it.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: सिर्फ़ पहली मूसलाधार बारिश की रातों में। गड्ढे से आती गहरी भैंस जैसी आवाज़ के पीछे जाइए — गोल, रंगीन मेंढक गले की थैली फुलाए तैरता मिलेगा। हाथ में लेते ही फूलकर गेंद बन जाए तो यही है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Snout–vent length50–75 mm
Head widthabout a third of body length
Eggs per clutch500–1,500
Tadpole, full grown20–30 mm

Head and mouth: The narrow-mouthed frogs are named for it: the gape is tiny, the tongue small, suited to picking up ants one by one. The eyes are small; the tympanum hidden.

Body: Round and loose-skinned, able to swell greatly by swallowing air. The dorsal pattern of bright blotches on dark ground is unique among local frogs.

Limbs and feet: Short; the hind feet bear two prominent metatarsal tubercles used as spades for backward digging; the digit tips slightly expanded, helping it climb into tree holes.

Tadpole: A filter-feeding microhylid tadpole without horny beak or teeth, hanging mid-water and straining particles; transparent, with a dark gut visible.

हिंदी: मुँह बहुत छोटा, चींटी एक-एक करके उठाने लायक; आँखें छोटी, कान का पर्दा छिपा। शरीर ढीली त्वचा वाला, हवा निगलकर बहुत फूल सकता है। पिछले पैरों पर दो फावड़े जो पीछे की ओर खोदने के काम आते हैं; अँगुलियों के सिरे थोड़े चपटे, जिनसे पेड़ के खोखले में चढ़ता है। टैडपोल बिना चोंच के, पानी छानकर खाने वाला, पारदर्शी।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

A frog that is nearly invisible for eleven months. It lives in tree holes, under bark, in termite mounds and in the cracks of old walls, sometimes a metre or more above the ground, and eats ants and termites almost exclusively, which it picks from trails with its small mouth; it will sit at a termite swarm hole and take them as they emerge. Its skin secretion is sticky enough to glue the mouth of a snake and bitter enough to make it let go.

Breeding is the one public act. On the first nights of torrential rain the males gather at temporary pools, ditches and flooded ruts and call with a volume out of all proportion to their size; the females arrive, eggs are laid as a surface film, and within a few nights the pools are silent again. The tadpoles must finish in three to four weeks before the pool dries. The adults go back to their holes. A village with old trees, termite mounds and a few rain ditches has it; a tidied one does not.

हिंदी: ग्यारह महीने लगभग अदृश्य। पेड़ के खोखले, छाल के नीचे, दीमक की बाँबी और पुरानी दीवार की दरार में, कभी ज़मीन से एक मीटर ऊपर; लगभग सिर्फ़ चींटी और दीमक खाता है, दीमक के उड़ने के छेद पर बैठकर निकलते दीमक पकड़ता है। त्वचा का चिपचिपा कड़वा रस साँप का मुँह चिपका देता है। पहली मूसलाधार रातों में नर गड्ढों पर जमा होकर अपने आकार से कहीं ज़्यादा ज़ोर से बोलते हैं; कुछ रातों में अंडे, और फिर सन्नाटा। पुराने पेड़ों, बाँबियों और बरसाती गड्ढों वाला गाँव इसे रखता है।

हिंदी: जून-जुलाई की पहली भारी बारिश में कुछ रातें बोलना और अंडे; अंडे एक-दो दिन, टैडपोल तीन-चार हफ़्ते; फिर छोटे मेंढक पेड़ और बाँबी में; बाकी साल छिपा।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Ants and termites above all: Mound-building Termite swarms and trails, Tropical Fire Ant, Weaver Ant workers on the ground; occasionally small beetles.
  • Tadpoles: suspended particles, algae and bacteria.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Snakes: Checkered Keelback and Common Wolf Snake attempt it; the sticky skin defeats some.
  • Birds: Spotted Owlet, Barn Owl on calling males.
  • Mammals: Small Indian Mongoose, Jungle Cat.
  • In the pool: Giant Water Bug and dragonfly nymphs on tadpoles.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Termite control. A frog that eats termites by the hundred at the mound mouth is doing the farmer a service; it is one of several animals of the village that keep termite colonies in check.

Harmless. Neither bites nor is toxic to touch; the skin secretion is sticky and bitter but not dangerous; wash hands. Puffing up is a bluff.

A sign of old trees. Its need for tree holes and termite mounds makes it an indicator of groves and villages that have not been stripped of old timber.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: none recorded.

Today. Probably common but almost never seen; its status in the district is unknown, which is the point.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The frog's range in the Gangetic plain north of the Ganga is poorly documented; a confirmed record from Basti district, with a call recording, would be worth reporting to a herpetological journal.

हिंदी: दीमक की बाँबी के मुँह पर सैकड़ों दीमक खाने वाला मेंढक किसान का काम कर रहा है। काटता नहीं, ज़हरीला नहीं; फूलना बस धमकी है। पेड़ के खोखले और बाँबी की ज़रूरत इसे पुराने पेड़ों वाले गाँव का सूचक बनाती है। ज़िले में इसकी स्थिति अज्ञात है — आवाज़ की रिकॉर्डिंग के साथ एक पक्का रिकॉर्ड पत्रिका में छपने लायक होगा।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Known in the literature from the Gangetic plain, the Nepal tarai, Bihar and Bengal; its presence in Basti district is likely from habitat and range but has not, to our knowledge, been published. To be expected, if present, in groves with old trees and on the first stormy nights of the monsoon at rain-filled ditches. A call recording or a photograph of the painted back would be the first record for the district.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Sri Lanka, peninsular and northern India, Nepal, Bangladesh.

In India: The eastern and southern peninsula, Orissa, Bengal, Bihar, the Gangetic plain and the tarai; absent from the far north-west.

In Uttar Pradesh: Recorded from the eastern districts and the tarai; distribution poorly mapped.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected but unconfirmed.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. Is there a deep bellowing frog in the ditches on the first stormy night? / पहली तूफ़ानी रात को नालियों में गहरी आवाज़ वाला मेंढक है क्या? — Go out with a torch and a phone; record the call.
  2. Does it live in the termite mound by the grove? — Check the mound base with a torch at night in the monsoon.
  3. How many nights does the chorus last? — Note the first and last.
  4. Does it really inflate? / क्या यह सचमुच फूलता है? — Observe without holding it for long.
  5. How many days from the floating eggs to the froglets? — Watch one rut or ditch.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by H.W. Parker in 1934 as Kaloula taprobanica in A Monograph of the Frogs of the Family Microhylidae, p. 84, from Ceylon (Taprobane, the ancient name of Sri Lanka). Transferred to Uperodon by Peloso and colleagues in 2016 on molecular evidence, uniting the Indian Kaloula and Ramanella species.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Microhylidae, the narrow-mouthed frogs; order Anura. Uperodon holds a dozen South Asian species of globular, hole-dwelling frogs.

Phylogenetic notes. The microhylids are an old and worldwide family; the South Asian Uperodon lineage is distinct from the South-east Asian Kaloula it was long placed in.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern: wide range and presumed large population, though secretive and under-recorded.
