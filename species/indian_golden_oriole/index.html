पीलक / Indian Golden Oriole

Oriolus kundoo Sykes, 1832 — Oriolidae

पीलक — भारत की सबसे सुंदर पीली चिड़ियों में से एक है। इसका पूरा शरीर सोने जैसे पीले रंग का होता है और पंखों के सिरों पर काला रंग होता है। यह मई-जुलाई में भारत में आती है और अक्तूबर तक रहती है। इसकी आवाज़ सुखद और मधुर होती है — एक सीटी जैसी "पीऊ-पीऊ" या "होएई-होएई"। यह फलों को खाती है और बीजों को फैलाती है। आम और अंजीर के पेड़ों पर इसे आसानी से देखा जा सकता है।

Field tip: Listen for the loud, liquid call "hoe-ee" or "pee-oo" from tall fruit-bearing trees in spring and early summer. Often stays in the canopy, making observation difficult despite bright plumage. Males are more conspicuous than females due to brighter yellow. Search in mango groves and mixed deciduous forests.

Plumage (breeding male): Head, neck, back, and rump bright golden-yellow or bright yellow. Lesser wing coverts yellow; median and greater coverts mostly black with yellow edges, creating a striking pattern. Primaries black with broad yellow margins. Tail black with yellow outer web on central feathers, outer feathers yellow with black base and tips. Underparts golden-yellow; undertail coverts bright yellow.

Plumage (female and non-breeding male): Duller than breeding male. Crown and back olive-yellow or greenish-yellow. Wings and tail duller, with less contrast; flight feathers and tail may appear more brownish than pure black. Underparts yellowish or pale yellow.

Bare parts: Eyes dark brown or dark red; orbital ring slightly paler. Bill long, pinkish-red or reddish. Legs and feet dark grey or greenish-grey.

Sexes: Males in breeding plumage are bright golden-yellow with contrasting black and yellow wings; females are duller olive-yellow to greenish-yellow.

Juveniles: Similar to females; duller overall; tail and wings less contrasting.

Vocalisations

Indian Golden Oriole is highly vocal, particularly during breeding season.

  1. Advertising call: A loud, liquid, whistled "hoe-ee" or "pee-oo," often repeated in series, sometimes described as "ee-oo" or "po-ee." Call is delivered from high perches in the canopy, often without the bird being visible.
  2. Contact call: Softer chattering or "chuk-chuk-chuk" between mates and flock members.
  3. Duetting: Pairs sometimes engage in call-and-response duetting.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Canopy frugivore: Indian Golden Oriole feeds primarily on fruits, particularly figs, berries, and other wild and cultivated fruits. Moves actively through the canopy in a somewhat languid style, gleaning fruit from twigs and branches.

Insectivorous in breeding season: During breeding season, diet shifts to include more insects (especially during chick-rearing), with insects taken from foliage and sometimes in brief aerial sallies.

Migratory: A long-distance summer migrant. Arrives in India in spring (March-April), breeds across northern and central India (April-July), and departs in autumn (September-October) to winter in Africa and peninsular India.

Solitary and paired: Usually solitary or in pairs; occasionally joins mixed-species flocks during migration or in non-breeding areas. Males are vocal and territorial during breeding season.

Breeding and nesting: Monogamous. Constructs a remarkable suspended pouch nest woven from plant fibres, suspended from a branch. The nest is suspended with the entrance at the top, providing protection from predators and weather.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • March–April: Arrival in India; spring migration; males establish territories and call intensively
  • April–May: Pair formation; nest construction (remarkable woven pouch); egg-laying
  • May–June: Incubation (13–14 days); chick-rearing begins
  • June–July: Chicks hatch and grow in nest (fledging at 14–16 days)
  • July–August: Fledgling period; juveniles remain with parents and learn to forage
  • August–September: Pre-departure; flocks form; birds move southward
  • September–October: Departure for wintering grounds

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary frugivore diet:

  • Figs (Ficus spp., especially F. religiosa, F. benghalensis)
  • Berries of various wild shrubs
  • Cultivated fruits (mango, when available)
  • Drupes and wild fruits in season

Breeding season insects:

  • Caterpillars and insects from foliage
  • Grasshoppers and beetles
  • Flying insects (occasionally)

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Shikra (Accipiter badius) and other small raptors — hunt flying birds
  • Greater Spotted Eagle (Clanga clanga) — hunts during passage
  • Snakes — raid nests (less successful due to suspended nest design)
  • Crows (Corvus spp.) — may mob or harass; may attack chicks if nest is discovered

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Seed dispersal: By consuming and dispersing fig seeds and other fruit seeds across the landscape, Indian Golden Oriole contributes to forest regeneration and seed distribution.

Fruit crop utilization: In some agricultural areas, particularly those with mango and other fruit orchards, orioles may consume ripe fruit, though damage is generally minimal given their preference for wild fruits.

Protected status: Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (India). The species is legally protected throughout the country.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Indian Golden Oriole is expected as a summer visitor (March-October) in Basti and Ayodhya districts, particularly in areas with fruit-bearing trees (mango groves, mixed deciduous forest). Listen for the loud "hoe-ee" or "pee-oo" call from tall trees in spring and summer (April-July when most vocal). Though the bright yellow plumage is conspicuous, the bird often stays in the canopy and is difficult to observe. A first record for the atlas would be a photograph showing the bright yellow plumage and black wing and tail markings, or audio recording of the distinctive call, with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds across northern and central India (summer); winters in peninsular India, Africa, and other regions. Long-distance migrant with distinct breeding and non-breeding ranges.

In India: Summer breeding visitor to most of the mainland; winters in peninsular regions and coastal areas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Summer visitor to districts with suitable fruit-bearing trees and mixed deciduous forest.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected as a summer visitor, particularly in mango-growing areas and mixed deciduous forest.

Field rule: Golden-yellow plumage (male) + black and yellow wings and tail + loud "hoe-ee" call + canopy feeding + spring arrival = Indian Golden Oriole. Females are duller but share the olive-yellow coloration and distinctive call.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. This species was first described as Oriolus kundoo by William John Sykess in 1832 from a specimen collected in India, type locality in the Deccan region.

Subspecies. Two subspecies recognised: O. k. kundoo (nominate, Indian subcontinent including eastern UP) and O. k. ceylonensis (Sri Lanka). The nominate subspecies is larger and darker-winged than the Sri Lankan form.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Oriolidae (orioles). Oriolidae includes about 30 species of fruit-eating songbirds with remarkable woven nests, found in Africa, Asia, and Australia.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies place Indian Golden Oriole within a clade of Asian orioles; the genus Oriolus is predominantly Old World, with highest diversity in Africa.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC). The species is widespread, maintains stable populations during breeding season, and shows flexibility in habitat use across its range.
