साल / Sal

Shorea robusta C.F. Gaertn. — Dipterocarpaceae

साल — यह पेड़ नहीं, एक सम्मान है। वनों का राजा, जो उत्तर भारत के पहाड़ी और तराई जंगलों में मिलता है। ईंट पकाने, घर बनाने, और पूजा में सब जगह साल की लकड़ी है। अयोध्या इलाके में असली जंगली साल नहीं है, लेकिन मंदिरों और बागों में लगाए गए पेड़ दिख सकते हैं। नीचे सोहागीबरवा-गोरखपुर तराई में जंगल है।

Field tip: Sal is native to the nearby terai forests (Sohagibarwa–Gorakhpur) on the Nepal border. In Ayodhya, it is planted in temple grounds and groves, not wild. Any wild tree would be a significant botanical find.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: पेड़ बहुत बड़ा, सीधा तना। पत्ती खुरदरी, चमड़े जैसी। पीले फूलों की बालियाँ। फल पंखों वाला। अयोध्या में जंगली नहीं, सिर्फ़ लगाया हुआ।

Morphology आकारिकी

Trunk and bark: Tall, straight, with grey-brown, deeply fissured bark. Buttresses may be present at the base.

Leaves: Alternate, obovate to elliptic, rough above (due to fine tubercles), leathery, entire, with a short petiole.

Flowers: Small, pale yellow or greenish-yellow, fragrant, in terminal panicles.

Fruit: A nut with two large, oblong wings (a characteristic of the Dipterocarpaceae), ripening pale brown.

हिंदी: बहुत बड़ा पेड़, सीधा। पत्ती खुरदरी, चमड़े जैसी। फूल पीले, सुगंधित। फल के साथ दो बड़े पंख।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Sal is a forest tree of the terai and sal forests of the Himalayan foothills, from the Gangetic plain eastward to Assam. It forms extensive pure and mixed forests. It is drought-tolerant and grows on a wide range of soils. The flowers are visited by insects in the cool season. The winged fruits are dispersed by wind. The tree is the dominant timber tree of Indian forests and is heavily harvested.

Range note: The nearest natural sal forest to Ayodhya is the Sohagibarwa–Gorakhpur terai sal forest complex on the Nepal border. Sal is not wild in Basti or Ayodhya districts but is planted in temple groves and gardens. Any wild tree would be worth documenting.

हिंदी: तराई और पहाड़ी जंगलों का पेड़। गोरखपुर-सोहागीबरवा में बड़े जंगल हैं। अयोध्या में जंगली नहीं है, सिर्फ़ मंदिरों और बागों में लगाया।

Associated Species / Interactions संबंधित प्रजातियाँ

  • Companions in sal forests: Teak, rosewood, and other terai species.
  • Resin producers: Sal exudes resin used in various applications.

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Stem-borers and bark-beetles in standing timber.
  • Defoliating insects in natural forests.
  • No significant fungal diseases in well-managed forests.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Sacred and Cultural Use (documented):

  • Sacred tree. Sal is venerated in Hindu tradition and is planted in temple grounds. It is associated with ancient kingdoms and is mentioned in classical texts.
  • Ceremonial use. The tree is used in rituals and is considered sacred.

Timber Use (documented):

  • Major timber tree. The heartwood is hard, durable, and moderately resistant to decay. Used for railway sleepers, structural timber, and construction.
  • Fuel. Sapwood is used for fuel and charcoal.
  • Resin. Sal resin (dammar) is used in varnishes and traditional medicines.

Today. Sal timber is harvested in quantity from state forests; the tree is also planted in agroforestry and village groves.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The planting and cultivation history of sal in eastern UP temples and villages would merit documentation.

हिंदी: पवित्र पेड़। मंदिरों में पूजा में आता है। लकड़ी मजबूत, सड़ती नहीं। घर, रेलवे स्लीपर, सब में साल की लकड़ी है। राल भी निकलती है।

To be expected in Basti only as planted trees in temple groves and old village gardens. Any wild or feral trees should be recorded with GPS.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Native to the terai and sal forests of the Himalayan foothills from Pakistan through India and Nepal to Bhutan and into Myanmar.

In India: Extensive forests in the terai belt; widespread in central Indian dry deciduous forests.

In Uttar Pradesh: The nearest sal forests to Basti are in the Sohagibarwa–Gorakhpur terai on the Nepal border. Absent from the district except as planted trees.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Not wild; occasional planted trees in temple grounds and old groves.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many sal trees are in the temples and groves of your village or block, and how old are they? / क्या साल के पेड़ मंदिरों में हैं? — Walk and document each tree with location and girth.
  2. When and why were these sal trees planted? / साल के पेड़ कब लगाए गए? — Ask the priest and elders about planting history.
  3. Do any of them produce resin, and is it collected? — Check for tapping scars or collection containers.
  4. When does a planted sal tree first flower in your area? — Monitor a young tree annually.
  5. Are there any wild or feral sal trees outside temple grounds in the district? — Walk wastelands and forest margins; any tree found would be significant.

Field rule: A very tall tree with rough, leathery, obovate leaves and pale wood, with winged fruits, in a temple or forest setting, is sal.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Charles Friedrich Gaertner in 1801 in Carpologia 1: 359. The epithet robusta refers to the robust, strong nature of the tree.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Dipterocarpaceae, the dipterocarp family, in the order Malvales. The family is characteristic of tropical forests and is dominated by Shorea and related genera.

Phylogenetic notes. Shorea robusta is the type species of its genus and is closely related to other south Asian Shorea species.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Widespread in forests and planted settings; subject to selective logging.
