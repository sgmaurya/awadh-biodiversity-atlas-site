चितरोखा फाख्ता / Spotted Dove

Spilopelia chinensis (Scopoli, 1786) — Columbidae

चितरोखा फाख्ता — गाँव की सबसे आम कबूतर, जिसकी गर्दन पर काली-सफेद धारियों की वजह से इसे "चितरोखा" कहते हैं। इसकी कूइंग आवाज़ "कू-कू-कूकू" गाँव की सुबह का संगीत है। यह अकेले या जोड़े में चलती है, घर के छत पर बैठती है, और चावल के दाने खाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall dove (25–28 cm total length); slender build
PlumageGreyish-brown and buff overall; warmer tone than Laughing Dove
Head and neckRufous on crown; distinctive black and white striped patch on lower neck (spotted collar)
BreastPink or mauve-tinted
WingsSpotted white on wing coverts (gives "spotted" name); darker flights
TailGrey-brown with dark subterminal band; white tips visible in flight

Field tip: Look for the distinctive black-and-white striped patch on the lower neck (like a dotted necklace). Smaller and more rufous-toned than Collared Dove. More common than Laughing Dove in villages.

Plumage: Head rufous-buff; back and wing coverts greyish-buff with white spotting on the coverts. Breast and underparts buff to pinkish-buff. Primaries dark brown. Distinctive black-and-white striped patch on lower hind-neck — unique field mark. Tail greyish with black subterminal band; white tips prominent in flight.

Face: Orbital ring red; eye dark.

Legs and feet: Red.

Sexes: Similar; males slightly more rufous on head.

Juveniles: Duller, without the neck patch; spotted coverts less distinct.

Vocalisations

Spotted Dove has a distinctive cooing call, heard especially in breeding season and at dawn/dusk.

  1. Advertising and contact call: A melodious "koo-koo-kookoo" or "coo-coo-coocu", softer and more liquid than Laughing Dove's harsh call. Repeated from a perch.
  2. Alarm call: A sharp coo-wit when flushed.
  3. Feeding call: Softer cooings when flocks gather at grain.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ground feeder: Forages on the ground in small flocks (5–20 birds), picking up scattered grain, seeds and fallen fruit. Often seen pecking in village courtyards.

Pairs and flocks: Breeding birds are monogamous and pairs hold territories. Outside breeding season, birds often roost communally in trees and feed in loose flocks.

Nesting: Builds a flimsy platform nest of twigs in a tree, bush or on a building ledge. Both parents incubate.

Roosting: Roosts on wires, branches, or roof-tops; highly habituated to human presence.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • January–February: Pairs establish territories; cooing increases
  • March–July: Peak breeding; two broods common
  • April–June: Chicks fledged; families forage together
  • August–October: Post-breeding; flocks form and move locally
  • November–December: Wintering; less active, reduced calling

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary diet:

  • Seeds of grasses, weeds, and wild crops
  • Grain: fallen rice, wheat, millet after harvest
  • Small fruits and berries (occasionally)
  • Green shoots and leaves (minor)

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Shikra (Accipiter badius) — primary predator; hunts flying birds
  • Peregrine Falcon (Falco peregrinus) — diving strikes at doves in open
  • Eurasian Sparrowhawk (Accipiter nisus) — winter visitor; hunts doves in flight
  • Feral cats and mongooses — catch ground-feeding birds
  • Jungle Crow (Corvus macrorhynchos) — raids nests for eggs and chicks

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Grain feeding: Spotted Doves feed on fallen grain and waste in fields and markets. In some areas with large flocks, can cause minor crop damage, especially to newly sown grain. However, the birds also consume weed seeds and thus have some benefit.

Cultural significance: The dove has long been a symbol of peace and gentleness in Hindi literature and folklore. The cooing of doves is considered auspicious in many villages. Historically kept as caged birds (now illegal under Wildlife Protection Act).

Protected status: Schedule IV of Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972. Nest disturbance and bird trapping are illegal.

Spotted Dove is likely the most common dove of Basti and Ayodhya districts. Expected year-round in all cultivated areas, villages, orchards and roadsides. Most visible in early morning and evening when birds gather to feed on grain spilled in markets and fields. Listen for the distinctive coo-coo-cuckoo call from roof-tops and power lines.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across Asia from Pakistan through India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Indochina, Malaysia to China and Japan. Introduced to Africa, Middle East, and Australia.

In India: Common resident throughout the country up to about 2,000 m in the Himalayas.

In Uttar Pradesh: Abundant year-round resident in cultivated areas and villages throughout the state.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Very common resident in all agricultural and semi-urban areas.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

  1. Is Spotted Dove the most common dove in your village? / आपके गाँव में कौन सी कबूतर सबसे आम है? — Listen at dawn and evening; count how many Spotted, Laughing, and Collared Doves you hear calling in one morning.
  2. Where do spotted doves roost at night? / रात को कबूतर कहाँ सोती हैं? — Watch birds flying to roost at dusk; mark trees or buildings used.
  3. What happens to pairs after breeding? / प्रजनन के बाद क्या होता है? — No equipment needed — track a pair from spring through autumn.
  4. Do grain piles attract doves to damage crops? / दाने खाने वाली कबूतरें फसल को नुकसान पहुँचाती हैं? — Count doves in grain storage areas; watch for damage on sown fields.
  5. How many broods do doves raise each year? / साल में कितने बार बच्चे होते हैं? — Find and monitor active nests from March onwards (check weekly).

Field rule: Medium dove with black-and-white spotted necklace patch on lower neck + white spots on wing coverts + rufous tone + melodious "coo-coo-cuckoo" call = Spotted Dove. The neck patch is diagnostic.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Spilopelia chinensis was first described by Scopoli in 1786 as Columba chinensis based on specimens from China. The genus Spilopelia was erected later to accommodate this and related species.

Subspecies. Numerous subspecies across the Asian range; the nominate chinensis occurs in northern India and eastern UP.

Family and order placement. Order Columbiformes (doves and pigeons); family Columbidae. The genus Spilopelia contains three species, all Asian.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies show Spilopelia is derived from Streptopelia (collared doves), sharing a recent common ancestor with the Collared Dove.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern. The species is widespread, abundant, and has actually increased its range through expansion into urban areas and agricultural landscapes.
