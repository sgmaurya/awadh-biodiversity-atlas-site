बड़ा करवानक / Great Thick-knee

Esacus recurvirostris (Cuvier, 1829) — Burhinidae

बड़ा करवानक — यह बड़ी चिड़िया नदी की बालू और बजरी पर रहती है। इसकी टाँगें बहुत मोटी होती हैं, जिससे इसे पहचानना आसान है। सिर भी बड़ा होता है, और आँखें बड़ी-बड़ी होती हैं। यह रात को ज़्यादा सक्रिय होता है। दिन में यह रेत पर बैठकर आराम करता है और रात को कीड़े खोजता है। घाघरा और राप्ती नदियों पर इसे देख सकते हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeLarge thick-knee, 38–41 cm
PlumageGreyish-brown; cryptic colouration
HeadLarge; relatively flat crown; prominent forehead
EyesLarge, pale yellow or golden; nocturnal appearance
BillLong, slightly curved upward (recurved); pale yellow
LegsVery thick and long; yellowish-green; thick "knees" (joint enlargement) are diagnostic
TailShort, often hidden when standing
BehaviourStands upright; freezes when threatened; often on sandbars

Field tip: On river sandbars in eastern UP, look for a large, grey-brown bird standing upright on very thick, long yellowish-green legs. The combination of large head, pale yellow eyes, long curved bill, and extremely thick legs is unmistakable. Often seen standing still on open sand and gravel. Most active at dawn, dusk, and night.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length380–410 mm
Wing (flattened chord)230–260 mm
Tail100–120 mm
Bill50–65 mm
Tarsus70–82 mm (notably thick for its size)
Weight500–700 g

Plumage: Greyish-brown, cryptic and streaked. Crown brown with some streaking. Back and wings brown with buff and darker streaking. Wing coverts buff-brown with darker markings. Rump and uppertail coverts slightly paler. Underparts buff-white with brown streaking on breast. Throat white. Outer tail feathers grey-brown with darker barring.

Head: Large, relatively flat-crowned, broad and bulky. Forehead prominent. Lore pale. Eyes very large, pale yellow to golden, suited for nocturnal feeding.

Bill: Long, pale yellow to olive-yellow; slightly curved upward (recurved), particularly visible in profile. Suited for probing in sand and gravel.

Legs: Extremely thick and long; yellowish-green to greenish. The knee joint is notably enlarged, giving the bird its name "thick-knee". This thick-kneed appearance is the most distinctive feature.

Eyes: Large, positioned laterally on head; suited for detecting movement in dim light.

Sexes: Similar; slight size differences, males marginally larger.

Juveniles: Duller than adults; yellow eye less developed initially.

Vocalisations

Primarily nocturnal callers; calls given mostly at night:

  1. Main call: A loud, wailing or bleating call, reminiscent of distant curlew; described as a loud kree-EW or wailing note.
  2. Alarm call: A sharp kook or chip when disturbed.
  3. Flight call: Harsh calls given in flight.
हिंदी: रात को तेज़ आवाज़ करता है। दिन में शांत रहता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Insectivore: Feeds primarily on ground-dwelling insects:

  • Crickets and grasshoppers
  • Ground beetles and coleopterans
  • Aquatic insects near water edge
  • Occasionally small reptiles or crustaceans

Feeding technique: Walks slowly across sand and gravel, probing into sand with its long, curved bill to extract insects. Also picks insects from the surface. Feeds primarily at night; less active by day.

Nocturnal behaviour: Primarily crepuscular to nocturnal; roosts by day on sandbars and becomes active in late afternoon, through the night, and into early morning.

Territorial: Pairs occupy breeding territories on suitable river sandbars; some territorial behaviour year-round.

Breeding: Monogamous; both parents defend territory and participate in chick-rearing.

River specialist: Entirely dependent on river sandbars and shingle islands with minimal vegetation. Adaptation to nocturnal foraging may be an adaptation to avoid competition with diurnal plovers.

Sedentary: Largely resident year-round in the same river system; minimal migration.

हिंदी: रात को कीड़े खोजता है। नदी की बालू पर ही रहता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round on suitable river systems in eastern UP.

Breeding: February to June; peak March–May. Less vocal than other plovers; breeding activity less conspicuous.

Nesting: Ground nest — a simple scrape in sand or gravel with minimal lining. Eggs are cryptically coloured to blend with surrounding sand.

Eggs: Typically 2 (occasionally 3) buff or olive eggs with dark markings; incubation approximately 24 days by both parents.

Chicks: Precocial; leave nest within hours. Cryptically coloured. Parents guard chicks.

Fledging: Chicks become independent at approximately 35–40 days.

Roosting: By day on open sand and gravel; often in small groups.

Water level dependency: Breeding success tied to availability of suitable sandbars; flooding may inundate nests.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Snakes: Large snakes near water edge
  • Monitor lizards: Depredate eggs
  • Crows and corvids: Depredate eggs and young
  • Jackals: May hunt adults
  • Habitat loss: Loss of suitable sandbars due to river management
  • Flooding: Monsoon flooding of nesting sites
हिंदी: साँप, गोह, कौवे का खतरा। बाढ़ घोंसलों को नष्ट कर देती है।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: Listed in Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, amended 2022. All wild birds are protected; hunting is prohibited.

Near Threatened status: Classified as Near Threatened (NT) by the IUCN Red List. Population decline driven by:

  • Loss and degradation of river sandbar habitat
  • Water management practices (dams, barrages) that reduce natural sandbar formation
  • Sand mining from rivers
  • Human disturbance at nesting sites
  • Flooding control measures that prevent natural river dynamics

Ecosystem role: As a specialist insectivore of river sandbars, the Great Thick-knee plays a niche ecological role in floodplain ecosystems.

Habitat conservation: The species is an indicator of healthy, undisturbed river systems with natural sandbar formation and minimal human interference.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

The Great Thick-knee is expected as a resident on the sandbars and shingle islands of the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers in Basti and Ayodhya districts, particularly around Parvati Arga, where Yashmita-Ulman & Singh (2025) listed it. The species should be looked for on open sandbars, especially during low-water periods (February–May and October–November) when sandbars are most exposed. The bird is most conspicuous at dusk and dawn when it becomes active; listen for the wailing nocturnal call. The combination of large head, very thick legs, and long curved bill is diagnostic. A first record would be a clear photograph showing the thick legs, large eyes, and curved bill, with date and location.

हिंदी: पार्वती अर्घा पक्षी विहार में देख सकते हैं। घाघरा और राप्ती नदियों पर बालू के टीलों पर।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia, particularly on major river systems.

In India: Distributed on suitable river systems throughout mainland India, from the Himalayas to peninsular rivers.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present on major rivers including the Ganges, Gomti, Ghaghra, and Rapti; populations have declined significantly.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected on the Ghaghra, Rapti, and Saryu river systems. Recorded at Parvati Arga. Current status uncertain; species appears to be rare to absent from much of the region.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Georges Cuvier in 1829. Type locality: India.

Subspecies. Monotypic — Esacus recurvirostris recurvirostris. No subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Burhinidae (thick-knees or stone curlews). The genus Esacus contains the great and beach thick-knees; E. recurvirostris is the species of the Indian subcontinent.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies place Esacus recurvirostris as basal within the thick-knee clade. The thick knees, long curved bill, and nocturnal behaviour represent unique adaptations within the charadriiform group.

IUCN status rationale. Classified as Near Threatened (NT) due to:

  • Loss and degradation of river sandbar habitat
  • Water management practices that reduce natural sandbar formation
  • Sand mining
  • Human disturbance
  • Flooding control measures

Population is declining across its range.
