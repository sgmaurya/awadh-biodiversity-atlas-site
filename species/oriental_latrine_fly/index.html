हरी मक्खी / Oriental Latrine Fly

Chrysomya megacephala (Fabricius, 1794) — Calliphoridae

हरी मक्खी — लेटरिन के पास, कूड़े के ढेर, मरे हुए जानवर के पास बैठी हुई एक चमकीली, हरी-नीली-काली मक्खी। इसका सिर बड़ा, पेट पर लाल या नारंगी धब्बे। यह गंदे स्थान पर अंडे देती है। अंडे दो-तीन दिन में लार्वे (मैगट) बन जाते हैं और गली मछली की तरह खोदते-खाते हैं। यह घर के अंदर भी आ जाती है, खाने पर बैठती है, और पेचिश, दस्त और अन्य बीमारियाँ फैलाती है।

Field tip: A shiny green or blue-green fly, larger than the house fly, with a notably large head, hovering around latrines, garbage or food. It is attracted to odours of decay and is aggressive in competing with other flies for breeding substrate.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: हरी, चमकीली मक्खी, सिर बड़ा; लेटरिन और कूड़े के पास बैठी; घर में भी आ जाती है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Body length6–8 mm
Wing length7–9 mm

Structure: Head notably large, dominating the body; compound eyes large, touching on the frons (male) or separated (female). Antennae short, with a plumose arista (hair). Thorax is robust, covered with fine setae and sensory bristles. Scutellum prominent. Abdomen is segmented, metallic green with sometimes reddish or orange areas, particularly on the ventral surface. Legs are moderately long, dark, with fine hairs and sticky pads that allow the fly to walk on smooth surfaces. The proboscis is a siphon-like structure.

Life stages: Eggs are 0.5–1 mm; larvae (maggots) are cylindrical, whitish, up to 10 mm; pupae are dark, barrel-shaped, 4–6 mm.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A carrion fly of warm regions, highly synanthropic (living in human proximity). Adults are strong fliers, attracted to volatile odours from decomposing organic matter — particularly human and animal faeces in latrines, exposed sewage, and garbage. Females lay hundreds of eggs directly into moist organic material. Larvae develop rapidly in warm conditions, passing through three instars in 3–7 days. The maggots feed voraciously on decaying tissue, accelerating decomposition. Pupation occurs in the substrate or in adjacent soil; new adults emerge 1–2 weeks after egg-laying, allowing 3–4 generations per month in the warm season.

Adults are omnivorous, feeding on liquid substrates: decomposing matter, fruit juices, blood, and any available food. They readily enter human dwellings, especially near food preparation areas and toilets. During feeding, they regurgitate digestive enzymes onto solid food to predigest it, thus contaminating surfaces with faecal pathogens.

हिंदी: लेटरिन पर अंडे देती है; तीन दिन में लार्वे निकल आते हैं; एक हफ़्ते में मक्खी बन जाती है। गर्मी में हर महीने चार पीढ़ियाँ। घर में आकर खाने पर बैठती है, बीमारी फैलाती है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Feeding substrates (larvae):

  • Human faeces (sewage, latrines)
  • Animal faeces and carcasses
  • Food waste and garbage
  • Decomposing plant matter
  • Meat and fish waste

Adult diet:

  • Decomposing organic matter
  • Fruit juices and fermenting materials
  • Blood and serum
  • Liquid food scraps

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid flies: Tachinidae parasitize larvae
  • Wasps: parasitoid wasps attack larvae and pupae
  • Spiders: hunting spiders and web spiders catch adults
  • Birds: insectivorous birds, particularly near latrines
  • Beetles: some ground beetles and other flies are predatory on larvae

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

What it does to people. The Oriental Latrine Fly is an important vector of pathogenic bacteria and viruses in areas with poor sanitation. The fly breeds in human faeces and then directly contaminates food with enteric pathogens. The bacteria Salmonella, Shigella, E. coli O157:H7, Vibrio cholerae, and Staphylococcus aureus are readily isolated from the fly's body and faeces. Viruses including enteroviruses, rotavirus and hepatitis A are similarly transmitted. The fly is a major mechanical vector of bacterial diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid, and cholera in sanitation-poor areas. Unlike the house fly, this species shows a strong preference for open sewage and latrines, making it a particular hazard in villages with inadequate sanitation.

The presence of the fly is a sign of sanitation failure. In studies in India, high populations of Chrysomya flies are correlated with high incidence of diarrhoeal disease in the same area.

Not medical advice: This atlas does not provide medical treatment guidance. If symptoms of infectious diarrhoea, dysentery, or typhoid develop, consult a doctor or visit a health center. The information here is for prevention and sanitation, not treatment.

Prevention: (1) construct and maintain sanitary latrines with proper lids; (2) cover human and animal faeces; (3) cover food and water; (4) wash hands thoroughly after defecation and before eating; (5) screen windows and doors; (6) dispose of garbage away from living areas; (7) keep the house and surroundings clean and dry.

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use: none recorded for the region.

Today. Still a major disease vector in areas with poor sanitation. Public health programs prioritize latrines and hand-washing to reduce the fly's role in diarrhoeal disease transmission.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). The seasonal dynamics of Chrysomya megacephala populations and their role in pathogen transmission in eastern UP's villages have not been quantified.

हिंदी: यह मक्खी सब प्रकार की बीमारियाँ फैलाती है — दस्त, पेचिश, हैज़ा, टायफ़ाइड। रोकथाम: शौचालय को ढक्कन लगाइए, खाना ढक दीजिए, हाथ धोइए। अगर दस्त लगे तो डॉक्टर के पास जाइए।

Expected near open latrines, sewage, and garbage throughout the district, particularly during the warm months. The fly is abundant in villages with poor sanitation and open defecation. A specimen or clear photograph of a green-coloured blow fly near a latrine or decomposing matter is a confirmed record.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Tropical Asia, Africa, Australia and Pacific islands; expanding northward in warm regions.

In India: Widespread in all regions with warm climate and poor sanitation; particularly abundant in the Indo-Gangetic plain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common in areas with inadequate sanitation.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected near open latrines and in areas with poor waste management.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Fabricius described Musca megacephala in 1794 in Systema Antliatorum. The genus Chrysomya is from Greek meaning "golden fly"; megacephala means "large-headed."

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Calliphoridae (blow flies or flesh flies), order Diptera, suborder Brachycera. The Calliphoridae are more derived than Muscidae and are specialized for carrion ecology.

Phylogenetic notes. Chrysomya is an Indo-Australian genus, relatively recent invaders of Africa and the Pacific; they have displaced older Lucilia species in some regions due to competitive superiority.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. Abundant and thriving in human-dominated environments; expanding range.
