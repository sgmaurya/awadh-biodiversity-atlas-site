बीट वेबवर्म पतंगा / Beet Webworm

Spoladea recurvalis (Fabricius, 1798) — Crambidae

बीट वेबवर्म पतंगा — यह एक छोटी रात की पतंगा है जो चुकंदर, पालक, और दूसरी सब्जियों की पत्तियों को खाती है। इसकी सफ़ेद या हल्के भूरे रंग की पत्तियों पर जालीदार बुनावट बनी होती है। इसका कैटरपिलर हरा या पीला होता है और पत्तियों को खाता है, अक्सर अपने चारों तरफ़ एक रेशमी जाल बुनता हुआ। गर्मी के मौसम में इसके कई पीढ़ी एक साथ होती हैं। बगीचों और खेतों में यह एक मामूली पर परिचित कीट है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall moth; wingspan 20–24 mm
ColourPale greyish-white or buff with fine reticulate (net-like) markings
WingsForewings narrow with faint crosslines; hindwings pale
PatternDistinctive fine mesh or net-like pattern on forewings
LarvaGreen to yellowish-green; smooth; found within silken webbing

Field tip: A small, pale greyish moth with delicate net-like wing patterns. The larva is found on beet, chard, and related plants, often within silken webbing on the leaf surface.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: छोटी, सफ़ेद-भूरी पतंगा। पंखों पर जाली जैसा निशान। कैटरपिलर हरा, पत्तियों पर रेशमी जाल बनाता है।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements):

MeasurementRange
Wingspan20–24 mm
Body length8–10 mm
Forewing length9–11 mm

Adult description: A small crambid moth with a pale greyish-white or buff-coloured body. The forewings are narrow and elongate with fine reticulate (net-like) crosslines and a characteristic mesh pattern that gives the species its common name "webworm." The hindwings are plain pale grey or white. Antennae are filiform (thread-like). Head and thorax are pale greyish-brown.

Larval Stage

Caterpillar: Green to yellowish-green, smooth-bodied, reaching 8–10 mm in length. The caterpillar is gregarious, often found in groups within silken webbing on the leaf surface. It feeds on the underside of leaves, skeletonizing the tissue. The larva's presence is indicated by the fine silken webbing and the characteristic feeding pattern.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Stage / MonthEvent
EggLaid on host plant leaves; hatches in 3–5 days
Larva (instar 1–4)2–3 weeks on host plant; feeds within silken webbing
Prepupa1–2 days; larva stops feeding and pupates
PupaWithin silken cocoon on host plant or debris; 5–8 days in warm weather
AdultEmerges from cocoon; lifespan 2–3 weeks; multiple generations per year
Warm seasonContinuous generations from April to November
हिंदी: गर्मी में तेज़ी से पीढ़ियाँ आती हैं। कैटरपिलर 2–3 हफ़्तों में बड़ा हो जाता है, फिर प्यूपा बनता है।

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Larval hosts:

  • Beet (Beta vulgaris)
  • Chard and Swiss chard (Beta vulgaris subsp.)
  • Spinach (Spinacia oleracea)
  • Amaranth (Amaranthus spp.)
  • Lambsquarters (Chenopodium album)
  • Purslane (Portulaca oleracea)

Food preference: All parts of the plant are susceptible, but tender young leaves are preferred.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoid wasps: Small wasps lay eggs in caterpillars and pupae.
  • Parasitoid flies: Tachinid flies parasitize larvae.
  • Ground beetles: Feed on eggs and small larvae.
  • Spiders: Hunt adult moths and larvae.
  • Birds: Small insectivorous birds feed on larvae and adults.
  • Entomopathogenic fungi: Beauveria and Metarhizium species.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Pest status. The Beet Webworm is a minor to moderate pest of beet, chard, and related crops. Larval damage is characterized by the skeletonization of leaves and the presence of silken webbing. Economic damage is usually minor unless populations are high or the crop is young.

Today. Common in vegetable gardens and beet-growing regions. Populations are generally kept in check by natural enemies, and it is rarely a major pest requiring control measures.

Research direction (a lead, not a result). Biology and seasonal phenology of Spoladea recurvalis in eastern Uttar Pradesh; host-plant variety and pest severity relationships; natural enemy complex and biological control potential.

हिंदी: चुकंदर और पालक के बागों में आम है। ज़्यादा नुकसान नहीं होता, अगर बहुत ज़्यादा न हों।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Expected on beet, chard, and spinach crops throughout Basti district and eastern Uttar Pradesh, particularly during warm months (April–November). The moth is present year-round in warm climates but shows peak activity during summer and monsoon. Larval damage is visible on host plant leaves as silken webbing and skeletonized tissue, particularly on the undersides of leaves. The first local record would be a photograph of the larva or adult with place and date.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across tropical and subtropical regions of Africa, Asia, and the Pacific Islands. Distributed throughout the Indian subcontinent.

In India: Common throughout the country in vegetable-growing regions and areas with beet and chard cultivation.

In Uttar Pradesh: Present in all vegetable-growing districts, particularly in areas with beet and chard crops.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in vegetable gardens and beet-growing areas.

Research Questions शोध प्रश्न

Anyone can investigate these | कोई भी इन प्रश्नों की जाँच कर सकता है

  1. How many larval generations occur in a single beet-growing season in eastern UP? / एक मौसम में कितनी पीढ़ियाँ आती हैं? — Grow beets from seed; count cohorts monthly.
  2. Which host plant is preferred when multiple suitable plants are available? — Plant beet, chard, spinach, and amaranth side by side; count larval damage.
  3. What percentage of pupae are parasitized in field-grown beet crops? — Collect pupae; rear in containers; record parasite emergence.
  4. How does pesticide spray affect moth abundance on beet crops? — Compare density before and after spray.
  5. Can cultural practices such as crop rotation reduce pest pressure? — Compare beet fields with and without rotation.

Field rule: A pale greyish-white small moth with delicate net-like pattern on the wings, found on or near beet, chard, or spinach plants, is likely the Beet Webworm.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Fabricius in 1798 in Supplementum Entomologiae Systematicae from specimens collected in Asia. Type locality is Asia. The genus Spoladea was established by Guenée; the specific epithet recurvalis is Latin, referring to some feature of the wing pattern.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Lepidoptera, family Crambidae (grass moths and relatives). The Crambidae are characterized by the porrect labial palps (forward-projecting mouthparts) visible in adults.

Phylogenetic notes. Spoladea recurvalis is placed within the subfamily Spilomelinae and is related to other phytophagous crambids.

IUCN status rationale. Not formally assessed. Abundant and widely distributed; no conservation concerns.
