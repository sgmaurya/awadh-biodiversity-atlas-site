ग्रेटर स्पॉटेड ईगल / Greater Spotted Eagle

Clanga clanga (Pallas, 1811) — Accipitridae

ग्रेटर स्पॉटेड ईगल — यूरोप और मध्य एशिया से सर्दियों में आने वाली एक बड़ी और शक्तिशाली शिकारी चिड़िया। इसके काले भूरे पंख, सफेद धब्बे और पीली आँखें इसे पहचान देती हैं। पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक इसे बड़ी नदियों, दलदलों और खुली घास के मैदानों में तेजी से उड़ते या हवा में मँडराते हुए देखा जा सकता है। यह मछली, पक्षी और छोटे जानवरों का शिकार करता है।

Field tip: A large, dark eagle with white spots on wings and back, best seen when perched. Yellow eyes and yellow feet (feathered) distinguish it from other large eagles. In flight, shows broad wings and direct powerful beats, soaring over wetlands.

Morphology आकारिकी

Size: 65–72 cm long; females significantly larger and heavier than males.

Plumage: Overall dark brown to blackish-brown. Crown and nape darker. White spots and patches on wing coverts, back, and scapulars — more prominent in fresh plumage. Underparts dark brown. Tail dark brown.

Bill: Heavy, hooked, dark with pale cere; adapted for tearing prey.

Eyes: Large, dark brown to yellow; intense and predatory.

Legs and feet: Yellow; feathered to toes; powerful talons.

Sexes: Females significantly larger (up to 30% heavier) than males. Both sexes similar in plumage.

Juveniles: Similar to adults but duller; spots less prominent.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Call: A harsh, barking kek-kek-kek, given in aggressive contexts.
  2. Alarm call: A louder, more insistent series of calls when defending territory.
  3. Silent hunter: Usually silent while hunting.
हिंदी: तीक्ष्ण केक-केक-केक जैसी आवाज़। शिकार करते समय चुप रहता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Powerful hunter: Greater Spotted Eagles hunt by soaring over water and open ground, or from a perch, spotting and diving rapidly on prey. They feed on:

  • Fish (particularly pike and catfish)
  • Waterfowl (ducks, coots, grebes)
  • Waders (especially in shallow wetlands)
  • Small mammals (rodents, shrews, hares)
  • Occasionally large insects and frogs

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs during winter. Males and females often forage independently, meeting at communal roost sites at night.

Feeding time: Active throughout the day, hunting at dawn and dusk when prey is most active.

Roost behaviour: Nocturnal roosting in tall trees near water; roost trees are used consistently through the winter.

Predators: may take young birds. Adults have few natural predators.

Hunting strategy: Hunts from high soar, spotting prey from distance, or from prominent perch overlooking water. Dives steeply with wings folded, striking prey with talons.

हिंदी: मछली और पक्षियों का शिकार करता है। हवा में मँडराता है या पेड़ पर बैठकर शिकार करता है। रात को पेड़ों पर सोता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Breeds in Europe and Central Asia (April–August); absent during breeding season. Returns to eastern UP from October and remains through April.

Arrival and departure: First birds arrive in October; peak numbers in January–February; most depart by March–April.

Courtship: Takes place on breeding grounds. Males perform spectacular aerial displays and call frequently.

Moult: Partial moult occurs during winter; feathers renewed for return to breeding grounds.

Site fidelity: Many individuals return to the same wintering territories year after year.

हिंदी: अक्टूबर में आता है, अप्रैल तक रहता है। यूरोप में प्रजनन करता है। सर्दियों में अपने इलाके में शिकार करता है।

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: (young birds), Peregrine Falcon (chicks in nest)
  • Mammalian predators: None for adults; chicks vulnerable to wild boar and large carnivores in nest
  • Parasites: Feather parasites, blood parasites
हिंदी: बड़ी शिकारी चिड़ियाँ इसका शिकार कर सकती हैं।

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: The Greater Spotted Eagle is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (Schedule I, amended 2022). Hunting is illegal in India.

Vulnerable to persecution: Traditionally persecuted as a threat to fisheries and game birds, though actual predation on valuable species is minimal.

Fish predator: The Greater Spotted Eagle feeds on fish, including some species of commercial interest, but does not significantly impact wild fisheries due to its solitary nature and low population density.

Indicator of wetland health: As a top predator, the presence of Greater Spotted Eagles indicates reasonably healthy populations of fish and waterfowl.

Pollution sensitivity: Vulnerable to lead poisoning from ammunition in shot birds; bioaccumulation of pesticides in prey.

Historical records: Mentioned in Mughal hunting records as a game bird and as a competitor for game.

Cultural value: Recognized by birdwatchers as a rare and impressive winter visitor; conservation-sensitive species.

हिंदी: कानून से पूरी तरह सुरक्षित है। इसका शिकार करना कानूनी रूप से निषिद्ध है।

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

Not a field record. Written from published accounts for eastern Uttar Pradesh. No confirmed local sighting yet — if you have seen it, that is what the atlas needs.
अभी तक स्थानीय पुष्टि नहीं। यह विवरण प्रकाशित स्रोतों से है, किसी अवलोकन से नहीं।

Winter range: Expected in eastern Uttar Pradesh from October to April on large wetlands and open country. Documented in Bakhira Bird Sanctuary surveys (Mishra et al., 2019).

Habitat preference: Large wetlands, particularly the Ghaghra and Rapti river systems and associated lakes, rich in fish and waterfowl.

Peak occurrence: January–February, when populations from European and Central Asian breeding grounds have moved south.

Nearest published record: Recorded in Bakhira Bird Sanctuary by Mishra et al. (2019).

What a first record would be: A dated photograph showing the bird clearly in flight or perched near a wetland in a specific location (with GPS coordinates and date) submitted through the About page of this atlas. The large size, dark plumage with white spots, yellow eyes, and feathered legs are diagnostic.

हिंदी: यह पक्षी स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित है, न कि देखे गए बतौर प्रेक्षण। अक्टूबर से अप्रैल तक बड़ी नदियों और दलदलों पर देखे जा सकते हैं। पहला रिकॉर्ड के लिए स्पष्ट फोटोग्राफ चाहिए।

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds from Eastern Europe to Siberia. Winters across Europe, Africa, and Asia. Monotypic species.

In India: Distributed as a winter visitor on large river systems and wetlands, particularly in northern and eastern India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to the Ganga and its tributaries, particularly the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regular but uncommon winter visitor to Bakhira Bird Sanctuary and other large wetlands, documented in published surveys.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. The species was described by Peter Simon Pallas in 1811 in Zoographia Rosso-Asiatica, as Aquila clanga. Later moved to the genus Clanga based on molecular evidence.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Accipitriformes; family Accipitridae (hawks, eagles, and Old World vultures). The genus Clanga contains the spotted eagles, a clade of large Palearctic breeding eagles.

Phylogenetic notes. Closely related to Indian Spotted Eagle (C. hastata) and other spotted eagles; the genus Clanga forms a distinct clade within Accipitridae.

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Vulnerable (VU) globally due to population declines from habitat loss, persecution, and other threats. Populations in Central Asia have declined significantly. In India, protected under the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 (Schedule I).
