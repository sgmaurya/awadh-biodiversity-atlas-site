मंडुआ / Finger Millet

Eleusine coracana (L.) Gaertn. — Poaceae

मंडुआ — एक महत्वपूर्ण अनाज फसल है जो दक्षिण और पूर्वी भारत में मुख्य रूप से उगाई जाती है। यह एक सीधे बढ़ने वाला, कॉम्पैक्ट, छोटा घास (30–100 सेमी) है। पत्तियाँ पतली, लंबी, कठोर होती हैं। पत्तियों की कनिष्ठा सीधे स्पाइकलेट्स से बनी होती है जो 4–8 उँगलियों की तरह फैली होती हैं। दाने अत्यंत छोटे, गोल, भूरे या काले रंग के होते हैं। मंडुए की दलिया, रोटी और खीर बनाई जाती है। इसे पशु के लिए चारा भी दिया जाता है। पोषक तत्वों से भरपूर है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
FormCompact, erect annual grass, 30–100 cm tall, tufted at base
LeavesAlternate, linear, 15–45 cm long, 0.5–1.2 cm wide, rigid, pungent
InflorescenceUniquely finger-like panicles; 4–8 spike-like primary branches (digit-like) radiating from the top, 3–10 cm long
SpikeletsSmall, sessile, arranged in two rows on each digit
GrainsExtremely small, round, 0.2 cm diameter, red-brown or black; hard endosperm

Field tip: A compact grass with stiff linear leaves and distinctive finger-like panicles (4–8 digits spreading from the top) bearing tiny dark grains.

Root system: Fibrous roots with adventitious roots; moderately deep-rooted in dry conditions.

Culm: Solid, smooth, with distinct nodes; sometimes branched at base.

Associated Species / Interactions सहजीवी संबंध

  • Bird seed: Mature finger-like panicles are eaten by munias, sparrows, and finches
  • Insect host: Hosts various grain moths and beetles
  • Fodder value: Entire plant, especially when green, provides good livestock feed
  • Crop rotation: Part of monsoon and winter crop rotations

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Sowing: Sown in June–July (Kharif) or September–October (Rabi)
  • Germination: Emerges within 5–7 days with adequate moisture
  • Vegetative growth: Grows through July–August or October–November; develops leaves and tillers
  • Panicle emergence: Finger-like panicles emerge in August–September or November–December
  • Flowering: Spikelets on each digit mature over several days
  • Grain maturation: Grains mature through September–October or January–February
  • Harvest: Mature panicles are harvested in September–October or January–February; threshed to yield grain

Pests and Diseases कीट और रोग

  • Smut: Ustilago species cause grain blackening and grain loss
  • Leaf spot: Bipolaris and Alternaria cause small necrotic spots on leaves
  • Shoot fly: Atherigona larvae feed on seedling growing points
  • Armyworms: Occasional defoliation in monsoon

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Nutritious cereal grain crop.

  • Grain preparation: Grains are ground into fine flour for making roti, porridge, and traditional flatbreads (ragi dosa in South India)
  • Nutritional value: Rich in protein, fiber, calcium, and iron; higher calcium content than most cereals
  • Baby food: Finger millet flour is used to make nutritious porridge for infants and young children
  • Traditional dishes: Cooked whole grains eaten as khichdi or rice-like preparations
  • Desserts: Ground flour used for sweet preparations like halwa, laddu, and kheer
  • Fodder: Green plants provide good livestock feed
  • Cultivars: Varieties include VL Mandua-105, K-7, and local landraces

Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use

  • Calcium source: Considered a rich source of calcium in traditional diets
  • Lactating mother food: Given to nursing mothers for nutritional support
  • Strength food: Traditionally given to children and elderly for physical strength
  • Festival food: Part of traditional festival preparations

Eleusine coracana is expected as a monsoon and winter cereal crop in Basti district. Look for it in fields from June through October or from September through February, recognizable by compact grass plants with distinctive finger-like grain panicles. The first local record would be a photograph with date and location.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Domesticated in Africa; now cultivated in tropical Asia, with major cultivation in India and Africa.

In India: Widely cultivated in South India, parts of North India, and the Northeast; major producer states include Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Uttarakhand.

In Uttar Pradesh: Cultivated in limited areas, mainly in foothills and plane districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Cultivated resident; grown by farmers in monsoon and winter seasons.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus first described the species as Cynosurus coracanus in 1753. Justus Gaertner transferred it to Eleusine coracana in 1788. The genus name Eleusine is from Eleusis, ancient Greece, where Demeter was worshipped. The specific epithet coracana is from Sanskrit kurakkan, the Indian name for the crop.

Subspecies. Monotypic; cultivars vary by grain color (white, red, brown, black) and plant height (dwarf, tall).

Family and order placement. Order Poales, family Poaceae (grass family), tribe Cynotinae.

Phylogenetic notes. Eleusine coracana was domesticated in Africa, likely in the Ethiopian highlands, from the wild grass Eleusine africana, over 5,000 years ago, and spread to Asia through ancient trade routes.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated. As a widely cultivated crop, it faces no conservation threats.
