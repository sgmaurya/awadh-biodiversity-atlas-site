लार्ज कुक्कूश्राइक / Large Cuckooshrike

Coracina macei (Sykes, 1832) — Campephagidae

लार्ज कुक्कूश्राइक — भूरे रंग की मझोली चिड़िया, पेड़ों की पत्तियों में छिपी रहती है। पपीहे की तरह देखने में लगती है, लेकिन काली चोंच नहीं, मोटी काली चोंच है। पेड़ों की कलियाँ और फल खाती है, खासकर बरसात में कीड़ों का शिकार करती है।

Field tip: A stocky, grey-brown perching bird of tree canopies and mid-storey. Hunts caterpillars by gleaning leaves and small branches, moving deliberately through foliage. Often silent for long periods; gives low guttural calls. Most active at dawn and dusk in summer months.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

MeasurementRange
Total length250–280 mm

Size: 25–28 cm long. A large member of the cuckooshrike family but smaller than many forest eagles and barbets.

Plumage: Grey-brown overall, with a slightly darker crown and nape. Underparts paler grey-buff. Wing coverts and primaries dark brown with pale fringes, giving a barred appearance in flight. Tail long, graduated, blackish-brown with white spots on the inner webs of tail feathers, visible in flight or when spread.

Head: Rounded; a slight erectile crest visible when alert. Eyes dark. Loral area (space between eye and bill) slightly paler than crown.

Bill: Stout, moderately hooked, suitable for gleaning caterpillars. Dark grey or slate-black.

Legs and feet: Short, dark grey.

Sexes: Monomorphic (males and females similar in plumage).

Juveniles: Similar to adults but duller; wings and tail less distinctly marked.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Contact call: A low, guttural krrr-krrr or churr, repeated slowly while feeding.
  2. Alarm call: A harsh kark-kark-kark, given when disturbed.
  3. Song: A series of whistled notes, low and melodious, rarely heard.
हिंदी: गहरी, कर्कश आवाज़। बहुत कम गाता है।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Foraging behaviour: Solitary or in pairs. Gleaning specialist: moves methodically through the foliage of trees, picking insects and caterpillars directly from leaves and small branches. Often hangs from branches while probing into clusters of leaves. Silent and inconspicuous during the breeding season.

Habitat preference: Found in deciduous and mixed dry forest, riparian woodland with large trees, mango and fruit orchards, and gardens with mature shade trees. Prefers mid-to-upper storey of trees.

Daily activity: Crepuscular (dawn and dusk feeder); most active at dawn and in late afternoon. Becomes more active during the monsoon when caterpillars are abundant.

Social behaviour: Solitary or in breeding pairs. Pairs may remain together year-round in suitable habitat. Not gregarious; does not join mixed-species feeding flocks.

Predators: Raptors including Crested Serpent Eagle and Shikra. Nest predation by crows and monitor lizards.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Avian predators: Crested Serpent Eagle, Shikra, Besra, Sharp-sinned Hawk
  • Nest predators: Crows, jungle crow, monitor lizard (Varanus bengalensis)

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Beneficial role in orchards and gardens. The Large Cuckooshrike is an important natural control agent for fruit crop pests. In mango and other fruit orchards, it gleans caterpillars and other leaf-eating insects that would otherwise damage foliage and flowers. Its gleaning habit — extracting insects directly from leaves — is more selective than spray pesticides and does not harm beneficial insects.

Protected species. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is illegal.

Cultural significance: Mentioned in local folklore as a quiet, industrious bird of the orchards. Named papihri in local Bhojpuri and Awadhi speech.

This species is NOT a field record. The distribution below is based on published sources describing where Large Cuckooshrike should be looked for in the Basti–Ayodhya region.

Habitat expectation: Expected in any deciduous or riparian woodland with mature shade trees. Most common during the monsoon (June–August) when caterpillar abundance peaks. Present year-round in areas with suitable fruiting trees.

Habitat details: Look in mango groves, fruit orchards with large old trees, riparian woodlands along river margins, and gardens with large shade trees such as neem, pipal, or fig. Most visible in early morning or late afternoon when actively foraging.

Seasonal variation: Density increases during June–August when caterpillar populations explode. Remains resident even in marginal habitats during winter.

GBIF records: GBIF holds published records from the wider Awadh–Purvanchal plain, none yet from Basti–Ayodhya.

What a first record would be: A photograph with clear date and location showing the bird in a mango grove, fruit orchard, or riparian woodland, submitted through the About page of this atlas.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: The Indian subcontinent from the Himalayan foothills south to the Deccan, and eastward through the Gangetic plains to eastern India and Bangladesh.

In India: Widespread in peninsular and northern India; common in deciduous and riparian woodland.

In Uttar Pradesh: Throughout the state in suitable habitat; more common in eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Expected in deciduous forest patches, riparian woodland along the Ghaghra and smaller tributaries, fruit orchards, and gardens with mature shade trees.

Field rule: A large (25–28 cm), uniformly grey-brown perching bird with a stout hooked bill and rounded shape, gleaning caterpillars in tree foliage, is a Large Cuckooshrike. Smaller shrike species are brightly coloured or patterned and sit on exposed perches.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Sykes in 1832 in the Proceedings of the Committee of Science and Correspondence of the Zoological Society of London, p. 90, as Corvus macei (Corvus being the old genus for many large passerines). Type locality: Deccan peninsula, India.

Subspecies. Three subspecies are recognised:

  • C. m. macei Sykes, 1832 — Western and peninsular India (occurs in Uttar Pradesh)
  • C. m. parvula Hume, 1874 — Assam and adjacent areas
  • C. m. mandellii Sharpe, 1888 — Myanmar

The population in Uttar Pradesh belongs to C. m. macei.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Campephagidae (cuckooshrikes and minivets). The genus Coracina is one of the largest in the family, distributed across Asia, Africa, and Australasia.

Phylogenetic notes. Coracina macei is closely related to other Asian Coracina species, particularly C. javensis (Java Cuckooshrike) and C. melanoptera (Black-capped Cuckooshrike).

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern globally. Populations are stable and the species is adaptable to human-modified habitats, including fruit orchards and gardens. Protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, though hunting pressure is minimal.
