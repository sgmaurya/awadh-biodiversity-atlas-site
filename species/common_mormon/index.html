सामान्य मॉर्मन / Common Mormon

Papilio polytes Linnaeus, 1758 — Papilionidae

सामान्य मॉर्मन (कॉमन मॉर्मन) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के उद्यानों, नींबू के बागों और जंगलों में पाई जाने वाली एक बहुत ही सामान्य और बड़ी पूंछदार तितली (swallowtail butterfly) है। इस प्रजाति में नर और मादा के रूपों में अत्यधिक भिन्नता पाई जाती है। नर साधारण, चमकीले काले रंग का होता है जिसके पिछले पंखों पर सफ़ेद धब्बों की एक पट्टी (black with a white spots band) होती है। इसके विपरीत, मादा तितली अत्यंत विविधतापूर्ण (polymorphic) होती है और इसकी तीन अलग-अलग आकृतियाँ पाई जाती हैं, जिनमें से कुछ आकृतियाँ अत्यधिक ज़हरीली 'क्रिमसन रोज़' (Crimson Rose) तितली की हूबहू नकल करती हैं (Batesian mimicry)। इसकी इल्ली (caterpillar) नींबू, संतरा और मीठे नीम (कढ़ी पत्ता) की पत्तियों को खाती है, जिसके कारण इसे 'नींबू की तितली' (nimbu-titli) भी कहा जाता है। वयस्क तितलियाँ फूलों का रस पीती हैं और परागण (pollination) में सहायक होती हैं।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
WingspanLarge swallowtail butterfly; wingspan around 90–110 mm
MaleVelvet-black above; hindwing has a prominent diagonal row of creamy-white spots; has short tails
Female (Form 1)cyrus form — resembles the male, black with white spots on hindwing
Female (Form 2)stichius form — mimics the toxic Common Rose; has white stripes and bright red spots on hindwing
Female (Form 3)romulus form — mimics the toxic Crimson Rose; has extensive red and white markings on hindwing
FlightStrong, fluttering flight; stays close to the ground when searching for host plants

Field tip: Look around lemon or curry leaf bushes in village backyards. Watch for a large black butterfly fluttering around the leaves. If it is black with white spots, it could be a male or a female of form cyrus. If it has large red spots on its hindwings, check if it is a female mimicking a toxic Rose butterfly. The male is shown in the field tip.

Adult Butterfly:

  • Male: The dorsal surface of the wings is jet-black. The forewing has a row of small white spots near the margin. The hindwing has a broad, curved band composed of 5–7 oblong creamy-white spots. It has a well-developed, narrow tail extending from the hindwing.
  • Female forms:
  1. Form cyrus: Morphologically identical to the male, differing only in having a tiny red spot at the anal angle of the hindwing.
  2. Form stichius: Black head and body. The hindwing has five white central streaks and a series of large, brick-red spots along the outer margin, closely mimicking the toxic Common Rose (Pachliopta aristolochiae).
  3. Form romulus: The forewing has grey-white streaks between the veins. The hindwing has a large white central patch and deep crimson-red spots, mimicking the toxic Crimson Rose (Pachliopta hector).

Egg: Spherical, smooth, pale yellow or orange-yellow, laid singly on the upper or lower surface of young host leaves.

Larval (Caterpillar) Stage

The caterpillar undergoes five growth stages (instars), showing a dramatic change in camouflage.

  • Early instars (1st to 4th): Dark brown-black with irregular white patches, looking exactly like a fresh bird dropping. This mimicry protects them from birds while they are small and slow.
  • Fifth (final) instar: Turns bright grass-green. It develops two large, black-and-white eyespots (eyelike markings) on the metathorax, connected by a dark band. This makes the front of the caterpillar look like a small snake head. If threatened, it shoots out a fleshy, Y-shaped orange organ (osmeterium) from behind its head, which releases a strong, sour, pineapple-like smell containing organic acids to scare away ants and wasps.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

  • Batesian Mimicry: The female polymorphism is a classic example of Batesian mimicry. The toxic model butterflies (Crimson Rose and Common Rose) feed on toxic Aristolochia plants as caterpillars and accumulate cardenolides, making them lethal to birds. The harmless female Common Mormon mimics these models, gaining protection from predators who associate their red-and-white patterns with toxicity.
  • Mud-puddling: Males are frequently observed mud-puddling. They gather on wet sand near riverbanks or village drains, sucking up water to extract dissolved sodium minerals, which are transferred to the female during mating to increase egg viability.
  • Flight style: Males fly rapidly in search of mates, while egg-laying females fly slowly, hovering carefully around citrus leaves to check them with chemical receptors on their feet.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Reproduction: Multivoltine (produces 5 to 6 generations per year in UP, breeding from spring to late autumn).
  • Pupation: The mature green caterpillar attaches itself to a citrus twig using a silk pad at the tail and a silk girdle (loop) around the middle. It moults into a green or brown chrysalis (pupa) shaped like a curved leaf.
  • Developmental times (at 28–32°C):
  • Egg stage: 3 to 5 days.
  • Caterpillar stage: 15 to 20 days.
  • Chrysalis (pupa) stage: 10 to 14 days (can undergo diapause during winter, emerging as adults in spring).

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary larval host plants (Rutaceae):

  • Cultivated Citrus: Lemon (Citrus limon), Sweet Orange (Citrus sinensis), and Lime (Citrus aurantiifolia).
  • Other hosts: Curry Leaf plant (Murraya koenigii) and Orange Jessamine (Murraya paniculata).

Adult food source:

  • Nectar from flowers, including Indian Lantana (Lantana camara), Hibiscus, and Bougainvillea.

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Parasitoids: Small wasps (such as Trichogramma species) parasitize the eggs, and ichneumonid wasps parasitize the pupa.
  • Predators: House Sparrows, Shikras, and garden lizards catch flying adults. Praying mantises and paper wasps target young caterpillars on leaves.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Minor citrus pest. The caterpillars feed on citrus leaves, occasionally defoliating young lime and lemon saplings in nurseries. However, on mature orchard trees, their damage is minimal and rarely requires chemical treatment.

Educational value: They are valued in school gardens for teaching students about butterfly metamorphosis, Batesian mimicry, and insect-plant interactions.

A common resident throughout the Basti district. They are regularly observed in village orchards, backyard gardens, and nurseries in the Harraiya, Vikramjot, and Basti blocks. Homeowners with curry leaf bushes (kadi patta) regularly report finding the bird-dropping-like caterpillars on the leaves.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia, Southeast Asia, and parts of East Asia.

In India: Widespread and common throughout all plains, hills, and island regions.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common state-wide in all wooded, agricultural, and urban districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regularly recorded in all blocks where citrus host plants are grown.

Field rule: Large black swallowtail butterfly with cream-white spots on hindwings (male) or a female mimicking toxic red-spotted Rose butterflies, whose caterpillars eat citrus leaves, is the Common Mormon.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Carl Linnaeus described the Common Mormon as Papilio polytes in 1758 in his Systema Naturae (ed. 10, vol. 1, p. 460). The type locality was Asia. The genus name Papilio is Latin for a butterfly. The specific name polytes comes from the Ancient Greek polutes, meaning many-citizen or polymorphic, referencing the multiple female forms.

Subspecies. Several subspecies are recognized. The subspecies resident in India and UP is:

SubspeciesAuthorityRangeNotes
P. p. romulusFabricius, 1793India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Sri LankaUP subspecies; has highly developed female polymorphism

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Lepidoptera, superfamily Papilionoidea, family Papilionidae (swallowtails), subfamily Papilioninae.

Phylogenetic notes. Papilio polytes belongs to the polytes species group, representing a lineage that has evolved complex mimicry systems to survive high bird predation in tropical Asia.

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) by the IUCN Red List. It has a massive range and is highly secure.
