पीली थैली मकड़ी / Yellow Sac Spider

Cheiracanthium melanostomum (Thorell, 1895) — Cheiracanthiidae

पीली थैली मकड़ी (येलो सैक स्पाइडर / काली-मुँह वाली मकड़ी) — पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश के धान के खेतों, बगीचों और झाड़ियों में व्यापक रूप से पाई जाने वाली एक छोटी, सक्रिय और रात में शिकार करने वाली मकड़ी है। इसका शरीर हल्का पीला-हरा (pale yellow-green) होता है, और इसके जबड़ों के सिरे विशिष्ट रूप से काले रंग के (black-tipped mouthparts) होते हैं, जिसे इसकी वैज्ञानिक प्रजाति के नाम 'मेलेनोस्टोमम' (melanostomum = काले मुँह वाली) से दर्शाया गया है। यह शिकार पकड़ने के लिए कोई जाला नहीं बनाती (does not build a capture web), बल्कि रात के समय पौधों पर घूम-घूम कर सक्रिय रूप से कीटों का शिकार करती है। दिन के समय आराम करने और अंडे देने के लिए यह पत्ती को मोड़कर या सिल्क से एक ट्यूब जैसी थैली (silken retreating sac) बनाती है। यह खेतों में हानिकारक कीटों को नियंत्रित करने में महत्वपूर्ण भूमिका निभाती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

FeatureDescription
SizeSmall spider; female body length around 8–10 mm, male slightly smaller
ColourationUniform pale yellow-green to straw-yellow body; carapace is slightly darker
CheliceraeBlack-tipped jaws (melanostomum = black-mouthed), contrasting with the yellow head
No Capture WebFound wandering on foliage at night; does not spin horizontal or vertical orb webs
Daytime RetreatHides inside a small, white, tube-like silk sac built inside curled or rolled leaves

Field tip: Look in home gardens or rice fields. Inspect folded leaves of plants. If you find a leaf rolled up and sealed with dense white silk, peel it open gently. You will find a pale yellow spider with black mouthparts resting inside its silk sac. The female is larger than the male and is shown in the field tip.

Adult Spider:

  • Cephalothorax: Slightly longer than wide, yellowish-orange to light green, with a smooth surface and no distinct markings. The eyes are small, arranged in two rows of four.
  • Chelicerae (Jaws): Strong, projecting forward, pale yellow at the base but turning shiny brownish-black at the tips (distinctive "black mouth").
  • Abdomen: Oval, slightly elongated, pale yellow-green, containing a faint central heart-mark on the front half.
  • Legs: Long, pale yellow, with short black spines. The first pair of legs is significantly longer than the others, used to feel for prey in the dark.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

  • Active Nocturnal Hunter: Unlike web-spinning spiders that wait for prey, this species is a wandering hunter. It emerges from its retreat sac at dusk, running across leaves and stems to actively stalk and pounce on small insects.
  • Daytime Sac building: During the day, it protects itself from heat and predators by building a "sac retreat." It uses silk to tie a leaf edge together or curls a grass blade into a tube, lining the interior with dense, soft white silk.
  • Biocontrol values: It is a dominant predator in UP agricultural ecosystems, consuming high counts of plant hoppers, caterpillars, and aphids daily.

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

  • Reproduction: Breeds during the post-monsoon months (August to October).
  • Maternal care: The female builds a large, double-walled silk retreat sac where she deposits a round, yellow egg sac containing 40–80 eggs. She stays inside the sac with her eggs, guarding them continuously without feeding.
  • Matriphagy: In some related Cheiracanthium species, the mother may allow her newly hatched spiderlings to consume her body as their first meal (matriphagy) before they disperse, ensuring their survival.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

Primary agricultural insect prey:

  • Rice pests: Brown planthoppers (Nilaparvata lugens), green leafhoppers, and young larvae of the Yellow Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas).
  • Garden pests: Aphids, whiteflies, and young caterpillars of the Tobacco Cutworm (Spodoptera litura).

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Predators: Mud-dauber wasps hunt and paralyze them to stock nests. Jumping spiders (Salticidae) and garden lizards feed on wandering adults.
  • Birds: Insectivorous birds (like the Plain Prinia Prinia inornata) search rolled leaves to pull them out of their silk sacs.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Crucial biocontrol agent. Yellow Sac Spiders are highly beneficial in rice fields. They actively hunt crop-damaging bugs at night, reducing the need for chemical sprays.

Venom safety (No venom alarmism):

  • The Bite Issue: Cheiracanthium melanostomum can bite humans if it is accidentally pressed or trapped against the skin (such as inside folded clothing, shoes, or bedsheets).
  • Bite Symptoms: The bite causes immediate, sharp, localized pain (similar to a wasp sting), followed by mild swelling and redness. In rare cases, a small localized skin sore (ulceration) can develop at the bite site.
  • Medical Context: The bite is NOT medically dangerous or life-threatening. The pain and swelling fade within 24–48 hours. The spider should be treated as a helpful insect eater rather than a dangerous threat.

A common resident throughout the Basti district, regularly recorded in paddy fields, vegetable gardens, and guava orchards in the Harraiya, Basti, and Vikramjot blocks. Farmers often find their white silk retreats when handling harvested straw or weeding fields.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Widespread across South Asia (India, Bangladesh, Myanmar) and parts of Southeast Asia.

In India: Widespread in agricultural and forested zones across all states.

In Uttar Pradesh: Common state-wide in all vegetable and grain-farming districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Regularly recorded in all farming blocks.

Field rule: Small, pale yellow-green spider (8–10 mm) with distinct jet-black-tipped jaws, hiding in a tube-like white silk sac inside a rolled leaf during the day, is the Yellow Sac Spider (C. melanostomum). Note: C. triviale and C. indicum are co-occurring sister species in UP, separable only by microscopy.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Tamerlan Thorell described the Yellow Sac Spider as Eutitha melanostoma in 1895 in his Descriptive Catalogue of the Spiders of Burma (p. 248). The species was later placed in the genus Cheiracanthium by Simon. The genus name Cheiracanthium is derived from the Greek cheir (hand) and akantha (spine), referencing the spiny legs. The specific name melanostomum means black-mouthed, referencing the dark jaws.

Subspecies. Monotypic — no subspecies are currently recognised.

Family and order placement. Placed in the order Araneae, infraorder Araneomorphae, family Cheiracanthiidae (sac spiders), subfamily Cheiracanthiinae.

Phylogenetic notes. Cheiracanthium melanostomum belongs to the Old World clade of wandering spiders. Three Cheiracanthium species are documented in UP — C. melanostomum, C. triviale, and C. indicum — and they are field-inseparable without microscopic examination. GBIF records for this species group may also include synonymized names from older literature (e.g., C. punctorium, which is a Eurasian species not established in UP). C. melanostomum is reliably identified by its consistently jet-black cheliceral tips (melanostomum = black-mouthed).

IUCN status rationale. Not evaluated (NE) by the IUCN Red List. It has a secure and stable population.
