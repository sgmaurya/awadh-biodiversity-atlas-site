नदी टिटहरी / River Lapwing

Vanellus duvaucelii (Lessons, 1831) — Charadriidae

नदी टिटहरी — यह चिड़िया पूर्वी उत्तर प्रदेश में सिर्फ नदियों के रेतीले किनारों (sandbars) पर पाई जाती है। यह घाघरा और राप्ती नदियों के नंगे रेत और बजरी के टीलों पर रहती है, जहाँ कोई पेड़-पौधा नहीं होता। यह बहुत छोटी टिटहरी है, और इसके पंखों पर तीव्र आवाजें होती हैं। इसकी रक्षा करना आवश्यक है क्योंकि नदियों को बाँधों और बाढ़ नियंत्रण से नुकसान हो रहा है।

Field tip: The River Lapwing is the smallest lapwing in the region and is strictly found on exposed river sandbanks and shingle islands. The spiky crown and small size are diagnostic. It is noisy and territorial, giving harsh calls. Look on sandbars of the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers, not on cultivated land or in grassland like other lapwings.

Size: 22–25 cm long; wingspan 50–58 cm. Weight 70–100 g. The smallest lapwing in the region.

Plumage: Crown and upper back grey-brown to brown. Lower back and rump darker. Wings brown with prominent rufous patch. Underparts white. The most distinctive feature is the extremely spiky, prominent crown feathers — appearing like a small crest or crown.

Head: Small with pronounced spiky crown feathers (almost always erect, giving the head a distinctive "prickly" appearance). Face brownish.

Bill: Small, relatively short, dark coloured.

Legs and feet: Long, yellow or golden-yellow; placed far forward on body (typical of plovers). This allows rapid running.

Eyes: Dark; positioned to look downward while foraging.

Sexes: Females slightly duller than males. Plumage differences subtle.

Juveniles: Duller, with softer crown feathers initially.

Vocalisations. Highly vocal and territorial:

  1. Alarm and display call: A loud, harsh kiip-kiip or kit-kit-kit, repeated rapidly; frequency 2.0–4.0 kHz. Given from perches and in flight.
  2. Contact call: Sharp peeping between pair members.
  3. Dive display: Complex vocalizations during courtship dives.

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Ground forager: River Lapwings feed on:

  • Insects and insect larvae (particularly aquatic insects)
  • Small invertebrates
  • Occasionally small crustaceans
  • Seeds and plant matter

Feeding technique: Runs in short bursts on open ground, stopping abruptly to pick insects from the surface or probe in soft sand/mud. Uses visual foraging rather than tactile.

Social behaviour: Solitary or in pairs; highly territorial. Males defend feeding and breeding territories aggressively.

Habitat specialization: Strictly dependent on exposed river sandbanks, shingle bars, and islands with minimal or no vegetation. Avoids vegetated banks and cultivated land.

Breeding strategy: Monogamous, pairs remain together year-round. Highly territorial and aggressive toward intruders.

Temporal patterns: Year-round resident where suitable habitat exists. More vocal during breeding season (February–May and August–October).

Cryptic nesting: Nests on bare sand/shingle; eggs are camouflaged. Both parents incubate and defend territory fiercely.

Predators: Crows, monitor lizards, monitor animals, occasionally raptors; flooding and human disturbance at nesting sites.

Conservation concern: Highly vulnerable to river management practices (dams, barrages, flow regulation) that eliminate suitable sandbank habitat.

हिंदी: नदी के रेतीले किनारों पर एकमात्र पक्षी है। बहुत आक्रामक और क्षेत्रीय है। बाढ़ नियंत्रण से घोंसले में पानी आ जाता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Resident: Present year-round on suitable river sandbanks in eastern UP; no migration, but local movements in response to water levels.

Breeding: Two main breeding seasons: February–May and August–October (pre-monsoon and post-monsoon). Peak during months of lower water levels when sandbars are exposed.

Nesting: Ground nest — a simple scrape in sand or shingle on exposed sandbank or island. Minimal lining; eggs are camouflaged by their colouration. Nest placement on open sand leaves eggs vulnerable.

Eggs: 3–4 buff or olive eggs with dark markings. Both parents incubate ~24 days. Chicks precocial; leave nest within hours.

Chicks: Precocial but depend on parents for protection and guidance. Fledge at ~35 days.

Roosting: On sandbars or islands; prefers open sand with no cover.

Moulting: Complex moult pattern; begins post-breeding season.

Water level dependency: Breeding success is directly tied to water levels — too high inundates nests; too low exposes chicks to predators.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected species: Listed in Schedule IV of the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, amended 2022. Additionally, the species is Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List globally due to widespread habitat loss.

Vulnerable status (global): The River Lapwing's vulnerability is driven by:

  • Dam construction eliminating sandbank habitat
  • Water flow regulation preventing natural sandbar formation
  • Sand mining from rivers
  • Flooding of nesting sites during monsoon
  • Human disturbance at nesting sites

Habitat dependence: Unlike other lapwings that can adapt to cultivated fields and disturbed areas, River Lapwing requires pristine, undisturbed river systems with natural sandbar formation.

Range contraction: Populations have declined across its range due to river management practices.

Conservation priority: Urgent habitat conservation needed in eastern UP to maintain breeding populations.

Expected Locally स्थानीय रूप से अपेक्षित

This species is RANGE-ONLY. It is expected in eastern UP ONLY on the sandbanks of the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers, not in cultivated land, grassland, or other habitats like other lapwing species.

Strict habitat requirement: River Lapwings are found exclusively on exposed river sandbanks, shingle islands, and unvegetated river margins. They do NOT occur in fields, on cultivated land, or in wetlands with vegetation, unlike other lapwing species.

Distribution along rivers: Expected wherever the Ghaghra and Rapti rivers have exposed sandbanks with minimal vegetation cover.

Seasonal availability: Present year-round but more visible and vocal during breeding seasons (February–May and August–October) when sandbanks are most exposed due to lower water levels.

Habitat vulnerability: Sandbank habitat is ephemeral and dependent on natural river dynamics. Barrages, dams, and water management practices that prevent natural sandbar formation have eliminated much suitable habitat.

Nearest published record: The Ghaghra and Rapti river systems, particularly around Balrampur district and areas with active sandbank formation, historically hosted breeding populations. Recent records are scarce, indicating population decline.

What a first record would be: A dated photograph clearly showing the small spiky-crowned lapwing on a river sandbank, with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page. Records from specific river sections with GPS coordinates are valuable for conservation.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Indian subcontinent, particularly Indo-Gangetic plains. Monotypic species (single subspecies).

In India: Found on suitable river systems throughout the Indo-Gangetic plains, with populations in Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and Assam. Distribution is fragmented and populations are declining.

In Uttar Pradesh: Restricted to major river systems, particularly the Ganges, Gomti, Ghaghra, and Rapti. Populations have declined significantly.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Historically present on the Ghaghra and Rapti river sandbanks. Current status needs assessment; believed to be declining.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by René Primevère Lesson in 1831 as Charadrius duvaucelii. Type locality is India.

Subspecies. Monotypic — Vanellus duvaucelii duvaucelii. No subspecies are recognised.

Family and order placement. Order Charadriiformes; family Charadriidae (plovers and lapwings). The genus Vanellus contains lapwings found globally; V. duvaucelii is endemic to the Indian subcontinent.

Phylogenetic notes. Molecular studies confirm Vanellus duvaucelii as distinct from other Vanellus species. The adaptation to river-specific nesting (sandy substrates, open habitats) represents a specialized ecological niche.

IUCN status rationale. The species is assessed as Vulnerable (VU) on the IUCN Red List. Primary threats are:

  • Loss and degradation of river sandbank habitat due to dam construction and water regulation
  • Sand mining
  • Flooding of nesting sites during monsoon
  • Human disturbance at breeding sites
  • Climate change affecting monsoon patterns and river flow

The species' dependence on specific, limited habitat makes it particularly vulnerable to river management practices.
