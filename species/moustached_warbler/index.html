मूस्टैच्ड वार्बलर / Moustached Warbler

Acrocephalus melanopogon (Temminck, 1823) — Acrocephalidae

मूस्टैच्ड वार्बलर — यह एक छोटी, भूरी गायन चिड़िया है जो सर्दियों में यूरोप और मध्य एशिया से भारत आती है। इसका नाम नर के चेहरे पर काली "मूंछें" (काली लोरल पट्टियों) के कारण है। ये रीड़ों में छिपती रहती हैं और रीड़ों के गहरे भाग में रहना पसंद करती हैं। सर्दियों में कम गाती हैं पर उड़ते समय कड़कड़ाती आवाज़ निकालती हैं।

Field tip: A small brown warbler in dense reed beds. Males are identifiable by the distinctive black "moustache" or loral stripe. Very similar to other reed warblers but the black facial marks on males are diagnostic. Stays in the densest part of reed beds.

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics:

Size: 12–13 cm long; wingspan 17–22 cm. Weight 10–16 g. A small warbler.

Plumage: Entire plumage warm brown or buff-brown. Crown slightly darker than back. Back and rump warm brown. Wings darker brown. Tail brown, notched at the tip. Underparts buff-white to pale cream, with buff-brown wash on flanks and undertail coverts.

Male facial pattern: Distinctive diagnostic feature — black loral patch extending from lore to eye (the "moustache"), and dark eye-stripe or dark eye-line extending backward. This feature is absent or much reduced in females.

Female plumage: Similar to male but lacks the bold black facial marks, showing just a pale loral patch.

Legs and feet: Thin, pale brown or flesh-coloured.

Bill: Thin, pointed, dark with pale base — typical warbler bill for gleaning insects.

Eyes: Dark iris; alert.

Vocalisations. Call repertoire:

  1. Song: Rich, continuous warbling song with repeated phrases. Heard mainly during breeding season and early spring migration.
  2. Flight call: Sharp, crackling tzik-tzik-tzik or zerp-zerp, given in flight or when flushed.
  3. Contact calls: Soft chirps between birds.
हिंदी: सुंदर गायन करता है। उड़ते समय तेजी से "त्जिक-त्जिक" की आवाज़। नर की काली मूंछें पहचान का अच्छा निशान हैं।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकी

Reed-dwelling gleaner: Moustached Warblers are entirely dependent on dense reed beds and tall wetland vegetation. They cling to reed stems and leaves, gleaning insects methodically.

Feeding: Entirely insectivorous:

  • Small insects and insect larvae gleaned from reed stems and leaves
  • Tiny invertebrates picked from reeds
  • Occasionally tiny berries

Feeding behaviour: Active gleaner, constantly moving through reed stems, picking insects from vegetation. Often hops vertically up reed stems.

Skulking behaviour: Extremely skulking; stays deep in reed beds. Often heard but difficult to see well. More cryptic than Blyth's Reed Warbler.

Territorial: Establish individual wintering territories within reed beds. Males sing from elevated reed perches, especially at dawn and dusk in early spring.

Alert behaviour: Wary; give sharp flight calls when flushed from reeds.

Flight: Direct flight on whirring wings. Distinctive crackling flight calls help identification.

हिंदी: घनी रीड़ों में छिपा रहता है। रीड़ों की पूरी लंबाई पर चढ़ता है। कीड़े को धीरे से चुनता है।

Life Cycle / Phenology जीवन चक्र

Winter visitor: Arrives in eastern UP from October, with main arrival November. Departs February–April, with most birds gone by April.

Breeding range: Southern Europe, Mediterranean region, and central Asia. Breeds April–August in extensive reed beds.

Migration: Long-distance migrant. Nocturnal migrant, detected by flight calls.

Wintering: Solitary on individual reed beds. Return to same wintering territories year after year.

Moult: Complete moult occurs before arrival; breeding plumage acquired on wintering grounds.

Timing in UP: Early arrival October (vanguard); main arrival November; peak December–February; departure March–April.

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Protected status: All wild birds in India are protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting is illegal.

Ecological value: Important insectivore in reed beds; helps control aquatic insects.

Wetland health indicator: Presence indicates productive reed beds with dense vegetation and good insect fauna.

Reed bed ecology: Highly dependent on reed bed health; indicator species of reed bed condition.

Moustached Warbler is a winter visitor to northern India, using reed beds. GBIF holds published records from these districts. The species is expected in dense reed beds throughout eastern Uttar Pradesh, particularly at large wetlands and oxbow lakes with extensive reed beds. Moustached Warblers are highly cryptic and skulking, staying in the densest parts of reed beds, so they may be more common than records suggest; detection depends on hearing the distinctive crackling flight call or observing males with their characteristic black facial marks. Peak occurrence should be November–February. A first record would require a dated photograph with GPS coordinates and date, submitted through the About page; alternatively, a recording of the distinctive "tzik-tzik" flight call would constitute strong evidence.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Breeds in southern Europe, Mediterranean region, and central Asia. Winters across the Mediterranean, North Africa, and central Asia into India.

In India: Winter visitor to reed beds and wetlands across northern and central India.

In Uttar Pradesh: Winter visitor to reed beds and wetland margins throughout the state, particularly in eastern districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Winter visitor to extensive reed beds. Expected October–April. Often underrecorded due to cryptic behaviour; likely more common than records suggest.

Field rule: A small brown warbler in dense reed beds with males showing distinctive black "moustache" marks (loral and eye-stripe) is Moustached Warbler. The crackling "tzik-tzik" flight call and bold facial marks on males are the best identification features.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Temminck in 1823 in Planches Coloriées d'Oiseaux. Type locality: Mediterranean region.

Subspecies. Monotypic — A. melanopogon is the only recognised subspecies.

Family and order placement. Order Passeriformes; family Acrocephalidae (marsh and reed warblers). Genus Acrocephalus contains about 18 species of marsh and reed warblers, found across Europe, Asia and Africa.

Phylogenetic notes. Acrocephalus melanopogon is closely related to other Acrocephalus species, particularly other small, dense-reed-dependent warblers.

IUCN status rationale. Assessed as Least Concern (LC) globally. The global population is stable and large. The species is dependent on reed beds but is adaptable to various wetland types with dense reed vegetation.
