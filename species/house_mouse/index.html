चुहिया / House Mouse

Mus musculus Linnaeus, 1758 — Muridae

चुहिया — सबसे छोटा और सबसे आम घरेलू चूहा, जो रसोई के दराज़, अलमारियों और अनाज की थैलियों में घुसकर खाना खाता है। बिल्कुल नाज़ुक और पतला शरीर, गहरी भूरी-काली पीठ, सफ़ेद पेट, और पतली पूँछ जो शरीर की लंबाई के बराबर होती है। बहुत तेज़ सूँघने की शक्ति और तेज़ सुनवाई। रात को घर के हर कोने में दौड़ता है, अनाज और खाने की चीज़ें बर्बाद करता है, और बीमारियाँ फैलाता है। पर Barn Owl इसका मुख्य भक्षक है, और उल्लुओं की आबादी सीधे इसी चूहे की संख्या पर निर्भर करती है।

Quick Identification त्वरित पहचान

TailAbout equal to or slightly longer than head-body length; thin and naked
FurDark grey-brown dorsally; white, cream, or buff ventrally; fine and short
EarsLarge relative to head size; nearly hairless and translucent

Field tip: A tiny, delicate mouse found in kitchens, pantries, and grain stores. If you can hold it in one hand, it is a house mouse. The small size and large ears are the quickest ID. Much smaller and more graceful than even young house rats.

हिंदी — कैसे पहचानें: बहुत छोटा, नाज़ुक, भूरा-काला, बड़े कान। घर के हर कोने में दिखता है। पूँछ शरीर के बराबर पतली। घर के चूहे से छोटा।

Morphology आकारिकी

Biometrics (typical measurements for local populations):

MeasurementRange
Head-body length7–10 cm
Tail length7–10 cm
Weight20–35 g

Body: Delicate and graceful, much smaller and more slender than Rattus rattus. The dorsal fur is dark grey-brown to nearly black; the ventral fur is white, cream, or pale grey. Fur is short and fine.

Head: Small and pointed. Ears are noticeably large relative to head size—much more prominent than in house rats—and are nearly hairless, allowing blood vessels to be visible (thermoregulation). Whiskers are long and sensitive.

Eyes: Small, round, and black; good night vision; eyes shine red under torchlight.

Tail: Thin, naked (scaly), and about equal in length to the head-body (distinguished from some subspecific variants). The tail is used for balance in climbing.

Incisors: Small and continuously growing; gnaw marks are smaller and more delicate than those of house rats.

Scent glands: Numerous dorsal scent glands mark the home range.

हिंदी: बहुत नाज़ुक, छोटा सिर, बड़े कान, पतली पूँछ।

Behaviour & Ecology व्यवहार और पारिस्थितिकता

A strict commensal of humans, adapted to living entirely inside human buildings. Prefers warm, protected microclimates (kitchens, grain stores, under cupboards). It is highly nocturnal, emerging only after dark to forage.

Unlike the house rat, the house mouse is less of a climber but more agile on horizontal surfaces. It can squeeze through tiny cracks (a 6 mm gap is sufficient for an adult). Speed is not its advantage; stealth and small size are.

It is more social than the house rat; multiple individuals may live in a shared nest, and females show some maternal cooperation. Very high breeding rate: females produce 5–10 litters per year with 5–6 pups per litter.

Omnivorous but prefers cereal grains and seeds. Damage is less than house rats per individual, but overall damage can be substantial due to the higher number of individuals and their high reproductive rate.

Unlike house rats, house mice are less likely to defecate while feeding, so fecal contamination is somewhat less; however, urine contamination is still a disease vector concern.

Host Plants or Prey आहार

  • Cereal grains (wheat, rice, maize)
  • Seeds and pulses
  • Nuts and dried fruit
  • Stored legumes
  • Food scraps and kitchen waste
  • Plant roots (occasionally)
  • Insects and their larvae (opportunistically)

Predators / Natural Enemies शत्रु

  • Birds of prey: Barn Owl, Spotted Owlet, jungle owlet
  • Snakes: Common Wolf Snake, Indian Rat Snake
  • Mammals: Small Indian Mongoose, small Indian civet, feral cat, weasel
  • Insects: Rarely taken by large centipedes or spiders
  • People: Killed with traps, poisons, and stomped

Agricultural / Human Relevance कृषि प्रासंगिकता

Damage: Less per individual than house rats but still significant in the aggregate due to high numbers. In grain stores the loss is smaller than that caused by rats but still substantial. In kitchens and food stores, the tiny size allows access to containers and shelves that rats cannot reach.

Food contamination: Droppings and urine contaminate food; disease vector concern is similar to house rats but less direct due to smaller urine volume per individual.

Disease risk: The house mouse is a potential vector for leptospirosis, salmonellosis, and mouse-borne hantavirus (HFRS), though local prevalence is unknown.

Control: Similar to house rats: traps, poisons, and natural predators (Barn Owl. The extremely high reproductive rate means that chemical control without predator support will fail; the population rebounds within weeks.

Today. The house mouse persists universally. Maintaining healthy populations of Barn Owl is more effective long-term than trapping or poison.

Universal in all habitations of Basti district, particularly in kitchens, grain storage, and warehouses. More common indoors than the house rat. Seen at night in kitchens and store areas; droppings are a reliable sign of presence.

Distribution वितरण

Global range: Originally domesticated in the Fertile Crescent alongside wheat; now distributed worldwide as a commensal of humans.

In India: Universal throughout the country, from plains to mountain settlements wherever humans store grain.

In Uttar Pradesh: Universal across all districts.

In Basti district / eastern UP: Universal; present in all blocks and habitations.

Field rule: A delicate, tiny mouse with large ears, small eyes, and a thin tail found in kitchens and grain stores is a House Mouse.

Taxonomy & Classification वर्गीकरण

Original description. Described by Carl Linnaeus in 1758 in Systema Naturae, 10th edition, as Mus musculus. The epithet musculus is Latin for "little mouse."

Subspecies. Multiple subspecies are recognized; Indian populations are usually assigned to Mus musculus musculus, though M. m. molossinus (from eastern Asia) may also occur.

Family and order placement. Muridae, the family of mice and rats; order Rodentia, class Mammalia.

Phylogenetic notes. Mus musculus is among the first organisms to be domesticated alongside human agriculture in the Fertile Crescent. Genetic evidence suggests the initial domestication event occurred around 10,000 years ago. The species is now the standard laboratory mouse, with the entire genome sequenced.

IUCN status rationale. Least Concern. Extremely widespread and abundant; no conservation concern.
