सर्दियों के पक्षी कहाँ हैं: बखिरा, पार्वती अरगा और अयोध्या की झीलेंWhere the winter birds are: Bakhira, Parvati Arga and the Ayodhya jheels

Current practice · GDE-0004 · written 2026-10-24

From October to March the oxbow lakes and floodplain marshes around Basti and Ayodhya fill with geese, ducks, waders and small songbirds from Central Asia, Siberia, Europe and the Himalayas. Where the published surveys say they are, when to go, what to look for first, and what a useful record is.

Why winter सर्दियाँ ही क्यों

Every autumn, as lakes freeze across Siberia, Central Asia, Tibet and the high Himalaya, the birds that bred there come south over the mountains. The Gangetic plain is the end of the road for many of them. They arrive from late September, settle on the big open waters through December and January, and leave again between late February and April. For the six months in between, a floodplain lake near Basti holds more species of bird than at any other time of year, and most of them are birds nobody in the village sees in summer.

In the atlas these species carry migration_status: winter_visitor, a "Winter visitor" mark on their cards, and the months they are expected. The list is at Migrants and visitors.

Where कहाँ

Bakhira, Sant Kabir Nagar बखिरा

Bakhira Tal lies across the district border from Basti, between Khalilabad and Basti town. The Ramsar Information Sheet gives its area as 2,894 hectares, fed by rain and by the Ami river, and calls it the largest natural floodplain wetland of eastern Uttar Pradesh. It was declared a Ramsar site on 2 February 2022. BirdLife International's Important Bird Area factsheet estimates 40,000 to 80,000 waterbirds in winter, with red-crested pochard, northern pintail and northern shoveler the most numerous, and notes that no complete bird checklist has yet been published for the site.

The one published winter study, by Mishra, Kumar and Kumar (2019), counted birds from October 2017 to March 2018. It recorded 32 species; the most numerous were red-crested pochard, common coot, greylag goose, gadwall, garganey and common teal, with redshank and little stint the commonest waders. Counts peaked in December and January, and most birds had left by the end of March. The reed beds of Phragmites that the Ramsar sheet singles out also hold the resident grey-headed swamphens and moorhens, so the lake is never empty.

Parvati Arga, Gonda पार्वती अरगा

Two oxbow lakes near Tarabganj, about forty kilometres from Gonda on the Mankapur road, on the far side of the Ghaghra from Ayodhya. A Ramsar site since 2 December 2019 (site 2416). A year-long point-count survey from March 2024 to February 2025 by Yashmita-Ulman and Singh (2025) recorded 140 species, of which 29 were winter visitors and one, the blue-tailed bee-eater, a summer visitor. Their winter list is the best published picture of what reaches this stretch of the Ghaghra: greylag and bar-headed geese, ruddy shelduck, common and red-crested pochard, gadwall, mallard, shoveler, teal, garganey; white and black storks; black-winged stilt, greenshank, little stint and snipe; and among the small birds, barn swallow, bluethroat, and white, grey and citrine wagtails. The same survey found Egyptian vulture, sarus crane, common pochard, Indian spotted eagle and river tern among the threatened species present.

The Ayodhya jheels अयोध्या की झीलें

Ayodhya district has no bird sanctuary, but it has lakes. Yashmita-Ulman and Singh (2022) surveyed nine unprotected wetlands and analysed the six with more than a handful of birds: Udaila Talab, Sirsa Jheel, Barunshahganj Talab, Jagdishpur Jheel, Samda Jheel and the Kosiparikrama nallah. Together they held 105 species, 42 of them winter visitors. The winter list runs from greylag goose, ruddy shelduck, gadwall, mallard, pintail, shoveler, teal, garganey, common pochard and tufted duck, through grey-headed lapwing, Kentish plover, greenshank, redshank, green and wood sandpipers, little and Temminck's stints and snipe, to steppe eagle, long-legged buzzard, marsh harrier, osprey and Blyth's reed warbler. The authors describe these wetlands as isolated, disturbed and unprotected, which is exactly why a local record from any of them matters.

Closer to home और पास

Nothing has been published for the ponds and river reaches of Basti district itself. From the species' habits in the handbooks and the three surveys above, the places to try are:

  • Oxbow lakes and large taals with open water and a muddy edge: the ducks, geese and

coot. The bigger and quieter the water, the more of them.

  • Sandbars and shallows of the Ghaghra, Saryu and Rapti: ruddy shelduck, bar-headed

goose, waders, osprey over the channel, river lapwing all year.

  • Wet paddy stubble and marsh edges: snipe, wood and green sandpipers, wagtails,

bluethroat in the reeds, marsh harrier quartering low in the afternoon.

  • Village gardens, orchards and walls: black redstart bobbing on a wall, Siberian

stonechat on a fence, Blyth's reed warbler ticking in a hedge. These three ask for no journey at all.

When कब

MonthsWhat the surveys and handbooks say
Late September to OctoberFirst arrivals: wagtails, barn swallows on passage, the first teal and shoveler, garganey passing through.
NovemberGeese and the big duck rafts build up; bluethroat, redstart and stonechat settle into their winter patches.
December to JanuaryPeak. Mishra et al. (2019) found the highest counts at Bakhira in these two months. The coldest, foggiest mornings are the best.
FebruaryStill full; the birds begin to feed hard before the journey.
MarchDeparture. Most had left Bakhira by the end of March in 2018. Garganey pass north in numbers in March and April.
AprilThe last stragglers; barn swallows on passage; summer visitors (pied cuckoo, golden oriole, bee-eaters) begin to arrive.

Go at first light. The birds feed and move in the first two hours, then sit out on the open water where heat haze makes them hard to see. Stay on the bank; a group of people walking to the edge pushes every duck on the lake to the far side.

What you will see first सबसे पहले क्या दिखेगा

  1. Geese: large, grey, in talking flocks on the water or grazing young wheat at the

edge. Two black bars on a white head is bar-headed goose; a plain grey-brown bird with an orange bill is greylag.

  1. An orange duck the size of a goose, often in pairs on a sandbar: [ruddy

shelduck](../species/ruddy_shelduck/), the chakwa of the old songs.

  1. Rafts of ducks far out. Pick the easy ones first: the huge spoon bill of the

shoveler, the pin tail and chocolate head of the pintail, the orange puffball head of the red-crested pochard. The grey-brown gadwall is the one you will learn last.

  1. Small grey birds running at the water's edge: the sandpipers and stints. Start by

separating the leg colour, red (redshank) or green (greenshank), and the size.

  1. A dark bird on a wall or fence that keeps shivering its tail: [black

redstart](../species/black_redstart/). Everyone has one in the village in winter and nobody has a name for it.

For a bird you hear but cannot see, every bird page links to the Cornell Lab's Merlin.

Rules and courtesies नियम

  • Every bird named here is protected under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972. Hunting

waterfowl, once common on these lakes, is an offence, and so is disturbing them.

  • Bakhira and Parvati Arga are notified sanctuaries and Ramsar sites. Entry is through

the forest department; local boatmen know the rules.

  • Keep away from the water's edge at the roosts at dusk. Do not use lights or recorded

calls.

What a useful record is उपयोगी अभिलेख क्या है

A photograph, even a poor one, with the place and the date. For a flock, a rough count and the time of day. Send it through the About page. A record from any lake in Basti district, or from Samda, Sirsa or Udaila in Ayodhya, would be the first for this atlas. Birders who keep lists on eBird should keep doing so; a link to the checklist is a record too.

What we do not know जो हमें नहीं पता

  • Which lakes in Basti district hold wintering ducks at all. No survey exists.
  • Whether the Bakhira flocks move to smaller waters nearby, and which ones.
  • The arrival and departure dates for any species in any year here. These are the simplest

facts a school can collect: the first and last date each winter for the geese on the nearest taal.

  • The full checklist of Bakhira. BirdLife says it has never been published.
